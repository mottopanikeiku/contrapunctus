IIComposers

1803–1869

Hector Berlioz

He made orchestral colour, the position of a sound in space, and the return of a remembered melody into structural events. 1805–1847

Fanny Hensel

Her Berlin musical gatherings supported a body of songs, piano cycles and chamber works whose public publication began only near the end of her life. 1809–1847

Felix Mendelssohn

A composer-conductor who turned quicksilver string writing, Bachian counterpoint and remembered landscapes into tightly organised concert music. 1811–1886

Franz Liszt

The touring pianist who made a single theme change character, and eventually stripped virtuosity down to a few unresolved notes. 1813–1883

Richard Wagner

An opera composer who made the orchestra remember what the characters conceal, and built a theatre for the result. 1839–1881

Modest Mussorgsky

He made Russian speech, public crowds and the noises of ordinary life determine the shape of opera. 1840–1893

Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky

He used the timing of dance and theatre to make a symphony’s return, interruption or collapse physically audible.
 1844–1908

Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov

A naval officer turned teacher who made orchestral register and symmetrical scales the machinery of Russian fairy-tale opera.
