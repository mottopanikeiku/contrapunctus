Life

In February 2007 Simon Rattle was rehearsing Tevot with the Berlin Philharmonic. The violins needed several attempts at their very high notes. Thomas Adès, who had written them, sat listening; when Rattle called him up to the podium, he could scarcely say anything. He told Tom Service afterwards that he had been overcome by the sound. Rattle shouted in his ear: “You have to say, thank you!” Rattle had commissioned Asyla for Birmingham a decade earlier and took it to his inaugural concert as Berlin’s chief conductor in 2002.

1971–94Training and early works to Arcadiana

Adès was born in London on 1 March 1971. His mother, Dawn, is an art historian; his father, Timothy, a poet and translator. He studied piano with Paul Berkowitz and composition with Robert Saxton at the Guildhall School, then went to King’s College, Cambridge, from 1989 to 1992, studying with Alexander Goehr and Robin Holloway. He also had chamber-music coaching with György Kurtág, first in Hungary in 1988–89 and again at the International Musicians Seminar in 1993–94. Adès continued to perform as a pianist, playing other composers’ music as well as his own.

In Darknesse Visible (1992), he took John Dowland’s In darkness let me dwell to pieces. Notes from the song become piano tremolos; a line that a singer could sustain moves back and forth between the pianist’s hands. Living Toys (1993) is for fourteen players. A horn plays among gongs and small trumpets in Angels; the bull of Aurochs enters on a bass B. In H.A.L.’s Death, a slowing waltz accompanies the dying computer, and a sopranino recorder represents the astronaut.

The seven movements of Arcadiana (1994) refer to a gondolier in Venice, Mozart’s enchanted kingdom, Schubert’s water song and Watteau’s lovers embarking for Cythera. The central movement, Et… (tango mortale), takes its cue from Poussin’s Et in Arcadia ego, in which death is present in the pastoral retreat. In it a tango is interrupted by slides and pizzicato. Near the end, O Albion gives the four players a quiet chorale in full chords. One more movement follows, Lethe, named after the river of forgetting.

1995–2001Powder Her Face and Asyla

Powder Her Face reached the Cheltenham Festival in 1995. Philip Hensher’s libretto drew on Margaret, Duchess of Argyll, and the divorce that had made her sex life public property. The opera, with its crowded social world, is written for four singers and fifteen players. The instrumentalists’ equipment includes an accordion, fishing reels and scrap metal; the opening brass music passes straight into the Maid’s laughter. There are tangos and scraps of fashionable popular song, often interrupted by a misplaced accent or an unexpected note. The notorious sexual scene attracted the headlines.

AsylaThe Latin plural can mean sanctuaries or places of confinement. Adès uses the word in both senses for his four-movement orchestral work.

Rattle and the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra gave the first performance of Asyla in 1997. Cowbells are heard at the opening, and a bass oboe plays a low solo in the second movement. Six percussionists and an upright piano tuned a quarter-tone flat are added to the orchestra. In the third movement, Ecstasio, the orchestra plays dance-club music, repeating its figures with exact precision. In his 2007 interview with Service, Adès compared composing it to tuning a radio: “It was as if the music was on the radio, and I could tune my brain in and find it.” By the time of Tevot he used another image: “But now I think it’s more like flying a plane”.

A Hallé residency in 1993–95 led to chamber and orchestral performances. From 1999 to 2008 he directed the Aldeburgh Festival, the institution Britten had founded. His own piano repertory included Couperin and Janáček. In 2001 he played the première of his Piano Quintet with the Arditti Quartet in Melbourne. The piece has a sonata exposition with a repeat, and its players are soon counting different metres.

Equal divisions of the octaveA chain of major thirds divides an octave into three steps; minor thirds take four, whole tones six. When these lines move together, the chords they form keep changing. The Piano Quintet and The Tempest both use this device.

One instrumental line climbs by major thirds while another climbs by minor thirds, and an ordinary chord opens out into a harmony that is harder to name. In their conversations published in 2012, Adès told Service, “I don’t believe at all in the official distinction between tonal and atonal music.”

2004–13The Tempest and after

Words for singingMeredith Oakes rewrote Shakespeare for The Tempest. Her lines are short and rhyming, which leaves Adès room to stretch a syllable, repeat a phrase or have two people sing together.

Adès conducted the first performance of The Tempest at Covent Garden on 10 February 2004. Prospero, who governs an island and a daughter, is a baritone. Ariel is a soprano and sings high above him, and Caliban, a tenor, has some of the opera’s most lyrical music. Miranda and Ferdinand have a long duet in Act II. At the end Prospero drowns the book and breaks the staff through which he has commanded the others.

The Violin Concerto Concentric Paths, written for Anthony Marwood in 2005, places its greatest weight in the middle movement, Paths. The soloist plays chords across all four strings while brass chords descend beneath. Repeated harmonies return in changed scoring as the violin moves from rough multiple stops to a high, sustained line. The two outer movements, Rings and Rounds, are lighter. In the string quartet The Four Quarters (2010), the depiction of a day begins with high violin notes above slower viola and cello. Morning is represented by pizzicato. The last movement depicts a twenty-fifth hour, in dance-like bars of twenty-five semiquavers each.

From 2013Totentanz and The Exterminating Angel

At the 2013 Proms, Simon Keenlyside and Christianne Stotijn sang Totentanz. Keenlyside sang Death; Stotijn sang all the people he summoned. The German text came from the dance-of-death frieze formerly in Lübeck’s Marienkirche, destroyed during the Second World War. Pope, emperor and lesser mortals pass through the score in turn, and the last is an infant. For the infant the orchestra, until then violent, plays a lullaby. The singers repeat tanzen (to dance) while the music descends into the lowest instruments.

“Operas should instead be absurd in a way that is truer than reality,” Adès told Service in the conversations published in 2012. His next opera, The Exterminating Angel, opened at Salzburg in 2016. Tom Cairns wrote the libretto with Adès, based on Luis Buñuel and Luis Alcoriza’s 1962 film. In it, wealthy guests return from the opera, dine and then find that they cannot leave the room. An ondes Martenot is used for the sound of their invisible jailer. Leticia, who is an opera singer herself, has a very high soprano part. One character complains that there are no coffee spoons, and later a woman sings a lullaby to a dead lamb.

Boston appointed Adès its Symphony Orchestra’s artistic partner in 2016. Kirill Gerstein gave the première of his Piano Concerto there on 7 March 2019, with the composer conducting. The concerto is in three movements, fast, slow and fast, and has a formidable solo part. In the finale a three-chord call introduces a fast, tumbling theme. Later the piano introduces another theme, which Adès likened to a ball bouncing downstairs. The complete Dante Project was first given by the Royal Ballet in 2021, choreographed by Wayne McGregor and designed by Tacita Dean. Adès’s score is in three parts, Inferno, Purgatorio and Paradiso. It includes a ferocious piano solo for the suicides in Hell and, on the mountain of Purgatory, recorded voices from Jerusalem’s Great Ades Synagogue.

The Sound

♮Harmony

A bass can climb by major thirds while a middle part climbs by minor thirds and the top line takes whole-tone steps. Where the three lines meet they form changing chords, some familiar and some not. The Piano Quintet and The Tempest use this sort of mechanism. The whole-tone line needs twice as many steps as the bass to reach the next octave.

𝅘𝅥Rhythm & metre

In The Four Quarters, the second violin repeats a thirteen-beat pattern through Days while the other players play independent material. The finale, The Twenty-Fifth Hour, puts twenty-five semiquavers into each bar. Its recurring dance alternates a long step and a short one. The phrases still meet the barline, and the accents divide the bar unevenly.

𝄞Register & colour

The bass oboe’s solo in Asyla has a grainier, lower sound than the ordinary orchestral oboe. At the other extreme, Ariel in The Tempest sings so high that the words sound strange. Adès often sets his players far apart in register. In Nightfalls, the opening movement of The Four Quarters, high violin figures are held above slower viola and cello parts.

𝄋Form & recurrence

The Piano Quintet repeats its sonata exposition. Concentric Paths builds its long middle movement, a chaconne, over returning harmonies. Across these returns the solo violin moves from fierce four-string chords to a sustained line high above the orchestra, and the light dance-like finale follows.

𝄡Old music

The notes of Darknesse Visible come from Dowland’s In darkness let me dwell. Adès broke the song into tremolos and widely separated piano sounds, keeping its pitches but changing their registers and attack. In Arcadiana, a Schubert song gives one movement its title and figuration. Another movement of the quartet is a tango, interrupted by glissandos and pizzicato.

    In Angels a horn plays among gongs; in Aurochs a bull charges on a bass B. In H.A.L.’s Death the dying computer is given a slowing waltz, and the astronaut is played by a sopranino recorder.
  2. 1994
    ArcadianaString quartet
    The seven movements refer to imagined retreats, among them Schubert’s water, Watteau’s Cythera and an English chorale, O Albion. The central movement, a tango of death, refers to Poussin’s tomb inscription.
  3. 1995
    Powder Her FaceChamber opera in two acts; four singers and fifteen players
    Philip Hensher’s Duchess of Argyll is surrounded by servants, lovers and judges, all played by a small cast. The pit contains an accordion, fishing reels and scrap metal; a tango opens and closes the opera.
  4. 1997
    AsylaOp. 17Large orchestra with bass oboe, six percussionists and keyboards
    Written for Rattle and the Birmingham orchestra. The dance-club movement Ecstasio is the third of four. The upright piano is tuned a quarter-tone flat, so its notes sound a quarter-tone below the pitch of the rest of the orchestra.
  5. 2004
    The TempestOpera in three acts; soloists, chorus and orchestra
    Meredith Oakes rewrote Shakespeare in short, singable lines. Ariel is a very high soprano, Caliban a lyrical tenor, and both contest the baritone Prospero’s ownership of the island.
  6. 2005
    Violin Concerto “Concentric Paths”Violin and orchestra
    Written for Anthony Marwood, with a long central chaconne between the quicker Rings and Rounds. In the central movement the four-string chords eventually give way to a high line above the orchestra.
  7. 2010
    The Four QuartersString quartet
    The Emerson Quartet gave the première in 2011. Night is set in high violin notes and morning in pizzicato. The finale, depicting a twenty-fifth hour, is in 25/16.
  8. 2013
    TotentanzMezzo-soprano, baritone and large orchestra
    A baritone Death addresses a succession of people, all sung by one mezzo-soprano. The German text follows the figures in Lübeck’s lost dance-of-death frieze and ends with an infant and a lullaby.
  9. 2016
    The Exterminating AngelOpera in three acts; large cast, chorus and orchestra with ondes Martenot
    After Buñuel and Alcoriza’s film, with a libretto by Tom Cairns in collaboration with Adès. The guests cannot leave their dinner party; an ondes Martenot plays the unexplained force that keeps them there.
  10. 2018
    Concerto for Piano and OrchestraPiano and orchestra
    Kirill Gerstein gave the première in Boston in March 2019. In the finale a three-chord call is followed by a falling theme; a later theme was compared by Adès to a ball bouncing downstairs.

Sister Arts

Hogarth shows Ferdinand being brought to Miranda, with Prospero at her side and Ariel making music above them. Caliban, at the right, crushes a dove beneath his foot. The painting and Adès’s opera share a source in Shakespeare’s island, where a father watches his daughter fall in love while his servants carry out his supernatural work. Adès wrote Ariel’s part for a high soprano and Caliban’s for a lyrical tenor. These are the two figures at the edges of Hogarth’s scene.

William Shakespeare

Ariel’s song: Full fathom five

The Tempest, c. 1610–11; printed 1623
EnglishFull fathom five thy father lies. Of his bones are coral made. Those are pearls that were his eyes. Nothing of him that doth fade But doth suffer a sea change Into something rich and strange.

In Act I, scene 2, Ariel sings this to Ferdinand, who believes his father has drowned. Meredith Oakes’s operatic version, which shortens Shakespeare’s lines for Adès, begins “Five fathoms deep / Your father lies”. Ariel, a high soprano, leads Ferdinand towards Miranda. The king, whose bones the song says have become coral, is still alive.

Watteau submitted this painting to the French Royal Academy in 1717 as his reception piece. Its lovers gave Adès the fifth movement of Arcadiana, L’Embarquement. In his programme note he sent their boat towards Debussy’s L’Isle joyeuse. The other watery destinations in the quartet include a Venetian night, Schubert’s river and Lethe, the last of them.

Lineage

Adès
Passed toContemporary British operaInterval-cycle harmonyTwenty-first-century concerto writing

Adès has played Couperin and Janáček and worked with Dowland’s music at the piano. He has also engaged with Schubert and Debussy. Kurtág coached him as a chamber musician. In 2007 Adès arranged two of Nancarrow’s studies for player piano for two pianos, so that their competing speeds are played by human hands.
