GiacomoPuccini

Born 22 December 1858, Lucca, TuscanyDied 29 November 1924, Brussels

He could make a whole theatre wait with a woman at a window, then break its heart with a tune it had heard before.

Life

Puccini began at the organ. His family had supplied Lucca with church musicians for generations, and when his father Michele died in 1864, the five-year-old Giacomo was expected to continue the trade. He studied in the town, played in its churches and completed a Mass in 1880. But in 1876 he had heard Verdi’s Aida in Pisa. The theatre won. A scholarship helped him into the Milan Conservatory, where Antonio Bazzini and Amilcare Ponchielli taught him composition. His graduation piece of 1883, Capriccio sinfonico, contains the bustling music that would eventually open La bohème.

His first opera nearly went nowhere. He submitted Le Villi to the Sonzogno competition on the last possible day, 31 December 1883; it failed even to receive a mention. The librettist Ferdinando Fontana arranged a private hearing at the journalist Marco Sala’s house, with Arrigo Boito among the guests. A subscription paid for a performance at Milan’s Teatro Dal Verme the following May. Giulio Ricordi bought the opera and commissioned another. Edgar, first performed at La Scala in 1889, was a failure that Puccini spent years trying to repair.

1891–1904A house by the lake

In 1891 he settled at Torre del Lago, beside Lake Massaciuccoli, with Elvira Bonturi and their son Antonio. Elvira had left her husband for him; they could marry only after that husband died, and did so in 1904. Puccini hunted on the lake and acquired a taste for motor cars. Manon Lescaut, which opened in Turin in 1893, paid for a less precarious life. Massenet had already made an opera from Prévost’s novel. Puccini took the same young woman through four abrupt changes of fortune, ending with her death in a desert outside New Orleans.

Several writers had a hand in Manon. For the next three operas, Ricordi assembled a durable team: Luigi Illica worked out the action, Giuseppe Giacosa made the verse, and Puccini sent it back. Giacosa repeatedly threatened to leave. In an 1895 letter to Ricordi, Puccini defended the short last act of La bohème: “Brevity is a great merit.” Further down the same letter he worried about the printed slurs in the violin parts and asked to check them himself. A dying woman’s scene and a violinist’s bowing occupied the same sheet of paper.

1896–1904The garret, the prison, the window

Toscanini conducted the first La bohème in Turin on 1 February 1896. Its young Parisians burn a manuscript to keep warm, dodge the rent and go out for supper. Puccini was ruthless with Murger’s stories: an entire proposed act disappeared. In the Latin Quarter scene, children and street vendors crowd around the lovers while a military band passes. Back in the garret in Act IV, the orchestra brings back the music of Mimì’s first visit. The tune remembers that evening while the woman in the bed is dying.

Tosca began with Sarah Bernhardt. Puccini saw her in Sardou’s play in Milan in February 1889 and went to Turin to see her again a month later. His opera reached the stage in Rome in January 1900. Three violent chords introduce the police chief Scarpia before anybody sings. In Act II, Scarpia questions Tosca while her lover is tortured in the next room; a scream through the door undoes her resistance. Vissi d’arte stops the bargaining for a few minutes. Then Scarpia resumes it.

Butterfly’s versionsThe La Scala score has two acts. Puccini went on changing the work after Brescia; later versions differ in cuts, added music and the division of the long vigil.

Later that year, in London, Puccini saw David Belasco’s Madame Butterfly. The vigil fascinated him: a woman waiting through the night for a man who will destroy her. In the opera, the humming chorus and the orchestra carry the hours from evening to dawn. Pinkerton’s music includes The Star-Spangled Banner; Japanese melodies surround Butterfly’s household. She believes in the marriage long after everybody else understands its terms. At La Scala on 17 February 1904, the audience booed the opera. Puccini withdrew it, revised it and won his public back at Brescia that May.

1909–18A girl with a revolver

In 1909, the household at Torre del Lago broke apart. Elvira falsely accused their servant Doria Manfredi of having an affair with Puccini. Doria died by suicide; Elvira was convicted of defamation. Work on La fanciulla del West stalled. The new opera, again after Belasco, eventually opened at the Metropolitan in December 1910, with Emmy Destinn and Enrico Caruso, Toscanini conducting. Minnie runs a saloon in a California mining camp. She saves her outlaw lover at cards and later confronts the men who intend to hang him. This time the soprano leaves the stage alive.

VerismoThe name places him beside the everyday violence of Mascagni and Leoncavallo. Puccini’s theatre also has room for a Florentine inheritance fraud and a Chinese princess who sets riddles to her suitors.

The music is some of his boldest. Fanciulla opens with widely spaced augmented chords and whole-tone harmony; the miners’ homesick ballad grows into a chorus, and the saloon feels suddenly full of men who would like to be somewhere else. La rondine, first heard in Monte Carlo in 1917, grew from a Viennese operetta commission. Its waltzes accompany another attempted escape: a courtesan tries to begin an ordinary life with a young lover, then leaves him. In 1918 the Metropolitan staged Il trittico: a murder aboard a Seine barge, a nun’s loss of her child, and Gianni Schicchi, in which a roomful of greedy relatives watches a stranger steal their inheritance.

1920–26The unfinished kiss

For Turandot, Adami and Simoni turned to Carlo Gozzi’s fairy-tale theatre. In July 1920 Puccini wrote to Simoni: “I am already beginning to improvise in the evenings — and I am looking for chords and turns as Chinese as possible.” At Bagni di Lucca that summer, the collaborators visited Baron Fassini Camossi and heard his Chinese music box. Three of its melodies entered the opera. Around them Puccini built a city of gongs, massed voices and executions. Liù, the slave girl, sings quietly inside it.

The last duetBy March 1924 Puccini had orchestrated the opera through Liù’s funeral. The transformation of Turandot, from executioner to lover, still troubled him. He kept asking his librettists for another version.

In the autumn of 1924 he went to Brussels for treatment for throat cancer. He died there on 29 November, with the final duet still in sketches. Franco Alfano completed it; after Toscanini objected, Alfano made a substantially shorter second ending. At the first performance, at La Scala on 25 April 1926, Toscanini stopped after Liù’s funeral. At the next performance he conducted the completion too. The princess finally sang her declaration of love, but Puccini had never heard it.

The Sound

𝄋Dramatic memory

The orchestra remembers things the characters cannot bear to say. In the last act of La bohème, music from Mimì’s first meeting with Rodolfo returns beside her deathbed. It still carries the warmth of that evening. Puccini’s recurring motives can hurt most when they come back almost unchanged: the tune is familiar, and the room has become unbearable.

𝅘𝅥Vocal line

Puccini lets ordinary talk open into song. Mimì begins her introduction with her name and her work as a seamstress; by the time she describes the first sunlight of spring, the phrase has broadened across the orchestra. In Tosca, the clarinet sings the melody of E lucevan le stelle before Cavaradossi finds the words for his memories.

♯Harmony

The opening of La fanciulla del West spreads augmented chords across the orchestra and uses the whole-tone scale, whose equal steps remove the familiar pull of a semitone. Soon a homesick ballad is passing among the miners. Puccini moves easily between such strange chords and a tune anybody in the saloon might sing; both belong to his California.

𝄞Offstage sound

In Tosca, a cantata is being sung outside Scarpia’s room while he eats supper and plans an arrest. Later, Cavaradossi’s cries come through the torture-chamber door. At the start of Act III, a shepherd sings and Rome’s bells announce the dawn. Each sound places another person beyond the stage; the condemned man has to wait while the city wakes.

𝄾Waiting

Butterfly’s night vigil takes away the words. The chorus hums, the orchestra continues, and a woman watches for a ship’s officer who has already married somebody else. Puccini gives waiting its full duration on stage. The long pause makes the return of ordinary conversation cruel: at dawn the household must discover what the audience has feared all night.

    First performed together at the Metropolitan in December 1918. After the deaths in Il tabarro and Suor Angelica, Gianni Schicchi makes a dead man the means of a very profitable joke.

Sister Arts

Hohenstein’s Paris was built in Turin for the première on 1 February 1896. The café and streets give the lovers somewhere to sit while vendors, children and a military band fill the square around them. Musetta has to win back Marcello’s attention in the middle of all this traffic.

Henry Murger

Scènes de la vie de bohème — preface

1851
FrenchLa Bohème, c’est le stage de la vie artistique ; c’est la préface de l’Académie, de l’Hôtel-Dieu ou de la Morgue.
Our translationBohemia is the apprenticeship of artistic life; it is the preface to the Academy, the Hôtel-Dieu hospital or the morgue.

Murger’s preface offers his young artists three destinations, and two are places for the sick and the dead. Illica and Giacosa drew the opera from his stories and their stage adaptation. Mimì reaches the garret for the last time because she wants to be with Rodolfo. Murger’s preface is less sentimental about the chances of surviving poverty.

Lineage

Drew fromGiuseppe VerdiRichard WagnerAmilcare PonchielliFrench spoken theatre
Puccini
Passed toErich Wolfgang KorngoldFilm scoringTwentieth-century Italian opera

Verdi’s Aida helped draw him to the theatre; Wagner offered an orchestra that could remember earlier scenes. Puccini made that memory act quickly: a familiar phrase can tell us what a lover has forgotten or a policeman conceals. His whole-tone chords put Fanciulla beside Debussy, and his returning love music points towards the film scores of Korngold.
