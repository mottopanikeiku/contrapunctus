Giacomo Puccini

Born 22 December 1858, Lucca, Tuscany

An opera composer who made a remembered tune, a stage noise or a change of orchestral register determine the timing of a scene.

Life

Puccini was born into a dynasty of church musicians in Lucca in 1858. His father Michele died when Giacomo was five, and the expectation that the boy would inherit the family profession survived him. Puccini studied locally, played the organ and composed sacred music, including a Mass completed in 1880. Hearing Verdi’s Aida in Pisa in 1876 helped turn his attention towards the theatre. A scholarship enabled him to enter the Milan Conservatory in 1880, where he studied with Antonio Bazzini and Amilcare Ponchielli. His graduation work, the orchestral Capriccio sinfonico, already contains material he would reuse at the beginning of La bohème.

His first opera, Le Villi, failed to win the Sonzogno competition for which it was written, but a performance in Milan in 1884 attracted the publisher Giulio Ricordi. Ricordi supported revisions, further performances and the much less successful Edgar, premiered at La Scala in 1889. The partnership mattered because Puccini composed slowly, demanded repeated alterations to his texts and needed protection against a premature commercial verdict. The theatre was a collective workshop: librettist, publisher, designer and conductor all contributed practical decisions, although Puccini insisted on controlling the length and placement of the decisive moments.

Torre del LagoThe stage and the household

From 1891 Torre del Lago, beside Lake Massaciuccoli, became his principal home. He lived with Elvira Bonturi, who had left her husband, and their son Antonio, born in 1886; Puccini and Elvira could marry only after her first husband’s death, in 1904. Hunting, boats and motor cars occupied the time between work and theatre visits. In 1909 their servant Doria Manfredi died by suicide after Elvira accused her of an affair with Puccini. The accusation was unfounded. The ensuing legal scandal damaged the household and interrupted composition.

Manon Lescaut, first performed in Turin in 1893, established him internationally. Abbé Prévost’s novel had already furnished Jules Massenet with an opera; Puccini’s version concentrates on abrupt changes of situation. Several writers worked on the libretto. Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa then became the principal team for La bohème, Tosca and Madama Butterfly: Illica shaped scenarios and Giacosa the finished verse, under continual pressure from the composer. Puccini wanted words that could be sung intelligibly at the precise point where an action changed.

Beyond “verismo”The term links Puccini to the Italian theatrical realism of Mascagni and Leoncavallo. His subjects range further, to an eighteenth-century comedy, a Japanese tragedy and a Chinese fairy tale, and draw on varied harmonic resources.

1896–1904Three different clocks

La bohème opened in Turin in 1896 under Arturo Toscanini. Its four tableaux compress Henry Murger’s episodes of impoverished Parisian artists into a cycle of meeting, separation and recollection. The orchestra can recall a tune while the characters discuss something else: Mimì’s return in the last act makes the earlier music a measure of what has changed. In Act II, vendors, children, the military parade and the principals occupy the street simultaneously. Puccini organises their overlapping activity through distinct rhythms and entrances; the apparently casual crowd scene is carefully notated ensemble writing.

Tosca, premiered in Rome in 1900, takes Victorien Sardou’s play into three sharply specified Roman locations. Its opening chords announce Scarpia before he appears. Bells, an offstage cantata, a shepherd’s song and a firing squad establish space, public ritual and the time remaining to the protagonists. The second act’s torture is heard from another room while the main scene continues in front of us. Puccini can make an interruption or a silence more consequential than an aria, then allow “Vissi d’arte” to suspend the machinery briefly.

He saw David Belasco’s Madame Butterfly in London in 1900 and recognised the theatrical possibilities despite his limited English. The opera failed at La Scala in February 1904, then succeeded in a revised form at Brescia that May. Further revisions followed. The central waiting scene passes from evening to dawn through orchestral music and the wordless humming chorus. Japanese melodies coexist with Western lyricism and an American anthem quotation in a drama of unequal power. Butterfly’s confidence and the audience’s knowledge are deliberately allowed to diverge.

Versions matterButterfly exists in several authorial states. “The original version” means the two-act La Scala score; later versions incorporate differing revisions and cuts.

The later operasNew harmonic resources

Belasco supplied another subject in The Girl of the Golden West. La fanciulla del West, premiered at the Metropolitan Opera in 1910 with Emmy Destinn and Enrico Caruso under Toscanini, gives a California mining community unusually elaborate orchestral treatment. Whole-tone formations and unresolved added notes sit beside broad tonal melody. The heroine Minnie survives and wins the condemned man’s freedom by confronting the miners with their own memories of kindness. La rondine, first performed in Monte Carlo in 1917, grew from an operetta commission but became an opera about an attempted escape from a courtesan’s established life.

Il trittico reached New York in 1918: the river-barge murder of Il tabarro, the convent tragedy of Suor Angelica and the inheritance fraud of Gianni Schicchi form three contrasting one-act operas. Puccini next worked on Carlo Gozzi’s Turandot, with Giuseppe Adami and Renato Simoni. Its percussion, choral blocks, pentatonic melodies and harsh harmonic collisions extend the vocabulary well beyond Bohème. The unresolved problem was dramatic as much as musical: how to make Turandot’s transformation into a loving woman credible after Liù’s death.

Puccini died in Brussels in 1924 after treatment for throat cancer, leaving that final transformation unfinished. Franco Alfano completed the opera from the surviving sketches; Toscanini substantially shortened his conclusion. At the La Scala premiere in 1926, Toscanini stopped after the music Puccini had completed, following Liù’s funeral procession. Later performances used the completion. “Nessun dorma” therefore belongs to a fully composed portion of the opera, whereas the final duet and resolution require an editorial decision about music Puccini never brought to a finished score.

The sound

Harmony, line, rhythm, colour and form.

Dramatic memory

A motive can return in the orchestra without being acknowledged by the singer. In La bohème this makes the final act remember the first; in Tosca, Scarpia’s opening chords identify a coercive presence before he enters. Puccini uses recurring motives to control what the audience knows at each moment.

Vocal line

Conversation often moves in short repeated notes or small intervals, then expands into a sustained line at a dramatic turning point. The orchestra can prepare that expansion before the singer reaches it. An aria grows from the pulse, harmonic direction and remembered motives already established by the scene.

Harmony

Functional cadences coexist with whole-tone collections, pentatonic melody, parallel chords and dissonances retained as colour. La fanciulla del West makes particularly extensive use of whole-tone harmony; Turandot places simple pentatonic tunes within much more abrasive orchestral and choral structures.

Orchestral space

Offstage music is written into the action: the cantata in Tosca, the waiting vigil in Butterfly, the street bands and vendors in Bohème. A sound from outside the visible room can confirm an event or contradict what a character believes. Doubling the voice is only one of the orchestra’s functions.

Time & interruption

Puccini alternates measured waiting with abrupt acceleration. The dawn music of Tosca expands the interval before the execution; the murder at the end of Il tabarro collapses it. Rests, stage directions and orchestral transitions therefore belong to the dramatic construction as much as the sung text.

Documented use

Hohenstein designed the setting for the first production at Turin’s Teatro Regio on 1 February 1896. The crowded square is part of the composition’s working apparatus: café tables, street traffic, vendors and a passing military band must be visible or audible in relation to one another. Puccini’s separate musical groups inhabit the spaces that the designer provides.

Documented useHenry Murger

Scènes de la vie de bohème — preface

1851
FrenchLa Bohème, c’est le stage de la vie artistique ; c’est la préface de l’Académie, de l’Hôtel-Dieu ou de la Morgue.
English · Contrapunctus translationBohemia is the apprenticeship of artistic life; it is the preface to the Academy, the Hôtel-Dieu hospital or the morgue.

Murger names three possible destinations, only one of them success. Puccini’s librettists compressed characters and incidents from the stories and their theatrical adaptation. The final act makes the hospital-and-morgue possibilities concrete, giving the preface’s satire a tragic weight. The original preface sets out the stakes of “bohemian” life.

Later connectionsErich Wolfgang KorngoldFilm scoringTwentieth-century Italian opera

Puccini took Wagnerian thematic memory into an Italian theatre governed by the intelligibility of words and the timing of action. His later harmony belongs beside the innovations of Debussy and Strauss. Film composers inherited the practical possibility of letting an orchestra remember what the visible scene conceals.
