John Adams

Born 15 February 1947, Worcester, Massachusetts, United States

Repeated pulses support changing harmonies, long orchestral arguments and operas about the people who make public history.

Life

John Coolidge Adams was born in Worcester, Massachusetts, in 1947 and grew up in New England, where his father taught him the clarinet. His mother performed in amateur musical theatre; orchestral records, marching bands and popular song belonged to the same household as instrumental lessons. At Harvard he studied composition with teachers including Leon Kirchner, who had himself studied with Schoenberg, and became sufficiently accomplished as a clarinettist to substitute with the Boston Symphony Orchestra. That experience of playing inside an orchestra preceded his career as a composer and conductor of one.

In 1971 Adams moved to the San Francisco Bay Area. He taught at the San Francisco Conservatory from 1972 to 1982 and directed its New Music Ensemble, testing compositional ideas in student performances. Electronic music supplied another laboratory: oscillators and tape loops demonstrated how continuous motion could arise from very small amounts of material. The repeating patterns of Terry Riley, Steve Reich and Philip Glass offered alternatives to the postwar serial music he had encountered in academic settings. He retained pulse and consonance while allowing the process to change.

The Bay AreaGiving loops a direction

GatesIn an electronic circuit a gate changes a signal’s state. In Adams’s early piano music, a gate is an abrupt change of modal field, bypassing the dominant preparation of a conventional modulation.

Phrygian Gates (1977) translates that electronic conception into a continuous piano texture. Shifts of register, accent and harmonic field divide its changing Lydian and Phrygian areas. Shaker Loops followed in 1978, salvaging material from an unsuccessful string quartet called Wavemaker. Adams expanded the group to seven strings and replaced an over-restrictive scheme with four connected movements. The title joins bowed shaking and trilling to his childhood awareness of the Shaker community near Canterbury, New Hampshire.

Edo de Waart’s San Francisco Symphony gave these procedures an orchestral setting. Harmonium, premiered in 1981, sets John Donne and Emily Dickinson for large chorus and orchestra. A single repeated pitch acquires neighbours, becomes a chord and then a much larger accumulation of sound. The Dickinson movements place a slow meditation on death beside the rapid activity of Wild Nights. Adams became the orchestra’s composer-in-residence in 1982. Regular access to players allowed him to use repetition to prepare climaxes, contrasts and harmonic departures.

Harmonielehre (1984–85) gives this distinction its largest early demonstration. Its title borrows that of Schoenberg’s 1911 harmony textbook, but the three-movement orchestral work returns to the chromatic resources of Mahler, Sibelius and the young Schoenberg. Reiterated E minor chords launch a first movement with a long central relaxation. The slow movement, The Anfortas Wound, places an exposed trumpet line over shifting minor triads and reaches a dissonant climax explicitly indebted to Mahler’s Tenth. The finale builds towards E♭ major: the distance from the opening E minor is harmonic as well as emotional.

Public figuresAn opera behind the photograph

The fixed beatThe woodblock begins Short Ride in a Fast Machine in regular crotchets. The surrounding rhythms turn that reliable beat into a constraint through which the orchestra must pass.

The four-minute orchestral fanfare Short Ride in a Fast Machine (1986) compresses the rhythmic argument: woodblock, trumpets, woodwind and keyboards establish different divisions of a shared pulse, while the accumulating orchestra changes the weight of each accent. The following year Nixon in China opened at Houston Grand Opera. Peter Sellars proposed Richard Nixon’s 1972 visit as an operatic subject; Alice Goodman supplied a verse libretto. The public ceremonies of the first acts give way to private recollection, making the protagonists’ understanding of their own actions part of the drama.

The cast’s different vocal types resist a single tone of political caricature. Nixon is a baritone, Mao a high tenor, Madame Mao a coloratura soprano; Pat Nixon’s lyrical writing and Chou En-lai’s reflective baritone occupy different musical spaces. The orchestra includes four saxophones, synthesizer and piano alongside conventional winds and strings. Repeated figures can suggest ceremonial momentum or the compulsive reiteration of an idea. Dance rhythms coexist with continuous symphonic development. The last act reduces the scale of the public spectacle.

The Wound-Dresser, written in 1988 for Sanford Sylvan, brings another public history down to the scale of one voice. Adams begins his Whitman setting inside a Civil War hospital, omitting the poem’s prefatory invitation to recount heroic events. Solo violin and trumpet become partners to the baritone. Adams’s father was dying of Alzheimer’s disease while he worked on the piece, and he connected Whitman’s practical acts of nursing with his mother’s care. The setting gives each bodily detail room to register; the sentences keep their unequal lengths over a regular underlying motion.

After minimalismFaster counterpoint, slower testimony

Adams, Goodman and Sellars returned to recent history in The Death of Klinghoffer, premiered in Brussels in 1991. Its subject is the murder of the disabled Jewish-American passenger Leon Klinghoffer by Palestinian hijackers aboard the Achille Lauro in 1985. The opera’s opposing choruses and extended lyrical reflections provoked disputes about how art distributes sympathy and historical explanation. Meanwhile, instrumental works such as the Chamber Symphony of 1992 increased contrapuntal density and rhythmic unpredictability. Adams connected its writing both to Schoenberg’s chamber music and to the abrupt changes and virtuoso instrumental activity of cartoon scores.

History and poetryDoctor Atomic combines historical material with poetry and sacred texts. Oppenheimer’s Act I closing aria sets Donne’s Batter my heart as a theatrical interpretation of his predicament.

On the Transmigration of Souls, premiered by the New York Philharmonic in September 2002, uses orchestra, adult and children’s choirs, recorded voices and city sounds to commemorate the attacks of the previous year. Names and fragments of personal remembrance replace a narrative reconstruction; the work received the 2003 Pulitzer Prize for Music. Doctor Atomic, premiered in San Francisco in 2005, approaches the first atomic-bomb test through the scientists’ waiting. Sellars assembled the libretto. The conflict between measured clock time and subjective dread gives the familiar Adams pulse a different theatrical function.

Later stage works widened the historical range: Girls of the Golden West (2017), again with Sellars, draws on documents from the California Gold Rush. For Antony and Cleopatra, premiered in San Francisco in 2022, Adams adapted Shakespeare with consultation from director Elkhanah Pulitzer. His work as a conductor continues alongside composition, including performances of younger composers. The practical knowledge acquired as a clarinettist remains relevant to scores in which a player may have to sustain a local repeating pattern while hearing a different beat in another orchestral section.

The sound

Harmony, line, rhythm, colour and form.

Pulse & metre

Accents, rests and figures of unequal length pull against the common subdivision. In Short Ride in a Fast Machine, the woodblock makes this disagreement audible; in later works, metre changes and tuplets make the relationship less easily counted.

Harmony

Triads and modes remain identifiable, but they may change without functional preparation. Repetition holds a harmonic field steady long enough for a new bass or an altered pitch to register strongly. Harmonielehre connects this procedure to late-Romantic chromaticism.

Orchestration

Repeated keyboard and woodwind notes can supply a sharply defined surface while strings sustain longer arcs underneath. Saxophones and synthesizers in Nixon in China enlarge the pit’s associations beyond nineteenth-century opera. In The Wound-Dresser, exposed violin and trumpet solos replace that mass with specific instrumental interlocutors.

Form & counterpoint

Loops are raw material for a directed form. Adams can interrupt, accelerate, thicken or change them before an audible process has run its theoretical course. The Chamber Symphony increases the independence of the parts, putting rapid contrapuntal exchanges where an early minimalist piece might have maintained a more uniform texture.

Speech & drama

The natural stresses and unequal lengths of English sentences determine vocal rhythm. A repeating accompaniment may continue while the singer bends across its accents. Political ceremony in Nixon in China, nursing in The Wound-Dresser and waiting in Doctor Atomic therefore require different relationships between speech and pulse.

Works

A listening path, not a complete catalogue. “Hear it” plays the named recording or movement.

  1. 1977
    Phrygian GatesPiano
    A continuous span of repeating figures organised into changing Lydian and Phrygian fields. The “gates” are abrupt changes of harmonic state.
  2. 1978
    Shaker LoopsThree violins, viola, two cellos and double bass; string-orchestra version 1983
    Four linked movements: Shaking and Trembling, Hymning Slews, Loops and Verses, A Final Shaking. Bowed oscillations and trills generate a texture that changes density and speed.
  3. 1980–81
    HarmoniumMixed chorus and large orchestra
    Donne’s Negative Love followed by Dickinson’s Because I could not stop for Death and Wild Nights. The opening repeated note expands into a large-scale choral and orchestral accumulation.
  4. 1984–85
    HarmonielehreLarge orchestra with two tubas, two harps, piano and celesta
    Three movements linking repetitive processes with Mahlerian and Sibelian harmony. The opening E minor and final E♭ major stand a semitone apart; the middle movement contains a direct compositional homage to Mahler’s Tenth.
  5. 1986
    Short Ride in a Fast MachineLarge orchestra, woodblock and other percussion; optional synthesizers
    A four-minute fanfare premiered by Michael Tilson Thomas and the Pittsburgh Symphony. The clear crotchet pulse remains audible while the orchestra complicates the listener’s sense of its accents.
  6. 1987
    Nixon in ChinaOpera in three acts; soloists, chorus and orchestra
    Goodman’s verse libretto moves from the 1972 diplomatic visit’s public rituals to the participants’ private recollections. Four saxophones, synthesizer and piano contribute to the pit’s particular weight and attack.
  7. 1988
    The Wound-DresserBaritone and orchestra
    Whitman’s nursing of wounded Civil War soldiers, set for Sanford Sylvan. The vocal line follows the poem’s changing sentence lengths; solo violin and trumpet carry prominent responses.
  8. 2002
    On the Transmigration of SoulsAdult chorus, children’s chorus, orchestra and prerecorded sounds
    Commissioned by the New York Philharmonic in memory of 11 September 2001. Recorded names and fragments of remembrance coexist with orchestral and choral writing; awarded the 2003 Pulitzer Prize.
  9. 2004–05
    Doctor AtomicOpera in two acts; soloists, chorus, orchestra and sound design
    Oppenheimer and the Los Alamos community before the Trinity test. Sellars’s assembled libretto places scientific and military documents beside Donne, Baudelaire and sacred texts.
  10. 2022
    Antony and CleopatraOpera in two acts; soloists, chorus and orchestra
    Shakespeare adapted by Adams, with consultation from Elkhanah Pulitzer. Premiered by San Francisco Opera; the public language of empire and the lovers’ private speech occupy the same dramatic space.

Sister arts

Objects and texts, with the nature of each musical connection made explicit.

Shared context

The discarded Union Army jacket and canteen identify Homer’s reaper as a returning Civil War soldier. Painted in the year that Whitman published Drum-Taps, the canvas transfers attention from battle to the work of a surviving body. The link with Adams is shared subject matter: the war and its aftermath, which The Wound-Dresser approaches through bandages, amputations and bedside care.

Documented useWalt Whitman

The Wound-Dresser — excerpt

1865; later revised
EnglishBearing the bandages, water and sponge, Straight and swift to my wounded I go, Where they lie on the ground after the battle brought in, Where their priceless blood reddens the grass, the ground, Or to the rows of the hospital tent, or under the roof’d hospital, To the long rows of cots up and down each side I return, To each and all one after another I draw near, not one do I miss, An attendant follows holding a tray, he carries a refuse pail, Soon to be fill’d with clotted rags and blood, emptied, and fill’d again.

These lines begin Adams’s setting, though they occur well inside Whitman’s poem. The omission removes the old narrator’s introductory account of being asked for war stories and starts with practical labour. The sequence of unequal phrases gives the baritone a different rhythmic task from the orchestra’s recurring figures; the repetitions enact the return to each patient.

Documented useJohn Donne

Batter my heart, three-person’d God

Published 1633
EnglishBatter my heart, three-person’d God, for you As yet but knock, breathe, shine, and seek to mend; That I may rise and stand, o’erthrow me, and bend Your force to break, blow, burn, and make me new. I, like an usurp’d town to another due, Labor to admit you, but oh, to no end; Reason, your viceroy in me, me should defend, But is captiv’d, and proves weak or untrue. Yet dearly I love you, and would be lov’d fain, But am betroth’d unto your enemy; Divorce me, untie or break that knot again, Take me to you, imprison me, for I, Except you enthrall me, never shall be free, Nor ever chaste, except you ravish me.

Donne’s Holy Sonnet XIV supplies Oppenheimer’s aria at the end of Act I of Doctor Atomic. The poem asks for spiritual remaking through violent paradox; in the opera its language of breaking, burning and captivity is heard beside the impending test of an atomic weapon.

Later connectionsPostminimalist orchestral writingAmerican historical operaContemporary English-language music theatre

Adams’s relation to Schoenberg is partly a family tree through Kirchner and partly a sustained argument with musical history. The repeating cells learned from minimalism support forms with climaxes and reversals closer to the orchestral repertory. Mahler is an explicit source in Harmonielehre.
