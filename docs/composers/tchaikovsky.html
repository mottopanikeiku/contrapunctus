Born 7 May 1840, Votkinsk, RussiaDied 6 November 1893, Saint Petersburg

Life

At Kamenka on 6 July 1878, Tchaikovsky was explaining to Nadezhda von Meck how he composed. Ideas came to him in the middle of conversations, and he kept them in his head until he could sketch them on the first sheet of paper to hand. He held that waiting for the right mood was no way to work. “Inspiration is a guest who does not like to visit the idle,” he told her. He then stopped for dinner. At two o’clock he resumed, and went on to sketching, orchestration, fair copies and how far a symphony must keep to its traditional form.

1840–66Leaving the Ministry of Justice

He was born in Votkinsk, where his father managed an ironworks. He grew deeply attached to his French governess, Fanny Dürbach, and began piano lessons at the end of 1845. The profession chosen for him was the law. In 1850 he entered the preparatory class of the Imperial School of Jurisprudence in Saint Petersburg. Four years later his mother died of cholera. He graduated in 1859 and took a post at the Ministry of Justice, while giving more and more of his time to opera and concerts.

Anton Rubinstein’s new Saint Petersburg Conservatory gave him a way out of the civil service. Tchaikovsky enrolled in 1862, studied composition with Rubinstein and theory with Nikolai Zaremba, and in 1863 took leave from the ministry, resigning formally in 1867. In 1866 he moved to Moscow to teach harmony at the new conservatory run by Rubinstein’s brother Nikolai. He earned his living by teaching, writing criticism and translating textbooks, and composed in the remaining hours. He remained on the conservatory staff for twelve years.

MoscowA melody worth arguing over

Balakirev, the leader of the nationalist circle that included Mussorgsky and Rimsky-Korsakov, suggested Romeo and Juliet. He sent forceful advice about its design, and Tchaikovsky revised it repeatedly between 1869 and 1880. A wind chorale represents the Friar. The feud is set in fast string writing. The lovers’ theme is played by cor anglais and muted violas together.

Nikolai Rubinstein was an equally formidable listener. When Tchaikovsky played him the First Piano Concerto at the beginning of 1875, he greeted it with severe criticism. Hans von Bülow took it up and gave the première in Boston that October. The large D♭ major opening tune does not return after the introduction. Swan Lake followed in 1875–76. It was first staged in Moscow in 1877. The Petipa–Ivanov revival of 1895, with Riccardo Drigo’s alterations to the score, established much of the ballet in the form it is now performed.

1877–78The marriage and the letters

In July 1877 he married Antonina Milyukova, who had written to him declaring her love. Tchaikovsky was gay. He hoped that marriage would quieten gossip and give his life an acceptable public shape; Antonina expected a husband who could love her. By the autumn they were living apart. They never divorced. He went abroad, and while the marriage collapsed he continued work on the Fourth Symphony and Eugene Onegin.

Von Meck’s support made a different life possible. A wealthy widow, she began paying him an allowance in 1877, and the following year he was able to give up regular teaching. In her letters she asked him what a symphony meant and how a melody arrived. In Florence on 1 March 1878 he answered her question about the Fourth with a musical example of the opening fanfare: “This is Fate”. The fanfare interrupts the first movement’s uneasy 9/8 dance and returns in the finale, breaking into festivities built partly from In the Field Stood a Birch Tree.

By postTchaikovsky and von Meck agreed never to meet. More than 1,200 letters passed between them. She ended the allowance and the correspondence in 1890, citing financial difficulties. The end of the friendship hurt him deeply.

The letter’s postscript reads: “Yesterday I spent the evening at the National Theatre and laughed a great deal.” Italian comedy had delighted him. His letters record amusement beside despair, and his theatre does the same. In Onegin, a French guest, Monsieur Triquet, offers his little name-day song while jealousy gathers among the younger guests. The singer Yelizaveta Lavrovskaya had proposed Pushkin’s novel as an operatic subject in May 1877. Tchaikovsky went looking for a copy, read it again and spent a sleepless night planning his “lyrical scenes”. Moscow Conservatory students gave the première in 1879.

Tatyana writes her letter alone at night, with the orchestra accompanying her. Onegin answers with composure. Later, at her name-day party, the gossip that helps provoke Lensky’s fatal challenge is spread during a waltz. The final Saint Petersburg ball begins with a polonaise. Onegin finds Tatyana transformed and realises, several years too late, that he loves her.

The working yearsHouses, journeys, theatres

After 1878 Tchaikovsky travelled widely and rented country houses near Moscow, settling at Klin in 1892. He wrote the Violin Concerto in Switzerland in 1878, and the String Serenade and the 1812 overture in 1880. After Nikolai Rubinstein died, Tchaikovsky wrote the Piano Trio of 1881–82 in his memory. Its long variation movement builds to a climax, and then the opening lament returns as a funeral march. His operas ranged from Schiller’s The Maid of Orleans to Pushkin’s Mazepa. By the later 1880s he was conducting his music across Europe, and in 1891 he travelled to America for the opening celebrations of Carnegie Hall.

The Fifth Symphony, completed in 1888, brings its opening clarinet motto into every movement and at the end presents it in E major. The ballets made more particular demands. For The Sleeping Beauty, Petipa supplied a detailed plan of dances and stage action. Tchaikovsky took pleasure in filling it, with the courtly processions, Aurora’s long adagio with her four suitors and the quarrel between Carabosse and the Lilac Fairy. The première was in 1890. In the same year came The Queen of Spades, in which the Countess remembers a French song from her youth while Hermann pursues his secret of the three cards.

Trade secretOn 15 June 1891 Tchaikovsky asked his publisher Pyotr Jurgenson to order a celesta he had discovered in Paris. He wanted it for The Voyevoda and The Nutcracker, and feared that Rimsky-Korsakov or Glazunov would use it before he could.

The Nutcracker was first performed on the same evening as Iolanta in 1892. Its miniature overture leaves out cellos and double basses. Later in the ballet come the celesta in the Sugar Plum Fairy’s dance and the children’s chorus in the snow scene.

1893After the march

He conducted the Sixth Symphony in Saint Petersburg on 28 October 1893. The second movement is in five beats to the bar. The third ends with a loud march, and the finale follows, marked Adagio lamentoso. Its violin melody is divided in fragments between the two violin sections and is heard as a single line. The closing pages move down into B minor and end with the lower strings playing alone.

A private programmeWriting to his nephew Vladimir Davydov on 23 February 1893, Tchaikovsky said the symphony’s programme would remain an enigma. “You can’t imagine how blissful I feel,” he added, convinced that he still had music to write.

Nine days after the première he died of cholera. He had been proud of the new symphony and was making further plans. Other musicians were already working with his scores: the young Rachmaninoff had arranged The Sleeping Beauty for piano duet. In 1928 Stravinsky made a ballet, The Fairy’s Kiss, from Tchaikovsky’s songs and piano pieces, music he had written for the drawing room.

The Sound

𝅘𝅥Melody

The First Piano Concerto begins with the orchestra playing a tune in D♭ major while the pianist plays chords beneath it. The tune does not return. Elsewhere a small figure climbs through a sequence of new pitches, repeated each time at a higher point.

𝄾Rhythm & dance

The second movement of the Sixth Symphony is in 5/4, counted as two beats and then three. With the cellos’ phrasing, the 5/4 can pass for an ordinary dance, though it cannot be waltzed to. The first movement of the Fourth Symphony is in 9/8, which implies three beats to the bar; dotted rhythms and accents work against that three-beat pulse.

♮Harmony

The clarinet melody that opens the Fifth Symphony is in E minor. In the finale it returns in E major, with the third of the scale changed. Tchaikovsky often delays such arrivals. A bass note holds while harmonies move above it, or a repeated phrase climbs through one key after another.

𝄞Orchestration

In Romeo and Juliet the love theme is first played by cor anglais and muted violas in unison. In the overture to The Nutcracker the cellos and double basses are silent, and the string writing is given to divided violins and violas.

𝄋Form & recurrence

In the Fourth Symphony the opening fanfare returns several times and stops the music. It returns even in the finale, which is based on a folk song. In the Fifth the motto appears in all four movements, and its character changes from one appearance to the next. In the Sixth the march, the work’s biggest public triumph, comes one movement before the end, and a slow finale follows it.

𝄐Theatre

The dances in Eugene Onegin have consequences. Guests gossip during Tatyana’s name-day waltz; Onegin flirts with Olga; Lensky’s humiliation leads to the duel. The later polonaise introduces a much grander society and a Tatyana whom Onegin can scarcely recognise. Between these public scenes comes the letter scene, in which a young woman tries out her words, loses heart and begins again.

Works

A selection from the composer’s works.

  1. 1866; revised 1874
    Symphony No. 1 in G minor “Winter Daydreams”Op. 13Orchestra
    The first two movements have landscape titles; the finale works a Russian folk song into a vigorous contrapuntal finish.
  2. 1869–80
    Romeo and Juliet, overture-fantasyOrchestra
    Balakirev proposed the subject and argued over the music; Tchaikovsky made three principal versions. The love theme is first played by cor anglais and muted violas.
  3. 1874–75
    Piano Concerto No. 1 in B♭ minorOp. 23Piano and orchestra
    Hans von Bülow gave the première in Boston in 1875, after Nikolai Rubinstein had rejected the piece. The famous D♭ major opening tune disappears once the introduction is over.
    Recordings in the Hall
  4. 1875–76
    Swan LakeOp. 20Ballet in four acts
    The swan music returns in the ballet’s separate scenes. Many stage versions descend from the 1895 Petipa–Ivanov revival, whose score Riccardo Drigo altered after Tchaikovsky’s death.
  5. 1877–78
    Symphony No. 4 in F minorOp. 36Orchestra
    Dedicated to von Meck, with an opening fanfare he described to her as fate. The fanfare returns; between its returns come an oboe melody and a scherzo with the strings entirely pizzicato.
  6. 1877–78
    Eugene OneginOp. 24Opera; soloists, chorus and orchestra
    Tchaikovsky entrusted the première to conservatory students in 1879. The opera includes Tatyana’s letter and Lensky’s farewell.
  7. 1878
    Violin Concerto in D majorOp. 35Violin and orchestra
    The first movement places its cadenza before the recapitulation, and the cadenza leads into the return of the opening material. A G minor Canzonetta leads straight into the finale.
  8. 1878
    Children’s AlbumOp. 39Piano; twenty-four pieces
    A collection of piano pieces for children in the tradition of Schumann, made up of prayers, dances, pieces about dolls and songs from other countries. Old French Song uses only a few notes.
  9. 1880
    Serenade for Strings in C majorOp. 48String orchestra
    After a waltz and an elegy, the finale is built on Russian themes. Near the end the broad chords from the opening return, and the music then moves into a fast close.
  10. 1881–82
    Piano Trio in A minorOp. 50Piano, violin and cello
    Written in memory of Nikolai Rubinstein. The variation movement’s many changes of character eventually give way to the first movement’s lament and a funeral march.
  11. 1885
    Manfred Symphony in B minorOp. 58Orchestra with organ
    Byron’s tormented hero is the subject of four movements, outside the sequence of six numbered symphonies. The organ enters late, in the ending; the orchestra has not used it before that point.
  12. 1888
    Symphony No. 5 in E minorOp. 64Orchestra
    The opening clarinet motto returns in every movement, sometimes interrupting a song or dance. On its last appearance it is transformed into a march in E major.
  13. 1888–89
    The Sleeping BeautyOp. 66Ballet; prologue and three acts
    Petipa supplied the dances and stage requirements, and Tchaikovsky wrote music for them that he counted among his best. In the Rose Adagio Aurora dances with her four suitors in turn, accepting a rose from each.
  14. 1890
    The Queen of SpadesOp. 68Opera after Pushkin
    Hermann’s obsession with three winning cards wrecks his love for Liza. The opera includes the Countess’s old French song and the pastoral entertainment.
  15. 1891–92
    The NutcrackerOp. 71Ballet in two acts
    The familiar suite leaves out much of the complete ballet, including its wordless children’s chorus in the snow scene. The celesta was a new acquisition, and Tchaikovsky wanted it kept secret from rival composers.
    Recordings in the Hall
  16. 1893
    Symphony No. 6 in B minor “Pathétique”Op. 74Orchestra
    The march’s loud finish is followed by an Adagio lamentoso. In the last pages the orchestra is reduced to low strings and the symphony ends quietly in B minor.
    Recordings in the Hall

Sister Arts

Alexander Pushkin

Tatyana’s letter, from Eugene Onegin

Chapter III, published 1827
RussianЯ к вам пишу — чего же боле? Что я могу еще сказать? Теперь, я знаю, в вашей воле Меня презреньем наказать. Но вы, к моей несчастной доле Хоть каплю жалости храня, Вы не оставите меня.
Our translationI write to you — what more is there? What else can I say? Now, I know, it is in your power To punish me with contempt. But if for my unhappy fate You keep even a drop of pity, You will not abandon me.

Pushkin presents the letter as his translation of Tatyana’s French. In the opera she sings it herself, alone, through the night. Lavrovskaya suggested that Tchaikovsky set the novel; he went out to find a copy and stayed awake planning the scenes. Russian text of the letter.

James and Dorothy de Rothschild commissioned Bakst’s seven Sleeping Beauty panels in 1913, the year they married, for their London home. Bakst later used the same fairy tale in the theatre: in 1921 he designed Diaghilev’s London production of The Sleeping Princess, to Tchaikovsky’s ballet. This panel shows the hunting party reaching the edge of the enchanted, sleeping world.

William Shakespeare

Romeo and Juliet, Act II, scene 2

c. 1595
EnglishBut, soft! what light through yonder window breaks? It is the east, and Juliet is the sun. Arise, fair sun, and kill the envious moon, Who is already sick and pale with grief, That thou her maid art far more fair than she.

Balakirev suggested Shakespeare’s lovers as a subject and went on urging revisions into 1871. The overture’s love theme is first played by cor anglais and muted violas, after the music of the feud.

Passed toSergei RachmaninoffIgor StravinskySergei ProkofievDmitri Shostakovich

Some links are direct. Rachmaninoff arranged The Sleeping Beauty for piano duet, and Stravinsky built The Fairy’s Kiss from Tchaikovsky’s songs and piano pieces. The comparison with Prokofiev and Shostakovich is closest in their dance music, where each of them lets a waltz or march continue while the music around it goes badly wrong.
