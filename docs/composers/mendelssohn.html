Life

In 1825 the sixteen-year-old Felix Mendelssohn wrote a birthday present for his violin teacher, Eduard Rietz. It was scored for four violins, two violas and two cellos, and gave Rietz a first-violin part that rises above the other seven players in long arcs. Eleven years later the composer played second viola in a public performance of the E♭ major Octet. Writing to Karl Klingemann on 31 January 1836, he admitted to “heart palpitations”.

BerlinA house full of music

Felix Mendelssohn was born in Hamburg on 3 February 1809; the family moved to Berlin two years later. In 1812 his sister Fanny wrote to friends in Hamburg: “Felix has a strong voice and sings all day.” His father Abraham was a banker, his grandfather Moses the Jewish philosopher. Abraham and Lea Mendelssohn had their children baptised as Christians in 1816 and used the additional surname Bartholdy. Fanny and Felix learnt piano with Ludwig Berger and composition with Carl Friedrich Zelter.

The family’s money paid for players. At the family’s Sunday concerts the children heard their scores performed by experienced musicians. Felix wrote string symphonies and concertos at an age when most pupils were still learning to write a correct exercise. Zelter introduced him to Goethe in Weimar in 1821. His education also included drawing, languages and classical literature, and his German verse translation of a comedy by Terence was published in 1826.

The Octet’s scherzo is marked Allegro leggierissimo, as light as possible. Short detached notes pass between the players, and the movement ends high and quiet. In 1826 Mendelssohn used the same kind of writing in the overture to A Midsummer Night’s Dream, which he wrote at seventeen. It opens with four quiet wind chords, and then divided violins play fast in E minor. The lovers have melodic music, the tradesmen have heavier music, and Bottom’s donkey bray is imitated in the orchestra.

1829Bach and the British Isles

The Passion in 1829Mendelssohn’s Berlin performance used cuts and adapted instrumental parts. Bach’s music already had devoted performers, including Zelter and the Mendelssohn family. The 1829 performance brought the St Matthew Passion back before the public.

On 11 March 1829 he conducted Bach’s St Matthew Passion at the Berlin Sing-Akademie, with the actor and singer Eduard Devrient helping to organise the performance. The twenty-year-old had studied the score given to him by his grandmother Bella Salomon. He continued to work for earlier music in practical ways. He later brought Handel’s Israel in Egypt to Düsseldorf, programmed historical concerts in Leipzig and helped pay for a monument to Bach there.

That spring he made his first visit to Britain, playing and conducting in London before travelling through Scotland with Karl Klingemann. On 7 August, in Tobermory, he sent home a musical sketch of the low, falling phrase that begins The Hebrides. They visited Staffa and Fingal’s Cave the next day. The finished overture took several more years and revisions. The opening figure is first played quietly by bassoons and lower strings, and it stays audible as the rest of the orchestra enters and builds to full strength.

Numbering of the symphoniesThe numbered symphonies largely follow the order of publication. The ‘Reformation’, No. 5, was written before the ‘Italian’, No. 4; the ‘Scottish’, No. 3, was the last to be completed.

The ‘Italian’ Symphony, prompted by his stay in Italy, had its first performance in London in 1833. The first movement is in A major; the final Saltarello is in A minor and ends in that key. Mendelssohn revised the score and never authorised its publication. The ‘Scottish’, begun in 1829, was completed in 1842. Its four movements are played without a break, and the finale moves from an Allegro in A minor to a broad closing section in A major.

1835–43At the Gewandhaus

After a frustrating period as music director in Düsseldorf, Mendelssohn took charge of the Gewandhaus concerts in Leipzig in 1835. In 1837 he married Cécile Jeanrenaud; they had five children. The orchestra, the household and his correspondence took time from composition. In March 1839 he conducted the first public performance of Schubert’s ‘Great’ C major Symphony, whose manuscript Schumann had brought to his attention. In 1843 he founded the Leipzig Conservatoire, with Ferdinand David and Schumann among its teachers.

The D minor Piano Trio went through several rounds of revision. Mendelssohn completed a version in July 1839, rewrote it after a private performance, then revised the piano part again at Ferdinand Hiller’s urging. Further changes followed in the proofs. Mendelssohn himself played the piano part at the first public performance in February 1840, with David on violin and Carl Wittmann on cello.

The Songs without Words brought his music into domestic use. In each the melody is set over an accompaniment that a good amateur can often manage, though keeping the two distinct is harder. When Marc-André Souchay asked what particular pieces meant, Mendelssohn replied on 15 October 1842: “If you ask me what I was thinking, I say: precisely the song as it stands.”

1843–46For David and the king

Prussian court duties repeatedly drew him away from Leipzig. One result was the complete music for A Midsummer Night’s Dream, performed at Potsdam in October 1843, seventeen years after the overture. The new Nocturne set a horn melody beside two bassoons; the Wedding March added the trombones. At the end of the play the four wind chords from the opening of the overture returned.

The E minor Violin Concerto was finished in 1844 after years of discussion with David. The soloist enters near the start, plays the cadenza before the main theme returns, then continues in arpeggios beneath the orchestra. At the end of the movement a bassoon holds one note across the gap into the Andante. David gave the première in Leipzig in March 1845, with Niels Gade conducting.

St Paul, first performed in 1836, had already made Mendelssohn a favourite with choral societies. Elijah was first performed in Birmingham in August 1846, in William Bartholomew’s English translation. The chorus takes the part of a people suffering drought, calls on Baal and comments on the prophet’s fate. Mendelssohn continued to revise the work after the première, and the surviving performance materials contain replacement numbers and alterations in his own hand.

1847After Fanny

Fanny’s judgementThe siblings exchanged music and detailed criticism throughout their lives. Felix discouraged her from publishing under her own name. She decided to do so in 1846, and he sent his blessing after she had already made that decision.

Fanny died suddenly in Berlin on 14 May 1847. That summer Felix wrote the F minor String Quartet, Op. 80, a work of tremolos and fierce accents at odds with the gracious image attached to his name. His own health failed; after a series of strokes he died in Leipzig on 4 November, aged thirty-eight. Wagner’s later antisemitic attacks helped turn admiration into suspicion of an allegedly facile composer. Mendelssohn had left the ‘Italian’ Symphony unpublished, still dissatisfied with a piece audiences loved.

The Sound

𝄞Light bowing

The Octet’s scherzo is fast and quiet, with detached notes passed between eight players. In A Midsummer Night’s Dream, the violins divide into four parts for the fairy music. Its lightness depends on short bow strokes and clean gaps. A fraction too much sound from each player makes the whole passage heavy.

𝄢Fugal writing

The finale of the Octet begins in the second cello, and its running theme passes upwards through the ensemble. With eight parts available, Mendelssohn could go on adding voices long after a quartet would be full. The scherzo returns later in the movement. The writing shows what Mendelssohn had learned from Bach. He was sixteen when he wrote these quavers.

𝄋No empty pauses

In the Violin Concerto, the soloist is still playing arpeggios at the end of the cadenza when the theme returns. At the end of the movement, a bassoon holds its note after the rest of the orchestra has stopped and leads the music into C major.

𝅘𝅥Piano songs

In the first Song without Words, Op. 19b No. 1, melody and bass answer one another before the piece reaches a settled close. The tune’s phrases can lengthen at points where a cadence is expected. Across the collections, a pianist has to give the melody room without allowing its broken-chord accompaniment to lose the pulse.

♭Bright and dark

The ‘Italian’ Symphony begins in A major and ends with a Saltarello in A minor, played at full speed. In this music minor keys are not used only for sadness. In A Midsummer Night’s Dream the strings of the fairy music enter in E minor after the E major chords of the winds.

Sister Arts

Turner visited Staffa in 1831, two years after Mendelssohn, travelling from Tobermory on a tourist steamer. The small red point on the painted vessel is its furnace. The overture and the painting belong to the same enthusiasm for the Hebrides. Collection record.

William Shakespeare

A Midsummer Night’s Dream · Act V, scene 1

First published 1600
EnglishIf we shadows have offended, Think but this, and all is mended, That you have but slumbered here While these visions did appear. And this weak and idle theme, No more yielding but a dream,

Puck asks the audience to treat the evening’s confusions as a dream. Mendelssohn had known the play in German translation as a teenager. For the 1843 production he accompanied this farewell with the four wind chords from his 1826 overture.

Lineage

Drew fromJohann Sebastian BachGeorge Frideric HandelWolfgang Amadeus MozartLudwig van BeethovenFanny Hensel
Mendelssohn
Passed toRobert SchumannJohannes BrahmsArthur Sullivan

Fanny was his first close musical critic; Schumann became a colleague at the conservatoire and an advocate of his chamber music. His fast scherzos and his piano pieces in a singing style served later composers as models of the short piece. Arthur Sullivan received the first Mendelssohn Scholarship in 1856, which helped take him to Leipzig.
