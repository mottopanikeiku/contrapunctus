Manuel de Falla / V. The French Line

Noches en los jardines de España

1909–15 · Piano and orchestra

Three “symphonic impressions”: En el Generalife, Danza lejana and En los jardines de la Sierra de Córdoba. Begun in Paris; first played at the Teatro Real, Madrid, on 9 April 1916 by José Cubiles under Arbós.

25 min

The recording

  1. Complete: I. En el Generalife; II. Danza lejana; III. En los jardines de la Sierra de CórdobaClifford Curzon, piano; National Symphony Orchestra; Enrique Jordá · 1945
    24:46

    Clifford Curzon and Enrique Jordá play all three of Falla’s “symphonic impressions” on a six-side Decca set made in London in 1945. The first opens with a harp played close to its soundboard, and the piano is silent for the whole first page.

    Notes as it plays
    1. Harp near the soundboard

      Allegretto tranquillo e misterioso, in 6/8. The harp plays sulla tavola, close to the soundboard; violas play sul ponticello; cellos and basses pluck. The piano, the soloist, says nothing yet.

    2. The piano enters

      Just over a minute in, the piano enters with a fast, ringing figure marked “ben marcato”, over long wind and horn notes that die away (“perdendosi”).

    3. First swell

      At “Poco più animato” (dotted crotchet = 66) the first full-orchestra swell arrives: winds and harp enter together and the piano pours out broken chords.

    4. Begun in Paris

      Falla began the piece in Paris in 1909. It was first played at the Teatro Real in Madrid on 9 April 1916, with José Cubiles as soloist and Enrique Fernández Arbós conducting; Falla played it himself in Granada that June.

    5. In Falla’s own words

      Falla’s note says the three “symphonic impressions” aim only to “evoke places, sensations, and sentiments”, and that “melancholy and mystery have their part also.”

    6. A long rise and fall

      Here the movement builds across a minute or more to its biggest sound, the whole orchestra in full cry, then thins until it is almost silent before the second movement.

    7. Danza lejana

      Allegretto giusto, in 3/4. Four solo violas play a trill with mutes over pizzicato cellos and a solo double bass; flutes and cor anglais answer, pianissimo and marcato.

    8. The piano at Granada

      On 26 June 1916 Falla played the piano part himself at the Palace of Charles V in Granada, with the Madrid orchestra under Arturo Saco de Valle. Diaghilev and Massine were in the audience.

    9. Attacca subito

      Danza lejana ends on repeated piano chords growing louder, flutes and oboes rising in the last bar, and the score says “Attacca subito”. The third movement follows at once, Vivo, with cymbals, a piano chord and trills across the strings.

    10. Piano phrases over hazy strings

      After the opening storm, a calmer stretch brings what the Los Angeles Philharmonic’s note calls “crystalline, guitar-inspired” phrases for the piano over hazy strings; the drama returns soon after.

    11. The final five minutes

      The piece “roars through its final five minutes”, in the Los Angeles Philharmonic’s words, with harsh full-orchestra interruptions. On this three-disc set the closing portion, “En las sierras de Córdoba. Final”, fills the last side.

    12. A ballet that never was

      Diaghilev first wanted to make a ballet of Nights. Falla was not keen, and the contract the two men signed was for The Three-Cornered Hat instead.
