Giacomo Puccini / IV. Fin de siècle

La bohème

1893–95 · Opera in four tableaux

The first and last acts occupy the same garret. Familiar tunes return with Mimì, making the distance between those evenings painfully short.

3 recordings · 10 min

In the Hall

  1. Act I · O soave fanciullaNellie Melba, soprano; Enrico Caruso, tenor; orchestra uncredited · 24 March 1907
    3:16

    Moonlight falls on Mimì in the attic, and Rodolfo’s conversation becomes a declaration of love. Nellie Melba and Enrico Caruso sing the duet that sends the new lovers out into the Paris night.

    Notes as it plays
    1. A face in moonlight

      Rodolfo turns from the window and sees Mimì lit by the moon. “O soave fanciulla, o dolce viso” — “O gentle girl, O sweet face.” The tenor sings to her; the soprano’s answer admits how deeply his words have touched her.

    2. Friends below the window

      Rodolfo’s friends have been shouting from the courtyard, impatient to get to the Café Momus. He tells them to save two places. As they leave, Marcello calls back that the poet has found his poetry. Upstairs, Mimì is standing in the moonlight.

    3. Mimì makes a suggestion

      The grand declaration becomes a little negotiation. Mimì asks to go out with Rodolfo; he suggests staying in because it is cold outside. She promises to stay close.

    4. Two lovers leave together

      Rodolfo helps Mimì with her shawl and offers his arm. Before they leave, he asks her to say she loves him. “Io t’amo” — “I love you.” Their first meeting ends with a walk out into the cold.

  2. Act IV · O Mimì, tu più non torniEnrico Caruso, tenor; Antonio Scotti, baritone; orchestra uncredited · 17 March 1907
    3:05

    Back in the attic, Rodolfo and Marcello try to work while thinking about the women who have left them. Caruso and Scotti sing side by side: a poet remembering Mimì, a painter who keeps finding Musetta on his canvas.

    Notes as it plays
    1. Two absent women

      Rodolfo thinks of Mimì’s little hands, scented hair and white neck: “Mimì, mia breve gioventù” — “Mimì, my brief youth.” Beside him, Marcello thinks of Musetta. Tenor and baritone share a duet while each man speaks privately to someone who is gone.

    2. Musetta on every canvas

      Marcello cannot make his brush obey him. Whether he paints skies, earth, winter or spring, it produces Musetta’s dark eyes and mouth. He calls his own heart cowardly for waiting for her, and goes on waiting.

    3. The little bonnet

      Rodolfo has kept the bonnet Mimì hid beneath his pillow when she left. He takes it from the drawer and presses it to his heart. “Tutta sai la nostra felicità” — “You know all our happiness.”

    4. Bread for four friends

      After the duet, Rodolfo hides his emotion by asking the time. Marcello answers that it is yesterday’s lunchtime. Schaunard and Colline arrive with bread and a herring; the four friends pretend they are sitting down to a feast.

  3. Act I · Sì, mi chiamano MimìClaudia Muzio · 1911
    4:01

    Mimì comes to her neighbour’s attic to have her candle lit and stays to tell him about her life. Claudia Muzio sings Puccini’s portrait of an embroiderer whose small room gets the first sun of spring.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Her name is Lucia

      “Mi chiamano Mimì, ma il mio nome è Lucia” — “They call me Mimì, but my name is Lucia.” Rodolfo has told her he is a poet. Sitting in his attic, she answers with her own work: embroidering lilies and roses on linen or silk.

    2. Lunch alone, prayers at home

      Mimì repeats her name, then talks about making her own lunch, praying and living alone in a little white room. Puccini lets her talk lightly: after the returning opening phrase, the score moves into Allegretto moderato, with a skipping accompaniment.

    3. A candle and a key

      Mimì’s candle goes out; she drops her key in Rodolfo’s room. He finds it and quietly pockets it, prolonging their search in the dark. By the time she begins this aria, he has held her cold hand and told her about his poems.

    4. The first sun is hers

      “Il primo sole è mio” — “The first sunshine is mine.” At the thought of spring, Mimì stands up. The music broadens into Andante molto sostenuto, and her voice climbs above an accompaniment that grows towards the D-major climax.

    5. Back to the small room

      The flowers Mimì embroiders have no scent. Her song quietens, and she apologises for disturbing her neighbour so late. Puccini marks the last explanation “senza rigore di tempo, con naturalezza” — “without strict time, naturally”. The soprano talks over a held chord.

    How it is joined

    1. Mimì starts her introduction again

      The first song about flowers and poetry contracts into a brief exchange with Rodolfo. Mimì then repeats her introduction, marked Lentamente in 2/4. The rising phrase returns with chromatic notes and separated chords underneath; the soprano has room for pauses between her words.

    2. A pause before the spring sunshine

      A held pause ends Mimì’s account of looking over the roofs. The metre opens into common time, marked Andante molto sostenuto. G-major harmony supports the new melody; the bass then moves through E and A towards the D-major climax. The soprano’s short sentences have become a long, rising song.

    3. A held chord lets Mimì speak

      The earlier flower melody returns quietly. As Mimì explains that her embroidered flowers have no scent, the accompaniment settles into D major. Her final apology runs freely over the held chord, marked senza rigore di tempo, con naturalezza. A brief chord closes her introduction.
