Maurice Ravel / V. The French Line

Gaspard de la nuit

1908 · Piano; after Aloysius Bertrand

A water spirit, a gallows and a tormenting dwarf, with Bertrand’s poems printed before the movements. Between the keyboard terrors of Ondine and Scarbo, Le Gibet tolls an unwavering B♭.

3 recordings · 24 min

In the Hall

  1. I. Ondine — LentAndriy Makarevych, piano · 1 April 2014
    6:48

    A water spirit sings beneath a tremble of piano chords in Ondine, the first of Ravel’s three poems for piano. Andriy Makarevych plays the song and the water with the same two hands.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. A song under water

      The right hand trembles on closely packed chords, ppp. Below them the left hand sings a slower line, marked very soft and very expressive. The melody sits inside the water, several notes below the glittering top of the piano.

    2. A palace beneath the lake

      In Bertrand’s poem, Ondine touches a moonlit window and offers the man inside her ring. “Mon palais est bâti fluide, au fond du lac” — “My palace is built of water, at the bottom of the lake.” She wants him to marry her and become king of the lakes.

    3. The keyboard opens wide

      The arpeggios sweep from deep bass to high treble. Ravel sets the tune in chords among them, with swells from pp to forte inside a single wave. The pianist has to make those longer notes sing through the spray.

    4. He loves a mortal woman

      Ondine’s invitation fails. The man tells her that he loves a mortal woman. Bertrand’s water spirit sheds a few tears, bursts out laughing and vanishes in a shower down the blue windowpanes.

    5. The water falls silent

      Near the end, the accompaniment stops. A single line speaks in the middle of the keyboard, Très lent, with rests below it. For a few bars the song has no water around it.

    6. Laughter and a shower

      A two-handed rush breaks out, Rapide et brillant, then dissolves into quiet C♯-major arpeggios. Bertrand’s last image is “mes vitraux bleus” — “my blue stained-glass windows” — with water streaming down them.

    How it is joined

    1. The song loses its accompaniment

      The piano’s running figures climb to a held high note and stop under a fermata. Ravel then writes Très lent. A quiet, unaccompanied phrase follows in the middle of the keyboard; the bass staff is empty.

    2. A rush across both hands

      The bare phrase launches a rush of notes in both hands, marked Rapide et brillant. Ravel then asks the pianist to hold back gradually. C♯-major arpeggios return at ppp, in the opening tempo, and carry the music to its last quiet chord.

  2. II. Le Gibet — Très lentOE1FEU, piano · 3 April 2020
    7:05

    A B♭ bell sounds throughout Le Gibet, Ravel’s setting of Aloysius Bertrand’s gallows at sunset. Chords gather around it and fall away; the bell keeps its dreadful appointment.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. One note tolls

      B♭, in octaves, keeps sounding while slow chords move beneath it. Ravel marks the piano Très lent and asks for the soft pedal throughout. The bell has its own rhythm; the other notes lean across it.

    2. A bell beyond the horizon

      Bertrand’s poem searches among small, horrible sounds: a fly at the dead man’s ear, a spider making his necktie. Then comes the answer: “C’est la cloche qui tinte” — “It is the bell that tolls.” Beyond the town walls, the setting sun reddens the hanging body.

    3. Bertrand, read again

      Ravel and Ricardo Viñes read Aloysius Bertrand as students, among the Baudelaire, Poe and Mallarmé they devour. A new edition of 1908 sends Ravel back to him. From May to 5 September he writes Gaspard, “three romantic poems of transcendental virtuosity”; Viñes plays it on 9 January 1909.

    4. The bell between two hands

      Ravel spreads the piano across three staves. High chords and low chords move around the B♭, which passes between the hands. Even where the chords fill the keyboard, the repeated note keeps sounding inside them.

    5. A voice without expression

      A bare upper melody stands above the bell. Ravel marks it un peu en dehors, mais sans expression: slightly brought out, but without expression. The B♭ continues below, indifferent to the melody’s rise and fall.

    6. The chords return

      The piano fills out again in long, tied chords. The bell threads between them, still B♭. Ravel’s instruction from the first page remains in force: “without hurrying or slowing to the end”.

    7. A Bechstein in the Kaisersaal

      OE1FEU recorded this piece themselves, on a Bechstein D 282 concert grand in Vienna’s Kaisersaal, and dates it 3 April 2020. The recording is released under a Creative Commons Attribution–ShareAlike licence.

    8. The bell remains

      The last page lets the melodic fragments fall silent. Deep held notes remain under the bell, and the final bars shrink to ppp. The repeated B♭ lasts until the last chord.

    How it is joined

    1. Chords surround the bell

      The piano opens into three staves: low held notes, the recurring B♭ and chords high above it. The two hands share the bell while the upper chords are marked très lié, very smoothly joined. Its repeated pitch carries through each change of harmony.

    2. A melody above the low bell

      The fuller chords recede. A single upper line, marked pp and sans expression, crosses above the B♭ in the bass. Its long notes stretch across several tolls of the bell.

    3. The chords close round B♭

      The single upper line broadens into tied chords as the six-flat key signature returns. The B♭ bell continues between the hands, above a sustained low bass. Small crescendos swell the chords without changing the slow pace.

    4. The melody leaves the bell

      The high chords diminish and the piano returns to the opening’s slow bell-and-chord writing. Deep sustained notes support B♭ octaves; the repeated figure grows softer through p, pp and ppp. Ravel keeps the same pace to the end.

  3. III. Scarbo — ModéréAndriy Makarevych, piano · 1 April 2014
    9:39

    Scarbo is the creature who scratches at the bed curtains, vanishes and comes back larger than the room. Ravel gives him whispered repeated notes, enormous leaps and silences long enough to make the next sound a shock.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Something beneath the bed

      Three low notes rise out of the piano. A quiet repeated note trembles underneath a held chord, then stops. Ravel puts a fermata over the silence and marks it très long — very long.

    2. The dwarf springs up

      The piano accelerates through rising figures and arrives at Vif. The hands leap apart, pulling bass notes and high chords into the same quick dance. Sudden rests still cut holes in it.

    3. A nail on silk

      Bertrand’s narrator hears Scarbo laughing in his alcove, then “grincer son ongle sur la soie des courtines de mon lit” — “his nail scraping the silk curtains of my bed”. The dwarf spins on one foot and rolls around the room like a witch’s dropped spindle.

    4. Three notes, enormous hands

      The opening’s little rising figure keeps returning in new octaves. Repeated notes and leaps do much of the work: the pianist must turn tiny shapes into great bursts of chords without losing their sharp edges.

    5. The whisper comes back

      The opening returns with its low three-note figure, held chord and repeated-note tremble. This time the drumming sinks further into the bass. Ravel asks for the soft pedal and leaves the upper piano almost empty.

    6. As high as a cathedral

      The dwarf grows between the narrator and the moon “comme le clocher d’une cathédrale gothique” — “like the tower of a Gothic cathedral”. A little golden bell shakes on his pointed cap. Bertrand lets something absurd become immense.

    7. A candle goes out

      The piano retreats into tiny repeated chords and a final dart up the keys. Ravel writes Sans ralentir, without slowing. Bertrand’s last words are “et soudain il s’éteignait” — “and suddenly he went out”, like a candle.

    How it is joined

    1. The silence breaks into leaps

      After the held silences, rising piano figures accelerate. At Vif, the hands spring apart: low notes answer high chords, and a crescendo runs from pp to ff. Two bars of rests interrupt the burst before the moving accompaniment starts again.

    2. The low three notes return

      The running figures fall away and the opening’s three low notes return under the soft pedal. The held chord and repeated-note tremble follow them, with another fermata. Ravel then moves the drumming down into the bass at ppp.

    3. The chords become a flicker

      The wide arpeggios contract into quiet repeated chords above a low held bass. The pianist moves through short rests and little answering figures, then darts upwards to the last note. Sans ralentir keeps the ending quick.
