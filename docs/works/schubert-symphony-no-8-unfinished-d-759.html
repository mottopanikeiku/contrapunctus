Franz Schubert / III. Romanticism

Symphony No. 8 “Unfinished”, D. 759

1822 · Orchestra

2 recordings · 22 min

In the Hall

  1. I. Allegro moderatoPhiladelphia Orchestra, Leopold Stokowski · 28 and 30 April 1927
    10:28

    Schubert’s Unfinished begins with cellos and basses alone, then lets oboe and clarinet sing over murmuring violins. Leopold Stokowski conducts the Philadelphia Orchestra in this 1927 recording of its first movement.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Cellos and basses alone

      Eight bars for cellos and basses, pianissimo. Their dark B-minor phrase is the symphony’s first voice. The violins then begin their restless murmur, and oboe and clarinet sing together above it.

    2. The cellos sing in G

      After a brief horn-led bridge, the cellos begin a long melody in G major. The violins take it up over gently off-beat accompaniment. A tune this welcoming seems to promise a very different symphony.

    3. Chords cut through the song

      Heavy chords from the full orchestra interrupt the G-major melody. Schubert leaves silences between the blows, then takes fragments of the tune through the closing passage. The singing line has become an argument.

    4. The opening phrase in E

      The development starts quietly in E minor with the phrase that opened the symphony. The low strings bring it back, and the higher strings take it up. What first sounded like an introduction now supplies the movement’s central struggle.

    5. The trombones take over

      The trombones turn the opening phrase into a forceful declaration above the orchestra. E minor holds through the long climax. The tune once entrusted to the lowest strings has acquired a brass voice.

    6. Vienna hears it in 1865

      On 17 December 1865 Johann von Herbeck conducts the first performance in Vienna, forty-three years after Schubert begins the score. He follows its two completed movements with the busy D-major finale of Schubert’s Third Symphony.

    7. Two woodwinds come home

      Oboe and clarinet return over the violins’ murmur. Schubert brings back their melody quietly in B minor, leaving out the eight-bar introduction. The two woodwinds sound almost private after the trombones.

    8. The cello tune in D

      The cello melody returns in D major, a fourth below its first appearance in G. The tune keeps its easy swing, but its new key brings it closer to the surrounding B minor.

    9. Back to the low strings

      After the recapitulation reaches B major, the coda restores B minor and the low-string opening. Schubert makes the once-whispered phrase lead the final confrontation, ending with emphatic orchestral chords.

    How it is joined

    1. A B pedal, then E minor

      The exposition holds B in the second bassoon and first horn beneath an F♯-major chord. The development then begins softly in E minor: cellos and basses recover the opening phrase, and the higher strings take it up.

    2. The violin murmur returns

      The orchestral outburst recedes into soft flute and oboe phrases. Oboe and clarinet then recover the B-minor melody over murmuring violins and plucked lower strings. The eight-bar introduction is omitted.

    3. B major darkens to minor

      B major closes the recapitulation. The coda changes it to B minor and restores the opening phrase in the low strings. Its return begins the final build towards the orchestra’s closing chords.

  2. II. Andante con motoPhiladelphia Orchestra, Leopold Stokowski · 28 and 30 April 1927
    11:55

    The Unfinished Symphony’s Andante opens in E major with horns, bassoons and plucked basses. Its calm is repeatedly broken by a minor-key woodwind song and the full orchestra’s answer.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Horns above plucked basses

      Horns and bassoons sound a two-bar phrase over plucked double basses. The strings answer with a long E-major melody. Those first two bars will return at the very end, like a farewell the movement takes time to finish.

    2. Hanslick hears thunder clouds

      Reviewing the Vienna première in 1865, Eduard Hanslick hears “musical thunder clouds” crossing the Andante. He marvels at how much Schubert achieves with a few horn figures and a clarinet or oboe solo.

    3. Four notes open the door

      The first violins play four bare notes outlining C♯ minor. The clarinet begins the second melody in that key; the oboe continues it in C♯ major. A change of instrument and a raised third warm the same song.

    4. The orchestra interrupts

      The full orchestra takes over in C♯ minor. Its closing passage eventually reaches D♭ major — the same sounding tonic as C♯ major, now written with flats. A short bridge brings E major back into view.

    5. The string song returns

      The opening theme returns in E major. Its long string phrases and answering winds recover the first pages’ calm, after the closing theme’s orchestral force.

    6. The oboe goes first

      The second melody comes back in A minor. This time the oboe begins and the clarinet continues: Schubert exchanges their roles and lowers the tune a major third from its earlier C♯ minor.

    7. Two bars become a coda

      The little phrase that opened the movement grows into the coda’s first melody. Schubert keeps finding more to say with those two bars, drawing out the farewell before the final return to E major.

    8. Woodwinds in distant A♭

      The woodwinds sing the first theme in A♭ major. Its familiar shape survives the sudden distance from E. The brief violin figure then leads home, and the opening cadence has the last word.

    9. The manuscript goes further

      This E-major close is the last completed movement, but Schubert’s manuscript continues into a B-minor scherzo. Only its opening is fully orchestrated. He leaves the symphony in 1822 and lives another six years.

    How it is joined

    1. Four unaccompanied violin notes

      After the E-major first theme, four unaccompanied notes in the first violins outline C♯ minor. The clarinet takes up the new melody there; the oboe continues it in C♯ major.

    2. E major after the closing theme

      The full orchestra’s closing theme ends in D♭ major. A short transition restores E major, and the strings resume the opening melody with the winds answering them.

    3. The oboe takes the clarinet’s melody

      The second melody returns in A minor, a major third below its first appearance. The oboe takes the opening phrase and the clarinet continues, reversing their earlier order.

    4. The opening cadence grows

      The full-orchestra closing passage subsides. The two-bar E-major cadential figure from the opening supplies a new, extended melody for the coda; the later violin motto will carry it towards the woodwinds’ A♭-major recollection.
