Johannes Brahms / III. Romanticism

Symphony No. 4, Op. 98

1884–85 · Op. 98 · Orchestra

The finale builds variations on an eight-bar progression adapted from Bach’s BWV 150. Brahms saves the trombones until this last movement.

11 min

The recording

  1. IV. Allegro energico e passionatoMusopen Symphony · 2012
    11:08

    Eight notes, thirty variations, and an ending that stays in E minor: Brahms’s last symphonic finale is merciless. At its centre, a lone flute and a trombone chorale briefly slow the tread.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Eight chords without strings

      Winds and brass announce eight chords; the strings wait. The upper line climbs from E towards B, sharpening A to A♯ along the way. These eight bars will return thirty times, sometimes buried deep inside the orchestra.

    2. Bach’s bass, sharpened

      The pattern comes from the closing chorus of Bach’s cantata Nach dir, Herr, verlanget mich, BWV 150. Brahms adds a chromatic note. A♯ makes the climb towards B more urgent, and gives the finale a note it can worry at all the way to the coda.

    3. A flute in longer bars

      The bars double in length, from 3/4 to 3/2. A solo flute draws a hesitant melody out of the same eight-bar pattern, with little off-beat chords from strings and horns. The orchestra leaves it room to breathe.

    4. The trombones sing

      In E major, trombones sing a chorale with bassoons and horns. Violas and cellos answer with short rising arpeggios. The phrases leave rests between them, and their promised cadences keep arriving late.

    5. Unripe cherries in the mountains

      From Mürzzuschlag in August 1885, Brahms sends Elisabeth von Herzogenberg the first movement: “Cherries never get ripe for eating in these parts, so don’t be afraid to say if you don’t like the taste.” He adds: “I’m not at all eager to write a bad No. 4.”

    6. The opening chords return

      The opening chords come back with force, and 3/2 contracts to 3/4. A minor now stands plainly at the beginning, with A in the bass. The strings answer the winds with a descending scale.

    7. A detour through C

      The late variations make a quiet detour through C major. Winds move in thirds over plucked lower strings; violas and violins pass triplet arpeggios between them. A bass rising by semitones pulls the music back towards E minor.

    8. The pattern breaks open

      Più allegro: faster. The eight-bar pattern can no longer contain the music. Winds and brass drive upwards against cascading strings, then linger on the theme’s chromatic note, now spelt B♭. Timpani rolls and pauses hold it there.

    9. E minor to the end

      The last cadences hammer home E minor. Three-note upbeats repeatedly drive into the chord, and the timpani roll under the final blows. Brahms leaves the G♮ in place.

    How it is joined

    1. The chords leave gaps

      The harmony repeats, including the A♯ that presses towards B, but the orchestra separates its attacks. Horns and timpani sound on the first beat, plucked strings on the second. The third beat is empty. Trombones join the plucked answer for the first four bars.

    2. Twice the room for the flute

      A late dominant chord on B prepares the return to E minor. The metre changes from 3/4 to 3/2 while the note values keep their speed. A solo flute carries the melody through the longer bars, accompanied by short off-beat chords in horns and strings.

    3. The oboe leads into low brass

      A descending oboe phrase carries across the join, delaying the cadence from A major to E major. Trombones, bassoons and horns take over in chorale phrases. Violas and cellos answer with upward arpeggios; a horn line later links the two chorale variations.

    4. A minor cuts across the chorale

      The flute’s descending link breaks off on A minor. After the pause, winds restore the opening chords with A in the bass, and the broad 3/2 bars contract to 3/4. Strings enter with a forceful descending scale. The expected E-major cadence has slipped away.

    5. The eight bars overflow

      Wide string and horn leaps extend the final variation beyond eight bars. Timpani grow more insistent; an augmented chord hangs between E and C major. At Più allegro, winds and brass begin the rising theme over cascading strings, but its fifth note, respelt B♭, holds up the expected continuation.
