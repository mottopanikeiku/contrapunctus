Frédéric Chopin / III. Romanticism

Waltz, Op. 64 No. 1 (“Minute”)

1847 · Piano

Recordings

Historical and comparison recordings, outside the concert’s running order.

  1. Waltz in D♭ major, Op. 64 No. 1 (“Minute”)Sergei Rachmaninoff, piano (Steinway) · 5 April 1923
    2:01

    Rachmaninoff records Chopin’s Minute Waltz for Victor in 1923, bringing one composer-pianist’s hands to another’s quicksilver writing. A long melody and an unaccompanied trill interrupt the famous circling notes.

    Notes as it plays
    1. One hand starts the dance

      Only the right hand plays at first: a quick loop around A♭, marked leggiero. When the bass enters, its single low note and two answering chords make the three beats plain. The melody keeps running across them.

    2. Victor, April 1923

      On 5 April 1923 Rachmaninoff records this waltz for Victor. The day’s sessions also include his playing of a piano arrangement of Strauss’s The Blue Danube. The composer of vast concertos is at work on music for a single record side.

    3. A trill without a bass

      The accompaniment stops. A long trill occupies the right hand alone, hanging over the gap before the circling quavers resume. For a few bars, Chopin removes the waltz’s feet from the floor.

  2. Waltz in D♭ major, Op. 64 No. 1 (“Minute”)Olga Gurevich, piano · c. 2014
    1:47

    Chopin’s Minute Waltz spins on a handful of notes, then opens into a long singing phrase. Olga Gurevich plays it for Musopen’s Set Chopin Free project.

    Notes as it plays
    1. Four bars alone

      The right hand circles A♭, dipping to G♮ and rising to B♭. For four bars it has the keyboard to itself. Then the left hand supplies the waltz: a low bass note followed by two chords, while the little wheel above it keeps turning.

    2. A waltz to share

      Gurevich’s performance belongs to Musopen’s Set Chopin Free project. Musopen releases the recording into the public domain: anyone can copy it, teach with it or pass it on. A nineteenth-century salon piece finds a new home on the open web.

    3. The little wheel returns

      The circling quavers return alone. This time the left hand’s arrival is marked forte; three bars later, the opening tune climbs above them again. Chopin brings back the same little figure with a stronger push from below.

  3. Waltz in D♭ major, Op. 64 No. 1 (“Minute”)Muriel Nguyen Xuan, piano · 2007
    2:00

    Muriel Nguyen Xuan plays Chopin’s Minute Waltz on a Yamaha grand, recorded by Stéphane Magnenat in 2007. Between its whirling outer passages, a melody marked sostenuto stretches out over the same three-beat bass.

    Notes as it plays
    1. A small circle

      Molto vivace, very lively; leggiero, lightly. Chopin starts with quavers turning around A♭. The left hand waits four bars, then adds a bass note and two chords to each bar. The right hand soon escapes upwards into a tune.

    2. Two microphones and a piano

      Nguyen Xuan sits at a Yamaha grand; Magnenat records with two AKG microphones and Audacity running on Linux. They put both their names on the recording and make it freely shareable. The piano reaches this page through their 2007 collaboration.

    3. Twenty-four notes downhill

      The right hand leaps high, then runs down through twenty-four notes. The left hand leaves most of that descent bare. At the bottom, a final D♭ and a firm bass chord shut the waltz.
