Gioachino Rossini / III. Romanticism

Il barbiere di Siviglia

1816 · Comic opera in two acts

Beaumarchais through Cesare Sterbini: a barber, a resourceful young woman and two disguises for a tenor. The place to start.

2 recordings · 11 min

In the Hall

  1. OvertureChicago Symphony Orchestra; Fritz Reiner · 22 November 1958, Orchestra Hall, Chicago
    7:03

    A stately opening, a scurrying tune and a crescendo that keeps finding another gear. Fritz Reiner and the Chicago Symphony Orchestra play the overture that Rossini put to work in three different operas.

    Notes as it plays
    1. A stately opening

      The introduction moves slowly in E major. Loud orchestral chords interrupt the quieter phrases; the music makes a great ceremony of getting ready.

    2. The quick minor-key tune

      The Allegro vivo starts in E minor, with quick, light string figures. Rossini makes room for woodwind solos between the orchestral bursts.

    3. Three operas, one overture

      Before Figaro acquired this overture, it opened Aureliano in Palmira in 1813 and Elisabetta, regina d’Inghilterra in 1815. Two serious operas supplied the curtain-raiser for a comedy.

    4. The major-key finish

      The ending settles firmly in E major. Short figures repeat, the orchestra grows louder, and the cadence receives one emphatic reinforcement after another.

  2. Act I · Largo al factotumTitta Ruffo, baritone; Josef Pasternack, conductor · 19 April 1920
    4:08

    Figaro arrives at dawn with a guitar round his neck and a magnificent opinion of himself. Titta Ruffo sings his entrance aria on a Victor acoustic recording made in 1920.

    Notes as it plays
    1. The barber advertises himself

      The bouncing 6/8 carries Figaro’s self-portrait: always ready, always in demand, delighted with his trade. His name becomes a little fanfare of its own.

    2. Razors, combs and lancets

      Sterbini equips Figaro with razors, combs, lancets and scissors. Shaving is only part of the business: this barber also lets blood and carries messages between lovers.

    3. A crowd in one voice

      Figaro acts out his customers’ demands himself: a wig, a shave, a bloodletting. The repeated calls of his name leave him pleading for one person at a time, before he boasts that he is as quick as lightning.
