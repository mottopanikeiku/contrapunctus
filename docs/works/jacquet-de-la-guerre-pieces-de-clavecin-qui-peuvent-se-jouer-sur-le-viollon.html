Élisabeth Jacquet de La Guerre / I. The Baroque

Pièces de clavecin qui peuvent se joüer sur le viollon

1707 · EJG.05–06 · Harpsichord

Two suites, in D minor and G major, seventeen pieces in all, dedicated to Louis XIV.

2 recordings · 6 min

In the Hall

  1. Suite in G major · AllemandeIsabelle Nef, harpsichord
    3:04

    Isabelle Nef plays the Allemande from the G major suite of Élisabeth Jacquet de La Guerre’s Pièces de clavecin qui peuvent se joüer sur le viollon, published in 1707, on a 78 rpm disc from Éditions de l’Oiseau-Lyre. The first strain has eight bars and the second nine; each is played twice.

    Listening notes
    1. Allemande in G major, first strain

      The allemande is in four beats to the bar and in two strains. The first strain has eight bars. It begins on an upbeat B and closes on a D major chord; bar 1 opens on a G major chord, followed by running left-hand semiquavers.

    2. First strain repeated

      The first strain is played again from its upbeat B, at about 0:42, and closes on the same D major chord. The second ending, bar 9, leads into the second strain.

    3. Éditions de l’Oiseau-Lyre

      Louise Hanson-Dyer founded Éditions de l’Oiseau-Lyre in Paris as a music press. From December 1937 it also issued 78 rpm recordings, often to accompany printed scores. This disc, O. L. 75, is dated 1942 by the Bibliothèque nationale de France; an Oiseau-Lyre discography places its release in 1938 or 1939.

    4. Second strain, bars 10–18

      The second strain, marked Reprise, has nine bars, 10 to 18, against eight in the first. It begins on a spread D major chord. The recording plays it twice, the repeat starting at about 2:07, and ends on a G major chord at about 2:53.

    5. Titon du Tillet on her improvising, 1732

      In 1732 Titon du Tillet wrote that Jacquet de La Guerre had a marvellous talent for preluding and playing fantasias on the spot. He added that she sometimes kept a prelude and a fantasia going for half an hour.

  2. Suite in D minor · 2e GigueIsabelle Nef, harpsichord
    2:34

    The reverse of Isabelle Nef’s Oiseau-Lyre disc has the second gigue, headed 2e Gigue, of the D minor suite in Élisabeth Jacquet de La Guerre’s Pièces de clavecin of 1707. It is in six-eight time. The first strain, of 25 bars, is played twice, and the second, of 38 bars, once. The Allemande on the other side comes from the G major suite of the same book.

    Listening notes
    1. 2e Gigue, first strain

      The 2e Gigue is in six-eight time and in two strains, each with a repeat sign. The first strain has 25 bars. It begins on an upbeat A, reaches an E major chord with G♯ in bars 23–24 and closes on an A major chord.

    2. First strain repeated

      The first strain is played again from its upbeat A, at about 0:43, and closes on the same A major chord. The second ending, bar 26, leads into the second strain.

    3. Second strain, bars 27–64

      The second strain, headed Reprise, has 38 bars against 25 in the first. The right hand begins F5–E5–D5–E5. The recording plays the strain once and omits its printed repeat; it ends on a D minor chord.

    4. Final chord

      The gigue ends on a D minor chord at about 2:23, held for about four seconds. Surface noise fills the rest of the file.
