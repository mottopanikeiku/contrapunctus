Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart / II. Classical

Symphony No. 40, K. 550

1788 · Orchestra

4 recordings · 23 min

In the Hall

  1. I. Molto allegroMusopen Symphony (Commons credit; ensemble attribution disputed)
    5:59

    The first movement, Molto allegro, of the Symphony No. 40, K. 550, in G minor. The violas are already playing when the violins begin the first theme, and its falling semitone returns in fragments throughout the movement and in the second theme, which comes back changed.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. Opening, violas and violins

      The violas set up an accompaniment in repeated notes. Over it the violins repeat a note and fall a semitone, then repeat the figure. There is no opening chord before the theme.

    2. Second subject in B♭ major

      Strings and woodwinds exchange the second theme in B♭ major, the relative major. Its phrases are longer than those of the opening, and chromatic semitones run through the melody.

    3. Move to new lodgings, June 1788

      In June 1788 Mozart moved to cheaper lodgings outside central Vienna. In a letter to Michael Puchberg he wrote that he welcomed having fewer visitors, more time to work and a garden. When business called, he could still hire a carriage into town.

    4. Development in F♯ minor

      The development opens with the violin theme in F♯ minor, a remote key after the B♭ major close of the exposition. Soon only its first few notes remain. Strings and winds pass these fragments between them, each entry starting before the previous one has finished.

    5. Recapitulation in G minor

      The opening theme returns in the violins over the moving viola accompaniment. Mozart expands the transition and continues to work the short fragments between the first and second themes.

    6. Second subject in G minor

      In the recapitulation the second theme is in G minor, so both themes are now in the same key. Strings and winds still exchange its small, winding phrases, with some notes altered from its first appearance.

    7. Closing wind chords and final cadences

      Near the close, the winds hold quiet chords above the strings. The orchestra then returns to forceful chords and running notes for the last G minor cadences.

    Joins

    1. Dominant pause before the second theme

      The transition comes to rest on F major, the dominant of B♭, and breaks off. After the pause, quiet strings and woodwinds begin the second theme in B♭ major. Its phrases are longer and more chromatic than those of the forceful orchestral bridge before it. The tempo remains Molto allegro.

    2. B♭ major to F♯ minor

      The exposition closes in B♭ major. Mozart moved straight to F♯ minor, where the violins take up the first theme again over soft wind chords. Its repeated notes and falling semitone are still recognisable in this remote key. The melody soon breaks into shorter, overlapping entries.

    3. Dominant D before the main-theme return

      The harmony comes to rest on D, the dominant of G minor, and the last fragments of the theme grow quieter. Above a held horn D, the bassoon descends. The violins then play the full opening phrase again over the moving violas, back in G minor.

    4. Second theme returns in G minor

      The expanded transition reaches D major, the dominant of G minor. After another break the second theme enters, now in G minor. Strings and woodwinds continue their quiet exchange, and B♭ is the minor third above G.

  2. II. AndanteMusopen Symphony (Commons credit; ensemble attribution disputed)
    7:36

    The Andante is the second movement of the Symphony No. 40 in G minor, K. 550. The violas open it with repeated notes and the violins enter one group at a time, and the short figures between its phrases become more forceful in the middle section.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. Opening, staggered string entries

      The violas repeat a note. Second violins enter above them, then first violins higher still, each part starting before the previous one has finished. A melody grows out of these overlapping entries, and the woodwinds add replies.

    2. Last three symphonies, 1788

      Mozart entered his last three symphonies in his own catalogue on 26 June, 25 July and 10 August 1788. They were the E♭ major, this G minor work and the Jupiter, all dated within forty-five days.

    3. Short figures, strings and woodwinds

      In bars 20–32 short, quick figures pass between the strings and woodwinds, filling the spaces between the longer notes.

    4. Letter to Michael Puchberg, 1788

      On 27 June 1788 Mozart wrote to Michael Puchberg from his new lodgings in Vienna: “In the ten days I have lived here I have worked more than in two months at the other place.” He asked his friend to visit, adding that he was always at home.

    5. Development, bars 53–73

      The development takes up the repeated notes and the quick string figures. The harmonies grow sharper, and the wind play strong chords. The tempo stays at Andante.

    6. Recapitulation in E♭ major

      The repeated-note phrase returns in E♭ major, and the melody is built up again through the strings. The quick interjections also return.

    7. Revised version with clarinets

      Mozart later revised the symphony, adding two clarinets and rewriting the oboe parts to accommodate them. Both versions survive. The string writing of the Andante is the same in each, and only the wind scoring differs.

    8. Musopen Kickstarter campaign, 2010

      Musopen is a nonprofit that Aaron Dunn founded in 2006. In 2010 it raised more than $68,000 on Kickstarter to record public-domain classics that anyone may use freely. This movement was one of the recordings that resulted, and Commons credits the players only as “Musopen Symphony”.

    Joins

    1. Repeated notes after the B♭ cadence

      The exposition closes in B♭ major. The strings restart the repeated-note figure. Quick string runs follow, set against louder wind chords. The tempo stays Andante, and chromatic notes move the harmony away from the B♭ cadence.

    2. Return of the opening in E♭ major

      The development dies down into short woodwind replies above the strings. The repeated-note opening then returns in E♭ major, and the string entries overlap again. The rhythm of the opening phrase is unchanged, and the texture is lighter than in the crowded exchanges of the middle section.

  3. III. Menuetto: AllegrettoMusopen Symphony (Commons credit; ensemble attribution disputed)
    4:33

    The third movement of Mozart’s Symphony No. 40 in G minor, K. 550, is a minuet in three-beat bars, with the winds reinforcing the strings. In the Trio the key changes to G major and the orchestra divides into smaller groups.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. Opening of the minuet

      The strings begin with a rising, strongly accented phrase, reinforced by winds and horns. The minuet keeps three beats in the bar, and the melody is accented across them.

    2. Imitation in the second strain

      In the minuet’s second strain, bars 15–42, the string parts enter in imitation with fragments of the tune. The lower strings play against the violins above them.

    3. Trio in G major

      The Trio moves from G minor to G major. The strings play a phrase and the winds reply, with the horns joining the exchanges.

    4. Origin of the name “Trio”

      The middle dance is called the Trio because in the seventeenth century it was usually scored for three instruments. The name remained in use after the practice ended. Mozart scores his Trio for strings and winds in changing groups.

    5. Catalogue entry, 25 July 1788

      On 25 July 1788 Mozart entered this G minor symphony in his catalogue. The E♭ symphony had been entered on 26 June, and the C major Jupiter followed on 10 August.

    6. Menuetto da capo

      At the end of the Trio, Mozart marked “Menuetto da capo”, and the orchestra repeats the minuet, bars 1–42. G minor and the heavy accents return at once.

    7. Tonkünstler-Societät concerts, Vienna, 1791

      In April 1791 the Vienna Tonkünstler-Societät gave its concerts, and the programme announced “A Grand Symphony composed by Herr Mozart”, with Antonio Salieri conducting that part. This G minor symphony later gained a second version with clarinets. Both versions survive.

    Joins

    1. Minuet to Trio: G minor to G major

      The minuet ends in G minor and the Trio begins in G major. G stays the tonic and B♭ becomes B♮. The full orchestra stops, the strings begin a lighter phrase and the wind reply. No passage connects the two sections.

    2. Return of the Menuetto after the Trio

      The Trio closes in G major, and the da capo instruction brings back the minuet in G minor. Winds and horns reinforce the strings again. The change from B♮ to B♭ restores the opening key, and the rising phrase returns with its forceful accents.

  4. IV. Finale: Allegro assaiMusopen Symphony (Commons credit; ensemble attribution disputed)
    4:45

    The finale of Mozart’s Symphony No. 40 in G minor, K. 550, marked Allegro assai, opens with a rising string arpeggio played piano, and the full orchestra replies forte. The lyrical second theme later returns in G minor, and the movement stays in that key to the end.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. Opening theme, bars 1–16

      The strings rise through a G minor chord. The orchestra follows with a forceful rushing figure. Mozart alternates the spare rising phrase with the fuller, crowded reply throughout the opening.

    2. Second theme in B♭ major

      The second theme is in B♭ major, shared between violins and winds. It moves in longer note values than the material around it. The accompanying pulse continues underneath.

    3. Charles Rosen on Mozart’s suffering

      The pianist and critic Charles Rosen wrote: “In all of Mozart’s supreme expressions of suffering and terror … there is something shockingly voluptuous.”

    4. Opening of the development

      At the development, the opening leap returns in unison, with chromatic notes added. The strings then pass fragments of it between them in overlapping entries.

    5. Orchestra without trumpets or timpani

      The whole symphony is scored for horns, woodwinds and strings. Mozart left out trumpets and timpani, and the finale’s loudest passages are played by this comparatively small orchestra.

    6. Recapitulation, bar 206

      The rising figure returns piano in G minor, followed as before by its loud reply. After the chromatic passages of the development, it again outlines the notes of the tonic chord.

    7. Second theme in G minor

      The second theme, first heard in B♭ major, returns in G minor in the recapitulation. The movement stays in G minor to the final chords.

    8. Critical reception of K. 550

      Neal Zaslaw wrote of K. 550: “No symphony of Mozart’s, not even the ‘Jupiter’, has aroused so much comment as this one.” In the nineteenth century Otto Jahn heard in it “sorrow and complaining”, and in the finale “a wild merriment that seeks to drown sorrow”.

    Joins

    1. Dominant of B♭ before the second theme

      The transition comes to rest on F, the dominant of B♭ major. After the break, violins and woodwinds share the quieter second theme. It moves in longer notes than the rushing answer of the opening, and the Allegro assai tempo continues.

    2. From exposition to development

      The exposition closes in B♭ major. The development opens with the opening leap stretched into a chromatic line played in unison by the orchestra, with no supporting chord. The strings then take up fragments of it in imitation, each entry following the one before.

    3. Return from D to G minor

      Running fragments in the strings move to D, the dominant of G minor (bars 193–214). The opening arpeggio then returns quietly in the strings, over the same G minor harmony as at the start of the movement. The loud reply follows, as it does in the opening bars.

    4. Dominant preparation for the second theme

      In the recapitulation the transition prepares G minor through its dominant, D. Violins and winds then resume the quieter second theme in the home key. Where the exposition moved to B♭ major for this theme, the melody now returns in G minor, and the orchestral contrast between the groups is kept.
