Igor Stravinsky / VI. Modernism

Petrushka

1910–11 · Ballet in four tableaux; large orchestra with piano

A piano concert piece became a puppet’s life among the crowds at a St Petersburg fair. Stravinsky reduced and redistributed the original orchestration in his 1947 revision.

4 recordings · 35 min

In the Hall

  1. I. The Shrovetide FairOrchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    9:51

    At a St Petersburg fair, a flute sings above the wheeze of an imaginary accordion; street dancers compete for customers, and a magician brings three puppets to life. Ernest Ansermet conducts the original 1911 score, with its unruly piano at the heart of the orchestra.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Flute above an accordion

      A flute throws its tune across rocking clarinets and horns. Their alternating fifths and thirds imitate an accordion. Four solo cellos join the commotion: the fair already has several conversations going at once.

    2. The organ-grinder’s corner

      The organ-grinder keeps breaking into the crowd music. His little wind band slows to Meno mosso; flutes turn the handle while the clarinets supply the tune. Beside him, a street dancer beats time on a triangle.

    3. Two dancers compete

      At the other end of the stage, a second dancer starts her music box. The first answers with her triangle, and both acts continue together. Benois and Stravinsky put a small battle for customers inside the larger fair.

    4. The revellers come back

      The organ-grinder and music box fall silent. The barker resumes his sales pitch, and the dancing crowd returns. Stravinsky keeps cutting between the barker’s hurried announcements and the revellers’ familiar tune.

    5. Behind the little curtain

      Two drummers summon the crowd to the puppet theatre. The music slows to Lento: bassoons mutter, the double basses pluck, and celesta and harp scatter little descending figures. The magician has everyone’s attention.

    6. The magician plays

      The flute cadenza is the magician’s spell. Its runs climb and curl back, almost alone after the bustle of the fair. Stravinsky writes ad libitum, ma non accelerando: freely, without speeding up.

    7. Debussy’s letter

      On 13 April 1912, Debussy writes to Stravinsky about this spell: “a sort of magic in sound, a mysterious transformation of mechanical souls that become human”. He singles out the magician’s music for praise.

    8. The puppets spring to life

      Petrushka, the Ballerina and the Moor dance together. The piano hammers out chords with the woodwinds, while the strings pluck beneath them. Allegro giusto: after the magician’s freely paced flute, every joint seems to snap into time.

    9. Diaghilev hears a puppet

      In 1910 Diaghilev visits Stravinsky in Clarens expecting sketches for The Rite of Spring. Instead, Stravinsky plays him music about a puppet provoking an orchestra with furious piano arpeggios. Diaghilev wants a whole ballet. The piano keeps its starring part.

    How it is joined

    1. The organ-grinder interrupts

      The crowd’s rushing strings keep meeting a slower wind tune, marked Meno mosso. When the organ-grinder settles into his act, the strings die away; flutes repeat their little turns above clarinets and bass clarinet. The triangle supplies the dancer’s beat.

    2. Drums summon the crowd

      The theatre’s drummers cut through the crowd music. At Lento, bassoons begin their crooked little phrase above plucked double basses. Celesta, harp and muted strings join them, then the magician’s flute cadenza clears nearly everything else away.

    3. The piano starts the dance

      Harp glissandi and a final flourish of flute finish the spell. A pause separates them from Allegro giusto. The Russian Dance begins with piano and woodwind chords over a low C in the plucked basses; the piano’s running left hand keeps the dance moving between the string strokes.

  2. II. Petrushka’s roomOrchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    4:27

    Petrushka is kicked into his room and left alone with his fury. Two clarinets give him his unforgettable cry: C major and F♯ major, jammed together.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Two keys, one cry

      A foot kicks Petrushka through the door. His cry comes from two clarinets playing the notes of C major and F♯ major together. The piano catches the quarrel and sends it racing up and down the keyboard.

    2. Stars on cardboard

      Petrushka’s room has black cardboard walls painted with stars and a crescent moon. A portrait of the magician hangs above the hole the puppet punches in the wall. Even in his cell, his owner watches him.

    3. A brittle little band

      The furious outburst stops. A piano run climbs into Adagietto, where flutes repeat soft little figures and xylophone taps punctuate the piano’s high notes. The brass and strings leave Petrushka to this small, uncomfortable company.

    4. The ballet starts here

      Before there is a fairground, Stravinsky has this unhappy puppet at the piano. The piece he plays to Diaghilev becomes the second tableau of Petrushka. The whole bustling ballet grows around a creature shut in a room.

    5. An awkward courtship

      The Ballerina visits, and Petrushka tries to impress her. At Allegro, piano runs tumble between clipped wind phrases; the bars keep switching length. His eager leaps frighten her away.

    6. The clarinets remain

      The two clarinets bring back Petrushka’s cry in a slow cadenza. C major and F♯ major still rub against each other. The curtain closes on the same impossible pair of chords that speaks for him at the beginning.

    How it is joined

    1. A piano run after the shout

      A held orchestral chord breaks off at a pause. The piano climbs through a soft, glissando-like run into Adagietto. Flutes repeat a rocking figure above the piano’s low notes, while clarinets and xylophone add short taps. The brass and strings fall silent.

    2. Petrushka starts showing off

      The gentler flute-and-piano music turns into Allegro. Bassoons and oboes jab at repeated notes while the piano throws out arpeggios. Bars of two and three crotchets alternate; the strings switch between plucking and bowing as Petrushka dances.

    3. A clarinet cries after her

      The dance breaks off. A solo clarinet stretches its cadenza into a slow lament, marked molto ritardando and lamentoso assai. Quiet cellos hold beneath it; the piano answers with rushing arpeggios, followed by a cor anglais call.

  3. III. The Moor’s roomOrchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    7:24

    The Ballerina courts Petrushka’s rival with a cornet solo and a pair of borrowed waltzes. Her partner dances in two while she dances in three; Stravinsky lets the mismatch stand.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. A door into another room

      Offstage drums separate the scenes. Wind chords and plucked strings break in, marked Feroce stringendo — fierce and pressing forward. The bowed strings then begin their heavy, swooping figures.

    2. The heavy tread

      The pace slows to Sostenuto. Bassoons and tuba sound low in the orchestra; plucked strings and percussion establish a steady tread. The main tune belongs to clarinet and bass clarinet, doubling each other an octave apart.

    3. Palms painted on paper

      Benois gives this puppet a low sofa, a magnificent robe and paper walls painted with palms and exotic fruit. He plays with a coconut. The ballet’s colonial caricature is written into the room as plainly as its luxury.

    4. A cornet in her hands

      The Ballerina makes her entrance carrying a toy brass instrument. Her dance uses just cornet and military drum: a nimble tune above little drum strokes. For a moment, this enormous orchestra becomes a band of two.

    5. A borrowed ballroom

      The waltz begins with a solo bassoon accompaniment, marked grottesco. Cornet and flute take the melody. Stravinsky borrows the tunes from Joseph Lanner, bringing a little Viennese ballroom into the puppet theatre.

    6. Three steps against two

      The Ballerina’s waltz has three beats to the bar. Her partner keeps his heavy two-beat dance going underneath it. The cor anglais and contrabassoon join the lower strings in his tune, while her melody continues above.

    7. Petrushka at the door

      The couple prick up their ears. Stopped horns and muted trumpets creep into the waltz; Petrushka’s cry grows among them. His entrance turns the dance into a quarrel.

    8. Thrown out again

      The Ballerina runs away from the fight. The Moor drives Petrushka out of his room, and the curtain falls. Beyond these cardboard walls, the fair carries on into the evening.

    How it is joined

    1. Bassoons slow the tread

      The fierce string figures subside into Sostenuto. Bassoons hold a chord while the tuba settles on a long low note. Timpani and muted strings prepare the repeating accompaniment; clarinet and bass clarinet then begin the Moor’s tune in octaves.

    2. A band of two

      The low accompaniment stops. At Allegro, the military drum sets off alone; the cornet joins it with the Ballerina’s tune. Bassoons, tuba and strings all rest while her little performance occupies the room.

    3. Flute flourish, then a waltz

      A brief Appassionato outburst in winds and strings ends with a descending flute flourish and a pause. The bassoon starts a lilting accompaniment in three, marked Lento cantabile and grottesco. Cornet and flute add the waltz tune, sweetly marked sentimentalmente.

    4. The brass interrupts the couple

      At Vivace, the waltz thins to repeated viola chords and stopped horns. Muted trumpets and cornets bring back Petrushka’s cry, growing from pianissimo. The two dancers stop to hear him before he bursts into the room.

  4. IV. The Shrovetide Fair (evening)Orchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    13:23

    Evening brings nursemaids, coachmen and a performing bear back to the fair; then Petrushka’s quarrel spills out of the puppet theatre. The crowd goes home, snow falls, and two muted trumpets give the dead puppet the last laugh.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Evening at the fair

      The drums roll again and the curtain opens on the fair at dusk. The crowd’s dance returns in winds, brass and strings. The two cramped puppet rooms are behind us; the stage fills with people.

    2. The nursemaids’ song

      Bassoons bustle above plucked strings, and an oboe sings Down the Petersky Road. The nursemaids’ tune is an old Russian song. It starts modestly, then passes to the horns and spreads through the orchestra.

    3. Nijinsky behind the mask

      At the first performance, in Paris on 13 June 1911, Nijinsky dances Petrushka and Tamara Karsavina the Ballerina. Pierre Monteux conducts. Benois supplies the sets and costumes, and Fokine choreographs the puppets and their enormous crowd.

    4. A pipe and a bear

      The crowd makes room for a peasant with a performing bear. High clarinets play the peasant’s pipe; a solo tuba answers as the animal, heavy and ungainly. The two voices are several octaves apart.

    5. Banknotes in the crowd

      A merchant arrives with two women dancers, throws banknotes to the crowd and plays his accordion. Benois’s fair has room for money as well as magic. This attraction buys its audience.

    6. The coachmen plant their feet

      Clarinets and horns repeat a heavy pattern over tuba and timpani. The strings answer with chords marked pesante. These are the coachmen and grooms; when the nursemaids join them, the dance gains another tune without losing its tread.

    7. Masks in Bengal light

      The masqueraders arrive by the glow of Bengal lights. Flutes trade quick phrases above celesta and two harps, then strings and clarinets pick up the running figures. Death himself appears among the masks and invites the crowd to dance.

    8. Cries from the theatre

      Muted trumpets and cornets cry out from the puppet theatre. Petrushka runs into the fair, pursued by the Moor; the Ballerina tries to hold his attacker back. The dance breaks into a chase.

    9. Snow begins to fall

      Petrushka falls with his head broken. The music slows to Lento: two piccolos call softly above divided violas, and a clarinet sings a tender little phrase. Snow begins to fall as the crowd gathers around him.

    10. The puppet answers back

      The magician has shown the crowd a lifeless doll. Now Petrushka’s shadow appears above the theatre, thumbing its nose: two trumpets with metal mutes give him back his cry. The magician flees. A few plucked string notes close the ballet.

    How it is joined

    1. Bassoons prepare the song

      At Allegretto, busy bassoon figures and plucked strings replace the crowd’s full orchestra. Solo violins try out scraps of the tune before the oboe sings it. Repeated cello notes keep a small, steady pulse beneath the song.

    2. Clarinets pipe above the bear

      The crowd scatters. At Sostenuto, two high clarinets pipe in octaves above slowly stepping bassoons and low strings. The solo tuba answers their tune from far below, its clumsy imitation carrying the bear across the stage.

    3. Tuba and timpani set the tread

      The merchant’s party withdraws. Moderato begins the coachmen’s dance with repeated low notes in tuba and timpani. Clarinets and horns rock above them, then the strings add their heavy chords, marked pesante.

    4. Celesta and harps light the masks

      The coachmen’s massed dance breaks into Agitato. Flutes exchange short phrases while celesta and two harps play repeated notes between them. Plucked cellos and second violins enter below; the heavy brass is gone from this first glimpse of the masks.

    5. The trumpets stop the dance

      The orchestra’s repeated dance chords stop, leaving a muted trumpet call hanging in the air. A second trumpet joins it, then the cornets. The score broadens with Poco allargando before the puppets’ chase bursts onto the fairground.

    6. A tambourine falls to the floor

      The strings sweep upwards in glissandi as Petrushka is struck. A percussionist drops a tambourine close to the floor. At Lento, two soft piccolos and divided violas remain around the fallen puppet; clarinets join them in short, quiet phrases.

    7. Muted trumpets bring Petrushka back

      The departing crowd leaves soft horn figures and oboe notes behind. A piccolo trumpet in D, fitted with a metal mute, cuts across them; the second trumpet answers. Their arpeggios recall the puppet’s cry from his room while the low strings stay silent.
