Igor Stravinsky / VI. Modernism

Petrushka

1910–⁠11 · Ballet in four tableaux; large orchestra with piano

A piano concert piece became a puppet’s life among crowds at a St Petersburg fair. Stravinsky reduced and redistributed the original orchestration in his 1947 revision.

4 recordings · 35 min

In the Hall

  1. I. The Shrovetide FairOrchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    9:51

    The first tableau of Petrushka is set at a fair in St Petersburg, where a flute plays over an imitation of an accordion, street dancers compete for customers and a magician brings three puppets to life. Ernest Ansermet conducts the original 1911 score, which places a piano within the orchestra.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. Flute solo over accordion figure

      A flute plays its tune over clarinets and horns, which rock between alternating fifths and thirds in imitation of an accordion. Four solo cellos enter.

    2. Organ-grinder episode

      The organ-grinder’s music repeatedly interrupts the crowd music. The tempo slows to Meno mosso, and a small wind group takes over: flutes play the figure of the turning handle and clarinets play the tune. A street dancer beside him beats time on a triangle.

    3. Second dancer, rehearsals 15–⁠16

      At the other end of the stage a second dancer starts her music box. The first dancer plays her triangle, and the two acts continue at the same time. Benois and Stravinsky set this competition for customers within the larger fair scene.

    4. Return of the crowd

      The organ-grinder and music box stop. The barker resumes his sales pitch, and the dancing crowd returns. Stravinsky alternates the barker’s hurried announcements with the revellers’ tune from earlier.

    5. Drummers and the magician’s entrance

      Two drummers call the crowd to the puppet theatre. The tempo slows to Lento for the magician’s entrance. Bassoons play low, the double basses pluck, and celesta and harp play short descending figures.

    6. Flute cadenza, rehearsal 31

      At rehearsal 31, headed “The Magician Plays the Flute” in the score, the flute plays a cadenza after the music of the fair stops. Its runs rise and turn back down. Stravinsky marks it ad libitum, ma non accelerando, meaning freely, without speeding up.

    7. Letter from Debussy, 13 April 1912

      On 13 April 1912 Debussy wrote to Stravinsky about this music, calling it “a sort of magic in sound, a mysterious transformation of mechanical souls that become human”. He praised the magician’s music in particular.

    8. Russian Dance, rehearsal 33

      Petrushka, the Ballerina and the Moor dance together. The piano plays chords with the woodwinds while the strings play pizzicato beneath them. The tempo is Allegro giusto, after the magician’s flute solo in free time.

    9. Diaghilev’s visit to Clarens, 1910

      In 1910 Diaghilev visited Stravinsky in Clarens, expecting sketches for The Rite of Spring. Stravinsky played him music for piano and orchestra about a puppet, with furious piano arpeggios set against the orchestra. Diaghilev asked for a whole ballet, and the piano kept a leading part in the score.

    Joins

    1. The organ-grinder’s entry

      The rushing string figures of the crowd alternate with a slower wind tune marked Meno mosso. When the organ-grinder’s music begins in earnest, the strings fade out. Flutes repeat short turns above clarinets and bass clarinet, and the triangle plays the dancer’s beat.

    2. Theatre drummers lead to the Lento

      The theatre’s drummers play over the crowd music. At Lento, the bassoons begin a phrase above plucked double basses. Celesta, harp and muted strings join them. The flute then plays the magician’s cadenza while nearly all the rest of the orchestra stops.

    3. Into the Russian Dance

      Harp glissandi and a final flourish on the flute end the conjuring trick. A pause follows before the Allegro giusto. The Russian Dance begins with chords in the piano and woodwind over a low C in the plucked basses. Between the string strokes, the piano’s left hand plays running figures that keep the dance going.

  2. II. Petrushka’s roomOrchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    4:27

    In the second tableau of Petrushka, heard here in the 1911 original version, Petrushka is kicked into his room and left alone with his fury. Two clarinets play his cry, sounding C major and F♯ major together.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. C major and F♯ major clarinets

      A foot kicks Petrushka through the door. Two clarinets play arpeggios on C major and F♯ major together for his cry. The piano takes up the figure and runs it up and down the keyboard.

    2. Scenery of the second tableau

      Petrushka’s room has black cardboard walls painted with stars and a crescent moon. A portrait of the magician hangs above the hole the puppet punches in the wall.

    3. Adagietto, rehearsal 52

      The loud outburst stops. A piano run climbs into the Adagietto, where flutes repeat soft short figures and the xylophone plays with the piano’s high notes. Brass and strings rest.

    4. Origin of the second tableau

      Stravinsky wrote music for this unhappy puppet at the piano before the fairground scenes existed. By his own account, the piece he played to Diaghilev became the second tableau of Petrushka, and the rest of the ballet was built around it.

    5. Entrance of the Ballerina

      The Ballerina enters, and Petrushka tries to impress her. At Allegro, piano runs alternate with short wind phrases, and the metre changes from bar to bar. In the scenario his leaps alarm her, and she leaves.

    6. Clarinet cadenza, rehearsal 61

      The two clarinets bring back Petrushka’s cry in a slow cadenza. C major and F♯ major still sound together. The curtain closes on the same pair of chords that is associated with him at the beginning.

    Joins

    1. Piano run into the Adagietto

      A held orchestral chord breaks off at a pause. At rehearsal 52 the piano plays a soft, rising run, close to a glissando, into the Adagietto. Flutes repeat a rocking figure above the piano’s low notes, and clarinets and xylophone add short taps. Brass and strings rest.

    2. Flute and piano music to Allegro

      At rehearsals 55–⁠56 (Édition Russe score, pp. 69–⁠70) the music for flute and piano is followed by Allegro. Bassoons and oboes play repeated notes and the piano plays arpeggios. Bars of two crotchets alternate with bars of three. The strings move between pizzicato and arco while Petrushka dances.

    3. Clarinet cadenza after the dance

      The dance stops. A solo clarinet extends its cadenza, marked molto ritardando and lamentoso assai, over held notes in the cellos. The piano then plays rushing arpeggios, and a call on the cor anglais follows.

  3. III. The Moor’s roomOrchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    7:24

    The third tableau of Stravinsky’s Petrushka, heard here in the original 1911 version, takes place in the Moor’s room. The Ballerina courts Petrushka’s rival to a cornet solo and a pair of borrowed waltzes, and her partner dances in two while she dances in three.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. Offstage drums, rehearsals 62–⁠63

      Offstage drums separate the scenes. Wind chords and plucked strings enter, marked Feroce stringendo (fierce and pressing forward). The bowed strings then begin heavy, swooping figures.

    2. Sostenuto, clarinet and bass clarinet

      The tempo slows to Sostenuto. Bassoons and tuba play low in the orchestra, and plucked strings and percussion set a steady beat. Clarinet and bass clarinet play the main tune, doubled an octave apart.

    3. Scenery of the third tableau

      Benois gave this puppet a low sofa, a magnificent robe and paper walls painted with palms and exotic fruit. He plays with a coconut.

    4. Dance of the Ballerina, cornet and drum

      The Ballerina enters carrying a toy brass instrument. Her dance opens with solo cornet and military drum, the cornet playing a quick tune over short drum strokes.

    5. Waltz on tunes by Lanner

      The waltz begins with an accompaniment on solo bassoon, marked grottesco. Cornet and flute take the melody. The tunes are borrowed from Joseph Lanner.

    6. The Moor joins the Ballerina’s waltz

      The Ballerina’s waltz is in three beats to the bar. Underneath it, her partner continues his heavy dance in two. Cor anglais and contrabassoon double the lower strings in his tune, and her melody continues above.

    7. Petrushka’s entrance, rehearsals 76–⁠78

      The couple listen. Stopped horns and muted trumpets enter during the waltz, and Petrushka’s cry grows louder among them. He appears, and the quarrel begins.

    8. End of the third tableau

      The Ballerina runs away from the fight. The Moor drives Petrushka out of his room, and the curtain falls. Outside, the fair carries on into the evening.

    Joins

    1. Strings subside into Sostenuto

      The loud string figures die down into Sostenuto. Bassoons hold a chord and the tuba holds a long low note. Timpani and muted strings set up the repeating accompaniment. Clarinet and bass clarinet then begin the Moor’s tune in octaves.

    2. Drum and cornet alone

      The low accompaniment stops. At Allegro the military drum enters alone, and the cornet joins it with the Ballerina’s tune. Bassoons, tuba and strings rest for the length of her dance.

    3. Flute flourish, then a waltz

      A brief Appassionato outburst in winds and strings ends with a descending flute flourish and a pause. The bassoon then plays an accompaniment in triple time, marked Lento cantabile and grottesco. Cornet and flute enter with the waltz tune, marked sentimentalmente.

    4. Petrushka’s cry enters in the brass

      At Vivace (rehearsals 76–⁠77), the waltz thins to repeated viola chords and stopped horns. Muted trumpets and cornets play Petrushka’s cry again, with a crescendo from pianissimo. The two dancers stop when they hear him, and then Petrushka bursts into the room.

  4. IV. The Shrovetide Fair (evening)Orchestre de la Suisse Romande; Ernest Ansermet · 1957
    13:23

    The fourth tableau of Petrushka in the 1911 original version is set at the Shrovetide Fair in the evening. Nursemaids, coachmen and a performing bear return to the fair, Petrushka’s quarrel spills out of the puppet theatre, the crowd goes home, snow falls, and two muted trumpets play over the dead puppet.

    Listening notes · joins
    1. Fourth tableau: the fair at dusk

      The drums roll again and the curtain opens on the fair at dusk. The crowd’s dance returns in winds, brass and strings. After the two puppet rooms of the middle tableaux, the stage fills with people.

    2. Origins of the ballet, Switzerland, 1910

      Stravinsky’s concerto-like piece for piano and orchestra, composed in Switzerland in 1910, showed a puppet, suddenly alive, cavorting up the keyboard until the orchestra exploded. It ended, he wrote, in “the sorrowful and querulous collapse of the poor puppet.” Diaghilev visited expecting The Rite sketches, heard it and urged a ballet.

    3. The nursemaids’ song

      Bassoons bustle above plucked strings, and an oboe sings Down the Petersky Road. The nursemaids’ tune is an old Russian song. It starts modestly, then passes to the horns and spreads through the orchestra.

    4. First performance, Paris, 1911

      At the first performance, in Paris on 13 June 1911, Nijinsky danced Petrushka and Tamara Karsavina the Ballerina. Pierre Monteux conducted. Benois designed the sets and costumes, and Fokine choreographed the puppets and the large crowd.

    5. Peasant with a bear

      The crowd makes room for a peasant with a performing bear. High clarinets play the peasant’s pipe, and a solo tuba plays the bear. The two parts lie several octaves apart.

    6. The merchant and two dancers

      A merchant arrives with two women dancers, throws banknotes to the crowd and plays his accordion.

    7. Dance of the Coachmen and Grooms

      At rehearsal 108, Moderato, clarinets and horns repeat a heavy pattern over tuba and timpani, and the strings play chords marked pesante. This is the dance of the coachmen and grooms. The nursemaids join them at rehearsal 112, adding a second tune.

    8. The 1911 score

      This recording uses Stravinsky’s original 1911 score. After the second tableau the piano is scarcely heard again. When Stravinsky rescored the ballet in 1946–⁠47, he reduced the woodwind and brass and gave the piano considerably more to play.

    9. The masqueraders, rehearsal 117

      The masqueraders arrive by Bengal lights. Flutes exchange short phrases above celesta and two harps, and strings and clarinets then take over the running figures. In the original scenario, Death appears among the masks and invites the crowd to dance.

    10. Petrushka pursued by the Moor

      Muted trumpets and cornets play cries from the puppet theatre. Petrushka runs into the fair with the Moor in pursuit, and the Ballerina tries to hold the Moor back. The dance is followed by a chase.

    11. Death of Petrushka, Lento

      Petrushka falls with his head broken. The tempo slows to Lento. Two piccolos play softly above divided violas, and a clarinet plays a short phrase marked dolcissimo, while snow begins to fall and the crowd gathers around him.

    12. The magician shows the puppet

      A policeman fetches the magician. He picks up Petrushka’s body and shakes it, showing the crowd that it is a wooden puppet stuffed with sawdust. The crowd disperses.

    13. Petrushka’s ghost and final bars

      After the magician has shown the crowd a lifeless doll, Petrushka’s shadow appears above the theatre, thumbing its nose. Two trumpets with metal mutes play his cry, and the magician flees. A few plucked string notes close the ballet.

    Joins

    1. Bassoons prepare the song

      At Allegretto, bassoon figures and plucked strings take over from the full orchestra of the crowd scene. Solo violins play fragments of the tune before the oboe states it. Repeated cello notes hold a steady pulse beneath the song.

    2. Clarinets above the bear

      The crowd scatters. At Sostenuto (rehearsal 100), two high clarinets play in octaves above slowly stepping bassoons and low strings. The solo tuba imitates their tune from far below as the bear crosses the stage.

    3. Tuba and timpani begin the coachmen’s dance

      The merchant’s party leaves at rehearsal 107. At rehearsal 108, marked Moderato, the coachmen’s dance begins with repeated low notes in tuba and timpani. Clarinets and horns play a rocking figure above them. The strings then add heavy chords, marked pesante.

    4. Coachmen’s dance, then Agitato

      The coachmen’s massed dance breaks into Agitato. Flutes exchange short phrases while celesta and two harps play repeated notes between them. Plucked cellos and second violins enter below. The heavy brass is silent in this first glimpse of the masks.

    5. Trumpet calls end the dance

      The orchestra’s repeated dance chords stop, and a muted trumpet call continues alone. A second trumpet joins it, then the cornets. The score broadens with Poco allargando before the puppets’ chase begins on the fairground.

    6. The tambourine drop before rehearsal 129

      The strings play upward glissandi as Petrushka is struck. A percussionist holds a tambourine close to the floor and lets it fall. At Lento two soft piccolos and divided violas continue around the fallen puppet, and clarinets join them in short, quiet phrases.

    7. Muted trumpets bring Petrushka back

      The crowd departs, leaving soft horn figures and oboe notes. A piccolo trumpet in D, fitted with a metal mute, cuts across them, and the second trumpet follows. Their arpeggios are drawn from the puppet’s cry from his room. The low strings are silent.
