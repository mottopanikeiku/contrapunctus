Alexander Scriabin / IV. Fin de siècle

Piano Sonata No. 5, Op. 53

1907 · Op. 53 · Piano

A single movement whose violent opening rush keeps haunting the music. Its epigraph comes from the poem Scriabin wrote for The Poem of Ecstasy.

12 min

The recording

  1. Complete · Allegro impetuoso con stravaganzaVictor Merzhanov, piano
    11:47

    A low rumble shoots to the top of the piano; the reply is quiet and languid. Scriabin’s Fifth Sonata keeps both characters in a single movement, driving its soft opening song towards a triple-forte climax.

    Notes as it plays
    1. From the bottom of the piano

      A trill and rumbling left hand send a rush of notes upwards. A pause clears the keyboard for Languido: soft, sweet chords and a melody marked con voglia, with desire. The sonata begins with two kinds of motion.

    2. Joy, off the beat

      Presto con allegrezza — very fast, with joy — gives the sonata its skipping main theme. The right-hand chords fall against the left hand’s leaping accompaniment, making the phrase bounce across the beat.

    3. Meno vivo

      The slower Meno vivo brings another melody, marked accarezzevole: caressingly. Chromatic inner voices move beneath it. This quiet idea will acquire a much louder, more forceful character in the development.

    4. One movement from now on

      Written in 1907, the Fifth is Scriabin’s first numbered piano sonata in a single movement. He keeps that form for all five that follow. The slow, fast and ecstatic passages now have to belong to one continuous span.

    5. The composer’s own epigraph

      Above the first bar Scriabin prints lines from his own Poem of Ecstasy: “I call you to life, O mysterious forces!” The words summon timid beginnings of life from the dark depths of the creative spirit.

    6. The dance comes back

      After the development’s increasingly forceful treatment of the themes, the skipping Presto returns. The sonata revisits its earlier ideas before a final expansion of the quiet melody from the introduction.

    7. Estatico at full force

      The soft introductory song reaches estatico and triple forte, high above repeated chords. Scriabin crowns it in E♭ major rather than the sonata’s F♯. The last page releases another upward rush and stops without a settling final chord.
