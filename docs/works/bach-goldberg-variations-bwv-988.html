Johann Sebastian Bach / I. The Baroque

Goldberg Variations, BWV 988

1741 · BWV 988 · Two-manual harpsichord

Thirty variations on the opening aria’s bass and harmonies, with a canon at every third step through No. 27. A medley of popular tunes precedes the aria’s return.

5 min

The recording

  1. AriaShelley Katz, piano
    4:52

    Shelley Katz plays the Aria that opens the Goldberg Variations: a richly ornamented tune above a bass that takes its time. Those quiet bass notes will support thirty variations before the Aria returns.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. The bass takes its time

      The right hand curls around its melody with trills and leaning notes. Below it, the bass descends G–F♯–E–D, one step per bar. Bach builds the thirty variations that follow on this bass and its harmonies; the ornate tune belongs to the Aria.

    2. A sleepless count

      In the story Forkel tells in his 1802 biography, Count Keyserlingk cannot sleep. Goldberg waits in an adjoining room to play for him, and Bach supplies the variations. The count rewards Bach with a golden cup filled with a hundred louis d’or.

    3. G returns beneath the ornaments

      After a cadence in D major, the first half starts again on G. The slow descending bass returns beneath the decorated melody. These sixteen bars get a second telling before the Aria moves on.

    4. A turn towards E minor

      The second half begins over D, then moves towards E minor. The melody keeps its ornaments, but the bass is more restless now. It takes eight bars to reach the E-minor cadence, then another eight to find G major again.

    5. Anna Magdalena’s two pages

      Anna Magdalena copies the Aria into two blank pages of her music notebook. The book begins in 1725; the handwriting places this entry around 1740. The music sits among the pieces collected and played in the Bach household.

    6. The second half starts again

      The G-major cadence sends the pianist back to the second half. Its E-minor passage returns, followed by the longer runs towards home. The final G sits beneath a fermata, with the melody still turning gently around it.

    How it is joined

    1. From D major back to G

      The first half closes on a D-major chord, with D in the bass. The repeat returns directly to the opening G. Above the renewed G–F♯–E–D bass descent, the right hand begins its ornamented melody again.

    2. The bass moves on from D

      The D-major cadence ends the first half for the second time. The next bar keeps D in the bass, then moves down to C; the following bars turn towards E minor. The pianist keeps the same three-beat pulse and the right hand’s elaborate ornaments.

    3. G major, then the second half

      The second half closes in G major, with a fermata over the final bar. Its repeat sends the pianist back to bar 17 and the bass D. The passage through E minor and the return to G major are heard a second time.
