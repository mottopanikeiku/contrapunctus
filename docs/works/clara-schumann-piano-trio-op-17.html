Clara Schumann / III. Romanticism

Piano Trio, Op. 17

1846 · Op. 17 · Piano, violin and cello

Written in Dresden and admired by Mendelssohn and Joachim. Its four movements include a lyrical Andante and a finale with a fugue.

2 recordings · 13 min

In the Hall

  1. I. Allegro moderatoThe Mannes-Gimpel-Silva Trio: Leopold Mannes, piano; Bronislav Gimpel, violin; Luigi Silva, cello · 1951
    7:46

    A falling fifth in the violin becomes the seed of an argument shared by all three players. Clara Schumann writes the second theme in B♭ major, brings it home in G major, then closes the movement in G minor.

    Notes as it plays
    1. A falling fifth

      The violin’s melody falls from D to G, then climbs to E♭. Beneath it the piano rocks in quavers. When the piano takes up the tune, violin and cello add answering lines: all three players have something to say.

    2. The piano leans across

      The second theme settles in B♭ major. The piano’s long notes lean across the beat, then break into quick repeated figures. Violin and cello answer with rising phrases whose diminished sevenths put an edge on the major key.

    3. The fifth changes shape

      Across the development, fragments of the opening melody pass between cello and violin. Its falling fifth expands or contracts as the harmony changes. The piano moves from descending chords into rushing arpeggios beneath the strings’ argument.

    4. The violin comes home

      The violin recovers its opening melody in G minor, with the piano’s rocking quavers underneath. After the development’s close exchanges, the original phrase returns almost unchanged.

    5. The same tune, G major

      The piano’s second theme returns in G major, brightening the home key. Clara preserves its offbeat long notes and quick replies. A brief excursion to B major takes the music far from the opening’s two flats.

    6. G minor claims the ending

      G minor takes hold of the coda. The opening melody returns in violin and cello, then breaks into shorter answering figures. A sustained G in the bass anchors the final chain of cadences.

  2. III. AndanteThe Mannes-Gimpel-Silva Trio: Leopold Mannes, piano; Bronislav Gimpel, violin; Luigi Silva, cello · 1951
    5:40

    The piano sings alone before violin and cello take their turns with the melody. An agitated middle interrupts this G-major song; the return gives the tune to the cello over rippling piano arpeggios.

    Notes as it plays
    1. The piano sings alone

      The piano begins an eight-bar song in G major, rocking gently in six-eight time. The violin then takes the melody while the cello plucks its accompaniment. Clara leaves room around the tune before giving each string player a turn.

    2. Her first chamber work

      Clara writes the Trio between May and September 1846. She already knows the chamber music of Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven from years at the piano; this is her first chamber composition.

    3. The song grows restless

      Più animato — more animated. Quick notes disturb the strings’ long phrases, and the piano answers with forceful chords. The movement’s middle section turns the gentle song into an argument in minor keys.

    4. The cello carries the song

      The opening tempo and G-major melody return, now in the cello. The violin plucks while the piano sends broad arpeggios up from the bass. A tune first heard at the keyboard has acquired a warmer, lower voice.

    5. Three players on Decca

      Leopold Mannes plays the piano, Bronislav Gimpel the violin and Luigi Silva the cello. Their Decca LP couples Clara’s Trio with a short B♭-major trio by Beethoven.
