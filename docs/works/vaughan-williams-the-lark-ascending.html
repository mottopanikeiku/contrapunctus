Ralph Vaughan Williams / VI. Modernism

The Lark Ascending

1914; 1920 · Violin and orchestra

Prefaced by lines of Meredith’s poem and dedicated to Marie Hall, who played the first performances of the piano version (1920) and the orchestral version (1921).

14 min

Recording

  1. The Lark AscendingDavid Wise, violin; Liverpool Philharmonic Orchestra; Malcolm Sargent · 1947
    14:05

    David Wise and the Liverpool Philharmonic under Malcolm Sargent recorded Vaughan Williams’s The Lark Ascending, a Romance for violin and orchestra, on 18 April 1947. The solo violin represents a skylark and opens with a cadenza marked pianissimo, after which the flutes and oboe play folk-like tunes.

    Listening notes
    1. Introduction and cadenza

      A short introduction for woodwind and muted strings leads to the soloist’s cadenza, marked pianissimo and sur la touche. In his 2014 note for Hyperion, Philip Borg-Wheeler likened the violin’s animated flurries to the song of a skylark.

    2. Meredith’s lines in the score

      Vaughan Williams prefaced the score with three passages from the poem by his near-neighbour George Meredith, among them “He rises and begins to round, / He drops the silver chain of sound”.

    3. Orchestral re-entry, 6/8 melody

      At the re-entry of the orchestra the solo violin takes up the 6/8 melody introduced towards the end of the cadenza. Occasional arabesques recall skylark song.

    4. Dedication to Marie Hall

      Vaughan Williams dedicated The Lark Ascending to the violinist Marie Hall. She gave the first performance with the pianist Geoffrey Mendham at Shirehampton Public Hall, Bristol, on 15 December 1920, and the first orchestral performance at Queen’s Hall the following June.

    5. Second cadenza, solo violin

      The orchestra stops and the violin plays a second cadenza alone, shorter than the first.

    6. Allegretto tranquillo episode, flutes

      The cadenza leads to a contrasting episode, Allegretto tranquillo. The flutes play a new melody and the violin plays around it.

    7. Recording, Liverpool, 18 April 1947

      David Wise recorded this version with the Liverpool Philharmonic under Malcolm Sargent in the Philharmonic Hall, Liverpool, on 18 April 1947. Columbia issued it on two 78-rpm discs, DX 1386 and DX 1387.

    8. Allegro tranquillo, violin and triangle

      The Allegro tranquillo begins with solo violin trills and arabesques, punctuated by an off-beat triangle. In each new episode the violin part takes a firmer melodic shape and keeps some of the skylark song.

    9. Oboe melody, scherzando, F major

      The key changes to F major and the time to 6/8. Five bars in, the oboe plays a further new melody, marked scherzando.

    10. Return of the flute melody

      The flutes’ melody returns, now played by the solo violin and marked Allegretto molto tranquillo.

    11. Return of the 6/8 section

      After the violin plays the flute melody, the earlier 6/8 section returns.

    12. Unaccompanied violin, close

      The orchestra stops, and the violin plays unaccompanied in skylark mode to the end of the piece.
