Arnold Schoenberg / VI. Modernism

String Quartet No. 2, Op. 10

1907–08 · Op. 10 · String quartet; soprano in movements III and IV

A soprano enters for two Stefan George settings. The finale loses its key signature but finds an F♯ major chord at the end.

10 min

The recording

  1. IV. Entrückung — Sehr langsamCarmel String Quartet; Rona Israel-Kolatt, soprano · 2007
    10:28

    A soprano joins four strings to sing Stefan George’s vision of leaving the earth. In Entrückung, Schoenberg lets the familiar pull of a home key disappear, then closes on a luminous F♯-major chord.

    Notes as it plays · how it is joined
    1. Four strings take flight

      The strings begin alone, their chromatic lines slipping free of a home key. Schoenberg describes this introduction as a departure from earth: gravity loosens its grip, the air grows thinner, and earthly troubles fall away.

    2. Air from another planet

      “Ich fühle luft von anderem planeten” — “I feel the air of another planet.” George’s first line names the strange place the strings have reached. The soprano sings among four independent instrumental lines.

    3. Faces fading from sight

      In George’s poem, friendly faces fade in the darkness. Trees and paths become unrecognisable; even the beloved’s shadow disappears. The speaker floats above ravines and clouds, until only a spark of sacred fire remains.

    4. Laughter in Vienna

      At the Bösendorfersaal on 21 December 1908, the Rosé Quartet and Marie Gutheil-Schoder face a hostile audience. In his later account, Schoenberg remembers laughter starting in the scherzo and continuing through the two movements with soprano.

    5. The strings remain

      The poem ends; the quartet continues alone. Four bowed instruments carry the long coda towards the F♯-major chord that will close the movement.

    6. The hall falls silent

      Schoenberg’s memory of that first performance changes at the coda. The audience has interrupted even the soprano. Once she finishes, the four strings play to the end without audible disturbance.

    7. F♯ major at last

      F♯, A♯ and C♯: a plain major chord closes this extraordinary finale. The quartet began in F♯ minor. Its final major third is a small, unmistakable light.

    How it is joined

    1. The soprano joins the quartet

      The soprano joins the strings over harmony that has yet to settle into a key. Her line adds George’s words to the quartet’s chromatic phrases; the cello remains a moving voice within the five-part writing.

    2. Four strings after the poem

      After the soprano’s final phrase, two violins, viola and cello continue alone. The coda has time to unfold as an instrumental conclusion, eventually reaching F♯ major.

    3. The final major third

      The strings settle on F♯ major. A♯ replaces the A♮ of the quartet’s original F♯ minor, and the final held chord brings the four instruments together.
