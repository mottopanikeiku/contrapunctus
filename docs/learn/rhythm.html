Why can a steady beat feel off balance?

Rhythm: pulse and accent can diverge

Hear itReadMore examplesPerformancesContinue

Keep a steady beat while the notes regroup: the accents can shift without the pulse speeding up.

Synthesised example

The idea in detail

Metre groups recurring pulses into a hierarchy of stronger and weaker positions. Rhythm is the actual pattern of attacks, durations and silences placed against that grouping. A melody can contradict the metre while the accompaniment maintains it. In a hemiola, six equal units are regrouped from two groups of three into three groups of two, or the reverse. Across two bars of 3/4, three minims can therefore displace the expected two sets of three crotchets without changing the underlying duration.

Hemiola is not a tempo changeThe span stays the same. What changes is the location of the grouping accents. Renaissance cadences, Baroque dances and Brahms’s phrase extensions use the device in different textures.

Additive rhythm constructs a longer group from unequal smaller units. A 7/8 bar can divide 2+2+3. Balkan aksak patterns combine short and long groups, commonly in a 2:3 relation. Bartók’s six dances in Bulgarian rhythm at the end of Mikrokosmos, Nos. 148–153, make such groupings explicit; No. 148 uses 4+2+3. The larger pulses depend on the grouping as well as the notated denominator.

Stravinsky uses changing bar lengths in the “Sacrificial Dance” of The Rite of Spring, making a steady small unit support unevenly returning accents. Elsewhere in the ballet, displaced accents operate within an unchanged metre. Messiaen’s added values modify an existing duration with a short note, a rest or a dot. Extending a two-quaver unit to three quavers shifts everything that follows by one quaver; a new time signature may record the effect, but the generative idea is the addition.

Non-retrogradable rhythmA duration sequence such as 2–3–2 reads identically forwards and backwards. Messiaen uses this separate property alongside added values. Asymmetry and rhythmic palindromes are independent properties.

Polyrhythm sounds different regular divisions of the same span simultaneously. In three against two, one part makes three equally spaced attacks while another makes two. Dividing the span into six subunits locates the first part at 0, 2 and 4, the second at 0 and 3. Both return together at 6. This differs from alternating a bar in three with a bar in two, and from polymetre, where parts maintain different metrical groupings. The examples keep the pitches simple to focus the comparison on the attacks.
