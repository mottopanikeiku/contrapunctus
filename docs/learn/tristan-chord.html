Why does this chord leave us waiting?

The Tristan chord: four notes, several readings

The idea in detail

Near the beginning of Wagner’s Tristan und Isolde, the notes F–B–D♯–G♯ sound together. Their spelling matters. Above F, B is an augmented fourth, D♯ an augmented sixth, and G♯ an augmented ninth when placed in the upper register. The ensuing phrase reaches a dominant seventh on E, E–G♯–B–D. Within the implied A minor, one unstable sonority has moved to another. The opening repeatedly develops this condition of arrival without final settlement.

A familiar collection transformedRespell the chord F–A♭–C♭–E♭ and it becomes a half-diminished seventh. The novelty lies in the phrase’s voice-leading, rhythm, orchestration and withheld continuation.

One functional reading hears G♯ as an appoggiatura, a leaning note that moves to A. The resulting F–A–B–D♯ has the pitch content of a French augmented sixth in A minor. It belongs to the predominant region and prepares E. This reading explains the chord through a familiar tonal function; its account must also follow the actual chromatic motion. Other readings emphasise a decorated subdominant or interpret the initial notes as dissonant approaches to the dominant itself.

A linear reading follows the individual voices before assigning a root to every vertical slice. The upper line’s G♯–A–A♯–B forms a melody through the changing chords. Which notes count as structural and which as appoggiaturas depends on the span being analysed. A pitch-class description identifies the collection while leaving tonal function and the phrase’s direction open. These approaches answer different questions.

The example’s scopeThe two sustained chords in the opening example show the exact pitch collections F–B–D♯–G♯ and E–G♯–B–D. Their durations and connecting voice-leading are schematic.

The opera was first performed in Munich in 1865. Its influence on later harmony comes from the extension of these procedures across entire scenes. A dominant can remain intelligible while its expected tonic is postponed, redirected or concealed by another line. Debussy later quotes the opening in “Golliwogg’s Cakewalk” from Children’s Corner: a recognisable dramatic manner becomes material for interruption and parody. Tonal expectation makes the original chord’s deferred resolution audible.
