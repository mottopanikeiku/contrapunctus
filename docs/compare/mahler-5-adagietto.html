The comparison

Mahler writes for strings and harp, with a violin melody that keeps reaching across the bar-line. Mengelberg’s Concertgebouw players slide together between notes and press forward through the little pickups, then linger when the theme returns. The Peabody Symphony Orchestra supplies the second performance, drawn from its complete recording of the Fifth.

  3. 03

    6:55 in the reference

    The harp returns beneath the opening tune. Mengelberg begins this return more slowly, giving the familiar notes extra room.

  4. 04

    High violins, rising cellos

    9:15 in the reference

    High violins sustain the line while the cellos climb below. Mahler asks for broad bow strokes as the phrase approaches its last great swell.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Willem Mengelberg, Concertgebouw Orchestra, 1926: match score 0.863. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
