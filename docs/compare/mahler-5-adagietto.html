The comparison

Mahler scored the movement for strings and harp, with a violin melody that repeatedly crosses the bar-line. Mengelberg’s Concertgebouw players slide together between notes and push forward through the short pickups, then slow down when the theme returns. The second performance is by the Peabody Symphony Orchestra, taken from its complete recording of the Fifth.

    Violin melody over harp

    The violins play the melody over plucked harp notes. The phrase places its weight on notes that resolve only after a delay.

  2. 02

    First climax

    2:55 in the reference

    The violins rise higher, with harp arpeggios beneath them. The phrase grows louder, then falls.

  3. 03

    Return of the opening melody

    6:55 in the reference

    The harp returns beneath the opening tune. Mengelberg takes the start of this return more slowly.

  4. 04

    High violins, ascending cellos

    9:15 in the reference

    High violins sustain the line while the cellos climb below. Mahler asked for broad bow strokes as the phrase approaches its last swell.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Willem Mengelberg, Concertgebouw Orchestra, 1926: match score 0.863. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
