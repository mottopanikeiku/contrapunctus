The comparison

The Prelude opens with three low notes, A–G♯–C♯. Rachmaninoff recorded the Prelude in 1919, 1921 and 1928, for Edison and later for Victor. Martin Wörner recorded it in 2005.

Passages

Passages compared

Each version plays in card order, for up to 12 seconds of reference music or until the next marked passage, whichever comes first.

  1. 01

    Opening three-note motif and chords

    0:05 in the reference

    The piece opens with three low notes, A–G♯–C♯. The chords that answer them are much quieter.

  2. 02

    Melody within the triplets

    1:46 in the reference

    A descending melody runs inside the right hand’s triplets, while the left hand keeps changing the bass beneath it.

  3. 03

    Triplet chords before the return

    2:18 in the reference

    The triplets become thick chords, passed between the hands and moving down the keyboard towards the return of the opening theme.

  4. 04

    Coda over a low C♯

    3:12 in the reference

    The chords grow softer over a low C♯; shorter answers and rests lead the Prelude to its final quiet chord.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Sergei Rachmaninoff, 1921: match score 0.967. Matched passages available.
  • Sergei Rachmaninoff, 1928: match score 0.923. Matched passages available.
  • Martin Wörner, 2005: match score 0.904. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
