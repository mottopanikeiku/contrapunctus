The comparison

D’Albert’s early Odeon recording and Cortot’s 1920 Victor side are compared with Edward Neeman’s modern Musopen performance. Cortot’s original stream couples the Black Keys Étude first: the Butterfly passage begins around 1:36.5 and has not been cut into a new audio file. D’Albert is mapped only through the shared opening 40 reference seconds because the later path jumps across extra material; no whole-work tempo claim is made from that path. Cortot’s map excludes the noisy tail, and neither historical audio stream is retuned.

Alignment evidence & limitations

Chroma features describe pitch-class content. Dynamic time warping matches their sequence, then interpolates between sampled pairs of recording times. When switching from a non-reference recording, its map is inverted first, then the chosen performer’s map is applied. Repeated timestamps use the midpoint of the matching reference span.

  • Eugen d’Albert, 1912–13: median chroma confidence 0.920. Automatic alignment remains provisional.
  • Alfred Cortot, 1920: median chroma confidence 0.871. Automatic alignment remains provisional.

Trims retain their original recording offsets. No timing is extrapolated outside a map. Confidence measures feature similarity, not historical or musical certainty.
