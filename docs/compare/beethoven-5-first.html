The comparison

In November 1913, Arthur Nikisch recorded Beethoven’s Fifth with the Berlin Philharmonic, playing into a recording horn. Odeon’s house orchestra had recorded it two years earlier. Mengelberg’s Concertgebouw and the Skidmore College Orchestra are compared with them here at the first three Gs. Each conductor sets how long the fourth note is held.

Passages

Passages compared

Each version plays in card order, for up to 12 seconds of reference music or until the next marked passage, whichever comes first.

  1. 01

    Opening motto, repeated

    1:42 in the reference

    Three Gs fall to E♭; three Fs follow and fall to a held D. Beethoven marked fermatas here, and their length is left to the conductor.

  2. 02

    Rising string passage

    2:10 in the reference

    The strings climb, passing the short rhythm from part to part. Woodwinds and horns answer before the orchestra plays louder.

  3. 03

    Horn call and second subject

    2:30 in the reference

    A short chord and a pause precede the horns. Their wide call leads into the violins’ soft E♭ major tune.

  4. 04

    Development

    3:23 in the reference

    The horns, in the familiar rhythm, move down to G; the strings play D♭ against it.

  5. 05

    Start of the coda

    6:42 in the reference

    The violins hold a high C while the bass changes beneath it. The cadence that would end the movement leads instead into a long coda.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Odeon Orchestra, 1911: match score 0.885. Matched passages available.
  • Arthur Nikisch, 1913: match score 0.924. Matched passages available.
  • Willem Mengelberg, 1940: match score 0.906. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
