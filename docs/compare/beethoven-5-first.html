The comparison

The Odeon recording’s Kark/1910 attribution is disputed by James Hepokoski’s research, which dates the recording to August 1911 and infers Künneke as its unnamed conductor; Nikisch’s November 1913 session is securely documented. Nikisch omits the exposition repeat, so his map begins at the modern reference’s repeated exposition, 1:42.6, rather than falsely equating the two complete durations. Mengelberg is represented by one unjoined disc side, with mapping limited to the shared exposition and its repeat. Acoustic recording balances and historic transfer speeds complicate claims about original orchestral tone or exact tempo.

Alignment evidence & limitations

Chroma features describe pitch-class content. Dynamic time warping matches their sequence, then interpolates between sampled pairs of recording times. When switching from a non-reference recording, its map is inverted first, then the chosen performer’s map is applied. Repeated timestamps use the midpoint of the matching reference span.

Trims retain their original recording offsets. No timing is extrapolated outside a map. Confidence measures feature similarity, not historical or musical certainty.
