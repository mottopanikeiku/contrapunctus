The comparison

The F♯ major melody is heavily ornamented, and the middle section is faster. Chopin marked it doppio movimento, twice the motion, and asked for the opening of that passage to be played sotto voce. Paderewski’s recordings, made twenty years apart, are listed here alongside those of Liszt’s pupil d’Albert and the younger pianists Faulkner and Prati.

  2. 02

    F♯ minor phrase, dolcissimo

    The F♯ minor phrase falls from its high notes, its little runs marked dolcissimo — very sweetly.

  3. 03

    Middle section, dotted melody

    1:26 in the reference

    A dotted tune sits above quick notes in the same hand. The bass leaps beneath it. The opening is quiet.

  4. 04

    Climax of the middle section

    1:50 in the reference

    The dotted rhythm continues, the piano grows louder and the middle section reaches its climax.

  5. 05

    Pause before the opening returns

    2:08 in the reference

    The repeated figure slows and dies away. After a pause the opening melody returns.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Eugen d’Albert, 1916: match score 0.895. Matched passages available.
  • Ignacy Jan Paderewski, 1917: match score 0.817. Check the matched passages by ear before using these timings.
  • Ignacy Jan Paderewski, 1937: match score 0.936. Matched passages available.
  • Patrizia Prati, 2015: match score 0.967. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
