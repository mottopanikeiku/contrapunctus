The comparison

The comparison places d’Albert’s 1916 recording beside Paderewski’s 1917 and 1937 accounts, then Luke Faulkner and Patrizia Prati’s modern recordings. Paderewski’s 1909 essay describes rubato as expressive flexibility, not time that must always be repaid mechanically within a bar. Prati’s live Madrid performance is dated 24 November 2015; Faulkner’s recording date is not established. Recorded amplitude and piano colour reflect different transfer chains and should not be mistaken for direct measurements of touch.

Alignment evidence & limitations

Chroma features describe pitch-class content. Dynamic time warping matches their sequence, then interpolates between sampled pairs of recording times. When switching from a non-reference recording, its map is inverted first, then the chosen performer’s map is applied. Repeated timestamps use the midpoint of the matching reference span.

  • Eugen d’Albert, 1916: median chroma confidence 0.895. Automatic alignment remains provisional.
  • Paderewski, 1917: median chroma confidence 0.817. Use with caution: inspect the correspondence before relying on these timings.
  • Paderewski, 1937: median chroma confidence 0.936. Automatic alignment remains provisional.
  • Patrizia Prati, 2015 (live): median chroma confidence 0.967. Automatic alignment remains provisional.

Trims retain their original recording offsets. No timing is extrapolated outside a map. Confidence measures feature similarity, not historical or musical certainty.
