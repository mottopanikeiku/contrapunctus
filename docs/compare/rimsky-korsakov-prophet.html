The comparison

A traveller meets a six-winged seraph in the desert; the angel tears out his heart and puts a burning coal in its place. Rimsky-⁠Korsakov set Pushkin’s poem for bass voice; the song begins quietly and ends with God’s command to speak. Chaliapin recorded it with Percy Pitt in 1921 and again with Albert Coates in 1927.

Passages

Passages compared

Each version plays in card order, for up to 12 seconds of reference music or until the next marked passage, whichever comes first.

  1. 01

    Opening phrases and seraph’s appearance

    0:35 in the reference

    The bass sings in measured phrases. When the seraph appears, flowing notes in the accompaniment surround the voice.

  2. 02

    Rising vocal line

    1:20 in the reference

    The bass rises higher over a moving accompaniment, describing the sounds that fill the traveller’s newly opened ears.

  3. 03

    Instrumental interlude

    1:55 in the reference

    The accompaniment continues between the sung phrases, growing softer and slowing before the story resumes.

  4. 04

    God’s command, con forza e maestoso

    3:20 in the reference

    The bass sings God’s charge above chords. Rimsky-⁠Korsakov marked the passage con forza e maestoso (forcefully and majestically).

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Fyodor Chaliapin, Albert Coates: match score 0.950. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
