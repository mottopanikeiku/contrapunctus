The comparison

Chopin writes the right hand in fast figuration over the five black keys, with detached chords and octaves underneath. A loud phrase returns quietly. Cortot and Neeman play through to a final rapid passage in octaves.

Passages compared

Each version plays in card order, for up to 12 seconds of reference music or until the next marked passage, whichever comes first.

  1. 01

    Opening: loud and quiet phrases

    0:12 in the reference

    The right-hand triplets run over detached left-hand chords; loud phrases are answered quickly and quietly.

  2. 02

    Extended phrase in D♭ major

    0:30 in the reference

    The triplets continue in D♭ major as the left hand extends the phrase beyond the four-bar groups of the opening.

  3. 03

    Return of the opening in G♭

    1:00 in the reference

    The opening figure returns in G♭ major, then grows quieter towards a passage marked very delicate and dying away.

  4. 04

    Arpeggios and closing octaves

    1:36 in the reference

    Wide arpeggios cover the keyboard before both hands finish in a fast, detached run of black-key octaves.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Alfred Cortot, 1920: match score 0.833. Check the matched passages by ear before using these timings.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
