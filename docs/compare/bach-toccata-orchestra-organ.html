The comparison

At Philadelphia’s Academy of Music on 6 April 1927, Stokowski recorded his orchestral version of Bach’s D minor Toccata and Fugue. He gave the organist’s flourishes to a large orchestra that included six horns, four trumpets and four trombones. Ninety years later, Norbert Schenk recorded the work on the church organ in Solms-⁠Albshausen, with one player at the instrument.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.
