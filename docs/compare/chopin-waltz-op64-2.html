The comparison

Paderewski, Pachmann, Rummel and Gurevich all begin with the same opening, a melody held back over a bass that keeps moving. Chopin sets quick quavers running between that C♯ minor melody and a second one in D♭ major. The circling refrain returns repeatedly and closes the piece.

  1. 01

    Opening waltz

    0:05 in the reference

    Held notes cross the bar line, then a quick figure follows. The bass keeps its pattern of one note and two chords.

  2. 02

    0:30 in the reference

    The opening melody completes another phrase, with short flourishes placed between longer notes. The waltz accompaniment continues.

  3. 03

    Più mosso in quavers

    0:42 in the reference

    The right hand plays chains of quavers, marked più mosso (more movement). The left hand continues beneath it.

  4. 04

    Falling and rising quaver figure

    0:55 in the reference

    The quick tune falls, rises and falls again, its turns fitted across the left hand’s three-beat pattern.

  5. 05

    Più lento in D♭ major

    1:20 in the reference

    The piano moves to D♭ major and a broader melody. The marking Più lento calls for a slower tempo.

  6. 06

    Return of the opening melody

    2:44 in the reference

    The opening C♯ minor melody returns with its held notes and short answering flourishes, and the quick refrain closes the piece.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Ignacy Jan Paderewski, 1917: match score 0.839. Check the matched passages by ear before using these timings.
  • Vladimir de Pachmann, 1925: match score 0.929. Matched passages available.
  • Walter Morse Rummel, 1943: match score 0.813. Check the matched passages by ear before using these timings.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
