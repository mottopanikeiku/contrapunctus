The comparison

Handel sets the choir shouting together, then sends a tune chasing from one voice to the next. Edison’s Oratorio Chorus recorded it in 1916; Sargent’s performance comes from the Royal Albert Hall in 1926. Scherchen’s brisk 1953 account prompted Owen Jensen, writing in the <i>New Zealand Listener</i> in June 1954, to joke about “Handelian traffic inspectors”.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Oratorio Chorus: match score 0.962. Matched passages available.
  • Malcolm Sargent, Royal Choral Society: match score 0.966. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
