The comparison

Edison released the Oratorio Chorus on Diamond Disc in May 1916; Sargent’s Royal Albert Hall concert recording dates from 2 April 1926, and Scherchen’s account from 1953. The provisional maps align Scherchen’s 0–199 seconds with Edison’s 0–219 and Sargent’s 0–258, excluding the final transfer tails, with median chroma confidences of 0.962 and 0.966 respectively. Compare phrase lengths and clarity rather than absolute level: acoustic balance, recording space and successive transfers all affect what survives. Edison’s issued recording qualifies under the US pre-1926 rule, while the Sargent and Scherchen recordings carry territorial European recording-rights bases, with US status unestablished.

Alignment evidence & limitations

Chroma features describe pitch-class content. Dynamic time warping matches their sequence, then interpolates between sampled pairs of recording times. When switching from a non-reference recording, its map is inverted first, then the chosen performer’s map is applied. Repeated timestamps use the midpoint of the matching reference span.

  • Oratorio Chorus / Edison: median chroma confidence 0.962. Automatic alignment remains provisional.

Trims retain their original recording offsets. No timing is extrapolated outside a map. Confidence measures feature similarity, not historical or musical certainty.
