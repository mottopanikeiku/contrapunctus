The comparison

Handel sets the choir singing together in chords, then passes a tune from one voice to the next. Edison’s Oratorio Chorus recorded it in 1916; Sargent’s performance comes from the Royal Albert Hall in 1926. Scherchen’s fast 1953 account led Owen Jensen, writing in the New Zealand Listener in June 1954, to joke about “Handelian traffic inspectors”.

Passages compared

Each version plays in card order, for up to 12 seconds of reference music or until the next marked passage, whichever comes first.

  1. 01

    Orchestral opening and choir entry

    0:00 in the reference

    The violins play the rhythm first. The choir enters three bars later, all four voices singing ‘Hallelujah’ together.

  2. 02

    “The kingdom of this world”

    1:10 in the reference

    The choir moves together in measured notes above a descending bass. Trumpets and drums fall silent.

  3. 03

    “King of kings”

    2:00 in the reference

    The sopranos hold their line above the quick answers in the lower voices, then enter higher with each new phrase.

  4. 04

    Final cadence in D major

    3:06 in the reference

    Choir and orchestra stop. After the rest the choir sings one broad ‘Hallelujah’, ending on a held D major chord.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Oratorio Chorus: match score 0.962. Matched passages available.
  • Malcolm Sargent, Royal Choral Society: match score 0.966. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
