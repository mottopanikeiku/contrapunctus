The comparison

John Harrison, Robert Turizziani and the Wichita State University Chamber Players recorded this movement live on 6 February 2000. Commons credits the other file to the Modena Chamber Orchestra, but its embedded author tag names Orpheus and its session date remains unknown. The uploader lowered that source by 20 cents without documenting the method, so neither original tuning nor original playback speed can be inferred from this file. The automatic map covers the complete 215.30- and 214.03-second files, with median confidence 0.950; use its provisional correspondence to compare phrase grouping rather than to resolve those provenance questions.

Side by side, by ear

Listen at the joins

Hear up to 12 seconds of reference music in each version, in card order. Each excerpt stops at the matched endpoint, or the next marked passage if sooner.

  1. 01

    The opening refrain

    0:05 in the reference

    Compare the grouping and separation of the repeated phrases. Playback levels are not a calibrated measure of the ensembles’ dynamics.

  2. 02

    Space between bird calls

    0:48 in the reference

    Follow the short violin figures and the space left between them. Do not mistake a different source pitch for different written ornament.

  3. 03

    The compact return

    1:08 in the reference

    Listen for the refrain’s second phrase rather than a complete restart; compare how the players collect the separate calls into one group.

  4. 04

    Pressure across the storm

    1:56 in the reference

    Compare the sustained drive of the rapid figures with the weight of the preceding refrain, not merely the fastest isolated note.

  5. 05

    Follow the refrain’s grouping as the music regains E major. The matching time is approximate, not an identified bar line.
