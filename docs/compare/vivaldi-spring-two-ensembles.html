The comparison

Vivaldi’s Spring opens in E major with a refrain for the strings. Bird calls interrupt it, then a storm, and the refrain returns between these episodes. John Harrison recorded it live with the Wichita State University Chamber Players in 2000; the second performance comes from Musopen.

Passages compared

Each version plays in card order, for up to 12 seconds of reference music or until the next marked passage, whichever comes first.

  1. 01

    Opening ritornello in E major

    0:05 in the reference

    The strings open with short, repeated phrases in E major that represent the arrival of spring.

  2. 02

    Birdsong episode, violins

    0:48 in the reference

    The violins trade trills and short rising and falling calls, with rests between one call and the next.

  3. 03

    Second phrase of the ritornello

    1:08 in the reference

    Only the second phrase of the refrain returns, and the orchestra takes over the violin calls heard before it.

  4. 04

    Storm episode

    1:56 in the reference

    Rapid violin figures break into the refrain; during the storm the music moves through B major towards C♯ minor.

  5. 05

    Ritornello from C♯ minor to E major

    2:46 in the reference

    The refrain starts in C♯ minor and moves back to E major, where the strings play the opening tune again.

  6. 06

    Final ritornello in E major

    3:21 in the reference

    The orchestra brings back the refrain in E major after the solo violin’s last passages.

How the recordings are matched

The matching follows sequences of pitches, using a method called dynamic time warping. Every recording is mapped to the reference, so switching between two versions goes through that shared clock. Where several reference times match one point, the middle time is used.

  • Modena Chamber Orchestra: match score 0.950. Matched passages available.

Switching stays within the matched span, and trimmed recordings keep their original clock times. Match scores describe similarity in the sound.
