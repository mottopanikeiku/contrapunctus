The comparison

Harrison and Elgar’s 1919–20 acoustic recording compresses the whole concerto onto four sides issued in 1921; the Skidmore file contains only the complete finale. The comparison deliberately aligns just 85–124 seconds of Skidmore with 710–745 seconds of the already-joined Harrison stream, a continuous early-finale overlap rather than music on opposite sides of a cut. Its median chroma confidence is 0.950, but the timing remains provisional and the acoustic transfer changes which details of cello and orchestra are audible. Neither the full-file durations nor this short overlap establish Elgar’s tempo for the omitted music.

Alignment evidence & limitations

Chroma features describe pitch-class content. Dynamic time warping matches their sequence, then interpolates between sampled pairs of recording times. When switching from a non-reference recording, its map is inverted first, then the chosen performer’s map is applied. Repeated timestamps use the midpoint of the matching reference span.

  • Harrison / Elgar: median chroma confidence 0.950. Automatic alignment remains provisional.

Trims retain their original recording offsets. No timing is extrapolated outside a map. Confidence measures feature similarity, not historical or musical certainty.
