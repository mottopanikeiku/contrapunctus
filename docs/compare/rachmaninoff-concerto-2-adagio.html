The comparison

Rachmaninoff, Stokowski and the Philadelphia Orchestra recorded the Adagio’s first side on 10 April 1929; Richter’s reference recording with Wisłocki and the Warsaw National Philharmonic Orchestra dates from 1959. Only HMV D.B. 1334’s Adagio opening is used, matching 0–214.4 seconds of Richter to 3–174 seconds of the historical transfer and excluding the later cadenza and return. The provisional map has median chroma confidence 0.854 after shifting the historical pitch-class features down one semitone, without altering the audio. Surface noise weakens some local matches, so these passages invite comparison of broad phrasing rather than beat-exact rubato or speed-corrected historical tempo.

Alignment evidence & limitations

Chroma features describe pitch-class content. Dynamic time warping matches their sequence, then interpolates between sampled pairs of recording times. When switching from a non-reference recording, its map is inverted first, then the chosen performer’s map is applied. Repeated timestamps use the midpoint of the matching reference span.

  • Rachmaninoff / Stokowski · first side: median chroma confidence 0.854. Automatic alignment remains provisional.

Trims retain their original recording offsets. No timing is extrapolated outside a map. Confidence measures feature similarity, not historical or musical certainty.
