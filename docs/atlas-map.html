How to read this map · sources & method

Gold dots mark actual projected coordinates. Individual portrait callouts have leaders no longer than 26 screen pixels, shortened near the coast. Overlapping portrait circles merge into a screen-space medallion, recomputed as you zoom; a group spanning several cities is named for its most represented city, with “area” added. Hover, focus or click to open the portrait chooser. Its ring is a menu, not relocated geographic positions: all underlying gold dots stay fixed. Death crosses remain on their recorded points.

The annual primary location is an explicitly dated ongoing post, then a residence, then another recorded stay; the latest record breaks ties. This keeps a summer home from displacing a simultaneous professional post. Birth, childhood, study, home, work and exile records provide the residence chronology. When no dated stay covers the year, the last primary place is carried forward to the next residence record or death; a dashed portrait frame identifies that carry-forward. All seasonal homes remain on the full route. Short visits and premieres do not move a residence. This annual model is not a daily itinerary or proof of continued residence.

City groups join coordinates within 7 km of a fixed centre. “Who was here?” combines the same primary-residence chronology used by the cameos with explicitly dated events. The labels distinguish resident (from residence records) from documented in this year; residence-derived overlaps are separately labelled. Authored dates are never extended: an omitted event end remains a point event, and any carry-forward is stored as a separate model interval. Premieres do not establish attendance, and burials do not establish living presence, so neither enters the encounter calculation. No overlap proves a meeting; source dates and precision remain visible in each life’s records.

A focused life opens on its European places; overseas places have their own gilded inset. A life with no European record opens in its recorded region instead. Each stop retains an exact gold dot. Number lists are stacked at the named city’s true centre, never scattered across the sea; the card lists every stop individually. Arrow-labelled connectors end at the inset frame, not at invented positions in the Atlantic. The two maps have independent scales.

Lines join place records in date order, not historical roads or actual transit routes. Dotted influence lines connect musical evidence to a place; an origin may be an earlier residence, never an assertion of a meeting. Posthumous premieres are marked separately. Exact normalised work-title matches offer recordings of that work; otherwise the card explicitly offers other recordings by the composer. Only published recordings are offered.

Land, coastline and present-day country outlines: Natural Earth 1:50m, public domain, via the pinned source revision. Mercator projection and Douglas–Peucker simplification are computed at build time. Country outlines are orientation aids, not historical political borders. The map has no tile server. Place evidence and status appear in each composer’s card; unresearched composers simply do not appear.
