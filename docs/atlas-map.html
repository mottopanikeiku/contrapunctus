How to read this map · sources & method

The portrait marks the latest active residence or post recorded by that year. When no stay covers the year, a dashed frame marks the last recorded place, not proof of continued residence. Short visits and premieres do not move a residence. Overlapping seasonal homes cannot be resolved into a daily itinerary at this annual scale. A cross marks death; later burial records may move it.

City groups join coordinates within 7 km of a fixed centre. Encounters use only documented, explicitly dated presence: omitted end dates are point events. Premieres do not establish the composer’s attendance, and burials do not establish living presence, so neither enters the encounter calculation. Overlapping date ranges are evidence of possible co-presence, not proof of a meeting. Annual labels retain the source’s precision in the individual records.

Numbered lines join place records in date order, not historical roads or actual transit routes. Dotted influence lines connect musical evidence to a place; an origin may be an earlier residence, never an assertion of a meeting. Posthumous premieres are marked separately. Exact normalised work-title matches offer recordings of that work; otherwise the card explicitly offers other recordings by the composer. Only published recordings are offered.

Land, coastline and present-day country outlines: Natural Earth 1:50m, public domain, via the pinned source revision. Mercator projection and Douglas–Peucker simplification are computed at build time. Country outlines are orientation aids, not historical political borders. The map has no tile server. Place evidence and status appear in each composer’s card; unresearched composers simply do not appear.
