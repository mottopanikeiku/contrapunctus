How to read this map · sources & method

Gold dots mark the actual projected coordinates. Portraits are callouts connected by leaders no longer than 34.1 screen pixels; they are not displaced geographic locations. More than four living composers in a city collapse to a named medallion: hover, focus or click to open its ring of portraits. Death crosses remain on their recorded point.

The annual primary location is an explicitly dated ongoing post, then a residence, then another recorded stay; the latest record breaks ties. This keeps a summer home from displacing a simultaneous professional post. All seasonal homes remain on the full route. When no stay covers the year, a dashed portrait frame marks the last recorded place, not proof of continued residence. Short visits and premieres do not move a residence. This annual view cannot resolve overlapping places into a daily itinerary. A cross marks death; later burial records may move it.

City groups join coordinates within 7 km of a fixed centre. Encounters use only documented, explicitly dated presence: omitted end dates are point events. Premieres do not establish attendance, and burials do not establish living presence, so neither enters the encounter calculation. Overlapping date ranges are evidence of possible co-presence, not proof of a meeting. Annual labels retain the source’s precision in the individual records.

A focused life opens on its European places; overseas places have their own gilded inset. A life with no European record opens in its recorded region instead. Each stop retains an exact gold dot. Number lists are stacked at the named city’s true centre, never scattered across the sea; the card lists every stop individually. Arrow-labelled connectors end at the inset frame, not at invented positions in the Atlantic. The two maps have independent scales.

Lines join place records in date order, not historical roads or actual transit routes. Dotted influence lines connect musical evidence to a place; an origin may be an earlier residence, never an assertion of a meeting. Posthumous premieres are marked separately. Exact normalised work-title matches offer recordings of that work; otherwise the card explicitly offers other recordings by the composer. Only published recordings are offered.

Land, coastline and present-day country outlines: Natural Earth 1:50m, public domain, via the pinned source revision. Mercator projection and Douglas–Peucker simplification are computed at build time. Country outlines are orientation aids, not historical political borders. The map has no tile server. Place evidence and status appear in each composer’s card; unresearched composers simply do not appear.

A listener’s atlas built for reading slowly. Dates follow the standard biographies; catalogue numbers follow the modern thematic catalogues (BWV, K., D., Op.). Where a story is legend rather than record, the text says so.
