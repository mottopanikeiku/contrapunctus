Sergei Prokofiev 1891–1953

Toccata in D minor, Op. 11

1912 · D minor · Piano
Martha Argerich · About this recording

How can repeated notes keep changing their weight and direction?

One note, two hands

The opening shares repeated Ds between the hands before chromatic neighbours widen the pitch field. Listen for the distinction between the running semiquavers and the accents laid across them: regular small notes can carry shifting emphasis.

How we know

Placed from the score’s proportions; timing approximate. The score specifies the repeated-note opening. D dominates the report’s first 25 seconds, with frequent attacks; this supports the opening span, not an exact note alignment.

Around the music
A classmate’s fingers

Prokofiev dedicated the Toccata to Nikolay Shtember, his fellow pupil in Anna Esipova’s piano class at the St Petersburg Conservatory. Written in spring 1912, it tested Shtember’s technique and the composer’s own.

Listening notes

Two hands share a stream of repeated notes; chromatic lines and displaced accents keep changing what those notes mean. Martha Argerich plays the Toccata Prokofiev wrote while he was still a St Petersburg Conservatory student. Select a note to hear its moment.
