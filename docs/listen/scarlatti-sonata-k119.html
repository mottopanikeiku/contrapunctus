Sonata in D major, K. 119Allegro

How do repeated passages lead towards different harmonic destinations?

Remember a gesture, not every note

Choose one short figure from the opening and keep its shape in mind. Repetition gives you another chance to recognise it; the interesting question is whether a familiar gesture leads towards the same destination.

How we know

General description of this stretch. This is a listening prompt about repetition. The manuscript shows recurring short keyboard figures; it does not supply timings for Meyer’s performance: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Scarlatti,_Sonate_K._119_-_ms._Venise_XV,22.jpg

Around the music
Following a royal pupil

Scarlatti entered Portuguese royal service in 1719. In 1729 he followed his pupil Maria Bárbara to Spain after her marriage to the Spanish crown prince, continuing as her music master.

Listening notes

A keyboard sonata built from repeated attacks, changes of register and chords containing notes that refuse to agree. Its two repeated halves give Scarlatti room to make the same disturbances lead towards different keys. Select a note to hear its moment.
