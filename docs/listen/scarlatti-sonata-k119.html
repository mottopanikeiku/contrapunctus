The form 0:00

Two halves, each repeated

The opening establishes D major with a sharply articulated keyboard figure. This sonata has two repeated halves, not the later three-part plan of exposition, development and recapitulation. The first half moves away from D; the second has to find its way back.

How we know

General description of this stretch. Opening D-major estimate r=0.85–0.93 through 0:15, with 3.0–4.4 onsets/s. The later A-major closing region at 0:50–1:05 and D-major restart around 1:08 corroborate the score’s binary first half and repeat.

Around the music
An opera composer’s son

Domenico was born in Naples in 1685, the same year as Bach and Handel. His father Alessandro was an established opera composer. Domenico also wrote operas and sacred music before the keyboard sonatas became the centre of his surviving output.

Listening notes

A keyboard sonata built from repeated attacks, changes of register and chords containing notes that refuse to agree. Its two repeated halves give Scarlatti room to make the same disturbances lead towards different keys. Select a note to hear its moment.
