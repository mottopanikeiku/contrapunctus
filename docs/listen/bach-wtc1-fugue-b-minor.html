One voice, twelve notes

One voice gives the subject without accompaniment. Its line includes all twelve notes of the chromatic scale, but this is not twelve-tone music: the notes lean towards and away from a clear B minor centre.

How we know

General description of this stretch. Opening is exceptionally quiet, −31.8 to −34.3 dB at 0:00–0:05, with only 1.0–1.4 onsets/s through 0:10. B-minor pitch classes begin the line; the score supplies the chromatic subject, not the approximate local key estimator.

Around the music
A family trade

Bach’s father was a town musician in Eisenach. After both parents died, the ten-year-old Johann Sebastian went to live with his elder brother Johann Christoph, an organist in Ohrdruf. Keyboard playing was a household profession before it became his own employment.

Listening notes

The final fugue of Book I has four voices and a subject that uses all twelve notes of the chromatic scale. Listen for the subject’s semitone pairs inside the longer line: dissonance is built into its identity, not added afterwards. Select a note to hear its moment.
