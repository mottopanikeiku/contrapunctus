One melody, two sections

The first and second violins share the opening line, each supplying notes missing from the other part. What sounds like one descending melody is assembled across the orchestra; neither violin section has the complete tune.

Around the music
Written at Klin

Tchaikovsky composed the Sixth in February and March 1893 and orchestrated it that summer. His letters describe unusually strong confidence in the work, alongside repeated dissatisfaction with details of the instrumentation.

Listening notes

After the preceding movement’s march, Tchaikovsky writes a slow finale that ends in B minor rather than a victorious major key. Listen for a melody divided between the violin sections, and for the bass taking over as the upper instruments fall silent. Select a note to hear its moment.
