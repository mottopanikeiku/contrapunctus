Follow a whole phrase

Give the opening line time to extend beyond its first gesture. Listen for how a phrase gathers sound and then releases it; the withdrawal is part of its shape, not just empty space before the next phrase.

How we know

Located by measuring this recording. The opening analysis shows repeated changes in recorded level across sustained stretches. This cue follows their broad shape without identifying a particular orchestral part.

Around the music
The slow ending was planned

On 23 February 1893, Tchaikovsky told his nephew Vladimir Davydov that the finale would be a prolonged Adagio rather than a noisy Allegro. He also said that its private programme would remain an enigma for listeners to guess.

Listening notes

After the preceding movement’s march, Tchaikovsky writes a slow finale rather than a triumphant conclusion. Follow the repeated growth and withdrawal of sound, and the long decline towards the ending in B minor. Select a note to hear its moment.
