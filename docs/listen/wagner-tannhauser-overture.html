The pilgrims before Venus

A slow hymn opens the overture. In the opera it belongs to pilgrims travelling to Rome; its sustained phrases establish a contrast with the restless writing associated with Venus’s mountain.

How we know

Placed from the score’s proportions; timing approximate. Marc Mandel’s Boston Symphony account identifies the opening pilgrims’ music. The recording’s quiet, low-activity first minute supports the opening span, without a note-level alignment.

Around the music
A court post in Dresden

Wagner became conductor of the Royal Saxon Court in Dresden in February 1843. He composed Tannhäuser while holding that post and conducted its first performance there on 19 October 1845.

Listening notes

A pilgrims’ hymn frames the restless music of Venus’s mountain. This concert-band arrangement transfers Wagner’s opposition between sustained chorale and rushing figuration to wind, brass and percussion. Select a note to hear its moment.
