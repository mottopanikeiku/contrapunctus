An answer to Rodolfo

Rodolfo has described his life as a poet; Mimì responds with her own. Throughout the aria, compare short, speech-like phrases with sustained singing: both belong to the same first conversation.

How we know

General description of this stretch. The Act I scene supplies the exchange; this is a listening frame, not word-by-word synchronisation.

Around the music
Paris through two librettists

Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa adapted Henry Murger’s stories for Puccini. In Act I, Mimì comes to the Paris attic asking for a light for her candle; conversation follows while she and Rodolfo search for her lost key.

Listening notes

Mimì answers Rodolfo’s introduction by describing her work as an embroiderer and her solitary life. How does Puccini move between speech-like phrases and a sustained vocal line without stopping the conversation? Select a note to hear its moment.
