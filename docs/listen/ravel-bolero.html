Maurice Ravel 1875–1937

Boléro

1928 · C major · Orchestra
Ferenc Fricsay (conductor), Berlin Symphonic Orchestra · About this recording

What changes when the melodies and rhythm barely change?

Two tunes, one crescendo

Ravel repeats two melodies over a two-bar drum rhythm in three beats. Each melodic statement lasts eighteen bars. The growing orchestra supplies most of the large-scale change, while the repeated bass keeps the same tonal foundation.

How we know

Documented fact. The score supplies the two-bar rhythm and eighteen-bar statement scheme. This is an overview of the piece, not a precisely timed drum entrance.

Around the music
Written for the ballet

Boléro was conceived for Ida Rubinstein’s dance company and first staged in Paris in 1928. Its repeated rhythmic foundation belongs to a work originally made for dancers, before it became a concert-hall piece.

Listening notes

Two melodies and a two-bar drum rhythm survive an almost continuous crescendo. Ravel makes the changing instruments do the work that changing themes and keys usually do. Select a note to hear its moment.
