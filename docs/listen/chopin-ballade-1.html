Ballade No. 1, Op. 23Largo — Moderato

1831–35 · G minor · Piano

How does a quiet theme become a forceful return?

Before the regular pulse

The introduction sets rising octaves against pauses and quieter chords. Listen for the change from a single line spread across the keyboard to melody with accompaniment, rather than expecting the main theme’s pace from the first note.

How we know

General description of this stretch. Orrin Howard’s LA Phil note describes the octave introduction and its transition to the first theme. The report shows contrasting attacks and quiet spaces in the opening half-minute. https://www.laphil.com/works/ballade-no-1-in-g-minor-op-23

Around the music
Schumann’s favourite

Schumann reported telling Chopin during his 1836 Leipzig visit that the G minor Ballade was his favourite of Chopin’s works. In that account, Chopin replied that he preferred it too. This records an exchange of judgement, not a hidden literary programme.

Listening notes

Chopin gives the piano the title of a narrative poem without supplying a plot. Two themes return in altered proportions, and a fast coda overturns the measured pace of the opening. Select a note to hear its moment.
