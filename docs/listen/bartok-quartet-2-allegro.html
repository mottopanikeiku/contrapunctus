How can one repeated note drive a whole movement?

A pitch that stays put

Through the opening stretch, listen for repeated notes beneath the moving lines. A fixed pitch can provide an anchor without supplying a new chord on every beat; changing accents keep repetition from becoming a regular tread.

How we know

General description of this stretch. Roger Parker’s Gresham lecture describes the opening repeated-note accompaniment. The analysis finds densely packed attacks through the first minute; this is a passage-level listening guide. https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/bartok-quartet-no-2

Around the music
Beyond Hungarian folk song

Bartók’s collecting took him as far as Biskra in Algeria. Roger Parker connects this movement’s narrow melodies, ornaments and repeated rhythms with that North African fieldwork, rather than treating it as an arrangement of a Hungarian dance.

Listening notes

The fast movement of Bartók’s Second Quartet sets repeated notes against shifting accents. Its narrow melodic figures owe something to the Arab music he recorded in Algeria, rather than to a Hungarian dance tune. Select a note to hear its moment.
