Piano Concerto No. 2, Op. 18II. Adagio sostenuto

1900–01 · E major · Piano and orchestra
Sviatoslav Richter (piano), Warsaw National Philharmonic Orchestra, Stanisław Wisłocki (conductor) · recorded 1959 · About this recording

How does a song survive the movement’s restless middle?

Before the pulse settles

Listen to the spacing of the opening sounds before the smaller, repeating notes establish a more regular motion. A slow introduction can make the arrival of an accompaniment feel like a change of pace without a sudden increase in speed.

How we know

Located by measuring this recording. The recording analysis finds a sustained opening, followed by more distinct attacks around the half-minute mark. It does not identify individual instruments.

Around the music
Heard before the first movement

This Adagio and the finale were performed in Moscow on 15 December 1900, before the complete concerto was ready. Rachmaninoff played and Alexander Siloti conducted at a benefit for the Ladies’ Charity Prison Committee.

Listening notes

A slow movement with a faster centre. The piano’s circling accompaniment supports a long melody; the contrasting middle grows into a cadenza before the opening material returns. Select a note to hear its moment.
