Jean Sibelius 1865–1957

Valse triste, Op. 44 No. 1

1903–04 · G minor · Cello and piano (arrangement) · arrangement
Judith Bokor (cello) · recorded 5 June 1925 · About this recording

How can a waltz keep its pulse while its melody stretches?

A dance in three

Across the opening, listen for the three-beat accompaniment beneath the cello’s longer phrases. A waltz supplies a regular step, but the melody need not stop at each bar line; the arrangement separates the two roles between cello and piano.

How we know

General description of this stretch. The score establishes the waltz metre and the source identifies the cello-and-piano arrangement. The recording’s quiet opening grows around 0:10–0:15; no individual entrance is being timed.

Around the music
The partner is Death

Sibelius wrote the music in 1903 for Kuolema, a play by his brother-in-law Arvid Järnefelt. In the scene behind this waltz, an old woman dreams of a masked ball; her partner turns out to be Death.

Listening notes

A dying woman dreams that she is dancing: this waltz began as theatre music, not a ballroom miniature. Judith Bokor’s 1925 recording gives the orchestral melody to cello with piano accompaniment. Select a note to hear its moment.
