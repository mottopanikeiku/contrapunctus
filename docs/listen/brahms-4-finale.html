Eight bars, thirty variations

The opening presents an eight-bar theme that underpins thirty variations. This is a passacaglia: a recurring pattern controls the harmony while the music above and around it changes.

How we know

Placed from the score’s proportions; timing approximate. The score establishes the eight-bar scheme, also described in Jan Swafford’s Boston Symphony programme note. The opening gives way to quieter writing around 0:15–0:25.

Around the music
Bach’s pattern, altered

Brahms adapted the finale’s recurring pattern from a Bach cantata, adding a chromatic note. A Baroque variation procedure becomes the organising principle of a nineteenth-century symphonic finale.

Listening notes

Brahms ends his last symphony with thirty variations on an eight-bar pattern derived from Bach. The repeating foundation survives changes of rhythm, register and orchestration, but the final cadence refuses the customary escape into the major. Select a note to hear its moment.
