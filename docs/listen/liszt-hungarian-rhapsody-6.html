Franz Liszt 1811–1886

Hungarian Rhapsody No. 6, S. 244/6

Published 1853 · D♭ major · Piano
Martha Argerich · About this recording

How does repeated octave writing turn a tune into momentum?

The form 0:00

Four sections, one pianist

The opening has the measured tread of a march. Liszt sets it beside a quick dance, a slower song and an extended fast finale; the contrasts of pace are part of the design, not interruptions of it.

How we know

Documented fact. The score gives the contrasting tempo headings. Leslie Howard’s Hyperion notes describe the work’s four-section design.

Around the music
Published in a larger group

The sixth rhapsody appeared in 1853, alongside Nos. 3–15. Liszt reused and reshaped material from his earlier Hungarian pieces rather than composing the whole collection from newly invented melodies.

Listening notes

Four contrasting sections move from march to quick dance, slow declamation and an octave-driven finale. Listen for the changes of pace as well as the changes of register. Select a note to hear its moment.
