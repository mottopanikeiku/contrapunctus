How does the accompaniment change the character of a melody?

Two against three

In the opening, the melody groups notes in pairs above an accompaniment divided into threes. Try following the two layers separately: they organise the same underlying pulse differently.

How we know

General description of this stretch. Emilia Zanetti’s Santa Cecilia programme note describes the opening’s duple figure over triplet arpeggios. This is an opening-texture guide, not an attack-time claim. https://www.flaminioonline.it/Guide/Prokofiev/Prokofiev-Sonata14.html

Around the music
Growing out of a sonatina

The first movement developed from an earlier sonatina. The scherzo also reused earlier work, written for Lyadov’s class; the final two movements were added to complete the four-movement sonata in 1912.

Listening notes

Written in 1912, the Second Sonata puts long melodies, a short scherzo and a tarantella finale into four movements. Listen for the accompaniment: changing the notes beneath a melody often changes its character more drastically than changing the melody itself. Select a note to hear its moment.
