Claude Debussy 1862–1918

Préludes, Book IX. La cathédrale engloutie

1910 · C major · Piano
Marcelle Meyer (piano) · recorded 15 February 1956 · About this recording

How can one piano suggest bells, an organ and distance?

In the music 0:00

Fifths before full chords

The opening uses bare fifths rather than immediately fixing a major or minor chord. Listen to the space between the rising groups of notes: the piano’s resonance continues where the fingers are no longer making new attacks.

How we know

Placed from the score’s proportions; timing approximate. The score begins with parallel fifths. The report shows a quiet, sparsely attacked opening; it does not align individual intervals to seconds.

Around the music
The drowned city of Ys

The title refers to the Breton legend of Ys, a city swallowed by the sea. The story associated with the prelude describes a submerged cathedral whose bells, chants and organ can still be heard. These are legendary images, not sounds added to the recording.

Listening notes

A submerged cathedral takes shape through piano resonance: bare intervals grow into broad chords, then recede. Marcelle Meyer’s 1956 recording makes the distance between the quiet opening and the full keyboard part of the form. Select a note to hear its moment.
