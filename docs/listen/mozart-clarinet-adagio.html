A melody shaped for breath

The slow movement opens with the solo clarinet’s melody rather than a long orchestral introduction. Across these phrases, listen for the relationship between the singing line and the space around it.

How we know

Placed from the score’s proportions; timing approximate. The opening solo melody is specified in the score and Calvin Dotsey’s Houston Symphony account. The recording begins with a relatively quiet D-centred passage; individual breaths are not identified.

Around the music
A wider clarinet range

Mozart wrote the concerto for Anton Stadler’s extended-range clarinet. William McColl plays a basset clarinet of his own construction in this recording, rather than a surviving instrument from Stadler’s lifetime.

Listening notes

Mozart wrote this concerto for a clarinet with extra low notes, not the standard orchestral instrument. William McColl plays a basset clarinet he built himself: listen to the distance between its low register and the singing upper line. Select a note to hear its moment.
