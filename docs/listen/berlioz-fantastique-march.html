How can a march turn a melody into an interruption?

An execution in a dream

This is the fourth of five movements. In Berlioz’s printed programme, an opium-dazed musician dreams that he has murdered his beloved and is being led to execution. Listen for the contrast between the march’s heavy tread and its more ceremonial passages.

How we know

Documented fact. Berlioz’s published programme, part four, describes the dream and the march’s contrasting characters.

Around the music
The beloved as melody

Berlioz’s programme calls the recurring melody an idée fixe: a persistent idea. It represents the beloved across all five movements, so the march belongs to a larger musical narrative rather than an independent military scene.

Listening notes

Berlioz places an execution march inside a symphony, with a brief recollection of the beloved’s melody near its end. What happens to the regular tread when that recollection interrupts it? Select a note to hear its moment.
