Sources & method

Recording provenance, the evidence behind a claim, and the difference between a written bar and a measured time.

MethodRecordingsDossiersScoresDownloadsCitation

How claims are labelled

Evidence belongs to each field

Documented
Stated in a cited source. This identifies the source’s claim; it does not guarantee that the source is right.
Inferred
Estimated from the evidence, not explicitly documented. The note gives the reasoning.
Disputed
Sources disagree. The dossier retains the alternatives and their sources.
Unknown
Not established from the sources consulted. An upload date is not a session date; an uploader is not necessarily a performer or transfer engineer.
Not applicable
The field does not apply. This is not a substitute for ‘unknown’.

What a cue time means

score-aligned
A written bar is matched to its performed occurrence in this recording. Repeats are counted separately.
score-proportion
Placement estimated from the score’s proportions. Tempo changes and pauses can move the event away from the estimated time.
audio-feature
A measured feature of this recording, such as a silence or a level change, locates the event. It does not by itself identify a chord or instrument.
documented
A fact from a cited source; it need not identify an audible event at that second.
general
A description of the surrounding stretch, not a claim about one instant.

Provisional alignment

A machine has matched score events to the recording; a named listener has not yet checked the published alignment against the score. A provisional bar time is an estimate, not an exact onset. ‘Reviewed’ records a listening check, with its scope and reviewer in the score data. Cue precision distinguishes a moment (about ±2 seconds) from a passage; neither label removes uncertainty in the underlying alignment.

Rights statements report the sources and their qualifications, not worldwide legal clearance. The composition, edition, performance, recording and digital transfer can have different rights.
