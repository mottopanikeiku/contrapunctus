Listen first

Listening routes

Follow a returning melody, an independent voice or a change of instrument. Each button starts the recording without leaving the page.

A melody returns

Music keeps its listener oriented by bringing things back. Listen for what comes back unchanged, and what does not.

  1. Erik Satie · Trois Gymnopédies

    I. Lent et douloureux

    The whole piece is played twice; the second ending is not the first.

  2. Ludwig van Beethoven · Symphony No. 5, Op. 67

    I. Allegro con brio

    An exposition repeated note for note, then a recapitulation that is not.

  3. Antonio Vivaldi · The Four Seasons · Spring, RV 269

    I. Allegro

    A refrain for the full band frames each solo episode.

  4. Joseph Haydn · Symphony No. 94 “Surprise”

    II. Andante

    One plain tune, varied each time it returns.

  5. Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart · Symphony No. 40, K. 550

    I. Molto allegro

    The second theme returns, but in the minor.

Hear independent voices

Counterpoint means several melodies at once. Try following one line, then another.

  1. Johann Sebastian Bach · The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book I · BWV 869

    Fugue in B minor

    Four voices enter one by one with the same subject.

  2. François Couperin · Les Nations

    La Piémontaise · Sonade

    Three written parts shared by four players.

  3. Johann Sebastian Bach · Brandenburg Concerto No. 5, BWV 1050

    I. Allegro

    A harpsichord that steps out of the band.

  4. Johannes Brahms · Symphony No. 4 in E minor, Op. 98

    IV. Allegro energico e passionato

    Thirty variations over one repeating eight-bar pattern.

  5. George Frideric Handel · Messiah, HWV 56

    Hallelujah

    A chorus alternating block chords with imitation.

Listen for changing colour

The same notes can sound utterly different depending on who plays them, and how.

  1. Maurice Ravel · Boléro

    One melody, passed from instrument to instrument for a quarter of an hour.

  2. Gustav Mahler · Symphony No. 5

    IV. Adagietto — Sehr langsam

    An orchestra reduced to strings and harp.

  3. Claude Debussy · Préludes, Book I

    X. La cathédrale engloutie

    A piano made to sound like bells under water.

  4. Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov · Scheherazade, Op. 35

    III. The Young Prince and the Young Princess

    A teacher of orchestration showing his craft.
