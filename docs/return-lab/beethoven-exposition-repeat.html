Beethoven: the same exposition twice

What differs when the score changes nothing?

The repeat sign sends bars 1–124 back to bar 1 with the same notes, instruments and written markings. This is the control: two passes through one exposition in the same recording, not the later recapitulation. Timing is aligned automatically, not checked by ear; measured differences are provisional and do not establish intention.

    Measured evidence

    What this recording does

    Timing and level can differ because of the score, the performers, the instruments or the recording. These measurements do not establish which.

    Across the 124 corresponding bars, the return takes 100.5 seconds against 101.9 seconds the first time — 1% shorter.

    How to read these measurements

    Bar durations run from one aligned bar onset to the next. The last bar runs to the end of the recording, including its decay. Paired totals omit columns without a counterpart.

    Relative level is the arithmetic mean of the recording’s normalised amplitude-envelope samples at two samples per second, from the sample containing each bar’s onset to the sample containing its end. It is not a decibel reading, a written dynamic, or a comparison between recordings.

    Alignment is provisional and has not been checked by ear. The note map is not an independent transcription of what the musicians played.

    Score-alignment method

    Public-domain LilyPond/MIDI → voice-aware performed event table; tuning estimated with librosa; Sync Toolbox 1.4.2 memory-restricted multiscale DTW at 50 frames/s, chroma + DLNCO. First/last attacks anchored by score-pitch energy rises in documented inspection windows; section anchors refined by DTW and checked against existing passage labels. Note-offs are warped notation, not measured key releases; final piano holds extend to an estimated decay threshold. No aural review. See tools/align/REPORT.md.

    Provisional. Automatic timings have not been checked by ear. Report an alignment problem.
