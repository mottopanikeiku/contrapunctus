## Removed or narrowed

- Replaced the claim that the third act of *Moses und Aron* survives as text alone: the Center documents musical sketches as well.
- Removed generic claims that twelve-tone writing automatically supplies unity or replaces every function of tonality; explained the specific row, dances and scoring instead.
- Removed defensive art-comparison and copyright boilerplate; preserved all image files and the original poem.
- Corrected the Berlin chronology to the start of teaching in 1926, and made the long interruption in *Gurre-Lieder*'s orchestration explicit.

## Fact-check (independent)

MEAS: 42 grouped claim checks: the 27 source-table entries above, independently checked against fetched sources, plus the 15 supplementary checks below. Repeated assertions in life, sound, works and captions are counted together; this is a count of claim groups, not individual dates or words. Six distinct quotation passages were checked, including the poem excerpt and its translation. Interpretative descriptions and recording recommendations remain editorial judgements.

### Corrections

- Portrait caption “c. 1905–06” → “c. 1907”, following the owning Wien Museum’s current record for inventory 78103. The image filename is unchanged. The Center’s older biography gives 1906; the museum now dates the painting “around 1907”: https://sammlung.wienmuseum.at/en/object/568137-portraet-arnold-schoenberg/ .
- Bach and Brahms literally “on his desk” / “on the music desk” → continued learning from them / their remaining his models. The fetched account documents artistic allegiance, not the physical placement of these scores; the desk anecdote in the Center’s First Quartet essay concerns Brahms keeping Bach and Beethoven nearby: https://www.chandos.net/chanimages/Booklets/CH9939.pdf ; https://schoenberg.at/en/schoenberg/kompositionen/first-string-quartet-in-d-minor-op-7 .
- Wrote the *Brettl-Lieder* while conducting in Berlin → wrote them in 1901 before taking up the Berlin post, which they helped secure. The songs date from April–September; he took up the appointment on 16 December: https://schoenberg.at/en/schoenberg/kompositionen/brettl-songs .
- Finished *Pelleas und Melisande* after returning to Vienna in 1903 → finished it in Berlin in 1903, then returned to Vienna that year. The work essay specifies February; the ensemble’s historical account places the Berlin residence from December 1901 to summer 1903: https://schoenberg.at/en/schoenberg/kompositionen/pelleas-and-melisande-op-5 ; https://www.ensembleintercontemporain.com/en/2020/04/arnold-schonberg-pierrot-lunaire-op-21/ .
- Diary quotation dated only January 1912 → 23 January 1912, the actual entry, retaining the faithful translation: https://schoenberg.at/de/schoenberg/kompositionen/fuenf-orchesterstuecke-op-16 .
- The 1946 illness “preceded the String Trio” → most of the Trio was written after the illness. Work had already begun in June; most was composed from 20 August to 23 September after the heart attack: https://schoenberg.at/en/schoenberg/kompositionen/string-trio-op-45 .
- “The second piece” in the harmony paragraph → “This piece”, so that *Farben*, correctly identified elsewhere as Op. 16 No. 3, cannot be mistaken for No. 2: https://schoenberg.at/en/schoenberg/kompositionen/five-orchestral-pieces-op-16 .

### Quotations confirmed

- “I feel air from another planet”: faithful translation of Stefan George’s “Ich fühle luft von anderem planeten”, opening *Entrückung* in *Der siebente Ring* (1907), sung in Schoenberg’s 1908 finale; no conversational addressee: https://archive2.schoenberg.at/compositions/werke_einzelansicht.php?werke_id=404&herkunft=allewerke .
- “Perhaps even my enemies and adversaries might have felt something here.”: verbatim Schoenberg, introductory note for the Kolisch Quartet’s private recording in Los Angeles, 1936/37, recalling the December 1908 première: https://schoenberg.at/en/schoenberg/kompositionen/second-string-quartet-op-10 .
- “I’m missing the sounds! The colours.”: faithful own translation of Schoenberg’s diary, 23 January 1912, “Mir fehlen eben die Klänge! Die Farben.”, rehearsing the two-piano, eight-hand arrangement; no addressee: https://schoenberg.at/de/schoenberg/kompositionen/fuenf-orchesterstuecke-op-16 .
- “Art belongs to the unconscious!”: faithful own translation of “Und die Kunst gehört aber dem Unbewußten!”, Schoenberg to Wassily Kandinsky, Vienna, 24 January 1911; the same letter supports the expressive-colour and photographic-reproduction remarks in the painting note: https://archive.schoenberg.at/letters/letters.php?id_letters=180&action=view .
- “But life goes on.”: the fourth heading in Schoenberg’s private programme among the 1942 Piano Concerto sketches, with no public addressee; both prose occurrences retain his words: https://schoenberg.at/en/schoenberg/kompositionen/concerto-for-piano-and-orchestra-op-42 .
- *Mondestrunken*, first two stanzas: all eight lines match the Center’s libretto transcript in wording, and the English is a faithful own translation. The supplied original has “Nachts” capitalised and omits the transcript’s comma after “Gelüste”; these are punctuation/capitalisation variants, not different words, and the protected original is unchanged: https://archive2.schoenberg.at/compositions/werke_einzelansicht.php?werke_id=478&herkunft=allewerke .
