# Cage — quotation, fact, image and recording ledger

Researched and written 18 October 2026. The owned content files are `content/composers/cage.mjs`, `content/bibliography/cage.json`, `content/geo/cage.json`, `content/elsewhere/cage.json`, and this ledger. No hosted recording or dossier is being added. No build, formatter, art fetch or git command was run; integrated validation is recorded below.

## Method and source hierarchy

Sources were discovered through web search and then opened. Cage’s own retrospective statement supplies his remembered scenes and stated intentions, not an unquestioned chronology. Dates and places were checked against Paul van Emmerik’s scholarly chronology (maintained in the Cage Compendium mirror with Herbert Henck and András Wilheim credited), the John Cage Trust’s works catalogue, and institutional documents. The Trust’s current works pages link to the older catalogue on `data-johncage.org`; those catalogue pages were opened directly.

The life and sound descriptions distinguish documented procedures from editorial descriptions of their audible consequences. [INFERENCE] Sentences such as a dancer’s leap landing in a silence, a chord changing when a player stops, and the comparison of a found object with a tin can describe possibilities of the documented procedures. They do not report an audition or an otherwise undocumented event. Recording recommendations are editorial judgments supported by the named release documentation and, where available, the reviews reproduced there; no claim of a new listening session is made.

## Score examples: original schematics, no Cage score quotations

1. **Nested proportions.** Four 4/4 bars, crotchet = 72. Each bar has three events lasting 1, 1 and 2 crotchets. Section lengths are one bar on C4, one bar on E4, then two bars on G4: 1:1:2 at the larger scale. Total length is sixteen crotchets. The pitches, tempo and proportion are our educational construction; A documents the general small/large proportional method. This neither transcribes a Cage piece nor attempts to represent a prepared-piano timbre in ABC.
2. **Chance table.** The six-outcome mapping was fixed as `1=C4, 2=D4, 3=E4, 4=G4, 5=A4, 6=rest`, with one crotchet for every result. On 18 October 2026 the research session executed Python `import secrets; [secrets.randbelow(6) + 1 for _ in range(16)]` once. This uses sixteen independent uniform integer samples from 1 through 6 via the operating system random source; it is not a seeded reconstruction of Cage’s method or a claim about actual coin tosses. The returned sequence was **4, 5, 5, 2, 1, 6, 5, 5, 3, 6, 5, 3, 4, 2, 6, 2**. No result was discarded or reordered. Its complete ABC event sequence is `G A A D | C z A A | E z A E | G D z D |]`. There are four complete bars, with rests in positions 6, 10 and 15. These recorded outcomes make the displayed example reproducible without another random draw. T134 documents the historical distinction illustrated: a chance-based composition can produce a fixed performing text. Our six-entry table, equal durations, tonal collection and tempo are explicitly editorial simplifications.

The examples are ordinary piano schematics. No preparation chart is masquerading as notation, no Cage melody is guessed, and no copyrighted score passage is copied.

## Source concerns and deliberately omitted claims

- A is vivid retrospective evidence, with internally inconsistent dating of the Harvard anechoic visit and the name “Gira” where C identifies the music exchange partner as Gita Sarabhai. Neither the inconsistent dates nor the wrong sister’s name is reproduced.
- B’s biographical heading prints 1919–92 for Cage; birth is checked against C as 1912. B’s older 1938 prepared-piano formulation is superseded by T32’s begun-1938/completed-1940 distinction.
- The original 1952 *4′33″* movement timings and exact premiere noises are omitted. No engineer’s explanation of Cage’s body sounds is presented as established physiology.
- No absolute claim that the Black Mountain event was the first happening; no exact disputed August day. No assertion that all of its recollections agree.
- No unsupported attribution of the *Walden* vignette to Sophia Thoreau/Baker-Andrew. The publisher is labelled as publisher, and the selected image’s actual LoC chain is retained.
- No supposed PD status for historical recordings based merely on their age, availability or placement on the Internet Archive. No use of modern commercial Berman audio.
- No copied Cage score and no claim that our random schematic reconstructs the *I Ching* system. No pitch transcription from a preparation chart.
- No session year for Joste inferred from a review or release date. No claim that every Mode product is in stock or offers a free full stream.
- The four official label links were checked by reading their pages. No YouTube links, unofficial uploaders or invented time-stamped moments are published.

Checked 18 October 2026 against freshly fetched sources. Validator: `ok` for composer (2 scores, 3 images), bibliography, elsewhere and geo files after the edit below.

**Change**

- Life, 1912–38: “the things he could see and hear happened simultaneously, without anyone arranging them into a single performance” → “he noticed how many sights and sounds went on together in a single experience, and how much he enjoyed it.” Cage’s own words are “the multiplicity of simultaneous visual and audible events all going together in one’s experience and producing enjoyment”; the clause about nobody arranging them is not in the source. https://johncage.org/cage-autobiographical-statement

**Quotations confirmed**

- “My favorite music is the music I haven’t yet heard.” — Cage, “An Autobiographical Statement”; the Trust’s editor’s note: delivered at Southern Methodist University, 17 April 1990 (written for the Inamori Foundation after the Kyoto Prize, November 1989); first printed *Southwest Review*, 1991. Exact words (American spelling): https://johncage.org/cage-autobiographical-statement
- “Then I’ll spend my life knocking my head against that wall.” — same text, Schoenberg dialogue (Schoenberg: “You’ll come to a wall and won’t be able to get through.”). The page correctly tells it as Cage’s later account.
- “R. Mutt” and “Nearly Stationary” — Commons description of *Fountain* (“signed R. Mutt”); the quartet’s third part is titled “Nearly Stationary” in the Compendium catalogue (https://cagecompendium.org/music/).
