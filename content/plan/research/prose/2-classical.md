# Classical era prose sources

For `content/eras/2-classical.mjs`. Sources below were opened and read. The harmonic and phrase examples are deductions from the existing, unchanged ABC.

- [Haydn2032, No. 16: The Surprise](https://www.haydn2032.com/projekte/no16-the-surprise) — Christian Moritz-Bauer’s notes: Symphony No. 94 premiered at the Hanover Square Rooms on 23 March 1792; the slow movement’s simple theme and sudden chord; Haydn’s first London visit after the death of Prince Nikolaus. The sleeping-audience story is not presented as fact.
- [C. P. E. Bach, Essay on the True Manner of Playing the Clavier: editorial introduction](https://versuch.cpebach.org/v-html/intro-versuch-trans.html) and [contents](https://versuch.cpebach.org/v-html/toc-versuch-trans.html) — first editions in 1753 and 1762; chapters on fingering, ornaments and performance.
- [Digital Mozart Edition: letter to Leopold Mozart, Augsburg, 17 October 1777](https://dme.mozarteum.at/DME/objs/raradocs/transcr/pdf_eng/0352_WAM_LM_1777.pdf) — visit to Stein; escapement, even sound and knee lever. The date follows the scholarly Digital Mozart Edition.
- [Mozart’s letter in German](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/wolfgang-amadeus-mozart/books/mozarts-briefe/chapter/26/) — “schabt und arbeitet so lange, bis das Klavier alles tut”. The essay’s “scrapes and works until the piano can do everything” is our translation.
- [LA Phil: Beethoven, Symphony No. 5](https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-5-in-c-minor-op-67) — four-note opening rhythm; timpani-led transition; piccolo, contrabassoon and three trombones added in the finale; cold concert of 22 December 1808, also including the Sixth Symphony, Fourth Piano Concerto and Choral Fantasy. No claim of a universal orchestration “first” is retained.
- [Beethoven-Haus: Symphony No. 9](https://www.beethoven.de/en/work/view/5556714292117504/) — Vienna premiere on 7 May 1824 and the vocal text, including Beethoven’s “O Freunde, nicht diese Töne!” The English rendering “O friends, away with these sounds!” is our translation. The sequence in the essay does not imply that the solo quartet precedes the chorus’s first entrance.
- [English National Opera: The Marriage of Figaro](https://www.eno.org/operas/the-marriage-of-figaro/) — final-act disguises, Figaro’s jealousy and the Count’s request for forgiveness. These details are from the synopsis, not the unsuccessful Metropolitan Opera fetches.
- [National Gallery: Wright, An Experiment on a Bird in the Air Pump](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/joseph-wright-of-derby-an-experiment-on-a-bird-in-the-air-pump/) — 1768; cockatoo, frightened girls, reassuring father and distracted couple. The connection to Mozart ensembles is an editorial comparison, as recorded in the existing relation field.
- [Schiller’s first text at Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/An_die_Freude_(Schiller)) — consulted alongside the retained poem. The revised poem and its existing translation are unchanged; the former note’s unsatisfactorily sourced account of its revisions is omitted.

The hero metadata and all notation remain unchanged. Broad claims about a universal Classical style, publishers’ earnings and an inevitable progression from Haydn through Mozart to Beethoven were replaced with specific works and events.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against sources opened in this pass.

**Change**

- Beethoven’s recitative, “O friends, away with these sounds!” → “O friends, not these sounds!” (essay paragraph and `arts` poem note). The German is “O Freunde, nicht diese Töne!”; the Cleveland Orchestra prints “O friends, not these sounds!” and states that Beethoven wrote these words himself as the lead-in to Schiller. EXT: <https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/works/symphony-no-9-in-d-minor-op-125-choral>.

**Quotations**

- Mozart to Leopold, Augsburg, 17 October 1777: Stein “shaves and works until the clavier does everything” (DME translation); German “schabt und arbeitet so lange, bis das Klavier alles tut”. The essay’s “scrapes and works until the piano can do everything” is a faithful translation. The same letter has the escapement (hammers “fall back from the strings again the moment they hit them, whether one leaves the keys pressed or released”) and the knee lever (“as soon as one takes one’s knee off even a little, one does not hear anything sounding on at all”). MEAS: <https://dme.mozarteum.at/DME/objs/raradocs/transcr/pdf_eng/0352_WAM_LM_1777.pdf>.
- “O Freunde, nicht diese Töne!” — Beethoven’s own text, bass soloist, before Schiller’s verses. EXT: <https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/works/symphony-no-9-in-d-minor-op-125-choral>.
- Schiller, *An die Freude*, first stanza: wording is the revised text (“Was die Mode streng geteilt / Alle Menschen werden Brüder”). Schiller wrote the poem in 1785 and took the revised ode into the second volume of his collected poems in 1803; the 1808 posthumous text omitted the last stanza. EXT: <http://web.fu-berlin.de/phin/phin53/p53t1.htm> (Immer, PhiN 53, 2010); <https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Ode_an_die_Freude>. The `date` “1785; revised c. 1803” stands.

**Facts confirmed**

- *Surprise* Symphony, No. 94, first performed 23 March 1792 (Salomon concerts, Hanover Square Rooms); Haydn’s first London journey followed the death of Prince Nikolaus Esterházy. EXT: <https://www.haydn2032.com/projekte/no16-the-surprise>. Score (IMSLP, Andante in 2/4): violins marked *semplice* and *p*; the surprise chord is the later *ff* addition. MEAS: <https://s9.imslp.org/files/imglnks/usimg/d/d0/IMSLP933685-PMLP34746-094-imslp.pdf>.
- Beethoven’s concert of 22 December 1808: first performances of the Fifth and Sixth symphonies, brutally cold hall, Fourth Piano Concerto and Choral Fantasy on the programme; finale of the Fifth adds piccolo, three trombones and contrabassoon. EXT: <https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-5-in-c-minor-op-67>.
- Ninth Symphony first performed in Vienna on 7 May 1824. EXT: <https://www.beethoven.de/en/work/view/5556714292117504/>.
- C. P. E. Bach, *Versuch*, two parts, 1753 and 1762. EXT: <https://versuch.cpebach.org/v-html/intro-versuch-trans.html>.
- Wright, *An Experiment on a Bird in the Air Pump*, 1768: cockatoo, elder sister covering her eyes, father’s arm around her, couple absorbed in each other. EXT: <https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/joseph-wright-of-derby-an-experiment-on-a-bird-in-the-air-pump/>.
- *Le nozze di Figaro*, Act IV: Countess and Susanna exchange clothes, Figaro leaps to the conclusion that Susanna is unfaithful, the Count pursues “Susanna”, the real Countess reveals herself. EXT: <https://www.eno.org/operas/the-marriage-of-figaro/>.
- Fortepiano as a lightly built wooden instrument with less sustain than a modern piano: pointer only, <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortepiano>.
- Both ABC examples checked bar by bar (eight-bar period ending on G major then G7–C; C–G modulation with F♯ in D7). The captions match.

**Protected data**: no discrepancy found (David, *Oath of the Horatii*, 1784, Louvre; Schiller `original`).
