## Editorial decisions

- Removed the repeated comparisons with Reich’s processes; the rewritten page describes Feldman’s own mechanisms directly. The lineage arrays remain unchanged.
- Dropped the unhelpful claim that a five-hour piece makes recollection categorically impossible. The revised account describes the practical difficulty of recognising altered returns.
- The score examples remain the original invented schematics, with every ABC string preserved. Captions identify them explicitly; no published score bars are claimed.
- Preserved image files, portrait focus, biographical identity fields and reading list. No unverified new quotation was retained.

## Fact-check (independent)

Independent source-based pass over the epithet, portrait caption, facts, life and asides, sound, selected works, score captions, arts, recording notes and lineage prose. **85 distinct check units: F01–F80 below and five quotations Q1–Q5.** A unit is a named biographical event, musical feature, catalogue/recording entry or image attribution; closely related particulars are kept together and repetitions in different page sections are counted once. This is an accountable count of claim groups, not a count of every date, name or repeated sentence. All linked evidence below was fetched in this pass; the earlier ledger was a finding aid, not evidence by itself.

### Correction

- *Rothko Chapel* work note: “goes back to Feldman’s fifteenth year” → “was written when Feldman was fifteen”. A person’s fifteenth year is the year before their fifteenth birthday. Feldman’s own [programme note, reproduced by IRCAM](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/work/the-rothko-chapel), says that he composed the melody at fifteen; the life paragraph already had the correct age.

### Confirmed quotations

- Q1 — “Wasn’t that extraordinary?”: Feldman to Cage after the January 1950 Webern performance, as Feldman recalled to Alan Beckett; *International Times* No. 3, 14–27 November 1966. [Interview transcript](https://www.cnvill.net/mfbeckett.html).
- Q2 — “I owe him everything and I owe him nothing.”: Feldman speaking about Cage to Alan Beckett in the same November 1966 interview; the page extracts a continuous clause from “I think I would say that…”. [Interview transcript](https://www.cnvill.net/mfbeckett.html).
- Q3 — “like a ghost, like a memory”: Feldman explaining the returning melody of *The Viola in My Life* to Howard Skempton, London, 13 January 1977. [Authorised interview transcription, fetched mirror](https://paperzz.com/doc/8023863/morton-feldman--interview-with-howard-skempton--1977-).
- Q4 — “Mr Feldman, I don’t like opera.”: Beckett to Feldman at their 1976 meeting, reported by Feldman to Skempton on 13 January 1977, not presented as a surviving Beckett letter or independently recorded conversation. [Same transcript](https://paperzz.com/doc/8023863/morton-feldman--interview-with-howard-skempton--1977-).
- Q5 — “I don’t blame you!”: Feldman’s reported reply to Beckett, with the same speaker, addressee and retrospective attribution. [Same transcript](https://paperzz.com/doc/8023863/morton-feldman--interview-with-howard-skempton--1977-).
