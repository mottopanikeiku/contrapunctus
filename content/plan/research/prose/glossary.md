# Glossary prose sources

Sources for the rewrite of all 120 entries in `content/glossary.mjs`. The glossary schema has no `sources` field, so references and qualifications live here. Entry identifiers, aliases, kinds, cross-references and all listening coordinates are unchanged.

## Definitions and illustrative note patterns

- [Open Music Theory, glossary](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/back-matter/glossary/): formal functions, fugue terminology, harmony, counterpoint, metre, cadences and transitions. This is the principal reference for the technical definitions, including the distinction between a British ‘perfect cadence’ and a perfect authentic cadence, suspension preparation/resolution, medial caesura, dominant lock and liquidation.
- [Crane School of Music, Musical Terms and Concepts](https://www.potsdam.edu/academics/Crane/MusicTheory/Musical-Terms-and-Concepts): introductory definitions of texture, metre, imitation, form and chromatic harmony.
- [Open Music Theory, Collections and Scales](https://openmusictheory.github.io/scales2): diatonic modes, whole-tone and octatonic collections; their interval patterns and numbers of distinct transpositions. [Mode of limited transposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modes_of_limited_transposition), ‘Messiaen’s list’, confirms that the octatonic collection is his second mode.
- [Open Music Theory, lament progression](https://openmusictheory.github.io/popRockHarmony-lament.html): descending bass patterns and the examples of Purcell’s Dido and Bach’s *Crucifixus*.
- [Musical temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_temperament): equal and unequal temperaments. [ISO 16:1975](https://www.iso.org/standard/3601.html): A at 440 Hz as a reference pitch.

The simple C-major/A-minor chord progressions, modal white-note scales, suspension and appoggiatura examples, and enharmonic respellings are constructed teaching examples. They do not imply that a named composer used that exact passage. Site-specific definitions of evidence status, level trace and alignment follow `CONTRACTS.md` and retain the distinction between recorded sound, a score marking and a provisional time-to-bar match.

## People speaking and instruments

- **Mozart on rubato:** Wolfgang to his father Leopold, Augsburg, **23 October 1777**. “When they play the left hand always follows.” Checked in Friedrich Kerst’s collection, translated by Henry Edward Krehbiel, [*Mozart: The Man and the Artist, as Revealed in His Own Words*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4042/pg4042.html), excerpt **58**. The preceding sentence says that his own left hand keeps strict time. The glossary identifies speaker, addressee, place and date; it does not treat this one technique as the only kind of rubato.
- **Burney hearing C. P. E. Bach:** the after-dinner clavichord scene in Hamburg in **1772**, continuing almost to eleven at night, comes from Charles Burney, *The Present State of Music in Germany, the Netherlands, and United Provinces* (1775), vol. 2, p. 269, reproduced in the [Essential C. P. E. Bach introduction](https://www.cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/Essential-CPEB-Intro.pdf). The glossary paraphrases the scene.
- [Berlin Musikinstrumenten-Museum, collection themes](https://www.simpk.de/en/museum/collection/central-themes-of-the-collection.html): clavichord tangent contact, quiet tone and Bebung; historical instrument construction. No claim that a clavichord can swell an already struck note indefinitely.
- [Vienna Symphonic Library, violin](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/strings/violin): pizzicato, arco, tremolo, divisi and the two col legno techniques.
- [Buffet Crampon basset clarinet](https://www.buffetcrampon.com/products/basset-clarinet-17-6-bc1223-2-0): low written C and extended compass. [Soni Ventorum archive](https://soniventorum.com/soniventorum_archives.html): William McColl’s self-built basset clarinet in the December 1987 Mozart K. 622 performance conducted by Abraham Kaplan at the University of Washington.

## Claims removed or narrowed

- Removed the estimated A = 415 pitch for the Couperin recording: no performer or release documentation established it.
- Removed the claim that Wq 67 was written exclusively for clavichord, and the misleading suggestion that clavichord pressure can make a held sound swell like a bowed note.
- Removed an exact ‘first twelve-note piece’ date and priority claim for Schoenberg; the supported Op. 25 example replaces it.
- Removed unnecessary first-use/coinage claims for the leitmotif, and the compressed chronology claiming that all thirteen Liszt symphonic poems belonged to 1848–58.
- Replaced ‘every third Goldberg variation is a canon’ with the nine specified canons; avoided calling both final movements of Schoenberg’s Second Quartet atonal.
- Removed the implication that equal temperament makes all keys identical in colour, that every cadenza follows one harmonic placement, and that all sufficiently old recordings are automatically public domain everywhere.
- Dropped a precise three-note description of the Bach answer that had not been independently established; retained the supported key and contrapuntal role.
- Replaced machine-derived pitch/level commentary and claims of documentary influence based solely on audible similarity with definitions, score details and properly identified examples.
