# Fin de siècle era prose sources

For `content/eras/4-fin-de-siecle.mjs`. Sources below were opened and read. The harmonic circuit and heterophony captions describe the unchanged ABC examples.

- [Sibelius.fi archive: Fifth Symphony](https://sibelius.klubi.fi/english/musiikki/ork_sinf_05.htm) — sixteen swans on 21 April 1915; horn theme and woodwind melody in the finale; first performance on the fiftieth birthday in 1915, revisions and final version in 1919; six separated closing chords.
- [Sibelius.fi archive: Swedish biography, 1915–19](https://sibelius.klubi.fi/svenska/elamankaari/sib_viides_sinfonia.htm) — the diary quotation at the swan encounter: “Ett av mitt livs stora upplevelser! Herregud vilken skönhet”. The essay’s “One of the great experiences of my life! My God, what beauty!” is our translation. The page’s legacy encoding damages some accented characters but leaves the wording identifiable.
- [LA Phil: Mahler, Symphony No. 3](https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-3-mahler) — scoring, eight horns, two harpists and two timpanists, choruses; oboe minuet; distant brass solo; Nietzsche setting in movement four and children’s “bimm, bamm” in movement five. The essay calls the distant part a brass solo to avoid confusing the various posthorn and flügelhorn performance solutions. The page’s erroneous date for Nietzsche’s book is not used.
- [LA Phil: Strauss, Don Quixote](https://www.laphil.com/works/don-quixote) — 1897 composition; cello as Don Quixote; Sancho’s bass clarinet, tenor tuba and viola; sheep in variation two and wind machine in the imaginary flight.
- [LA Phil: Also sprach Zarathustra](https://www.laphil.com/works/also-sprach-zarathustra-op-30) — 1896; Nietzsche chapter headings; midnight bell; B-major upper winds and C in the bass at the close.
- [LA Phil: Rachmaninoff, Piano Concerto No. 2](https://www.laphil.com/works/piano-concerto-no-2-in-c-minor-op-18) — bell-like piano opening, followed by the orchestra’s C-minor theme. Its description of the opening chords as “in F major” is not adopted.
- [LA Phil: Scriabin, Prometheus](https://www.laphil.com/works/prometheus-poem-of-fire-op-60-scriabin) — composition in 1909–10; light-keyboard part, solo piano, optional wordless chorus and F♯-major ending; Scriabin’s participation in the 1911 Moscow premiere without projected lighting.
- [Société Gustav Mahler: The Song of the Earth](https://societemahler-france.org/en/the-song-of-the-earth%E2%80%8B/) — composition and orchestration in 1908–09; six movements for two alternating singers; spare farewell scoring, flute and clarinet retaining A over the final C-major chord. Also read [LA Phil’s work note](https://www.laphil.com/works/das-lied-von-der-erde), including the closing repetitions of “forever”.
- [Janáček: How he created](https://www.leosjanacek.eu/en/how-he-created/) — speech notation from 1897, street and train observations, contextual notes. The essay does not suggest he simply pasted recorded utterances into his operas.
- [Janáček: Jenůfa](https://www.leosjanacek.eu/en/jenufa/) — prose play, Brno premiere in 1904, Prague refusals and 1916 production, Kovařovic’s changes, Max Brod’s translation and Universal Edition.
- [English National Opera: Tosca](https://www.eno.org/operas/tosca/) — Cavaradossi’s memories, promised sham execution and actual death. Only plot elements shared with the original opera are used, not alterations specific to ENO’s current staging.
- [Secession: Beethoven Frieze](https://secession.at/beethovenfrieze) — 1902 exhibition, Klinger’s sculpture, Klimt’s work in an adjoining space, twenty-one artists under Josef Hoffmann, the frieze’s relationship to Beethoven’s Ninth.
- [Elgar Society: Pomp and Circumstance recordings](https://www.elgar.org/3pomp-d.htm) — 26 June 1914, roughly thirty London musicians, cuts to just over four minutes and opening with the trio tune. This was Elgar’s second acoustic session but his first recording of this march; the essay distinguishes those facts.
- [National Museum, Oslo: The Scream](https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no/en/collection/object/NG.M.00939) — the 1893 painting and Munch’s 1892 notebook account of walking with two friends, the blood-red sky and a scream through nature. The note paraphrases the museum; it does not quote an unavailable eMunch transcription.
- [Nietzsche: Das andere Tanzlied](https://www.textlog.de/nietzsche/schriften/zarathustra/das-andere-tanzlied) — midnight poem and numbered clock strokes. The existing poem and translation remain unchanged.

General assertions about the historical growth of score-fidelity and changing institutional power were removed instead of being given unsupported causal force. Hero metadata, image files, era dates and notation remain unchanged.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against sources opened in this pass. No change to `4-fin-de-siecle.mjs` was needed.

**Quotations**

- Sibelius, diary, 21 April 1915, after seeing sixteen swans: Swedish “Ett av mitt livs stora upplevelser! Herregud vilken skönhet”; English “One of the great experiences of my life! God, how beautiful”. The essay’s “My God, what beauty!” is a faithful rendering. He immediately wrote the thematic sequence in his diary. MEAS: <https://sibelius.klubi.fi/svenska/elamankaari/sib_viides_sinfonia.htm>; <https://sibelius.klubi.fi/english/musiikki/ork_sinf_05.htm>.
- Nietzsche, midnight song (`original`): “O Mensch! Gib acht! / Was spricht die tiefe Mitternacht? / „Ich schlief, ich schlief —, … Weh spricht: Vergeh! / Doch alle Lust will Ewigkeit —, / — will tiefe, tiefe Ewigkeit!“” — wording and line order match *Zarathustra* III, “Das andere Tanzlied” (1884), where the twelve clock strokes fall between the lines; the same song returns in Part IV (1885). MEAS: <https://www.textlog.de/nietzsche/schriften/zarathustra/das-andere-tanzlied>.
- Munch’s notebook, 1892 (MM T 2367): walking with two friends, the sky turned blood-red, “an infinite scream passing through nature” — paraphrased in the note. MEAS: <https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no/en/collection/object/NG.M.00939>.

**Facts confirmed**

- Sibelius Fifth: first version 8 December 1915 (his fiftieth birthday), second 1916, final 1919 (24 November); finale opens with rushing strings, horns carry the swan theme with woodwind song above; six final chords. EXT: <https://sibelius.klubi.fi/english/musiikki/ork_sinf_05.htm>.
- Mahler Symphony No. 3: eight horns, two harps, two timpanists, women’s and children’s choruses, an offstage flügelhorn (the posthorn part) in the third movement; the second-movement minuet opens with a delicate oboe melody; the fourth movement is a rocking-note setting of Nietzsche’s “O Mensch” for the solo voice; in the fifth the children’s chorus imitates bells (“bimm, bamm”). MEAS: <https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-3-mahler>. Scoring “timp(2) … hp(2), post hn” in <https://www.universaledition.com/en/Works/Symphony-No.-3/P0002956>.
- Strauss, *Don Quixote*, 1897: cello as the Don; bass clarinet and tenor tuba with solo viola for Sancho; variation II the sheep; wind machine in variation VII. MEAS: <https://www.laphil.com/works/don-quixote>. *Zarathustra*, 1896, eight chapter headings, bell at midnight. EXT: <https://www.laphil.com/works/also-sprach-zarathustra-op-30>.
- Scriabin, *Prometheus*, 1909–10; first performed in Moscow on 2 March 1911 (Old Style) with Scriabin as soloist and Koussevitzky conducting, without light; optional chorus on vowels; F♯-major close. EXT: <https://www.laphil.com/works/prometheus-poem-of-fire-op-60-scriabin>.
- *Das Lied von der Erde*: composed 1908 (completed 1909), six movements, two alternating voices; final C-major chord with A held by flute and clarinet; “ewig” repeated. EXT: <https://societemahler-france.org/en/the-song-of-the-earth%E2%80%8B/>; <https://www.laphil.com/works/das-lied-von-der-erde>.
- Janáček: speech melodies from 1897, notated with date, place and circumstance; *Jenůfa* set from Preissová’s prose play, premiered in Brno on 21 January 1904, refused by Prague’s National Theatre, staged there on 26 May 1916 with Kovařovic’s changes; Max Brod translated the libretto and brought it to Universal Edition. EXT: <https://www.leosjanacek.eu/en/jenufa/>; <https://www.leosjanacek.eu/en/how-he-created/>.
- *Tosca* Act III: execution promised as a sham; “E lucevan le stelle”; the execution is real. EXT: <https://www.eno.org/operas/tosca/>.
- Secession exhibition, 15 April–27 June 1902: Klimt’s *Beethoven Frieze* in the left-hand aisle, Klinger’s statue in the main hall, twenty-one artists under Josef Hoffmann; frieze based on Wagner’s reading of the Ninth; ends with the arts, a choir of angels and an embracing couple. EXT: <https://secession.at/beethovenfrieze>.
- Elgar, *Pomp and Circumstance* No. 1: recorded 26 June 1914 in his second acoustic session, his first recording of this march; severely cut to just over four minutes and begun with the trio tune; some thirty London players. EXT: <https://www.elgar.org/3pomp-d.htm>.
- Both ABC examples checked: C major–A♭ major–E major–C major circuit with common tones C, G♯/A♭, E; the flute line decorates the oboe’s G–A–G–E–D.

**Protected data**: no discrepancy found (Klimt, Munch `date` fields; Nietzsche `date` “1884; repeated in Part IV, 1885”).
