# Romantic era prose sources

For `content/eras/3-romantic.mjs`. Sources below were opened and read. The two captioned examples retain their original ABC; their harmonic and accent descriptions derive from those notes.

- [Wigmore Hall programme, 21 December 2025](https://media.wigmore-hall.org.uk/documents/Programme_21_December_2025_730pm.pdf) — Schubert’s Erlkönig, D 328, 1815; Goethe’s text and its speakers. The poem and translation already in the era file remain unchanged.
- [Cal Performances: Mark Padmore and Mitsuko Uchida, Winterreise](https://calperformances.org/program-notes/2023-24/mark-padmore-tenor-mitsuko-uchida-piano/) — Paul Thomason’s programme notes: twenty-four Müller poems, composition in 1827, the D-major turn near the end of Gute Nacht and the open fifths, bare feet and final question of Der Leiermann.
- [USC Polish Music Center: Mazur](https://polishmusic.usc.edu/research/dances/mazur/) — triple metre and displaced second- or third-beat accents; Chopin’s relationship to the dance. Also checked against [Elbphilharmonie: The Mazurka](https://www.elbphilharmonie.de/en/mediatheque/the-mazurka/1136).
- [Fanny Hensel: Das Jahr work entry](https://fannyhensel.de/?p=460) and [HenselPushers: Das Jahr](https://henselpushers.org/das-jahr) — 1841, twelve months and postlude. No claim that the cycle directly depicts the Italian journey is made.
- [Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin: Fanny Hensel](https://staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/die-staatsbibliothek/abteilungen/musik/sammlungen/bestaende/familie-mendelssohn/fanny-hensel-geb-mendelssohn-bartholdy-1805-1847) — revival of Sunday concerts in 1831, performing and conducting at Leipziger Straße 3, family restrictions. Its quotation from a letter to her sister Rebecka in 1844 reads “22 Equipagen auf dem Hof und Liszt und 8 Prinzessinnen im Saal”; our translation is “twenty-two carriages in the courtyard, and Liszt and eight princesses in the hall”. The source supplies the year, not a day or month.
- [Piano history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano) — checked the existing mechanical claims about Érard’s 1821 repetition action, Babcock’s 1825 Boston patent for an iron-framed square piano and the sustaining pedal. The Met object pages returned HTTP 429, so they are not claimed as read evidence.
- [Hector Berlioz Website: Symphonie fantastique](http://www.hberlioz.com/Scores/sfantastique.htm) — Michel Austin’s account: Paris premiere in 1830, Liszt’s attendance and transcription, the clarinet’s interrupted recollection before the execution, the transformed beloved and col legno strings in the finale.
- [The Berlioz Society: Berlioz’s writings](https://www.theberliozsociety.org.uk/pages/berlioz-s-writings) — orchestration treatise and practical learning from musicians. The essay omits the old unqualified 1844 publication date: the Society dates publication to 1843, while the first edition bears 1844.
- [LA Phil: Prelude and Liebestod from Tristan und Isolde](https://www.laphil.com/works/prelude-and-liebestod-from-tristan-und-isolde) — opening cello phrase and wind chord; successive unsettled harmonies. No assertion that one literal chord remains unresolved throughout the opera.
- [Schumann Portal: Clara Schumann’s concert tours](https://www.schumann-portal.de/Concert_tours_Clara.html) — her concert management, help from daughters and friends, railway travel and closely spaced appearances, changing repertoire.
- [LA Phil: Dvořák’s Slavonic Dances, Op. 46](https://www.laphil.com/works/slavonic-dances-op-46) — Simrock’s 1878 commission for piano duet, subsequent orchestration and instrumental forces.
- [Bach-Museum Leipzig](https://www.bachmuseumleipzig.de/en/bach-museum/museumfromhome-1) — Mendelssohn’s Berlin performance of the St Matthew Passion in 1829. The essay avoids calling this the first performance since Bach’s death.
- [Staatliche Museen zu Berlin: The Abbey in the Oakwood](https://visit.smb.museum/object/obj-968249) — monks and coffin, ruins, bare oaks and moon; exhibition in Berlin in 1810 with Monk by the Sea. The relationship to Winterreise is an editorial comparison.

The old general catalogue of national styles and unsupported claims about their political reception was replaced with the documented publishing history of the Slavonic Dances. No poem, artwork file, era identifier or notation was changed.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against sources opened in this pass.

**Change**

- “His orchestration treatise grew from this close attention to instruments and from conversations with the people who played them” → “… and from what orchestral players taught him”. The Berlioz Society says only that he “learned from orchestral professionals”; “conversations” is not attested. EXT: <https://www.theberliozsociety.org.uk/pages/berlioz-s-writings>.

**Protected data**

- Goethe, *Erlkönig*, line 7 of the `original`: the file already reads “Den Erlenkönig mit Kron’ und Schweif?” (the correction authorised by the lead; no further edit made). Goethe’s text has “Erlkönig” in line 6 and “Erlenkönig” in line 7. MEAS: <https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/johann-wolfgang-von-goethe/books/gedichte-ausgabe-letzter-hand/chapter/110/> (“Siehst, Vater, du den Erlkönig nicht? / Den Erlenkönig mit Kron und Schweif?”); the Wigmore Hall programme text prints the same line with the apostrophe, <https://media.wigmore-hall.org.uk/documents/Programme_21_December_2025_730pm.pdf>. The English rendering (“The Erlking with crown and trailing train?”) still matches. Poem date 1782 stands.

**Quotations**

- Hensel to her sister Rebecka, 1844, on a Sunday concert: “22 Equipagen auf dem Hof und Liszt und 8 Prinzessinnen im Saal”, rendered “twenty-two carriages in the courtyard, and Liszt and eight princesses in the hall”. The page text is cut off by ellipsis in the rendered view, but the page source carries the full quotation. MEAS: <https://staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/die-staatsbibliothek/abteilungen/musik/sammlungen/bestaende/familie-mendelssohn/fanny-hensel-geb-mendelssohn-bartholdy-1805-1847>. The same page: Sunday concerts revived by Fanny in 1831, as pianist and conductor, in the Gartensaal of Leipziger Straße 3.
- Goethe `original` (first stanzas) otherwise identical to the Gutenberg text above.

**Facts confirmed**

- *Erlkönig*, D 328, 1815; Winterreise composed 1827 (Part I February, Part II October), 24 Müller poems; “Gute Nacht” sets three verses in D minor, then the last verse in D major; “Der Leiermann”: barefoot on the ice, droning open fifths in the left hand. EXT: <https://calperformances.org/program-notes/2023-24/mark-padmore-tenor-mitsuko-uchida-piano/>.
- Hensel, *Das Jahr*: twelve piano pieces and a postlude, composed 1841 (August–December). EXT: <https://henselpushers.org/das-jahr>.
- Érard’s double-escapement action, 1821 (English patent no. 4,631), whose repetition lever permits repeating a note before the key has returned to rest (pointers: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_%C3%89rard>). Babcock’s one-piece cast-iron frame for the square piano, patent issued in Boston in 1825, to resist string tension. EXT: <https://www.colonialsociety.org/publications/3297/early-pianomaking-boston-1790-1830>.
- Berlioz: Liszt was struck by the first performance and later made a piano reduction; the March to the Scaffold quotes the idée fixe at the climax before the guillotine cuts it off; the finale uses col legno. EXT: <http://www.hberlioz.com/Scores/sfantastique.htm>.
- Tristan prelude: the cellos play four notes, then oboes, bassoons and English horn enter with the “Tristan chord”, an unresolved dissonance. EXT: <https://www.laphil.com/works/prelude-and-liebestod-from-tristan-und-isolde>.
- Clara Schumann: her last London journey, 1888, closed with *Carnaval*; she managed concerts with help from daughters and friends; trains from the 1840s; appearances one or two days apart; Beethoven, Bach, Schumann, Mendelssohn, Chopin, Schubert as her repertoire pillars. EXT: <https://www.schumann-portal.de/Concert_tours_Clara.html>.
- Dvořák, Slavonic Dances Op. 46: written 1878 at Simrock’s request for piano duet, orchestrated soon after; orchestra includes trumpets and cymbals. EXT: <https://www.laphil.com/works/slavonic-dances-op-46>.
- Mendelssohn’s Berlin performance of the *St Matthew Passion*, 1829. EXT: <https://www.bachmuseumleipzig.de/en/bach-museum/museumfromhome-1>.
- Friedrich, *Abtei im Eichwald*: ruined Gothic church, ancient oaks, snow, crescent moon; shown at the Berlin Academy in 1810 as a pendant to *Monk by the Sea*. EXT: <https://visit.smb.museum/object/obj-968249>.
- Both ABC examples checked bar by bar: C–E major with common E (G→G♯, C→B), E major to A minor, then F major sharing A and C; hemiola accents alternate 3+3 and 2+2+2. The captions match.

**Unresolved**: none.
