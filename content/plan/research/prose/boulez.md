# Boulez — quotation and fact sources

Research ledger for the prose rewrite of `content/composers/boulez.mjs`. Sources were located through web search and the pages or documents opened before use. Interpretative descriptions of a sound, performance or painting are distinguished below from documentary claims.

## Biography and institutions

The main chronological authority is the [Philharmonie de Paris / French Ministry of Culture archival biography](https://www.pierreboulez.org/default/biographie.aspx?_lg=en-US), whose dated entries link to manuscripts, photographs, broadcasts and programmes.

| Claim used | Evidence checked |
| --- | --- |
| Born in Montbrison on 26 March 1925; father wanted an engineering career; advanced mathematics in Lyon; move to Paris in 1943 | Archival biography, 1925 and 1941–43 entries. The September 1943 move makes him eighteen. Its dated account takes precedence over the compressed IRCAM biography, which says 1942. |
| Counterpoint with Andrée Vaurabourg; private study with Messiaen; harmony first prize in 1945; Debussy and Stravinsky in the classes | Archival biography, 19 April, 28 June and 8 December 1944; January and 11 June 1945. The December entry names *Jeux*, *The Rite of Spring*, *Petrushka* and *Les Noces*. |

The photocopy, Nigg and the lost manuscript are all explicit in the interview. |

| Eye trouble curtailed engagements in 2010; death at home in Baden-Baden on 5 January 2016 | Archival biography, 2010 and 2016. The first entry describes glaucoma, surgery and cancellations. The text does not claim 2010 was his final conducting year. |

## Attested quotations

All three quotations in the life are by Boulez. The interviewers and interview dates appear in the reader-facing prose. Only the words inside quotation marks are quoted; surrounding explanations are paraphrase.

| Quotation | Speaker, occasion and checked text |
| --- | --- |
| ‘You learned to listen without sound’ | Pierre Boulez to Jed Distler, London, late August 2000. [Complete interview, republished by Distler on 6 January 2016](https://jdistlerblog.wordpress.com/2016/01/06/an-interview-with-pierre-boulez-1925-2016/), Part I, question about composing at the piano. Boulez answers ‘Never’, describes the distracting horn and piano classes, then says: ‘You learned to listen without sound, and being very much aware.’ The excerpt stops before the comma. |
| ‘The recording is a little bit like having a picture of a mobile by Calder’ | Same interview, Part I, question about the recording of *Répons*. This is a complete sentence in the transcript. Boulez explains that a recording cannot surround the listener with the work’s sound. His following discussion distinguishes the score, unchanged after 1984, from the changing hardware. |
| ‘the material in fact composes with you, and you compose with the material’ | Pierre Boulez to Wolfgang Schaufler, Baden-Baden, December 2010, [Universal Edition interview on its Boulez page](https://www.universaledition.com/en/Contacts/Pierre-Boulez/). The phrase appears within his answer about *Dérive II* and his relationship to musical material. The page quotes the exact lower-case fragment, without silently capitalising it or adding words. Universal Edition supplies the English text; this is not a new translation. |

The Distler interview also supports the Guimet-related instrumental recollection, Boulez’s habit of composing without a piano, and the distinction between changing software/hardware and an unchanged *Répons* score. The Schaufler interview supports the Nigg/photocopy story, twelve-bar *Notations*, expanded orchestral versions, Wagner comparison, and the formal account of *Dérive 2*.

## Corrections, omissions and retained boundaries

- Corrected BBC tenure from 1969–75 to 1971–75: 1969 was the appointment announcement, not the start of service.
- Corrected *Rituel* from 1972–75 to 1974–75; removed an unspecified ‘later revisions’ label.
- Removed ‘later revisions’ from *Répons*. Boulez explicitly says the score stopped changing in 1984 while the hardware changed. A compressed IRCAM biography gives a conflicting later date; the detailed work record, chronology and composer’s own account are preferred.
- Removed the claim that the displayed French poem exactly follows the 1887 printed text; punctuation differs, and the original text was protected.
- Retained the original ‘oil and chalk on canvas’ after resolving conflicting general image records through Kakinuma’s published caption. The initial inability to open the Beyeler page did not remain a source gap.
- Replaced the second schematic’s misleading title. It is an invented transposition-and-union example, not a quoted Boulez chord or a claim that multiplying two identical thirds gives that set.
- The discussion of Cage’s disagreements, broad claims about concert reform, and generalisations about the entire discography were not needed for this life and were omitted rather than left as unsupported background.
- Preserved all protected identity/date/era values, portrait file and focus, both ABC strings, artwork files, French poem, reading list, and lineage arrays. Removed the obsolete `featured` field. No other composer or listening-room file was edited.
- No outstanding source gap is carried into a specific factual claim on the rewritten page.

## Fact-check (independent)

Independent second-family check of all reader-facing prose, facts, work notes, sound paragraphs, captions, arts, recordings and lineage prose. **74 claim units checked, including all three spoken quotations.** Each numbered unit below is an assertion or closely connected assertion bundle; repeated occurrences are counted once. The count is reproducible from this table and does not pretend that each date, name and repetition is a separate unit.

Labels: **EXT** = fetched documentary/analytical evidence; **MEAS** = direct inspection of displayed notation or image; **INFERENCE** = editorial interpretation. No audio measurements or simulations were used. Explicit 2025–2026 searches were followed by opening the 2025 archival catalogue, the 2025 Répons note and institutional work surveys before relying on older foundations.

### Corrections

- **EXT — Third Sonata, life and work note:** only two main sections completed → two main sections published complete during his lifetime. The 2025 catalogue says all five were composed in 1955–57, records further work to 1963 and identifies the posthumous 2019 Antiphonie fragments; its distinction between composition, completion and publication is more precise than the old wording: https://pierreboulez.org/doc/SYRACUSE/20/ . The new wording preserves the unfinished status without erasing the other formants.
- **EXT — Second Sonata, life:** unqualified final completion in February 1948 → begun in 1947. The archival chronology gives a February 1948 completion, but the 2025 work catalogue spans 1947–50. The replacement states their common starting point without mistaking an early completion for the end of all work: https://pierreboulez.org/doc/SYRACUSE/13/ ; https://www.pierreboulez.org/default/biographie.aspx?_lg=en-US .
- **EXT — Répons, life:** all score changes stopped in 1984 → the work reached its full length in 1984 and its electronics continued to change. The 2025 catalogue quotes Andrew Gerzso’s 2014 score preface, recording electronic additions in 1998/99 and slight modifications around 2003: https://pierreboulez.org/doc/SYRACUSE/45/ . Boulez’s 2000 statement to Distler remains historical testimony, not a sufficient account of the work’s subsequent history. The earlier ledger’s absolute no-later-revisions conclusion is superseded by this evidence.
- **EXT — Rituel work date:** 1974–75 → 1972–75, revised 1976 and 1987, following the broader composition and revision history in the 2025 catalogue: https://pierreboulez.org/Default/doc/SYRACUSE/42/rituel-in-memoriam-bruno-maderna-for-orchestra?_lg=en-US . This supersedes the earlier ledger’s assertion that 1972–75 was wrong.
- **EXT — Second Sonata work date:** 1947–48 → 1947–50, following the 2025 catalogue’s full composition span: https://pierreboulez.org/doc/SYRACUSE/13/ . The February 1948 completion in the chronology is an earlier milestone, not the end of the catalogue’s span.
- **EXT — Third Sonata work date:** 1955–57, unfinished → 1955–57, further work 1958–63, unfinished. The 2025 catalogue records both periods and sporadic later work: https://pierreboulez.org/doc/SYRACUSE/20/ . The three work-date fields are catalogue metadata, not the date fields used by the site timeline; the assignment’s timeline restriction therefore does not prevent these corrections.
