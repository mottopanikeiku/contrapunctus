# Precursors prose: sources and editorial decisions

Sources for the rewritten epithets and image captions in `content/precursors.mjs`. Slugs, names, image filenames, focus positions, life years, anchors and the hero’s catalogue metadata were retained. The existing empty `facts` arrays and null `quote` fields remain empty; the rewrite does not manufacture biographical facts to fill them. Captions are plain-text fields, so the new wording avoids inserting HTML work titles into them.

## Gregorian chant

- The epithet describes a single unaccompanied melodic line carrying liturgical words. [Kevin N. Moll’s plainchant chapter, East Carolina University](https://ecu.pressbooks.pub/earlymusicinthewest/chapter/plainchant-notation-and-theory/), supplies the chant-theory context; [Rebecca Maloy’s review of *Chant and Its Origins*](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/16936) supplies the historical setting.
- [Commons catalogue, *Codex 10127–10144*, fol. 90](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Codex_10127-10144_fol_90.png): opening page of the Mont-Blandin gradual, c. 800, Royal Library of Belgium. The caption states what the image is without the former unnecessary warning that it is not a composer portrait.

## Hildegard of Bingen

- [International Society of Hildegard von Bingen Studies, ‘Music’](https://www.hildegard-society.org/p/music.html?m=0): melodic leaps, wide-ranging chant and the women’s community. The epithet is a musical description, not a claim that every Hildegard chant has an exceptional range.
- [Hildegard Society FAQ](https://www.hildegard-society.org/p/faq.html?m=0): Rupertsberg and Hildegard’s musical life.
- [Commons catalogue for the retained image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hildegard_von_Bingen.jpg): illumination from the Rupertsberg *Scivias*, depicting divine inspiration communicated to a scribe. ‘Manuscript tradition’ avoids claiming that the displayed colour image is a surviving original leaf.

## Guillaume de Machaut

- [Alice V. Clark’s review of Anne Walters Robertson, *Guillaume de Machaut and Reims*](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/15661): cathedral canon at Reims; secular poetry and music as well as the Mass.
- [Commons catalogue for *Machaut 1.jpg*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Machaut_1.jpg): fourteenth-century image of Nature presenting Sense, Rhetoric and Music. The caption describes this allegory, not an actual audience with three visitors.

## Josquin des Prez

- [*Ave Maria … virgo serena*, text and analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ave_Maria_..._virgo_serena): the opening greeting passes through the four voices. The epithet refers to this particular motet discussed in the linked Origins lesson.
- [Commons catalogue for *Josquin.jpg*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Josquin.jpg): Charles Gustave Housez, 1881; a retrospective image freely based on an older woodcut. ‘As Housez imagined him’ conveys its status without a defensive second sentence.

## Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina

- [Francesco Saggio’s 2024 critical edition of *Missa Papae Marcelli*, University of Pavia record](https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/1510580): the Mass’s six-voice scoring and the disputed status of the seven-voice second Agnus Dei. The epithet refers to the six-voice Mass in the Origins lesson.
- [Commons catalogue for *Retrato de Palestrina.jpg*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Retrato_de_Palestrina.jpg): unknown painter, sixteenth century, portrait identified as Palestrina. This qualified identification is preserved.

## Claudio Monteverdi

- [Tim Carter’s Monteverdi chronology](https://sscm-jscm.org/jscm-issues/volume-18-no-1/claudio-monteverdi-a-chronology/) and [Mauro Masiero’s account of *L’Orfeo*](https://www.quinteparallele.net/compositori/lorfeo-di-monteverdi-le-basi/): the 1607 opera and Orpheus’s journey into the underworld. The epithet describes the drama, not a biographical journey by Monteverdi.
- [Commons catalogue for the Strozzi portrait](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bernardo_Strozzi_-_Claudio_Monteverdi_(c.1630).jpg): c. 1630; identification by comparison with the 1644 commemorative engraving. The caption keeps ‘identified as’.

## Dieterich Buxtehude

- [St Mary’s, Lübeck, ‘Dieterich Buxtehude’](https://www.st-marien-luebeck.de/en/music/dieterich-buxtehude): Bach’s journey on foot in 1705 to hear Buxtehude. No distance, precise route or fabricated reminiscence is added.
- [Commons catalogue for *BuxWV83 organ tablature.jpg*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BuxWV83_organ_tablature.jpg): a figured-bass realisation for *O dulcis Jesu, o amor cordis mei*, BuxWV 83, copied by Gustav/Gustaf Düben in 1681; Uppsala University Library, Düben collection, vocal manuscript 82:42, f. 17v. The caption identifies the copy and its function. It does not suggest that the image is the organ prelude used elsewhere in the site. The work’s full identity is recorded here rather than inserted as markup into the plain-text caption.

## Arcangelo Corelli

- [IMSLP, *Concerto grosso in G minor*, Op. 6 No. 8](https://imslp.org/wiki/Concerto_grosso_in_G_minor_%27Fatto_per_la_Notte_di_Natale%27%2C_Op.6_No.8_%28Corelli%2C_Arcangelo%29): the concertino of two violins and cello with the larger string ensemble and continuo. ‘Step out’ describes the concertino’s musical role.
- [Commons catalogue for the Hugh Howard portrait](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arcangelo_Corelli,_portrait_by_Hugh_Howard_(1697).jpg): the Stirling Smith version is dated 1697–98. Dates for other Howard versions have not been substituted for this image’s date.

## Henry Purcell

- [MIT OpenCourseWare, *Dido and Aeneas*: synopsis and notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21m-011-introduction-to-western-music-fall-2024/pages/week-5-dido-and-aeneas-synopsis-and-notes/): Dido’s final lament over a chromatically descending ground bass. The epithet refers to the downward part of the recurring ground, not a claim that every interval of the bass is a semitone.
- [Commons catalogue for NPG 1352](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:John_Closterman_(1660-1711)_(by_or_after)_-_Henry_Purcell_-_NPG_1352_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg): retained image, Closterman/studio attribution, 1695 and National Portrait Gallery ownership. The catalogue’s linked Wikidata description identifies it as by or after the studio. The caption keeps the existing year but qualifies it with ‘c.’ and does not assert that Purcell sat for it in that year.
- The [NPG catalogue](https://www.npg.org.uk/collections/search/portraitExtended/mw05172/Henry-Purcell) was discoverable in search but returned HTTP 403 on direct reading. Its search record gives ‘circa 1695 or after’ and ‘by or after the studio of John Closterman’; no further museum-specific provenance claim has been added.

## Editorial limits

The epithets now point to a person, a named dramatic character, or an audible musical event. Image captions retain useful attributions and dates while dropping ‘not a portrait’, ‘not a life portrait’ and ‘not the work heard here’ formulas. No uncertain original occasion, undocumented visual resemblance or invented quotation was added. The existing life dates were intentionally outside the rewrite’s scope and remain unchanged.

- Machaut: canon of Reims after nearly two decades with John of Bohemia (the Reims review in the origins ledger above, <https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/15661>); Hildegard’s Rupertsberg convent founded 1150 (<https://www.hildegard-society.org/p/faq.html?m=0>).
- Not re-checked against a source in this pass: the exact birth and death years of Hildegard (1098–1179), Machaut (c. 1300–1377), Josquin (c. 1450–1521), Palestrina (c. 1525–1594), Monteverdi (1567–1643), Buxtehude (c. 1637; death 1707 confirmed above), Corelli (1653–1713); these are standard biographical dates, unchanged.
