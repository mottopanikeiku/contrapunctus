# Rossini — quotation, fact and image sources

Sources below were opened and read on 18 October 2026. This ledger covers `content/composers/rossini.mjs`, the bibliography and geography. Recording-specific timings, transfers and rights belong to the individual listening dossiers.

## Principal documentary sources

- **ROF chronology:** Rossini Opera Festival, ‘Gioachino Rossini: Biographical sketch and opera chronology’: https://www.rossinioperafestival.it/en/foundation/rof-people/gioachino-rossini/ . Read the entries from 1792 through 1887. This is the principal source for the chronology, posts, travel, marriages and opera première years.
- **Museum biography:** Museo Nazionale Rossini, ‘Biography’: https://museonazionalerossini.it/eng/biography/ . Read in full. Supplements the training and identifies Malerbi’s introduction to Mozart and Haydn. Where it differs from the more detailed ROF chronology, dates not needed by this page have been omitted.
- **Cambridge front matter and introduction:** Emanuele Senici, ed., *The Cambridge Companion to Rossini* (2004): https://assets.cambridge.org/052180/7360/frontmatter/0521807360_frontmatter.htm . Confirms publication, contents, the singing/staging/editorial scope, and the preferred spelling ‘Gioachino’. The introduction explains the formative part played by particular singers and the place of ornament in melody.

## Claims deliberately omitted

- The familiar cooking anecdotes, a supposedly exact thirteen-day composition period for *Barber*, cats and other first-night mishaps, and the often-quoted Beethoven conversation: no adequate primary verification attempted; not needed here.
- An exact early date for the *Sei sonate a quattro*: the museum and ROF chronology disagree, so the set is not dated or included in the selected works.
- A single medical diagnosis or aesthetic cause for abandoning opera; an assertion that Rossini never composed after *Tell*.
- The date at which the Passy villa was finished, the orchestral *Petite messe* première day and the 1902 tomb-monument unveiling day, because the consulted institutional accounts differ.
- A five-act *Guillaume Tell* and a six-cello opening, both found in an otherwise useful programme note: excluded in favour of ROF’s four-act description and the note’s own five-solo-cello instrumentation list.
- Precise recording-session dates inferred from modern catalogue issue dates.
