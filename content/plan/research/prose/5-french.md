# French era prose sources

Checked 18 October 2026. The era schema has no `sources` field. Sources below were located by web search and then opened; claims are limited to what the opened pages support. Notation, metadata and poem text were preserved.

## Concerts, teaching and instruments

- [Marc Mandel, Boston Symphony: Prelude to The Afternoon of a Faun](https://www.bso.org/works/prelude-to-the-afternoon-of-a-faun): 22 December 1894 Société Nationale première, Gustave Doret, audience encore; solo-flute opening, harp and horns, absence of trumpets, trombones and timpani; Mallarmé's poem.
- [Marie-Gabrielle Soret, BnF, Société nationale de musique](https://comitehistoire.bnf.fr/dictionnaire-fonds/soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9-nationale-musique): February 1871 foundation by Saint-Saëns, Bussine and colleagues; Fauré's participation; purpose of securing performances of new French works.
- [IRCAM, Gabriel Fauré biography](https://brahms.ircam.fr/fr/composer/gabriel-faure/biography): École Niedermeyer and Saint-Saëns; organist in Rennes in 1866; Conservatoire teaching including Ravel; directorship from 1905.
- [Bru Zane, Danse sacrée et Danse profane](https://www.bruzanemediabase.com/en/exploration/works/danse-sacree-danse-profane-claude-debussy): Pleyel's 1904 commission, two-plane chromatic harp, competition with Érard, strings and harp, performance on pedal harp.
- [Herbert Glass, LA Phil, Introduction et Allegro](https://www.laphil.com/works/introduction-et-allegro): Érard's 1905 commission; flute/clarinet opening theme in thirds and the harp's growing solo role. The page's headline composition date differs from its commission date; the prose claims the latter only.
- [Helen M. Greenwald, Boston Symphony, Mother Goose Suite](https://www.bso.org/works/mother-goose-suite): piano-duet origins; Jeanne Leleu and Geneviève Durony's Paris première in 1910; orchestration; Beast as contrabassoon and Laideronnette's harp, celesta and glockenspiel.
- [BnF, Concert champêtre, FP 49](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark%3A/12148/cb13917056n): dedication to Landowska; Paris première, 3 May 1929, Landowska and Monteux.
- [Meg Ryan, LA Phil, Piano Concerto in G](https://www.laphil.com/works/piano-concerto-in-g): completion in 1931, opening whip-crack and jazz-inflected piano/wind writing. No claim adopted from the page's questionable tour-date summary.

## Notes, poems and pictures

- [Open Music Theory, Collections](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/collections/): pentatonic and whole-tone intervals. The C-based spellings and planing examples are elementary interval descriptions, also directly checkable against the unchanged ABC schematics.
- [Open Music Theory, Diatonic Modes](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheorycopy/chapter/diatonic-modes/): Lydian raised fourth and Mixolydian lowered seventh.
- [La cathédrale engloutie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_cath%C3%A9drale_engloutie): open fifths at the opening, C-major chordal statement and lower, quieter return; the drowned-cathedral legend. Corroborates the score-based cues already in the local Hall file.
- [Gerald Larner, Fauré's Clair de lune](https://geraldlarner.com/work/faure-clair-de-lune-op-46-no-2-1887): piano minuet, Verlaine's masked figures, independent speech-inflected vocal line.
- [Oxford Song, Clair de lune](https://oxfordsong.org/song/clair-de-lune-2): original French poem, especially the masks, minor-mode song of conquering love, and disbelief in happiness. No copyrighted English translation copied.
- [Mutopia, Satie Gymnopédie No. 1](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=37) and [LilyPond source](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/SatieE/gymnopedie_1/gymnopedie_1.ly): 1888; bass G/D on beat one, chords on beat two, four-bar introduction, return with changed ending and final D minor/F natural. The original writes out the return; Mutopia uses repeat signs.
- [Music in the Round, Gaspard de la nuit](https://musicintheround.co.uk/programmes/ravel-maurice-gaspard-de-la-nuit-2/): 1908, Bertrand's poems, Le Gibet's continuous B-flat octaves and Scarbo's repeated notes/goblin.
- [Grant Hiroshima, LA Phil, Gaspard de la nuit](https://www.laphil.com/works/gaspard-de-la-nuit): Le Gibet's three-stave notation and its tolling bell; Scarbo's leaps. The chapter's nightmare/pianist comparison is an authorial image.
- [Sylvia Parker, Claude Debussy's Gamelan, College Music Symposium 52 (2012)](https://symposium.music.org/index.php?id=6365&option=com_content&view=article): repeated visits to the Dutch section's Javanese village at the 1889 Exposition; dancers/gongs/tuned percussion and distinct tunings; Pagodes (1903), low B/F-sharp, pedal-sustained bass and simultaneous patterns at different speeds. Generalisations about an entire Javanese society in this article are not adopted.
- [Sibley Music Library, La Mer first-edition cover](https://www.esm.rochester.edu/sibley/la-mer2/): Durand's 1905 edition, Hokusai detail, omission of Fuji and retention of the wave.
- [Boulanger Initiative, Founding Story](https://www.boulangerinitiative.org/founding-story): first woman to win the composition Grand Prix in 1913.
- [Bru Zane, Faust et Hélène](https://www.bruzanemediabase.com/exploration/oeuvres/faust-helene-adenis-boulanger): winning cantata and 1913 date.
- [V&A, Parade 100](https://www.vam.ac.uk/event/nXLk4ray/parade-100): Paris première on 18 May 1917; Picasso, Satie and Massine collaboration. [IMSLP work catalogue](https://imslp.org/wiki/Parade_(Satie,_Erik)): Cocteau scenario, première and typewriter/sirens/pistol instrumentation. No claim about who first proposed the sound effects.
- [Peter Laki, Cleveland Orchestra, Ravel's Boléro](https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/posts/ravels-bolero): two alternating tunes, unbroken snare-drum pattern, accumulating orchestration. Ravel's quotation, “It was a factory which inspired my Boléro”, is attributed there to his 1932 London Evening Standard interview. Quotation retained with those particulars.
- [Kit Soden, Timbre and Orchestration Resource, Boléro](https://timbreandorchestration.org/learn-about-orchestration/scores-and-recordings/ravel/bolero): flute plus muted trumpet at the sixth melody statement. Used for the Seurat pairing, which remains labelled `editorial-comparison` in the data.
- [National Gallery, Bathers at Asnières](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/georges-seurat-bathers-at-asnieres): Seine bathers, distant factories, studies, Salon rejection in 1884, later yellow and blue dots on orange hat. The complete painting is not presented as a pointillist canvas.
- [Verlaine, Art poétique, in Jadis et naguère](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Jadis_et_nagu%C3%A8re_%281891%29/Jadis/Art_Po%C3%A9tique): original stanzas and collection date; nine-syllable lines can be counted directly. Existing original and translation left untouched.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 by a second reader. Every source below was opened and read; the quotation and each dated, placed or counted claim in `content/eras/5-french.mjs` was matched against it. `node tools/validate.mjs content/eras/5-french.mjs` prints `ok`.

### Changes

- Boléro paragraph: “he explained the scene he really wanted behind the dancers: “It was a factory which inspired my *Boléro*.”” → “he said which scene he wanted behind it: “It was a factory which inspired my *Boléro*. I would like it always to be played with a vast factory in the background.”” The first quoted sentence says what inspired the piece; the backdrop is named in the next sentence of the interview, and Ravel’s remark does not mention dancers. Source: [Peter Laki, Cleveland Orchestra](https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/posts/ravels-bolero), quoting the 1932 *London Evening Standard* interview.

### Confirmed quotation

- Ravel, 1932, *London Evening Standard*: “It was a factory which inspired my Boléro.” and “I would like it always to be played with a vast factory in the background.” are consecutive sentences in the interview as quoted by [Laki](https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/posts/ravels-bolero).

### Confirmed facts

- *Prélude à l’après-midi d’un faune*: first performed 22 December 1894 at the Société Nationale, Gustave Doret conducting; the piece was encored; trumpets, trombones and timpani absent ([BSO](https://www.bso.org/works/prelude-to-the-afternoon-of-a-faun)).
- Société Nationale de Musique created 25 February 1871 on the initiative of Saint-Saëns, Bussine and a group of young composers; Fauré among the early committee members ([BnF](https://comitehistoire.bnf.fr/dictionnaire-fonds/soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9-nationale-musique)).
- Fauré: Niedermeyer school and Saint-Saëns; organist at Rennes in 1866; teacher of Ravel at the Conservatoire; its director from 1905 ([IRCAM](https://brahms.ircam.fr/fr/composer/gabriel-faure/biography)).
- *Danse sacrée et danse profane*: April–May 1904, commissioned by Pleyel for its chromatic harp (two planes of strings, no pedals), a rival to Érard’s pedal harp; playable on a pedal harp ([Bru Zane](https://www.bruzanemediabase.com/en/exploration/works/danse-sacree-danse-profane-claude-debussy)).
- *Introduction et Allegro*: commissioned in 1905 by Érard; whole piece built on a theme in thirds stated by flute and clarinet, which the harp embellishes before taking centre stage ([LA Phil](https://www.laphil.com/works/introduction-et-allegro)).
- *Ma mère l’Oye*: four-hand original 1908–10; premiered by Conservatoire students Jeanne Leleu and Geneviève Durony on 20 April 1910 at the Société Musicale Indépendante, Paris; Beast as contrabassoon; Laideronnette’s court mimicked by harp, celesta, glockenspiel, piccolo and flute ([BSO, Helen Greenwald](https://www.bso.org/works/mother-goose-suite)).
- *Concert champêtre*: first performance Paris, 3 May 1929, Landowska and the Orchestre symphonique de Paris under Monteux ([BnF](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark%3A/12148/cb13917056n)).
- Piano Concerto in G: completed 1931; opens with the crack of a whip; blue notes and jazz inflection ([LA Phil](https://www.laphil.com/works/piano-concerto-in-g)).
- First *Gymnopédie* (1888), read in the [Mutopia LilyPond source](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/SatieE/gymnopedie_1/gymnopedie_1.ly): G then D in the bass with chords on beat two; four bars of rest in the melody staff, so the tune enters on the second beat of bar 5; the first ending closes on D major with F♯, the second on D minor with F♮. The source notes that the original writes the repeat out.
- *Gaspard de la nuit* (1908): *Le Gibet* has a continuous B♭ in octaves, notated on three staves; *Scarbo* has repeated notes and a goblin ([Music in the Round](https://musicintheround.co.uk/programmes/ravel-maurice-gaspard-de-la-nuit-2/); [LA Phil](https://www.laphil.com/works/gaspard-de-la-nuit)).
- Fauré’s *Clair de lune*: modal minuet in the piano, masked figures, a vocal line shaped by the inflections of the words ([Gerald Larner](https://geraldlarner.com/work/faure-clair-de-lune-op-46-no-2-1887)); Verlaine’s “Tout en chantant sur le mode mineur / L’amour vainqueur… Ils n’ont pas l’air de croire à leur bonheur” ([Oxford Song](https://oxfordsong.org/song/clair-de-lune-2)).
- Debussy at the 1889 Exposition: returned again and again to the Javanese kampong of the Dutch section; *Pagodes* (1903) opens on low B–F♯ blurred by pedal, evoking a gong, with tied bass notes sustained by pedal ([Parker, College Music Symposium 52](https://symposium.music.org/index.php?id=6365&option=com_content&view=article)).
- *La Mer* first edition (Durand, 1905): cover uses the left half of Hokusai’s *Great Wave*, with Mount Fuji not shown ([Sibley Music Library](https://www.esm.rochester.edu/sibley/la-mer2/)).
- Lili Boulanger: first woman to win the Prix de Rome composition prize, 1913, with *Faust et Hélène* ([Boulanger Initiative](https://www.boulangerinitiative.org/founding-story); [Bru Zane](https://www.bruzanemediabase.com/exploration/oeuvres/faust-helene-adenis-boulanger)).
- *Parade*: premiered in Paris 18 May 1917, Picasso designs, Massine choreography ([V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/event/nXLk4ray/parade-100)); typewriter, sirens and pistol in the percussion list ([IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Parade_(Satie,_Erik))).
- *Boléro*: two alternating melodic forms over an unbroken snare-drum rhythm; flute with muted trumpet at the sixth statement ([Laki](https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/posts/ravels-bolero); [Timbre and Orchestration Resource](https://timbreandorchestration.org/learn-about-orchestration/scores-and-recordings/ravel/bolero)).
- Seurat, *Bathers at Asnières*: men and boys on the Seine, chimneys of Clichy factories behind; extensive preparatory studies; rejected by the 1884 Salon; the orange hat reworked later with spots of yellow and blue ([National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/georges-seurat-bathers-at-asnieres)); retouching dated around 1886–87 in [WebExhibits](https://www.webexhibits.org/colorart/pointillism.html), which supports the unchanged `date` field.
- Verlaine, *Art poétique*: written April 1874 at Mons; first printed in *Paris moderne* in 1882, collected in *Jadis et naguère* in 1884; nine-syllable lines; stanzas 1 and 4 match the original field ([French Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Jadis_et_nagu%C3%A8re_%281891%29/Jadis/Art_Po%C3%A9tique); [fleche.org](http://fleche.org/stg/verlaine_art_poetique.html); [French Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_po%C3%A9tique_(Verlaine))).
