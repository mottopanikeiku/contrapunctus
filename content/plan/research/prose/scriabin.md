## Removed or qualified

- Removed confident diagnostic claims about synaesthesia; the score’s colour instructions are directly documented and sufficient.
- Removed the end-year of the professorship because the consulted sources conflict (1903/1904).
- Avoided turning the proposed Mysterium into an accomplished composition or assigning a date to the undated Sabaneev conversation.
- Both retained score examples are explicitly schematic, with no false bar attribution.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 30 claim groups: all 27 ledger entries above, the portrait date, and both schematic score captions. This includes the five distinct quoted fragments (the notebook’s “I am god” is also repeated in a heading), work dates/catalogue numbers/forces, musical descriptions, artwork data and recording particulars. Repeated facts across the page were checked together.

### Changes

- “Both graduated as pianists in 1892” → Scriabin graduated in piano in 1892, one year after Rachmaninoff. Rachmaninoff’s 1892 graduation was in composition; his piano diploma was awarded in 1891. [Moscow Conservatory on Scriabin](https://mosconsv.ru/ru/person/123586); [Boosey & Hawkes on Rachmaninoff](https://www.boosey.com/pages/Opera/composer/composer_main?composerid=2861&ttype=BIOGRAPHY).
- “Fire in two staves” / “adds two staves for light” → two lines on **one stave**. The printed *luce* part has two voices, distinguished by stem direction, not two staves. [Gawboy and Townsend, paragraph 13 and Example 2](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.12.18.2/mto.12.18.2.gawboy_townsend.html).
- Scriabin explaining his colours to Myers “in 1915” → **1914**. The primary article opens by locating the examination during Scriabin’s recent visit to England and was published in May 1914. Its page 113 supplies the quoted words. The 1915 date in the secondary article was not followed. [Myers, original scan, pp. 112–113](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/at-the-crossroads-of-the-senses/media/Myers_1914.pdf); [publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8295.1914.tb00246.x); [Baker’s biography on the 1914 London visit](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/scriabin-alexander-nikolaievich).
- Delville’s “friendship” with Scriabin → “collaboration”. The fetched scholarly analysis cited by the writer does not establish that relationship; the cover and contemporary account establish their artistic collaboration and shared Theosophical circle. [Engel, chapter VI, indexed passage](https://www.scriabin-association.com/yulii-engel-biography-of-scriabin-chapter-vi/); [surviving published cover](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Prometheus,_The_Poem_of_Fire,_Op._60,_by_Alexander_Scriabin,_cover,_1911,_from_Boston_Symphony_Orchestra_collection.jpg).

### Quotations confirmed

- “I am, and there is nothing outside me”: Scriabin’s private philosophical notebook, 1904, in Marina Frolova-Walker’s published translation. [Gresham lecture transcript, 20 May 2021](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2021-05-20-1800_FROLOVAWALKER-Scriabin-T.pdf).
- “I am god”: the same notebook passage and translation, also used for the section heading. [Frolova-Walker](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2021-05-20-1800_FROLOVAWALKER-Scriabin-T.pdf).
- “The colour underlines the tonality”: Scriabin to Charles S. Myers, during the 1914 English visit, printed on page 113 of Myers’s article. The original scan was fetched and read visually. [Myers](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/at-the-crossroads-of-the-senses/media/Myers_1914.pdf).
- “makes the tonality more evident”: the continuation of that same sentence, with its wording preserved. [Myers, p. 113](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/at-the-crossroads-of-the-senses/media/Myers_1914.pdf).
- “a whole sonata of insects”: Scriabin on the Tenth Sonata, reported by Leonid Sabaneev and quoted in Frolova-Walker’s translation. The memoir’s 1925 publication is confirmed by the scholarly bibliography; the page does not invent a date for the conversation. [Frolova-Walker](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2021-05-20-1800_FROLOVAWALKER-Scriabin-T.pdf); [Gawboy and Townsend, Sabaneev 2000 [1925]](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.12.18.2/mto.12.18.2.gawboy_townsend.html).

### Second independent pass (19 October 2026)

Re-read the page against sources fetched afresh; the first pass’s corrections were confirmed in place. One further change:

- “Alexander Mozer had built a small apparatus for Scriabin, but the equipment for the concert could not do what he wanted” → “…but Scriabin withdrew it from the concert because of technical difficulties”. The article states that Scriabin “ultimately decided to withdraw the instrument from the public premiere due to unspecified ‘technical difficulties’” (Sabaneev 1912); the claim that the concert’s equipment “could not do what he wanted” was an inference. The later remark that Mozer’s instrument would lose its impact in a large hall (paragraph 60) concerns the apparatus, not the evening’s equipment. [Gawboy and Townsend, paragraphs 6 and 60](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.12.18.2/mto.12.18.2.gawboy_townsend.html).

Re-verified or newly verified:

- Myers quotation, now read in the article’s own text layer: “Scriabin explains that ‘the colour underlines the tonality; it makes the tonality more evident.’” Myers opens with “During his recent visit to England”; the quotation is p. 113 in Part I. [Myers, scan with text layer](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/at-the-crossroads-of-the-senses/media/Myers_1914.pdf).
- “a whole sonata of insects”: in Sabaneev’s report of Scriabin on the Tenth Sonata, in the sentence ending “as in the Tenth Sonata – a whole sonata of insects…”; the notebook passage “I am, and there is nothing outside me … I am god” is introduced as “his private notes from 1904”; the First Sonata’s funeral march is linked in his notebook to “the first serious misfortune” at twenty. [Frolova-Walker, Gresham lecture transcript](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2021-05-20-1800_FROLOVAWALKER-Scriabin-T.pdf).
- Op. 11: Scriabin appended a date and place to each prelude; No. 5 was written at Amsterdam (1896), No. 14 at Dresden, Nos. 17, 18 and 23 at Vitznau on Lake Lucerne (1895); the key plan is Chopin’s, divided into four parts of six; No. 16 alternates 5/8 and 4/8; No. 19’s left-hand figures each start on the last semiquaver of the beat. [Nicholls, Hyperion Op. 11 notes](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dw.asp?dc=W9883_67057).
- The first theme of *Prometheus* is played by the horns at the opening (“first theme, played by the horns at the opening of the blue-violet Color Stage I”); soft mystic chord marked “smoky”; optional chorus on vowels and the final F♯ major chord. [Gawboy and Townsend, paragraph 49](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.12.18.2/mto.12.18.2.gawboy_townsend.html); [LA Phil notes](https://www.laphil.com/works/prometheus-poem-of-fire-op-60). Première 2 March 1911 (Old Style; 15 March New Style) under Koussevitzky: [Wikipedia, Prometheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus:_The_Poem_of_Fire).
- Early life: cadet corps from 1882, Zverev from 1885, Conservatory in 1888, graduation in 1892 with the small gold medal in Safonov’s class; mother Lyubov a pianist trained under Leschetizky who died when he was one; father a diplomat; aunt Lyubov Alexandrovna. [Moscow Conservatory biography](https://mosconsv.ru/ru/person/123586); [Croatian Scriabin Society](https://croatianscriabinsociety.com/en/biography/).
- *Poem of Ecstasy*: 8 horns, 5 trumpets, organ, bells; first heard in New York in December 1908 under Altschuler: [LA Phil notes](https://www.laphil.com/works/poem-of-ecstasy-op-54) (Altschuler and the Russian Symphony Orchestra of New York; “two years later”, i.e. after the abandoned 1906 visit).
- *Black Mass* nickname: coined by the pianist and Theosophist Alexei Podgaetsky, approved by Scriabin; the White Mass nickname for No. 7 was his own. Late sonatas 8–10 written in 1912–13 at Petrovskoye. [Nicholls, Hyperion complete-sonata notes](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dc.asp?dc=D_CDA67131%2F2); [Wikipedia, Sonata No. 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._9_(Scriabin)).
