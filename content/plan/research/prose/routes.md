# Listening routes — prose source record

This record supports the titles, introductions and per-recording notes in `content/routes.mjs`. The schema has no sources array. All route ids, recording ids and their order are unchanged. The linked sources below were found through web searches and opened and read; the existing listening-page evidence and recording metadata were also consulted. No words in the route prose purport to transcribe spoken conversation.

## Returns

- **Satie, First Gymnopédie:** [Mutopia edition and source information](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=37); the [complete LilyPond score](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/SatieE/gymnopedie_1/gymnopedie_1.ly) was read. The bass G/D falls on beat one, the accompanying chord on beat two. The first ending contains F♯ and closes in D major; the second replaces it with F♮ and closes in D minor. The route describes the returning opening and changed ending, avoiding the old loose claim that the whole piece is simply played twice.
- **Beethoven, Fifth Symphony, first movement:** [Mutopia's edition from Breitkopf & Härtel](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=941) is the score cited by the existing listening page, whose mapped exposition repeat is retained. [Indianapolis Symphony, programme note by Marianne Williams Tobias](https://www.indianapolissymphony.org/backstage/program-notes/beethoven-symphony-no-5/) explicitly places the oboe cadenza within the recapitulation. [LA Phil's note on Bernstein's Divertimento](https://www.laphil.com/works/divertimento) independently identifies the quoted oboe cadenza as coming from this movement. The route says the later return is interrupted by a lone oboe. Gerald Larner's otherwise useful account places the cadenza before the recapitulation; that placement was not adopted.
- **Vivaldi, Spring, first movement:** [José Rodríguez Alvira, Teoría, score-led analysis](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2021/vivaldi-seasons/primavera/01.php) follows the same John Harrison/Wichita recording used by the listening page. Supports the returning orchestral ritornello, the solo violins' birdsong and the thunderstorm episode. The short route and returns route describe different aspects of these same score events.
- **Haydn, Symphony No. 94, Andante:** [LA Phil programme note](https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-94-surprise) describes the simple string tune, its softer repetition and surprise chord, four variations and the orchestral coda. C major is established by the existing score and listening-page evidence. The new note retains the variation count and contrasts the quiet opening strings with the later orchestral sound.
- **Mozart, Symphony No. 40, first movement:** [ABC Classic, Sonata form and Mozart's Symphony No. 40](https://www.abc.net.au/listen/classic/read-and-watch/music-reads/mozart-symphony-no-40-sonata-form/10160386) identifies the second theme's relative-major presentation and G-minor recapitulation. For a G-minor movement that relative major is B♭ major. This is the specific change of key described in the route.

## Independent voices

- **Bach, B-minor fugue, Well-Tempered Clavier I:** [Vantage, Analysis: Unpacking the B-minor Fugue from WTC 1](https://www.vantagemusic.org/magazine/analysis-unpacking-the-b-minor-fugue-from-wtc-1/) and [Mutopia's Bach-Gesellschaft edition](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=1563), also cited by the listening page. The existing four-voice account is retained; the new note describes a pianist maintaining their separate lines. It makes no new claim about the entry order or precise pitches of the subject.
- **Couperin, La Piémontoise:** [Johan van Veen, MusicWeb review of Les Nations](https://www.musicweb-international.com/classrev/2015/Jul/Couperin_nations_8573347.htm) describes two treble parts with basso continuo and the original part-books' two upper parts, bowed bass and figured bass. [Early Music Review](https://earlymusicreview.com/francois-couperin-les-nations-1726/) also explains the trio-sonata scoring and flexible instrumental choices. The site's selected performance, documented in `content/listen/couperin-nations-piemontaise.mjs`, has four players. The route's three parts mean the two upper lines and their shared bass; it does not claim that the original publication consisted of only three part-books or that every performance uses four people.
- **Bach, Brandenburg Concerto No. 5:** [Netherlands Bach Society, BWV 1050](https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-1050) documents the harpsichord's emergence from accompaniment into a solo role and the flute/violin/keyboard ensemble. Its [comparison with the earlier version](https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-1050a) explicitly discusses the final version's extended cadenza. The route describes the exchange between soloists and the keyboard's eventual solo; it does not confuse the two versions or borrow a duration from the shorter one.
- **Brahms, Fourth Symphony, finale:** [Kelly Dean Hansen, detailed Op. 98 guide](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus98.html) identifies the thirty eight-bar variations and traces their changing instrumentation. The route's count and pattern length were already present and are retained. “Grow” is an editorial description of their development from the common pattern.
- **Handel, Hallelujah:** [National Arts Centre, Messiah programme notes](https://nac-cna.ca/en/event/notes/36096) discusses the chorus's homophonic opening and imitative treatment of “And He shall reign”. The existing listening page and its linked four-part score support soprano, alto, tenor and bass. The two route notes describe this alternation and the choir's exuberance. No claim about George II standing is included.

## Instrumental colour

- **Ravel, Boléro:** [Peter Laki, Cleveland Orchestra programme note](https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/posts/ravels-bolero) distinguishes the two recurring melodic strains and describes the continuous drum rhythm and accumulating orchestration. The old route's “one melody” was inaccurate and is corrected to two. No quarter-hour claim is attached to a recording with its own tempo.
- **Mahler, Fifth Symphony, Adagietto:** [Mahler Foundation's movement account](https://mahlerfoundation.org/mahler/compositions/symphony-no-5/symphony-no-5-movement-4-adagietto-sehr-langsam/) confirms the strings-and-harp scoring. Bowed phrases and plucked harp notes describe those instruments; no love-letter or funeral story is asserted.
- **Debussy, La cathédrale engloutie:** [work account and analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_cath%C3%A9drale_engloutie) describes the submerged-cathedral legend, bell sonorities, great organ passage and quiet return. The route gives the piano's succession of sounds without claiming that the piece contains an actual organ or recorded water. The French era source record documents the same passage.
- **Rimsky-Korsakov, Scheherazade, third movement:** [Paul Serotsky, MusicWeb programme note](https://www.musicweb-international.com/Programme_Notes/rimsky_scheheraz.htm), section “The Young Prince and the Young Princess”, describes the main string tune, its clarinet-led counterpart and the snare-drum pulse accompanying the quicker dance. The route describes these successive sounds and removes the old generic compliment about a teacher displaying his craft.

## Short recordings

- **Scriabin, Étude Op. 8 No. 12:** [work account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tude_in_D-sharp_minor%2C_Op._8%2C_No._12) supports the right-hand octave melody and the left hand's large jumps. No unverifiable account of Scriabin's feelings accompanies this physical description of the piano writing.
- **Elgar, Nimrod:** [Elgar Society's account of the Enigma Variations](https://www.elgar.org/3enigma.htm) identifies August Jaeger and describes the encouragement he gave Elgar when the composer was discouraged. The route calls this an orchestral portrait of that friend, without adding a fabricated conversation or an unsourced quotation.
- **Satie, Handel and Vivaldi:** sources above support the shorter notes as well as the notes in their other routes.
- **Verdi, La traviata, Prelude:** [Eagle Mountain Symphony, A Night at the Opera programme, 31 March 2023](https://eaglemountainsymphony.org/concerts/2023March/program.pdf), page 11, describes the very soft high strings, the main love theme, and its transfer to lower strings beneath the upper parts' decoration. The route describes that succession. Attempts to open Metropolitan Opera programme PDFs returned 404s; those inaccessible documents are not evidence for the note.
- **Rachmaninoff, Prelude in C♯ minor:** [Wikimedia Commons recording record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ASergei_Rachmaninoff_performs_Rachmaninoff%27s_Prelude_in_C_sharp_minor%2C_Op._3.ogg) identifies Rachmaninoff as pianist, the 1919 recording and its December 1920 release. The route retains 1919 as the recording date, not the publication date.

The short route retains the existing selection of complete pieces or movements and its under-four-minute premise; none of the referenced recordings was replaced. The new notes fill the six previously empty per-recording descriptions. The original instructions to listen or follow a line have been removed. No unsupported historical claims remain knowingly unresolved.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 by a second reader. Each source below was opened and read; every claim in the route titles, introductions and per-recording notes of `content/routes.mjs` was matched against a source, a score or the recording metadata. No change was needed. The file was not edited.

### Confirmed facts

- Satie, First *Gymnopédie*: bass G on beat one with the chord on beat two; the opening returns note for note until the closing bars; the first ending has F♯ and closes on D major, the second has F♮ and closes on D minor ([Mutopia LilyPond source](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/SatieE/gymnopedie_1/gymnopedie_1.ly)).
- Beethoven Fifth, first movement: the exposition repeat is written in the part files (`\repeat volta 2` in the [Mutopia LilyPond source](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/BeethovenLv/O67/Symphony5_1/Symphony5_1-lys.zip)); “within a traditional recapitulation” an expressive oboe cadenza stops the action ([Indianapolis Symphony](https://www.indianapolissymphony.org/backstage/program-notes/beethoven-symphony-no-5/)).
- Vivaldi, *Spring*, Allegro: returning orchestral ritornello, solo-violin birdsong, thunderstorm episode ([Teoría, Rodríguez Alvira](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2021/vivaldi-seasons/primavera/01.php)).
- Haydn 94, Andante: nursery-tune simplicity, gentle strings, “four ensuing variations”, orchestral coda; C major ([LA Phil](https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-94-surprise)).
- Mozart 40, first movement: second theme in the relative major (B♭), recapitulation in G minor ([ABC Classic](https://www.abc.net.au/listen/classic/read-and-watch/music-reads/mozart-symphony-no-40-sonata-form/10160386)).
- Bach B-minor Fugue, WTC I: four voices entering alto, tenor, bass, soprano, from the bar-level notes of the local listen file `content/listen/bach-wtc1-fugue-b-minor.mjs`.
- Couperin, *La Piémontoise*: the selected recording has four players (violin, flute, viola da gamba, keyboard) making two upper parts over a shared bass, from the recording metadata in the cached recordings data.
- Brandenburg 5, first movement: soloists and ensemble alternate, and the harpsichord “emerges as a soloist rather than an accompanying instrument” ([Netherlands Bach Society](https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-1050)). Brahms 4 finale: “30 continuous variations on an eight-bar stepwise rising theme” ([Kelly Dean Hansen](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus98.html)). Handel, *Messiah* choruses: textures move from unison declarations to layered counterpoint and chordal statements ([NAC programme note](https://nac-cna.ca/en/event/notes/36096)).
- Ravel, *Boléro*: two alternating tunes, unbroken snare-drum rhythm, accumulating orchestration ([Laki, Cleveland Orchestra](https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/posts/ravels-bolero)).
- Debussy, *La cathédrale engloutie*: open fifths, a C-major “organ” over a low C, a softer return lower down, per the score-based cues in the local listen file (Centenary Edition bars 1–6, 22–41, 72–83).
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Scheherazade*, third movement: main theme in flowing strings, rippling clarinet counter-subject, a central allegretto dance “where the snare-drum part is played on more than the snare-drum” ([Paul Serotsky, MusicWeb](https://www.musicweb-international.com/Programme_Notes/rimsky_scheheraz.htm)).
- Verdi, *La traviata* Prelude: very soft high strings, then the love theme on lower strings while the higher instruments decorate it ([Eagle Mountain Symphony notes](https://eaglemountainsymphony.org/concerts/2023March/program.pdf)).
- “Four minutes to spare”: stream durations in the cached recordings data are Scriabin 124 s, Elgar 174 s, Satie 184 s, Handel 201 s, Vivaldi 215 s, Verdi 224 s and Rachmaninoff 228 s, so every recording is under four minutes. Rachmaninoff’s recording session is dated 24 April 1919 in the same data.
- Elgar, “Nimrod”: portrait of August Jaeger, who encouraged Elgar when he was discouraged ([Elgar Society](https://www.elgar.org/3enigma.htm)).
