# Philip Glass — quotation and fact sources

Checked 19 October 2026. This ledger supports `content/composers/glass.mjs`, `content/bibliography/glass.json`, `content/geo/glass.json`, `content/elsewhere/glass.json` and `content/influence/glass.json`. Glass is living; the site hosts no recording of his music (every recording is under copyright), so there are no listen files or dossiers. His music is carried by the `elsewhere` file.

Abbreviations: **Page/Two Pages** = Tim Page, notes to *Two Pages / Contrary Motion / Music in Fifths / Music in Similar Motion* (Nonesuch 79326-2, 1994), https://philipglass.com/recordings/two_pages/ ; **Page/Einstein 1993** = Tim Page, notes to the Nonesuch *Einstein on the Beach* (1993), https://philipglass.com/recordings/einstein_on_the_beach_none/ ; **Page/LoC** = Tim Page, essay for the Library of Congress National Recording Registry (2012), https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/EinsteinOnTheBeach.pdf ; **FA 1999** = Fresh Air, 21 September 1999, Ira Glass interviews Philip Glass (recorded 7 September 1999, Field Museum, Chicago), https://freshairarchive.org/segments/ira-glass-interviews-philip-glass ; **FA 2015** = Fresh Air, 6 April 2015, https://freshairarchive.org/segments/philip-glass-legacy-future-its-all-around-us-0 ; **NEA** = https://www.arts.gov/honors/opera/philip-glass ; **PG** = pages on philipglass.com (official site), URLs given.

## Fact-check (independent)

Second reader, 19 October 2026. Every source below was fetched and read in this pass unless marked “not opened”.

**Sources re-read.** The NEA biography; both Fresh Air transcripts; Tim Page’s notes to *Two Pages* and to the 1993 *Einstein* on philipglass.com and his Library of Congress essay (PDF); *The New Yorker* of 6 December 1976 and Alex Ross’s “Sound and Vision”; the San Francisco Classical Voice outline; the BSO note on *Harmonium*; the *Los Angeles Times* piece on Jennifer Koh; the Staatliche Museen record ÄM 14145; philipglass.com pages for Music in Twelve Parts, Einstein (composition and CBS recording), Satyagraha, Akhnaten (composition, 1987 recording, Met recording), Glassworks (composition and recording), Façades, Koyaanisqatsi (film, composition, 1983 and 2009 recordings), The Thin Blue Line, String Quartets Nos. 1–9, Kronos album, Songs from Liquid Days, Symphonies Nos. 1, 4 and 15, Études, Kundun, The Hours, biography and news; Commons API records for the four images; Nominatim for four coordinates; Open Library’s JSON for the books; oEmbed and watch-page descriptions for the four YouTube uploads; Spotify oEmbed for the four albums; the four Apple Music pages.

**Verified.** The life, works, geo and influence files against these sources, including every quotation (Boulanger, “adding instead of dividing”, the Page quotations on additive process, cycles, functional harmony and Similar Motion, Eno, Wilson’s knee-play definition, Hermann, Morris, Ross, Gramophone, “a more general audience”, “maintaining the same general melodic configuration”) and the Shankar session account (about sixteen French musicians, no bar lines, Stockhausen and Cage, a session three or four days off). Oscar nominations for *Kundun*, *The Hours* and *Notes on a Scandal*: the Glass website’s news item of 9 October 2026 and the English Wikipedia article on Glass; oscars.org returned HTTP 403 and was not opened. Symphony No. 15’s première (5 July 2026) is on the composition page and in the news item of 6 July 2026.

**Corrected.**

1. Jane Hermann: *The New Yorker* says she “has had a large hand in bringing ‘Einstein’ to the house”, and credits Jean Rigg’s urging. “Who suggested taking it to the Met” replaced.
2. *Koyaanisqatsi* works note, “made between 1975 and 1982”: no opened source gives that range. Ross: shot in the 1970s, released 1983. Reworded.
3. First *Einstein* recording: Page calls it the 1978 recording; the philipglass.com CBS page (M4K 38875), Apple and the YouTube ℗ line say 1979. The entry now reads 1978–79 and says so.
4. Caption of the third ABC example said all four voices move in the same direction; the bass line is two held notes. Replaced by what the notation shows (the three upper voices play one figure at three pitches). All three examples are labelled invented schematics and their bar lengths add up.

**Kept, with the evidence.** Ensemble founding date: Page and the Glassworks page give 1968; the biography page says formed on his return in 1967; Glass in 2015, “maybe in ’69”; the NEA, 1971. The chapter follows Page. Quartet No. 2: premiere 1983 and heading 1984 on the composition page; the chapter says 1983. *Strung Out* and the Symphony No. 15 details rest on the Glass website only.

**Not opened.** oscars.org (403); the Met’s own pages; nonesuch.com (403).
