## Facts and where they come from

- Birth, parents, early talent: Library of Congress research guide https://guides.loc.gov/amy-beach (read); MacDowell artist page https://www.macdowell.org/artists/amy-beach (read) for the three waltzes composed at her grandfather’s farm in West Henniker; Chelsea 1871 and Boston 1875 from Gillard (LoC and the Chamber Music Society page say Boston 1875; Britannica says 1870).
- Debut 24 October 1883 at the Boston Music Hall; Boston Symphony debut 28 March 1885 under Gericke; marriage 2 December 1885; Dr Beach’s death 28 June 1910; mother’s death 18 February 1911; sailing 5 September 1911; MacDowell residencies from 1921 (eighteen, Watson studio); Society of American Women Composers; New York winter home from 1930; death by heart failure on 27 December 1944: Gillard; BSO programme book 1896 (debut date and repertory); MacDowell pages. Gillard dates the Society to 1924 and the LoC guide to 1925; the chapter gives no year.
- Mass in E♭, 7 February 1892: Gillard; Ebel; Hale’s 1896 column naming “Mrs. Beach’s Mass, Feb. 7, 1892”.
- *Eilende Wolken*, December 1892, with Damrosch and the New York Symphony Society, sung by Mrs Alves: BSO programme book 1896; Amy Beach Project orchestral works list https://www.amybeach.org/music/works-list/orchestral-works-and-works-with-orchestra/ ; Gillard (2–3 December).
- *Festival Jubilate*, 1 May 1893, Theodore Thomas: Amy Beach Project works list; Gillard.
- “Gaelic” Symphony: first performance at the Friday-afternoon rehearsal, 30 October 1896, and the Saturday-evening concert, 31 October (BSO programme book; Hale: “Saturday night”). Britannica and the Globe give 30 October; the Amy Beach Project gives 31 October. Conductor Emil Paur; scoring, movements, tempo marks and the first-movement opening (sixteen bars of chromatic string passage, then trumpets) from the programme book. The first movement borrowing from *Dark Is the Night*: Britannica https://www.britannica.com/topic/Gaelic-Symphony (read through Chromium). Dedication to Paur appears on the printed full score (IMSLP 27125, title page “Herrn Capellmeister Emil Paur gewidmet”), although the programme book says the manuscript bore none; the chapter does not mention it.
- Violin Sonata, 4 January 1897, Kneisel Quartet’s fourth concert, Association Hall, Mrs Beach assisting (“MS. First time”): BSO programme book 1896–97, concert announcement. The violinist is not named there and the chapter does not name one.
- Piano Concerto, 6–7 April 1900, Beach as soloist, Music Hall; scoring, four movements, dedication to Carreño: BSO programme book 1899–1900 https://archive.org/details/bostonsymphonysu189900bos (read). Gericke’s name is the conductor on the programme’s masthead; Gillard confirms Music Hall and 6 April.
- Piano Quintet, MS dated 14 December 1907, first performed 27 February 1908 by Beach and the Hoffmann String Quartet at Potter Hall, Boston; Brahms theme from the finale; two F♯ major chords: Nigel Simeone’s Hyperion notes https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dw.asp?dc=W21677_68295 (read).
- Hermit thrush pieces, Op. 92, 1921 (published 1922): MacDowell page; Amy Beach Project works list https://www.amybeach.org/music/works-list/ (read). Works-list details (Op. 3 cadenza 1888, Op. 37, Op. 44, Op. 89, Op. 149 premiere at Athens, Georgia, 27 February 1945) come from that page.
- Op. 44 first edition (A. P. Schmidt, 1900; dedicated to the Browning Society of Boston; No. 1 marked Allegro di molto; the layout of the setting): Internet Archive scan https://archive.org/details/per_women-composers-collection_the-years-at-the-spring_1900 (pages viewed as images).
- Colour–key associations: Clarke’s essay (table from the UNH Beach Papers).
- Cabildo libretto by “N. B. Stephens”: Amy Beach Project works list; the full name is not given there and is not printed.
- Recordings: Hyperion page (Ohlsson, Takács, recorded May 2019, release June 2020); Chandos CHAN 8958 https://www.chandos.net/products/catalogue/CHAN%208958 (read: Detroit Symphony, Järvi, 19–20 January 1991, movement timings); Silverstein/Kalish New World Records 1977 from Gillard’s discography.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026. Opened: Clarke’s WAM! essay; Gillard (*Women in World History*, encyclopedia.com); LoC research guide, LoC manuscripts guide and LoC blog; Boston Globe (Weininger); Arts Fuse (Blumhofer); MacDowell pages; DAHA “Transatlantic Debate”; Amy Beach Project works lists; Hyperion CDA68295; Chandos CHAN 8958; the 1896–97 BSO programme book (archive.org full text); Hale’s scrapbook OCR (archive.org); Wikipedia pages on the Gaelic Symphony and Amy Beach (pointers only).

**Corrected**

- *The mother and the keyboard.* The page said she “would not let her near the keyboard before she was six”. Clarke and Gillard say she would not *teach* her until six; the same essay has her coaxing to play before she could reach the keys, and the page itself has her playing three waltzes at four. Now: “would not teach her the piano until she was six.”
- *The Mass.* “The first mass by a woman it had performed” → “the first mass composed by an American woman” (Gillard, twice; LoC says only “first notable success”).

**Verified as written**

- Quotations: “I was not allowed to climb up on her lap…” and “I was happy and Dr. Beach was content” (Clarke, notes 2 and 5, quoting Beach); “My work has always been judged…” (Clarke, note 17); Dvořák’s “great and noble school of music”, New York *Herald*, Sunday 21 May 1893 (DAHA); “the dark, tragic, side!” from the December 1893 journal entry (Globe); “We of the North … should be far more likely to be influenced by the old English, Scotch, or Irish songs…”, told to the Boston *Herald* (Arts Fuse; the page paraphrases “We of the North” as “Northerners”; the *Herald* article’s date is not given by either source); Hale on the symphony, word for word in his scrapbook (“The only trace of woman that I find in this symphony is this same boisterousness”; context: he had just called her boisterousness “healthy, not merely vulgar”); Hale on the Concerto, “a disappointment in nearly every way” (Gillard, citing the *Boston Journal*); “one of the boys” (Clarke: Chadwick wrote it to her after the première); Sonneck’s letter of 7 January 1911 and the “Service in A” (LoC blog); Pfohl and the flying-machine remark (Gillard; originals not seen); the programme-book quotation about the “Keltic closing cadence”, which is in the *third* movement, Lento con molto espressione in E minor, 6-4 time, so the schematic in E minor and 6/4 is consistent; the programme book’s scoring (2 flutes … a set of 3 kettledrums), the sixteen bars of tremulous chromatic strings and the first theme “first in the trumpets”, and the folk-like counter-theme worked together with the first theme in the slow movement.
- Facts: birth, parents, Chelsea 1871 and Boston 1875, Perabo, Baermann, Hill, self-taught orchestration and translation of Berlioz and Gevaert, 24 October 1883 debut, 28 March 1885 under Gericke, marriage 2 December 1885 (Dr Beach, surgeon, widowed in 1880, 24 years older; fees to charity; fewer performances), Mass 7 February 1892 under Zerrahn, *Eilende Wolken* with C. Katie Alves, 2–3 December 1892, *Festival Jubilate* first performed 1 May 1893, Dr Beach’s fall and death on 28 June 1910, mother’s death 18 February 1911, sailing 5 September 1911, German tour from autumn 1912 with the symphony and concerto heard in Leipzig and Berlin (LoC), Craft, return in 1914, MacDowell (18 residencies from 1921, Watson studio, “Aunt Amy”, copyright to the Colony, the first to transcribe the thrush), SAWC first president (1924 per Gillard, 1925 per LoC; the page gives no year), New York from 1930, *Cabildo*, death of heart failure; the Centerville cottage bought with the proceeds of “Ecstasy” (Gillard); Violin Sonata premiered with Franz Kneisel (LoC manuscripts page). Colour table matches Clarke’s Table 1 (C white, E yellow, G red, A green, A♭ blue, D♭ violet, E♭ pink, F♯ and G♯ minor black), and “from when she was a young girl” is Clarke’s.
- Works: Op. 3 cadenza 1888, Op. 11/1 *Dark is the night* (Henley, 1890), Op. 17 and Op. 18 as above (Beach Project); Quintet theme from the second theme of the finale of Brahms’s quintet (bar 94), falling phrases in all three movements, “two triumphant chords of F sharp major” (Simeone, Hyperion); Quintet première at Potter Hall, 27 February 1908.
- Recordings: Hyperion CDA68295 (Ohlsson, Takács; recorded May 2019, Wyastone; released June 2020; Elgar coupling); Chandos CHAN 8958 (Detroit SO, Järvi; 19–20 January 1991; Orchestra Hall; released May 1992; movements 11:06, 7:45, 12:41, 9:20).
- Images: nothing re-checked beyond the validator’s Commons lookup.

**Score examples: compared note by note, no change needed**

- *Violin Sonata, violin part* (z B, C | E G B | c e g | a3, 3/4, K: A minor, ♩ = 120). Staff lines measured on the 300-dpi page; each head placed by step from the bottom line. The part has a four-bar multi-rest, a cue bar (piano chord and sixteenth run), then the violin enters in bar 6 with a quarter rest, B3, C4 (C4 on its ledger line, B3 hanging below it), p tranquillo. Bar 7 E4–G4–B4, bar 8 C5–E5–G5 (tenuto dashes over E and G), bar 9 A5 dotted half. All quarters. The full score (p. 12) agrees: the violin rests through piano bars 1–5 and enters in bar 6. The A5 is slurred or tied on into the next bar, which the excerpt omits. Caption (“after five bars of piano alone”, Allegro moderato, tranquillo) is correct.
- *The Year’s at the Spring*, voice, bars 1–4 (B♭ edition, 3/4, “Allegro di molto”, no metronome mark printed; this copy has two flats; the title page shows both the B♭ and D♭ incipits). Bar 1 quarter rest, triplet [F4 eighth, B♭4 quarter], tied to B♭4 eighth in the triplet [B♭4, F4, F4]; bar 2 G4 half tied to G4 eighth, eighth rest; bar 3 quarter rest, triplet [G4, C5 quarter] tied to C5 eighth, triplet [C5, G4, G4]; bar 4 (first bar of the second system, “morn;”) A4 half tied to A4 eighth, eighth rest. The ABC matches every pitch, tie and tuplet. Bars 3–4 are bars 1–2 a step higher, as the caption says. The piano is repeated chords throughout the first system.
- ♩ = 132 in the second ABC is a playback choice; the print gives only the word.

**Op. 44 and the sound/arts notes: verified**

- Poem note: the facing page (p. 2) carries the poem alone; the music (p. 3) sets the first four lines, “poco rall.” at “dew-pearled”, then “a tempo” and restarts at “The year’s at the spring”. The second pass drops “Morning’s at seven” and “The hillside’s dew-pearled” and runs “And day’s at the morn; The lark’s on the wing; The snail’s on the thorn; God’s in his heaven (twice), All’s right (twice) with the world”. The note now says the two lines are dropped.
- “Tied B♭ on ‘year’s’” (bar 1, tied quarter to eighth) and “held note at the climax on ‘right’” (p. 5: F5 dotted half tied over two bars at the *ff* climax; “world!” is also held) are correct. Dedication “To the Browning Society of Boston” and the three titles are on the cover.
- Audubon plate: the Boston Public Library copy of the same plate (no. 58, Havell LVIII) has the title “Hermit thrush : Turdus minor, Gmel. Male, 1. Female, 2.”, taken from the plate. Caption now says so. The NGA page itself returned 403; NGA accession 1945.8.58 and the medium (hand-coloured engraving and aquatint) come from the Commons file and a search summary.
- Silverstein/Kalish: Gillard’s discography lists the sonata “in *Recorded Anthology of American Music* (Joseph Silverstein, violin, Gilbert Kalish, piano, New World Records, 1977)”. Discogs: LP NW 268, 1977, Beach Op. 34 (28:41) with Foote’s Violin Sonata in G minor, Op. 20. New World’s site lists a CD, 80542, with both sonatas and Farwell and Orem pieces. Note and label rewritten from these.
- Piano Trio première, 15 January 1939, “at the MacDowell Club in New York”: Gillard gives it twice (chronology and prose), so the first pass’s “cut off” worry is settled. “Composed at the MacDowell Colony”: Gillard (Cabildo and the Trio were both written during her summers there).
- Op. 89: **corrected**. The works table said “composed in Rome” and credited the Chamber Music Society with the Inuit themes. Sources: Chamber Music Society “(1921–29) … builds on Inuit themes”; Leonarda notes by A. F. Block, “sketched out in 1921 at the MacDowell Colony … While wintering in Rome in 1929 Beach completed the String Quartet … three … Eskimo or Inuit tunes”; Gillard has only “In Rome, she composed her String Quartet”. Gates says the quartet was completed in Rome “during the winter of 1929-30”, which conflicts with Block and with the Rome trip of December 1928 – spring 1929; not followed. Table row is now 1921–29, sketched at MacDowell, finished in Rome, three Inuit melodies.
- “Ecstasy … according to her papers”: **corrected**. No source says “her papers”. Gillard (“purchased entirely with proceeds received from her famous song”) and Gates (“entirely paid for with royalties”) say the royalties paid for the cottage. Both the table row and the Centerville geo entry now say that without the attribution. Year 1893 stays (Amy Beach Project: Op. 19 “Three Songs (1893)”; Gates “written in 1893”; the LoC copy record, not opened, is catalogued 1892).
- “Britannica notes” in the *Songs as seed* aspect: the Britannica page returns 403 here, so the attribution could not be checked. Arts Fuse states the fact (first movement draws on *Dark Is the Night*, 1890; its first two themes come from it), so the sentence now states it without naming Britannica.

**Quotation and fact corrected in the life text**

- Gaelic Symphony abroad: the page said it “was heard in Leipzig and Berlin”. Gates (*Kapralova Society Journal*): the Piano Concerto was played with the Berlin Philharmonic and the Leipzig and Hamburg orchestras, and the Symphony “was also performed in Leipzig and Hamburg” (his note 60, *Musical Courier*, 11 February 1913, clipping at NYPL); Wikipedia and a Portland Youth Philharmonic essay quoting Block say the same. I found no source for Berlin and the first pass’s “(LoC)” citation is not in any LoC page I opened. Now “Leipzig and Hamburg”.
- Pfohl: the page printed Gillard’s trimmed form (“one need only mention the name of Amy Beach in order to refute the foolish prejudices …”). Gates quotes the *Hamburger Nachrichten* review of 3 December 1913 (his note 62, NYPL clipping) as “One need only mention the names of Amelie Nikisch and Amy Beach in order to refute this foolish prejudice concerning women composers.” The page now gives Gates’s wording, which is his English translation; the German was not seen. The review speaks of “the works performed here yesterday”, so the Hamburg concert was probably 2 December; the page and geo entry say only December 1913.
