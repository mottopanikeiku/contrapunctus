## Rights evidence

- **Reiner:** Internet Archive item (recording 5 Feb 1946; issued March 1947 as M-633/MM-633; side and matrix list; Brice on sides 1, 4, 6); Sony 2020 box-set notes via the Discogs API (CD 6: “Released February 24, 1947”; matrices XCO 35816–35821); [Discogs master 1172777](https://www.discogs.com/master/1172777) (“Initial release in 1947”). The March/February difference is noted in the dossier; `published: 1947`.
- **Curzon and Arbós:** a dated catalogue, *Catálogo general de discos Columbia (y Decca) … publicados hasta 1.º de enero de 1954* (Fábrica de Discos Columbia, San Sebastián; [ERESBIL scan](https://www.eresbil.eus/web/uploads/archivosGeneradorWeb/Catalogos/erelib_NL0212CAT_01_v.pdf)), text-extracted and read: Curzon/Jordá Noches on RG 16051–16053 with the work divided into six side-parts; Arbós Hat on AG 8043 (Danza del molinero / Los vecinos) and AG 8044 (Danza final). `published: 1954` is an upper bound, stated as such. MusicWeb International (Jonathan Woolf, July 2021) confirms the 78 set preceded the July 1951 LP remake. The recording dates (26 Sept 1945; 18 Apr 1928) come from the Internet Archive item notes only.
- **Barrientos/Falla:** DAHR matrix pages WLX308 (El paño moruno; Seguidilla murciana), WLX309 (Asturiana), WLX310 (Jota), all 31 Mar 1928, Paris, with Falla at the piano (Columbia (France) D11701; (France/Belgium) PFX-1). The Archivo’s 2021 exhibition credits date “Nana” and “Asturiana” to 1930, and the Archivo chronology records June 1930 sessions with Barrientos; a 2022 Melómano review dates the cycle’s recording 1928–30. Because the issue date of PFX-1 is not established, `published: 1930` is the latest date any source gives and the dossier says so. The recording is by a composer-pianist, so it is rights-sensitive: composition and recording remain separate.
- **Composition:** Falla died 14 Nov 1946; Spanish transitional rule gives life plus 80 years, so protection runs to the end of 2026 (CEDRO). Every dossier says the phonogram basis does not clear the composition. The Directive 2011/77/EU text could not be opened (EUR-Lex 403), so it is not cited.

## Timing method

Analysis files were used only to find formal joins. Side changes in the continuous files were located from short dips in the audio and cross-checked against published side contents (Columbia’s MM-633 side list; the 1954 catalogue’s division of Noches), which fixes the Noches movement joins to within about ten seconds. For El amor brujo the Ritual Fire Dance falls on side 3 (Nos. 7–8); the notes are written to remain true for a stretch of about a minute around their times, and the section joins for Nos. 1–6 and 11–13 are estimates. The Jota sections follow the printed score (long piano introduction; first couplet; piano; farewell couplet; piano; Tranquillo close). The Danza del molinero sections follow the markings of the piano score and the audible changes.

## Claim ledger for Hall notes

- **Noches:** score markings read from the Eschig 1923 full score ([scan](https://archive.org/details/nuitsdanslesjard00fall), M.E. 687): movement I printed p. 3 (Allegretto tranquillo e misterioso, dotted crotchet = 50; harp sulla tavola; violas sul ponticello; pizzicato cellos and basses; piano silent), p. 5 (piano ben marc.), p. 8 (Poco più animato, dotted crotchet = 66); II p. 35 (Allegretto giusto, crotchet = 100; four solo violas con sord.); p. 56 “Attacca subito” and p. 57 (III, Vivo, dotted crotchet = 132; cymbals ff vibrato). Programme-note claims from Jessica Schilling (LA Phil), quoting Falla’s own note; Diaghilev’s unrealised ballet from Roger Nichols (Hyperion); premiere details from the Archivo.
- **El amor brujo:** story from John Henken’s [LA Phil note](https://www.laphil.com/works/el-amor-brujo); Falla’s subtitle “pour chasser les mauvais esprits” and Allegro ma non troppo from the Chester 1921 piano edition of the Danse rituelle du feu ([scan](https://archive.org/details/danserituelleduf00fall)); Pantomima in 7/8 and Poulenc from Nichols and IMSLP; Carol Brice from the [Naumburg Foundation](https://www.naumburg.org/naumburg-winners/carol-brice) (first black musician to win the award; the Foundation says 1944, other sources say 1943).
- **Danza del molinero:** Chester 1921 piano score ([scan](https://archive.org/details/elsombrerodetres00fall)), printed pp. 38–41 (Moderato assai, crotchet = 60, “molto ritmico e pesante”, “pp subito”, “il canto”, “pochissimo più mosso”, “più animato”, “animando ancora sino il fine”, fff) and the printed synopsis and cast list; Henken’s LA Phil note on Suite No. 2.
- **Jota:** Eschig 1922 vocal score ([scan](https://archive.org/details/sietecancionespo00fall)), printed pp. 15–21 (3/8 Allegro vivo, dotted crotchet = 92; 3/4 Poco meno vivo; Iº Tempo; Tranquillo, crotchet = 76, “pp lontano”, ppp).

## Unresolved

- Session dates of the Curzon and Arbós sides rest on one Internet Archive description each; no second source was found.
- No hosted recording of the Ritual Fire Dance by itself; Rubinstein’s HMV D.A. 1151 (1930) was found but its dating rests on Internet Archive metadata alone and was not used.
