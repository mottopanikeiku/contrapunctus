Russian dates in the page are New Style (Gregorian); letter dates below give both calendars. Tchaikovsky Research supplies primary texts, translations and archival references. Its `?action=render` pages were readable where the ordinary URLs returned server errors. ## Editorial decisions

- Removed the obsolete `featured` field.
- Removed the defensive death-stories paragraph. The documented cholera diagnosis and date remain; the new Davydov quotation gives positive contemporary evidence of his state of mind while composing.
- The shared-context Bakst pairing remains shared context, not a claim that this later picture inspired Tchaikovsky.
- Retained the existing musical observations and work chronology; unsupported psychological conclusions and abstract formulations have been replaced by letters, stage action and specific orchestral choices.

### Quotations confirmed

- “Inspiration is a guest who does not like to visit the idle”: Letter 862, to von Meck, 24 June/6 July 1878, Kamenka (“Inspiration is a guest who does not like to visit the idle. It appears to those who summon it.”). https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/Letter_862?action=render
- “This is Fate”: Letter 763, to von Meck, 17 February/1 March 1878, Florence; the phrase follows a musical-example image of the symphony’s introduction. https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/Letter_763?action=render
- “Yesterday I spent the evening at the National Theatre and laughed a great deal”: closing lines of Letter 763 (the letter continues: Italian comedy “vulgar, devoid of subtlety and grace, but immensely enjoyable”). Same URL.
- “You can’t imagine how blissful I feel…”: Letter 4865, to Vladimir Davydov, dated 11 February 1893 (OS), i.e. 23 February (NS), from Klin; same letter says the programme “will remain an enigma to everyone”. https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/Letter_4865?action=render
- “lyrical scenes”: Tchaikovsky’s own description of *Onegin* (“lyrical scenes in 3 acts and 7 tableaux”); Lavrovskaya’s suggestion and the sleepless night from his letter to Modest of 18/30 May 1877 (letter number as in the earlier ledger). https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/Onegin?action=render
- Celesta note: Letter 4397 to Jurgenson, 3/15 June 1891, Maydanovo (new instrument found in Paris, wanted for *The Voyevoda* and “the ballet”, fear that Rimsky-Korsakov and Glazunov “will get wind of it”). https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/Letter_4397?action=render
