## Notation and editorial decisions

- Both ABC strings were left untouched. The rewritten captions describe the existing invented demonstrations, not quotations from Reich or Pärt. The first holds four discrete offsets of C–D–E–G; it does not imitate continuous acceleration. The second uses the nearest A-minor-triad note strictly above each melody note, including the B–C second. These statements come from the supplied notation; the general methods are supported by Reich and the Pärt Centre above.
- Retained all structural fields, artwork filenames and metadata, existing heading spans, and the Thoreau original. Source links formerly embedded in the two arts notes have moved into this authorised record; reader-facing internal links use roster slugs or the permitted Workshop anchors.
- Replaced unreferenced generalisations about postwar institutions, audience perception, acoustical interactions, the supposed limits of ordinary notation, and the effects of repeated listening with the named works and documented encounters above. Removed the old defensive explanation of the Mondrian/Reich pairing.
- No new verbatim speech quotation was added. The only retained literary excerpt was checked against Digital Thoreau. No unsupported quotation remains in the rewritten fields.
- Unavailable or incomplete pages were not used as evidence: the Saariaho technical PDF did not extract, and the attempted Messiaen *Réveil des oiseaux* publisher PDF and Philharmonie page failed. Saariaho’s publisher-hosted composer note and the readable *Oiseaux exotiques* note supply the facts actually used.
- No validation, tests, build, formatting, audio analysis, artwork fetching or git command was run. Integration validation is left to the parent agent.

### Changes

- *Prologue* aside: “In 2001, Éric Daubresse rebuilt the resonating instruments of Grisey’s *Prologue* as computer models. Garth Knox played the viola at the new version’s première…” → “Éric Daubresse rebuilt the resonating instruments… as computer models, and in 2001 Garth Knox played the viola at the new version’s première at IRCAM…”. The real-time version was created at IRCAM in 2000 and premiered on 3 April 2001; the five resonators (onde Martenot “palme” and gong, piano, tam-tam, snare drum) were analysed and modelled. Source: [François-Xavier Féron, IRCAM analysis of *Prologue*](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/analysis/analyse-de-i-prologue-i-%281976%29-de-gerard-grisey).
- *Empty Words* essay paragraph: “letters and vocal sounds remain, separated by silences” → “only letters and vocal sounds remain”; arts note: “letters and sounds among the silences” → “letters and sounds”. The Trust describes Part IV as “nothing but a virtual lullaby of letters and sounds”; no reliable source for silences between them was found (only a user review). Source: [John Cage Trust](https://johncage.org/empty-words).

### Confirmed quotation

- Thoreau, *Walden*, “Sounds”: “I did not read books the first summer; I hoed beans… I love a broad margin to my life.” Verbatim, and the next sentences describe the sunny doorway “from sunrise till noon” with birds singing ([Digital Thoreau](https://commons.digitalthoreau.org/walden/sounds/sounds-1-11/)).

### Confirmed facts

- *Peter Grimes*: first performance 7 June 1945, Sadler’s Wells, Pears as Grimes; inquest into an apprentice’s death, a second apprentice falls to his death ([Britten Pears Arts](https://www.brittenpearsarts.org/music/peter-grimes)). Passacaglia: seven-note bass repeated 39 times under variations on a theme introduced by the viola, variations not synchronised with the bass, propels the action into the scene of the second apprentice’s death ([LA Phil, Howard Posner](https://www.laphil.com/works/passacaglia-from-peter-grimes)).
- Boulez *Structures* Ia first performed in Paris in May 1952 by Boulez and Messiaen (the Boulez Foundation’s chronology gives 7 May, Comédie des Champs-Élysées; [pierreboulez.org](https://pierreboulez.org/default/biographie.aspx?_lg=en-US)); [musiquecontemporaine.info](https://www.musiquecontemporaine.info/zpiece-Boulez-StructurespremierLivre.php) gives 4 May at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées; the IRCAM entry gives 4 May with different pianists, but the Boulez Foundation assigns Grimaud and Loriod to the Cologne performance of 13 November 1953. The essay says only “May 1952” and the pair named by the Foundation. Work based on the first division of Messiaen’s *Mode de valeurs et d’intensités*, with serial control of pitch, duration, attack and dynamic ([IRCAM](https://brahms.ircam.fr/fr/works/work/7003)).
- *Oiseaux exotiques*: 1955–56, commissioned by Boulez, Domaine musical première March 1956 with Yvonne Loriod; prairie chicken in clarinet and oboe lower registers; snare drum and woodblock rhythms from Hindu and Greek music; final cadenza for bobolink and catbird as a two-part invention ([LA Phil](https://www.laphil.com/works/oiseaux-exotiques)).
- Schaeffer, 1948: five studies made and broadcast at French radio in 1948, including *Étude aux chemins de fer*; “closed groove” technique and varied playback speed ([IRCAM](https://resources.ircam.fr/fr/composer/pierre-schaeffer/workcourse); [Institut für Medienarchäologie](https://archiv.ima.or.at/en/cinq-etudes-de-bruits-1948/)).
- *Gesang der Jünglinge*: sine tones and a single boy’s voice superimposed on itself, text from the third chapter of Daniel ([IRCAM](https://resources.ircam.fr/en/work/gesang-der-junglinge)).
- *Partiels*: 1975, eighteen musicians, première 4 March 1976 ([IRCAM work page](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/works/work/8966/)); the Espaces acoustiques share the frame of a low-E spectrum ([IRCAM survey](https://resources.ircam.fr/fr/composer/gerard-grisey/workcourse)).
- *Lichtbogen* (1986): CRIME system analysing cello transitions from artificial harmonics to multiphonics; FORMES-based rhythms, transcribed with approximations into playable notation; nine musicians and live electronics ([Saariaho’s note, Wise Music](https://www.wisemusicclassical.com/work/4340/)).
- *4′33″*: 29 August 1952, Maverick Concert Hall, Woodstock, three movements, David Tudor ([Maverick Concerts](https://maverickconcerts.org/event/nothing-john-cage-and-433-2024/)). *Music of Changes*: composed 1951, I Ching chance procedures in composition, fixed notation including pedals ([John Cage Complete Works](https://www.data-johncage.org/pp/John-Cage-Work-Detail.cfm?work_ID=134)).
- Feldman: *Durations* have precisely written notes with performers beginning together and choosing durations within a general tempo; String Quartet No. 2 up to five and a half hours ([Universal Edition](https://www.universaledition.com/en/Contacts/Morton-Feldman/)). *Rothko Chapel*: Feldman in Houston for the February 1971 opening, donors’ request, fourteen paintings in the octagonal chapel, viola with timpani, humming choir, a melody written at fifteen over vibraphone ([LA Phil, John Henken](https://www.laphil.com/works/rothko-chapel)).
- *Atmosphères* (1961): 48-part mirror canon, twenty-eight violins down, twenty violas and cellos up, cluster compressed from three and a half octaves to a minor third; piccolos cut off and answered by basses six octaves below; piano strings brushed ([Paul Griffiths](https://americansymphony.org/concert-notes/atmosphres-1961/)).
- *Piano Phase*: late 1966 tape-loop experiment; 1967 work; one player gradually moves one beat ahead until unison returns, then the pattern changes ([Steve Reich’s note](https://stevereich.com/composition/piano-phase/)).
- *Shaker Loops*: grew from the failed quartet *Wavemaker*, quartet became septet, San Francisco Conservatory students, tape-loop idea, tremolo and trill pun, première 15 December 1978 ([Adams](https://www.earbox.com/work/shaker-loops/)). *Nixon in China*: Houston Grand Opera, 22 October 1987; Alice Goodman’s verse libretto; Sellars; replica of Air Force One from which Nixon, Pat and Kissinger meet a line of Chinese officials ([Adams](https://www.earbox.com/work/nixon-in-china/)).
- *Für Alina* (1976): melody grows by one note per bar to eight and shrinks again; tintinnabuli voice on the B minor triad; one break onto a “false” C♯ with the pedal point released ([Arvo Pärt Centre](https://www.arvopart.ee/en/arvo-part/work/477/)).
- Kurtág: striking keys with elbows, fists or palms as legitimate as fingers; dedications and memorials ([BMC booklet, Selection 2](https://bmcrecords.hu/en/albums/gyorgy-kurtag-games-jatekok-2nd-selection-played-by-gabor-csalog)); *Palm Stroke* (1) 0:18 and (2) 0:23, György Kurtág, Hungarian Radio, 20 November 1978 ([BMC](https://bmcrecords.hu/en/albums/gyorgy-and-marta-kurtag-play-kurtag)).
- Gubaidulina: first bayan work *De profundis* finished in 1978 and played on the grand piano in Lips’s class; Gosconcert required approval for the Scandinavian programme; the Ministry of Culture official asked what the cluster, glissando-with-cluster, tonal glissando and air-button signs were, then said “Play anything you like” ([Friedrich Lips, Free-Reed Journal](https://www.henrydoktorski.com/free-reed/essays/lipsyesterday.html)).
- *Asyla* (1997): rave-inspired third movement *Ecstasio*; six percussionists and two keyboard players; upright piano tuned a quarter-tone flat; “bag of metal knives & forks” ([Faber Music](https://www.fabermusic.com/shop/asyla-p584); [Faber instrumentation](https://www.fabermusic.com/music/asyla-2510)).
- First Aldeburgh Festival began 5 June 1948 with Arts Council help; *Albert Herring* at the Jubilee Hall, *Saint Nicolas* at the Parish Church ([Britten Pears Arts](https://www.brittenpearsarts.org/an-official-history-of-the-aldeburgh-festival)). *Owen Wingrave*: BBC2 broadcast 16 May 1971, filmed at Snape Maltings, commissioned by the head of BBC2, Attenborough ([Britten Pears Arts](https://www.brittenpearsarts.org/music/owen-wingrave); BBC television commissioned it in 1966 per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Wingrave)).
- *Empty Words*: 1974, drawn from Thoreau’s journals, four parts omitting sentences, phrases, words and syllables; Cage’s studio recording at Mills College, Oakland, 1978, some ten hours ([John Cage Trust](https://johncage.org/empty-words)).
- Mondrian: arrived in New York in 1940, fell for boogie-woogie; *Broadway Boogie Woogie* (1942–43) omits black and breaks bars into multicoloured segments ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/78682)).
- Score captions: C–D–E–G offset schematic starts the lower part on D, E and G in successive bars; the tintinnabuli schematic pairs each melody note with the nearest A-minor triad note strictly above it (A→C, B→C, C→E…) — checked directly against the unchanged ABC.
