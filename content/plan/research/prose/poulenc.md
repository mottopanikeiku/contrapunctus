# Francis Poulenc — prose sources

The rewrite preserves the ABC, image filenames, Apollinaire poem, reading list and lineage arrays. All five short quotations in the life come from Poulenc’s conversations with Claude Rostand, broadcast in 1953–54 and published in 1954. The English translations are ours; the French wording and locations are recorded below.

## Editorial decisions

The familiar “monk and rascal” slogan is not used in place of a life. The religious turn is dated to 1936, supported by the chronology and the corrected interview text. Gloria’s date is 1959–60. No unsourced private speech by Bernac, Duval or Roubert has been invented, and the often-quoted football/angels explanation of Gloria is omitted because an adequately dated conversational context was not established.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked all 31 claim groups in the source table against fetched sources, including the biography, musical and dramatic descriptions, work metadata, artwork captions and recording credits. One reader-facing correction was needed:

- Rocamadour quotation: “he told Rostand in 1954” → “he told Rostand in 1953”. The book appeared in 1954, but Radio France identifies the tenth interview and this passage as first broadcast on 15 December 1953. [INA–Radio France archive and transcript excerpt](https://www.radiofrance.fr/franceculture/podcasts/les-nuits-de-france-culture/entretiens-avec-francis-poulenc-10-18-religion-et-foi-1ere-diffusion-15-12-1953-paris-inter-5760885); [BnF publication record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb15043098z).

Quotations confirmed against the fetched French interview text; all five translations are faithful:

- “I owe him everything” — Poulenc to Rostand, third interview, first broadcast 27 October 1953; [French text, p. 16](https://www.rodoni.ch/A24/poulenc-rostand.pdf), independently repeated by the [Radio France archive](https://www.radiofrance.fr/franceculture/podcasts/les-nuits-de-france-culture/francis-poulenc-nul-ne-me-joue-mieux-qu-horowitz-1540639).
- “Put butter in the sauce!” — Poulenc’s own formulation, not Viñes’s; third interview, [French text, p. 18](https://www.rodoni.ch/A24/poulenc-rostand.pdf).
- “adorable bad music” — Poulenc describing his mother’s tastes; first interview, first broadcast 13 October 1953; [French text, p. 8](https://www.rodoni.ch/A24/poulenc-rostand.pdf), date confirmed in the [Radio France series archive](https://www.radiofrance.fr/franceculture/podcasts/serie-entretiens-avec-francis-poulenc).
- “It is my oxygen” — Poulenc on Debussy; second interview, first broadcast 20 October 1953; [French text, p. 14](https://www.rodoni.ch/A24/poulenc-rostand.pdf), date confirmed in the [Radio France series archive](https://www.radiofrance.fr/franceculture/podcasts/serie-entretiens-avec-francis-poulenc).
- “Rocamadour completed my return to the faith of my childhood” — Poulenc to Rostand, tenth interview, first broadcast 15 December 1953; [French text, p. 30](https://www.rodoni.ch/A24/poulenc-rostand.pdf) and [Radio France transcript excerpt](https://www.radiofrance.fr/franceculture/podcasts/les-nuits-de-france-culture/entretiens-avec-francis-poulenc-10-18-religion-et-foi-1ere-diffusion-15-12-1953-paris-inter-5760885).
- The five lines of “Le Dromadaire” match the [1911 text reproduced by Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Bestiaire,_ou_Cort%C3%A8ge_d%E2%80%99Orph%C3%A9e/Le_Dromadaire); the existing translation is faithful.

Supplementary checks replacing weaker references in the original ledger:

- Les Six’s membership and Collet’s two January 1920 articles: [Larousse, Dictionnaire de la musique](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/musdico/Groupe_des_six/168002).
- *Les Biches*: the [Bibliothèque historique de la Ville de Paris’s production record](https://ccfr.bnf.fr/portailccfr/ark:/16871/004b1891097) confirms the 1924 première and creative team; the [Salabert catalogue, pp. 14 and 32](https://poulenc.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/catalogue_oeuvres_poulenc_compressed.pdf) gives the old French choral text and the purely orchestral suite’s five movements, including the Rag-Mazurka. The omitted offstage choral numbers are also specified in [Paul Serotsky’s programme note](https://www.musicweb-international.com/programme_notes/poulenc_biches.htm).
- *Le Bestiaire*: Graham Johnson’s [Hyperion note](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dw.asp?dc=W12466_68021) confirms thirty poems, Dufy’s illustrations and Poulenc’s six-song selection; the [publisher catalogue](https://poulenc.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/catalogue_oeuvres_poulenc_compressed.pdf) gives their sequence and chamber scoring. The [association’s chronological catalogue](https://poulenc.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/catalogue_chrono_poulenc_compressed.pdf) confirms all nine FP numbers on the page.
- The 1920 portrait is dated 16 July 1920 in [Paris Musées’ own collection record](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/petit-palais/oeuvres/portrait-de-francis-poulenc). The 1922 group portrait’s secure identifications and Rouen location match [Bridgeman’s catalogue](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/en/blanche/le-groupe-des-six-1922-oil-on-canvas/oil-on-canvas/asset/4648110).
- The song postlude is specifically described in [John Check’s review of Nichols’s biography](https://newcriterion.com/dispatch/poulencs-voice/). The historical recording performers were checked against [Warner](https://www.warnerclassics.com/release/poulenc-oeuvres-completes), [Pristine Classical](https://www.pristineclassical.com/products/paco069) and the [MusicWeb Gloria review](https://musicweb-international.com/classRev/2007/Sept07/Poulenc_7477232.htm).

No unresolved factual claim remains.
