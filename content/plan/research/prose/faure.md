## Corrections and omissions

- Corrected Sarah Bernhardt to Mrs Patrick Campbell for the 1898 London *Pelléas* commission. Bernhardt is associated with a later production.
- Removed the old defensive claim that *Clair de lune* was not demonstrably inspired by this particular Watteau picture. The pairing is expressly a shared artistic context, and the new prose explains that context.
- Avoided attributing the *Requiem* to a single bereavement or settling the disputed authorship of its expanded orchestration. The source-backed version history remains in a margin note.
- No unsupported quotation was retained. No preserved image, ABC, poem text, reading entry or lineage-array member was changed.

### Confirmed (source, one line each)

- Montgauzy school and harmonium, October 1854 aged nine, eleven years at Niedermeyer, Niedermeyer’s death March 1861, Saint-Saëns and Schumann/Liszt/Wagner, sixty-year friendship, *Cantique* first prize, left July 1865, Rennes from January 1866 with piano pupils, cigarette during sermons, resignation after the ball, Saint-Sulpice under Widor and the two-organ improvisations, Société Nationale founded February 1871, First Violin Sonata January 1877, engagement July 1877 and broken November 1877, Madeleine choirmaster 1877, marriage 1883 and two sons, publisher bought songs outright, Requiem priest’s remark, Dubois/Massenet 1896, pupils, “Robespierre”, summer retreats, Sorbonne tribute 1922, quartet “without piano” letter, finished 11 September 1924 (text gives the month only), private performance refused for his hearing, state funeral at the Madeleine: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Faur%C3%A9) (Nectoux, Duchen, Jones).
- “une délivrance heureuse, une aspiration au bonheur d’au-delà”: interview with Louis Aguettant of **12 July 1902**, published in *Comœdia*, 1954, p. 6 ([René Gagnaux, Mon Musée Musical](http://www.rene-gagnaux.ch/inghelbrecht_desire-emile/faure_requiem.html)). The date had not been confirmed by an institutional source before.
- First Piano Quartet: pizzicato chords, piano between 6/8 and 2/4, muted strings in the trio, Adagio in C minor, finale rewritten 1883 three years after the first performance (so first performance 1880): [Stephen Johnson, Hyperion CDA66166](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dc.asp?dc=D_CDA66166).
- *L’Horizon chimérique*: Mirmont (1886–1914), first sung by dedicatee Charles Panzéra in May 1922 with his wife Magdeleine Panzéra-Baillot at the piano: [Graham Johnson, Hyperion](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dw.asp?dc=W4609_66165). Mirmont was born 2 December 1886 and killed on 28 or 29 November 1914, so “died at twenty-seven” stands ([fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jean_de_La_Ville_de_Mirmont&action=raw); English Wikipedia agrees).
- Hearing trouble from 1903 and director from 1905: [IRCAM](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/composer/gabriel-faure/biography).
- Sargent’s portrait “inscribed ‘souvenir affectuoux’”, Sargent a committed English advocate, Fauré a particular friend: [Langley, 2018](https://www.leannelangley.com/site/assets/files/1069/langley-_art_music-john_singer_sargent_2018.pdf), note 1 and pp. 17–18.
- Mrs Patrick Campbell’s commission for a London production in English, Koechlin orchestrating as Fauré finished each piece, *Fileuse* (violin triplets, solo oboe), *Sicilienne* (solo flute, harp, pizzicato strings), death-of-Mélisande prelude: [Orrin Howard, Los Angeles Philharmonic](https://www.laphil.com/works/suite-from-pelleas-and-melisande-faure).
- First *Requiem* performance 16 January 1888 at the Madeleine in a five-part version without violins or woodwinds (solo violin in the *Sanctus*, divided violas and cellos, double bass); seven-part church version completed by 1893; concert orchestration 1900; no *Dies irae*; national funeral at the Madeleine on 8 November 1924 with the *Requiem* performed under Gaubert, Panzéra among the soloists: [fr.wikipedia, *Requiem (Fauré)*](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_(Faur%C3%A9)).
- Letter to his wife of 9 September 1923: “I’ve started a Quartet for strings, without piano … enough to give all those people who are not Beethoven the jitters!” (French: “la frousse”); themes from an abandoned 1878 violin concerto; viola opening with a Phrygian F♮: [Roger Nichols, Hyperion booklet 67664-B](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/notes/67664-B.pdf), English p. 5. Composed at Annecy-le-Vieux from September 1923, the Andante first, written there 9–13 September: [Wikipedia, String Quartet (Fauré)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartet_(Faur%C3%A9)).
