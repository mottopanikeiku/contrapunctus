## Corrections and removals

The hymn caption no longer says that Haydn **transposes** the hymn to G major in the quartet: the hymn itself is already in G. The Blake note drops the defensive statement about undocumented contact and the unsupported suggestion of directly opposed programmes. It now offers a plainly identified visual/musical comparison. No biographical event was removed for lack of a source.

### Confirmed quotations

- “The whole company was extremely pleased, and so was I. I made four thousand gulden this evening. Such a thing can only be done in England.” — Haydn’s London notebook after his benefit of 4 May 1795, German “Die ganze Gesellschaft war äußerst vergnügt und auch ich. Ich machte diesen Abend vier tausend Gulden. So etwas kann man nur in England machen” (Bartha, *Gesammelte Briefe und Aufzeichnungen*, p. 553). Also: the 1795 concerts at the King’s Theatre “Opera Concerts”, orchestra of sixty, Haydn’s “place of honour at the keyboard” with the orchestra grouped round it. MEAS: Ulrich Wilker, Henle preface, https://www.henle.de/media/e3/5f/e8/1697725911/9072-1697725911-sync.pdf
- “I was cut off from the world … and so I had to become original.” — Haydn to Griesinger, *Biographische Notizen* (1810): “ich war von der Welt abgesondert. Niemand in meiner Nähe konnte mich an mir selbst irre machen und quälen, und so mußte ich original werden.” EXT: https://www.adelinde.net/joseph-haydn/ (quotes Griesinger in full); context of experiments, adding and cutting: same passage.
- “Before God, and as an honest man, I tell you that your son is the greatest composer I know, personally or by name” — Haydn to Leopold Mozart, Saturday 12 February 1785 (quartets K. 458, 464, 465 played that evening), in Leopold’s letter to Nannerl begun 14 February: “I say to you before God, as an honest man, your son is the greatest composer that I know in both person and name: he has taste, and beyond that the greatest knowledge of composition.” MEAS: https://dme.mozarteum.at/DME/objs/raradocs/transcr/pdf_eng/0847_LM_Nannerl_1785.pdf
- *Farewell* story told by Haydn in old age to Dies and Griesinger; musicians’ families at Eisenstadt; candles snuffed; two muted violins left; F♯ minor work. EXT: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._45_(Haydn)
- Op. 20: fugal finales in Nos. 5, 6 and 2 (two, three and four subjects); “Sun” nickname from J. J. Hummel’s title page; very high cello writing. MEAS: Christin Heitmann, Henle preface, https://www.henle.de/media/11/78/6f/1697725790/9208-1697725790-sync.pdf
- *Le Matin*: solo double bass and virtuoso bassoon in the trio; flute introduces the main theme. MEAS: Larner (above). *Hen* nickname from the clucking second theme (dotted repeated notes in solo oboe, B♭ major). EXT: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._83_(Haydn)
- F minor Variations Hob. XVII:6 (1793, for “Signora de Ployer”): first autograph part of eight pages, bars 1–145, closed with five bars of coda in major; second part, a four-page working score, adds the minor theme again and the large coda (bar 146 ff.). MEAS: Sonja Gerlach, Henle preface, https://www.henle.de/media/5d/a7/17/1690886054/0224-1690886054-sync.pdf
- *Gott erhalte*: melody G A | B A c B | A F♯ G e d | c B A B G | d (half note) matches the LilyPond source of the melody in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gott_erhalte_Franz_den_Kaiser, so the page’s ABC is correct.
- Biography: Rohrau birth, wheelwright father (harp), mother a cook, Hainburg at about six, St Stephen’s from 1740, dismissal and Spangler’s garret, Porpora, Fux, C. P. E. Bach (“I did not leave my clavier till I played them through”), 1779 contract, Anton’s dismissal of most musicians with Haydn’s nominal post and pension, first sight of the sea at fifty-eight, 1808 *Creation* performance with Salieri, Beethoven and an armchair, French officer Sulémy on 17 May 1809 singing an aria from *The Creation* (Griesinger: “Mit Würd’ und Hoheit angetan”). EXT: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn
- Recordings: Gardiner, Archiv, recorded 16 February 1995, Tooting — Gerald Finley (Raphael), Rodney Gilfry (Adam) and Donna Brown (Eve) as separate singers; Gramophone: “an excellent Raphael in Gerald Finley, and gains from having extra singers for Adam and Eve”, and “the superbly confident, precise attack”. MEAS: https://www.prestomusic.com/classical/products/7953960--haydn-the-creation (listing and review excerpt; Part III duets labelled Eva, Adam). Brendel’s Philips recording of Hob. XVI:34: “more exploratory with the rising arpeggios in the left hand, more characterful with the answers given by the right hand” — Tim Perry, https://www.musicweb-international.com/classrev/2007/Sept07/Haydn_cm546.htm
