## Qualifications and removals

No inherited biographical claim required deletion for lack of evidence. The Wq 240 and *Heilig* dates are made more precise. The song count remains fifty-four, following the critical edition rather than the chronology’s fifty-five. No supposed quotation about Mozart owing everything to Emanuel Bach is introduced. The original teaching notation and poem text have not been altered.

### Confirmed quotations

- “In composition and keyboard playing I never had any teacher other than my father” — autobiography, Corneilson translation “never had any other teacher than my father”; also Ruppin summons, 1740 service, Charlottenburg flute solo accompanied alone, salary increases, 1744 marriage to Johanna Maria Dannemann, “two sons and a daughter now living”, Frankfurt “music academy” and ceremonial music, Telemann as one of his godfathers. MEAS: https://www.cpebach.org/pdfs/resources/autobiography-English.pdf
- “A musician can move others only if he is moved himself” — *Versuch* Part I, third chapter (“Drittes Hauptstück. Vom Vortrage”), §13, 1753 print p. 122 (chapter begins p. 115): “Indem ein Musicus nicht anders rühren kan, er sey dann selbst gerührt”; the carved-figure comparison follows on p. 123 (“wie ein geschnitztes Bild vor dem Instrumente zu sitzen”). Introduction §§14–15 (separate passage): “Jeder Clavierist soll von Rechtswegen einen guten Flügel und auch ein gutes Clavicord haben … das Clavicord zur Erlernung des guten Vortrags und den Flügel, um die gehörige Kraft in die Finger zu kriegen”; fortepianos praised “wenn sie dauerhaft und gut gearbeitet sind” (Introduction §11). MEAS: https://musiktheorie.georgthoma.de/quellen/bach/bach-1753-versuch-1/ (page markers S. 115, 122–123 in the transcription).
- “Be less artful and give more sugar” — Bach to Johann Christoph Kühnau, 31 August 1784: “seyn Sie weniger künstlich und geben mehr Zucker”; “allow your diligence full rein” for unprinted work. Van Swieten commission of 1773 and Reichardt’s account (trial performance at Büsch’s house, Hamburg, 1774; Reichardt “führte sie mit seiner Violine”); No. 6 first movement ends on the dominant with a pause, then F♯ minor. MEAS: Sarah Adams, https://www.cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/III-2-Intro.pdf
- Wq 183: autograph “Ende d. 11 Nov. 1775” (No. 2) and “Fine d. 12 Juni 1776” (No. 4); Hamburg rehearsal reported 20 August 1776, “some forty” musicians; each symphony twice (review of 30 December 1780); Bach to Breitkopf, 25 January 1780, “elendes Papier”, dedication to the Prussian Crown Prince; No. 1 D major ending in E♭, E♭ slow movement; twelve obbligato parts, seven winds, separate cello and violone/continuo. MEAS: Kidger and Fisher, https://www.cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/III-3-Intro.pdf. Tempo order Allegro di molto – Largo – Presto: https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_in_D_major,_H.663_(Bach,_Carl_Philipp_Emanuel)
- *Heilig*: newspaper report of 25 October 1776 announcing St Michael’s performances on the following two days, angels “from high up over the church proper”, people’s choir from the organ, fugue by both choirs (an announcement, as the sentence about 1776 reports it); 9 April 1786 benefit concert for the medical poor-relief institute with the Credo of the B minor Mass, *Messiah* excerpts, Magnificat and *Heilig*. MEAS: https://cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/V-6-1-Intro.pdf. Introductory arietta for alto: https://imslp.org/wiki/Heilig,_H.778_(Bach,_Carl_Philipp_Emanuel)
- Wq 240: sections first performed in Hamburg on the Saturday before Easter 1774; complete performances 1778 and 1779; printed February 1787; autograph complete by 1774, revised 1777–78 and later. MEAS: Leisinger, https://cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/IV-FS-Wq240-Intro.pdf
- Gellert: 54 poems (1757); Bach’s settings 1758; Gellert wrote on 25 March 1758 that he had received a copy from Bach. MEAS: Berg, https://cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/VI-1-Intro.pdf. First stanza of “Die Ehre Gottes aus der Natur” matches https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Die_Himmel_r%C3%BChmen_des_Ewigen_Ehre
- Wq 67 composed 1787; violin version Wq 80 with autograph title “C. P. E. Bach’s Empfindungen”; *L’Irresoluë* (Wq 117/31) “continual shifting between 3/8 and 3/4”, composed 1756. MEAS: Wollny, https://www.cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/I-8-1-Intro.pdf and https://www.cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/I-8-2-Intro.pdf; [chronology](https://www.cpebach.org/timeline/chronology)
- Wq 170 composed 1750, orchestral parts shared by cello, keyboard and flute versions. MEAS: Nosow, https://www.cpebach.org/pdfs/introductions/III-6-Intro.pdf
- Chronology: installed in Hamburg 19 April 1768; twenty subscription concerts announced in the winter; Burney’s visit October 1772; 9 April 1786 “historical” concert; Wq 67 and Wq 80 in 1787; death 14 December 1788. MEAS: https://www.cpebach.org/timeline/chronology
- Beethoven to Breitkopf & Härtel, 26 July 1809 (HCB Br 78): asks for scores of Haydn, Mozart, Johann Sebastian and Emanuel Bach. MEAS: Beethoven-Haus booklet *Fuge*, https://internet.beethoven.de/pdf-web/fuge_begleitheft_de_screen (letter 17).
- Haydn on C. P. E. Bach’s first six sonatas (“I did not leave my clavier till I played them through”), recorded by Griesinger and Dies. EXT: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn (early-career section).
- Portrait: after Johann Philipp Bach, c. 1780, pastel, Berlin State Library (Mus. P Bach, C. P. E. I, 1). MEAS: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carl_Philipp_Emanuel_Bach.jpg
- Menzel, *Flute Concert of Frederick the Great at Sanssouci*: image viewed; Frederick stands at the centre with the flute, Bach at the keyboard at right with his head turned towards him. MEAS: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Adolph_Menzel_-_Fl%C3%B6tenkonzert_Friedrichs_des_Gro%C3%9Fen_in_Sanssouci_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg
- Pinnock recording: B♭ symphony’s slow movement “an orchestrated improvisation”; B minor symphony ends with “stabbing chords in a full-throttle Presto”. EXT: Sammut, https://www.earlymusicamerica.org/web-articles/english-concerts-c-p-e-bach-sparkles-anew/ (the review covers the Pentatone reissue of the 1979 Archiv recording).
