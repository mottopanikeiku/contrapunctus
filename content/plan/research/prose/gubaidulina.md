# Gubaidulina — quotation and fact sources

Research for the composer-page rewrite, 18 October 2026. The web sources cited below were located through search and their page text was opened and read; search summaries alone were not used as evidence. The three quotations in the life were checked in their full source contexts. The page distinguishes recollection from contemporary documentation and does not supply an exact year where the recollections disagree.

## Source cautions and editorial omissions

- Jeffries’s interview incorrectly gives Kazan as her birthplace. The protected Chistopol birth data and McBurney’s family chronology take precedence.
- The Shostakovich encounter is dated 1959 in the 2013 interview and 1958 in the Praemium Imperiale profile. The life identifies it as a student encounter and attributes the quotation to her later recollection, without choosing a year. The award profile also has imprecise education/Astreja chronology; those claims were not reused.
- Rigaudière’s survey gives an early-1991 move to Germany; the publisher biography and award chronology give 1992. The page retains 1992 and does not try to resolve a possible distinction between travel and settlement.
- Duffek’s English Passion note calls 2000 the 250th anniversary of Bach’s “birth”, an evident slip. The BSO memorial correctly identifies his death anniversary, which the page uses.
- The old *Musical Toys* reference to a clock was dropped: the checked contents identify other specific characters, and no clock title is listed.
- The old comparison with Pärt’s tintinnabuli was removed. It added a broad stylistic contrast without explaining an actual personal or musical connection; the new lineage note uses Gubaidulina’s own account of her models.
- No unsupported quotations were retained from the old page, which had no quoted testimony. The added quotations have the explicit attribution and textual checks recorded above.
- Naxos’s direct catalogue page returned 403, one BIS booklet URL returned 404, and two other research pages had certificate/access failures. None supplies a new claim solely on the strength of its search snippet; accessible sources above provide the evidence actually used.

## Fact-check (independent)

Rechecked all 42 grouped claims in the two tables above: the three quotations, biographical chronology, named collaborators and première personnel, work dates and forces, musical procedures, both score captions, art and Psalm text, portrait credit, recording metadata and lineage prose. The publisher biography, IRCAM survey, interviews, composer’s programme notes, Lips’s firsthand recollection, BSO memorial, scholarly *Chaconne* analysis, museum record and cited record reviews were independently fetched and compared with the page. The repeated descriptions in the life, works and sound panels were checked together.

### Change

- “My faith is my life. And it is my secret.” → “My faith is my life, and it is my secret.” The former faithfully translated the two Russian sentences, but was the writer’s own translation. The replacement matches the punctuation and wording of the published English translation in [Gasser’s interview](https://www.classicalarchives.com/feature/gubaidulina_interview.html). This is quotation alignment, not a change to her meaning.

### Quotations confirmed

- “I was convinced there was too little music in the world. We needed more.”: continuous excerpt of Gubaidulina’s answer to Stuart Jeffries, published 31 October 2013; [interview](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/oct/31/sofia-gubaidulina-unchained-melodies). Only the initial letter of the excerpt is capitalised.
- “My wish for you is that you should continue on your own incorrect path.”: exact English wording of Shostakovich’s advice as Gubaidulina recalled it to Jeffries in the [same interview](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/oct/31/sofia-gubaidulina-unchained-melodies). The page correctly identifies the addressee, later recollection and student context, without selecting a disputed encounter year.
- “My faith is my life, and it is my secret.”: exact published English translation of Gubaidulina’s written answer to Nolan Gasser, received 16 March 2012 and published 27 March; [English interview with Russian original](https://www.classicalarchives.com/feature/gubaidulina_interview.html).
- Latin Psalm extract: the three verses and their English meaning agree with [Clementine Vulgate Psalm 129](https://scriptura.fsspx.org/vulgata/ps/129), Psalm 130 in Hebrew numbering. The protected original remains unchanged.

### Additional independent corroboration

- *Musical Toys*’ previously retained 1969 composition year is explicitly confirmed by the [publisher’s work entry](https://www.boosey.com/pages/cr/catalogue/cat_detail?=&langid=1&musicid=3616), in addition to the fourteen contents in the library catalogue already cited.
- The bayan/free-bass explanation is supported by accordion specialist Robert Young McMahan’s [*Composer’s Guide to the Piano Accordion*, pp. 1–5](https://ameraccord.com/commissions/ComposersGuide.pdf): Russian bayan as a button-keyboard instrument, and free bass as a multi-octave single-note left-hand system.
- The Chicago Viola Concerto première is confirmed directly by the [publisher’s English metadata](https://www.boosey.com/cr/music/Sofia-Gubaidulina-Viola-Concerto/15298): 17 April 1997, Yuri Bashmet, Chicago Symphony Orchestra, Kent Nagano. The page’s less detailed 1997 statement is correct.
- *Johannes-Passion*’s organ is confirmed in the [publisher’s full work subtitle](https://www.boosey.com/cr/music/Sofia-Gubaidulina-St-John-Passion/15623), although the abbreviated orchestral scoring list does not repeat the solo organ.
- The portrait’s photographer, Mikhail Kozlovsky, and date, 3 November 2016, are confirmed by the [photograph’s source record and embedded metadata](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sofia_Gubaidulina_(2016-11-03).jpg). The underlying Tatarstan photo-report URL timed out; the retained Commons record includes its attribution and provenance.
- The painting’s identity, 1500 date, medium, figures and iconostasis provenance agree with the [Tretyakov Gallery record, inventory 29554](https://my.tretyakov.ru/app/masterpiece/8816).
