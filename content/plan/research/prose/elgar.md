## Corrections and exclusions

- The Government Art Collection dates Barker’s painting to **c. 1860**. The previous “undated” label is replaced.
- The Piano Quintet was completed in **early 1919**. Its work-list date is now **1918–19**, and the life no longer places all three late chamber works wholly within 1918.
- **Menuhin was sixteen** in July 1932. No correction to fifteen has been made; the recording dates are used instead. The biography’s account of recording only weeks before a 1933 Paris performance is superseded by the institutional discography.
- Removed the unsupported generalisations that Walton inherited a particular mixture of Elgarian brass and lyricism, and that Britten’s relationship could be summarised as admiration of orchestral craft. Britten’s documented *Gerontius* recording replaces the latter; the protected lineage arrays are unchanged.
- Removed the unbounded comparison asserting quicker basic tempos than “many later performances”. The specific, documented flexibility of Elgar’s *Nimrod* supplies the recording-era detail.
- Replaced old unsourced analytical claims about the Serenade’s independent lower parts and *The Swimmer*’s “unusually wide” vocal range with supported performance history and concrete descriptions. These omissions are not assertions that the older descriptions are false.
- The alleged link between the music’s “English character” and later public use was an interpretative generalisation, not a sourced biographical event, and has been removed.
- The larger unplayed *Enigma* theme remains unresolved. The life says so; no speculative solution is introduced. No other unresolved factual assertion is knowingly retained.
- Source errors encountered but not adopted include the Cello Concerto guide’s “sixty-six years” between 1919 and the 1965 recording, the Farrington booklet’s age of fifty at the completion of the First Symphony in 1908, and the university programme’s mistaken variation number for Alice.

### Quotations checked

1. **“I imagined Gerontius to be a man like us”** — exact opening words of Elgar’s letter to Jaeger, quoted with a citation to *Elgar and his Publishers*, p. 228, by Joanna Bullivant: https://www.newmanreview.org/newman-and-elgar-s-the-dream-of-gerontius/ . The exact date, **28 August 1900**, is independently supplied with letter identifier **EBM 8401** in Martin Bird’s article, *Elgar Society Journal*, April 2015, printed p. 21: https://elgarsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/ESJ-Apr-2015-final.pdf . These are checked published transcriptions/attributions, not a claim to have consulted the autograph.
2. **“a real large work and I think good and alive”** — exact words in Frank Beck’s account of Elgar’s late-July 1919 letter to Sidney Colvin, describing the nearly completed Cello Concerto: https://www.elgar.org/3cello-c.htm . The page appropriately gives only ‘late July’, not an invented day.
3. **Newman’s eight-line Angel farewell, beginning ‘Softly and gently’** — all words checked in the primary poem at https://www.newmanreader.org/works/verses/gerontius.html , concluding section, dated January 1865. The supplied original expands ‘ransom’d’ to ‘ransomed’ and modernises the apostrophe in ‘o’er’; those orthographic differences do not change its wording or metre. The first two stanzas, final promise of return, male Angel and purgatorial setting are supported. The protected poem original remains unchanged.
4. **Musical quotation, *Enigma* theme bars 1–2** — the displayed notation at https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d2/Enigma_theme.png agrees with the retained ABC’s G minor, 4/4, rests, pitches and reversed placement of quavers/crotchets. The second example is correctly labelled invented augmentation; its first bar’s eight pitches recur at twice the duration in bars 3–4. Neither caption misrepresents that schematic as an Elgar quotation.
