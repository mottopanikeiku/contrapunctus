## Removed or narrowed

- Removed the categorical “three successive approaches to tonality” from the facts panel; the biography names the actual works and changes.
- Removed the precise assertion that Stravinsky saw Hogarth’s series in Chicago in 1947. The publisher’s short work note repeats 1947, while Walsh’s chronology dates the Chicago exhibition visit to 1946 and the Auden collaboration to 1947. The page retains the documented Hogarth inspiration and the 1947 collaboration without conflating them.
- Removed the unspecific claim that Stravinsky’s recorded tempos disagree with published metronome marks: no individual recording/passage was documented here.
- Removed the unsupported generalisation that the early ballets affected Bartók and Prokofiev in the particular way asserted, and the vague Reich comparison from the lineage prose. The protected lineage arrays remain unchanged; the rewritten note names connections documented by the consulted sources.
- Removed rights/licensing prose from the Roerich art note. Image files, ABC strings and further reading are unchanged.

### Quotations confirmed (exact wording, speaker, addressee, date)

- “diabolical cascades of arpeggios” — Stravinsky, *An Autobiography* (1936), on the Petrushka piano piece: https://www.hellenicaworld.com/Music/Literature/IgorStravinsky/en/Autobiography.html
- “The picture of the old woman in a squirrel fur sticks in my mind.” — Stravinsky to Roerich, 26 September 1911, about “Augurs of Spring”; he sees her “running in front of the group, stopping sometimes and interrupting the rhythmic flow.” Van den Toorn, ch. 1: https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/stravinsky-and-the-rite-of-spring.html
- “that have been phonographically transcribed” — Stravinsky to his mother, 23 February 1916, from Switzerland; Caucasian folk songs from Jurgenson’s. Same van den Toorn page.
- Dinner with Diaghilev and Nijinsky “washed down with good claret”; Nijinsky “standing on a chair, screaming ‘sixteen, seventeen, eighteen’” while Stravinsky held him “by his clothes” — both from the 1936 *Autobiography* (paraphrased, correctly attributed to the later account in the prose).
