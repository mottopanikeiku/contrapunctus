## Removed or qualified

- The rewrite removed the photographer’s name but retained the Commons file’s 1913 date. The independent check below corrects that date from the Finnish Heritage Agency’s record of the original photograph.
- No claim that the Eighth’s complete score certainly burned. The evidence establishes extensive work and a later fire, not a recoverable inventory.
- No claim that Kajanus’s political interpretation of the Second was Sibelius’s authorised programme.
- Both score examples remain explicitly schematic; neither is represented as a quotation from a symphony.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 38 claim groups: the 32 entries above, plus the birth/death particulars, portrait, artwork media and collections, Vänskä’s complete-cycle attribution, and both schematic score captions. Repeated claims in the life, sound, works and lineage sections were checked together. The symphony and orchestral-work pages linked above were fetched independently; their chronology, instrumentation, keys, thematic descriptions and score examples support the retained musical details.

### Changes

- Portrait caption “Jean Sibelius in 1913” → “Jean Sibelius in 1906”. The Commons image is a crop of the photograph catalogued by the Finnish Heritage Agency as Daniel Nyblin, Helsinki, **12 September 1906**; the images were fetched and compared directly. The 1913 date belongs to the cited book reproduction, not the sitting. The protected image filename remains unchanged. [Original negative, HK19321130:15583-1906](https://www.finna.fi/Record/museovirasto.FD6773548C49AAC825355F704E183A0B); [Commons reproduction and publication provenance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jean_Sibelius,_1913.jpg).
- Eighth Symphony’s “surviving fragments” → the documented copying of twenty-three pages by Paul Voigt in 1933. The original wording treated disputed sketch identifications as settled. Correspondence and the copyist’s receipt provide firm evidence without that assumption. [Sibelius chronology](https://www.sibelius.klubi.fi/english/elamankaari/sib_kahdeksas_sinfonia.htm); [League of American Orchestras report quoting editor Timo Virtanen’s qualified attribution](https://symphony.org/possible-sketches-of-sibeliuss-eighth-symphony-discovered/).
- Ledger-only correction: Heifetz’s extended scale “E to G” → “E♭ to G”. The composer page already avoided naming the pitches. [Richard Ginell, The Strad](https://www.thestrad.com/reviews/fire-and-ice-the-best-recordings-of-the-sibelius-violin-concerto/7351.article).

### Quotations confirmed

- “Jean is now my musical name”: Sibelius to his uncle Pehr, 31 March 1886, in the Finnish Sibelius website’s published English translation. [Helsinki studies](https://www.sibelius.klubi.fi/english/elamankaari/sib_opinnot_helsinki.htm).
- “Wake up Janne, wake up and see what you are!”: Aino to Jean, 4 December 1904, following his drunken telephone call. [Ainola years](https://www.sibelius.klubi.fi/english/elamankaari/sib_ainola.htm).
- “strictness and style and deep logic”: Sibelius’s retrospective account of his conversation with Mahler in Helsinki in 1907; retained as a recollection, not a contemporary transcript. [Ainola years](https://www.sibelius.klubi.fi/english/elamankaari/sib_ainola.htm).
- “One of the great experiences of my life! God, how beautiful”: the published translation of Sibelius’s diary entry of 21 April 1915. The fuller entry also confirms sixteen swans circling above him and the connection with the Fifth’s finale. [Fifth and war years](https://www.sibelius.klubi.fi/english/elamankaari/sib_viides_sinfonia.htm); [diary extract](https://www.sibelius.klubi.fi/english/omin_sanoin/ominsanoin_14.htm).
- “my husband appeared calmer and his attitude was more optimistic”: Aino’s later recollection of manuscript burning in the 1940s. No exact date of the fire or inventory of destroyed works is asserted. [Destruction of the Eighth](https://www.sibelius.klubi.fi/english/elamankaari/sib_kahdeksannen_tuhoaminen.htm).
- “without permission”: Vaughan Williams’s dedication of his Fifth Symphony to Sibelius, premiered in 1943. [Robert Matthew-Walker, Hyperion](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dw.asp?dc=W20128_311900).
