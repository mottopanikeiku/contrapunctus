## Discrepancies noted and how they are handled

- *Wonderful Town*: the timeline gives 26 February 1953 and the work page 25 February; the chapter gives only the year.
- *Dybbuk*: the timeline gives the première on 16 May 1974; the About page dates the work 1975. The works list uses the première date.
- Serenade première: 12 September 1954 (Office); some secondary sources give 11 September.
- Cadmus’s painting: Commons says oil on canvas, the Office says oil, and search snippets from the Met and Naval History and Heritage Command say tempera. The chapter gives “Painting on canvas”. The Met essay and the Navy page could not be opened (HTTP 429 and a certificate error). The Whitney’s page supplies the 1934 notoriety and the Navy officials’ objection.
- Orchestra of the 1956 Serenade recording: see `bernstein-recordings.md`.

## Sound aspects

- Tonality: Office, Chichester Psalms page (1977 remark and the poem for the *Times*); Office, Symphony No. 2 page (Dirge note); Office, Mass page (idioms listed).
- The tritone: Gottlieb’s guide (“Throughout the entire score the interval of the tritone is prominently displayed”; “A Boy Like That” and “I Have a Love” “using precisely the same pitches and almost the same rhythm”, paraphrased as “nearly the same”).
- Metre: BSO note on “America” (6/8 and 3/4, “DA-da-da DA-da-da | DA DA DA”); Office, Chichester Psalms page (7/4; Psalm 131 in 10/4).
- Jazz and dance bands: Office, Fancy Free page (Gottlieb’s note; Bernstein’s plot summary; piano as a solo instrument); Office, Prelude, Fugue and Riffs page (first performance on *Omnibus*, 16 October 1955); Symphony No. 2 page (Masque).
- Texts: Office pages for Jeremiah (Lamentations in Hebrew), Kaddish, Chichester Psalms, Serenade (“no literal program”) and Symphony No. 2 (the “theatre music” remark from Bernstein’s programme note).
- Revision and reuse: Symphony No. 2 page (1965); Candide page (“six versions available to license”); Chichester Psalms page.

## Notation

The three examples are labelled as invented schematics and quote no Bernstein score; the works are in copyright and no score from the public domain exists.

1. A rising tritone, C to F♯, resolved up a semitone: the interval named in Gottlieb’s guide (“C, for example, to F♯ consists of three whole-steps”).
2. Alternating 6/8 and 3/4 bars: the pattern in the BSO note on “America”.
3. A rising minor seventh, B♭ to A♭: the interval named in the Office’s page for the *Chichester Psalms*. The pitches are chosen for the key the Office’s description implies (“B-flat majorish”) and are not stated by any source.

**Verified.** All dates, venues, scorings and premiere details in the life, works table, geo and influence files except the points below. Quotations checked for wording, speaker, occasion and date: Samuel Bernstein (*New Yorker*, 1958), Bernstein to Burton (interview of 20 November 1989; the druggist gave him “two little pills”), the Young People’s Concerts remark, both fragments of the Madison Square Garden remarks of 25 November 1963, Hussey, the 1977 press-conference remarks, the Dirge, Masque and “theatre music” remarks on Symphony No. 2, the “almost autobiographical protagonist” remark, “no literal program”, “a juke box wailing behind the curtain”. **Corrected.**

1. Hussey’s letter is of early December 1963. The Office page does not say that the “hint of *West Side Story*” sentence was in that letter, so the chapter now states the two separately.
2. The sabbatical was the 1964–65 season (Office Young People’s Concerts page; LOC chronology), not “1965”.
3. Felicia: the Daily Beast page opened here does not show the letter or any editors’ note, so “separated in 1976 and returned after her lung cancer was diagnosed” could not be sourced to it. The LOC chronology gives “1976 Trial separation”, “1977 Reconciliation”, “1978, June 16 Felicia Bernstein died”; the Office says she died of cancer. The English Wikipedia article on Bernstein (citing Simeone and Burton) adds a 1977 lung-cancer diagnosis, but the article on Felicia says breast cancer that had spread to the lungs, so the chapter now says only “cancer”. The Simeone bibliography note no longer claims that the Daily Beast reproduces an editors’ note, and says only that the letter is undated and was placed by the Daily Beast around the 1951 marriage.
4. *Jeremiah*: the Office says Bernstein entered it in the New England Conservatory competition (deadline 31 December 1942) and did not win, and that in May 1944 the New York Music Critics Circle voted it “outstanding new classical work of the season”. The works table said “written for” the competition and “best new American orchestral work”.
5. *Songfest* sets thirteen poets (Office list; Gottlieb’s programme note says 13), not twelve.
6. *Halil*: the Office timeline gives Tel Aviv for the première, the work page gives Sultan’s Pool, Jerusalem. The place was removed.

**Kept, with the evidence.**

- “Freiheit” for “Freude”: the Office’s Berlin Wall page (Geitel) calls it the “Ode to Freedom” but does not print the German words. The English Wikipedia article on Beethoven’s Ninth states the substitution at the Konzerthaus concert (it adds that Bernstein used “Freiheit” in two performances in 1989). The chapter keeps the sentence.
- Gottlieb as “music adviser”: the Office’s Serenade page says “longtime Bernstein music advisor Jack Gottlieb”; the Milken Archive adds that he was Bernstein’s Philharmonic assistant from 1958 to 1966.
- Chichester Psalms recording: the Office credits “℗1978 Polydor International GmbH, 415 965-2”; the NYPL catalogue and Presto list it as Deutsche Grammophon, so the label is kept.
- Candide recording: the Office Discography dates the Deutsche Grammophon recording 18 December 1989 and places it at Abbey Road; the Barbican concerts were in December 1989 (première date 12 December on the work page; 13 December on the YouTube video description). The chapter says only “December 1989”.
- Blitzstein as a friend after the Harvard *Cradle Will Rock*: marc-blitzstein.org says they met in 1939 when Bernstein led the Harvard production and calls it an “instant and lifelong” friendship. Ozawa as student and assistant: Baltimore Sun, 10 October 1990. Mauceri and the 1982 *Candide*: Office Candide page.
- *New Yorker* issue dates: the website dates the two parts 4 and 11 January 1958, the print issues 11 and 18 January.

**Not opened.** The Green-Wood page (curl failed), the OUPblog excerpt (HTTP 403), the *New York Times* obituary of Felicia Bernstein (403), and the text of Felicia’s letter at the Daily Beast (the page returned only its standfirst).
