## Editorial decisions

The mechanical finger-device story is omitted: the hand disability is documented, its precise cause is not established by the sources used. No clinical explanation is offered for the music’s changes of mood. The final paragraph reports the institutional facts and Clara’s exclusion plainly. Score captions retain the two examples exactly and identify their schematic nature.

## Fact-check (independent)

Coverage: **62 reader-facing claim groups checked**, including all factual components of the two identity entries, epithet, portrait caption, four facts, eighteen life paragraphs/asides, six sound sections, sixteen works, two score captions, three art entries, five recordings, lineage note and three reading entries. Repeated claims were checked wherever they appear, not counted again as independent historical discoveries. The ten quotation/quoted-text units are itemised below. Titles, opus numbers, forces, dates, counts, première statements, relationships, musical descriptions and art/recording metadata were included; aesthetic judgments remain editorial judgments. Wikipedia was not used as verification.

Result: **six corrected or clarified claim groups; no unresolved factual claims and no wrong protected identity/timeline dates found.** Protected ABC, image filenames, poem originals, reading list, IDs and lineage arrays were not changed. No tests, build, formatter, validator or art tooling was run.

### Quotations confirmed

- “sketches, beginnings of études” — Schumann’s public review of Chopin’s Op. 28, 1839; own translation of “Skizzen, Etüdenanfänge”: [German excerpt](https://www.koelnklavier.de/texte/komponisten/chopin-op28-58.html).
- “ruins, isolated eagle’s wings, all in a colourful, wild jumble” — same review; revised own translation of “Ruinen, einzelne Adlerfittiche, alles bunt und wild durcheinander”: [German excerpt](https://www.koelnklavier.de/texte/komponisten/chopin-op28-58.html).
- “Let each seek what suits him” — Schumann addressing his review’s readers, not a private addressee; “so suche jeder, was ihm frommt”: [German excerpt](https://www.koelnklavier.de/texte/komponisten/chopin-op28-58.html). The [journal contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Neue_Zeitschrift_f%C3%BCr_Musik/Inhaltsverzeichnis_1834%E2%80%931844_Nummern) independently identify R. S., 19 November 1839, pp. 161–163, including the Preludes on pp. 162–163.
- “a deep lament for you” — Robert to Clara Wieck, 18 March 1838, specifically the Fantasie’s first movement; “eine tiefe Klage um Dich”: [Henle critical preface](https://www.henle.de/media/04/6c/e5/1690899702/9926-1690899702-sync.pdf).
- “composing and writing, laughing and weeping all at once” — Robert to Clara, Vienna, 11 March 1839, on Humoreske; “componirte und schrieb und lachte und weinte durcheinander”: [Henle critical preface](https://www.henle.de/media/04/6c/e5/1690899702/9926-1690899702-sync.pdf).
- “inner voice” / innere Stimme — the Humoreske score marking, not a quotation invented for Clara; her explanatory letter is to Georg Henschel, 14 May 1883, and says the line is imagined or inwardly hummed, not played: [Henle critical preface](https://www.henle.de/media/04/6c/e5/1690899702/9926-1690899702-sync.pdf).
- “an orchestra of lamenting and loudly rejoicing voices” — Schumann describing Brahms’s piano playing to the journal’s readers, Neue Bahnen, 28 October 1853; “ein Orchester von wehklagenden und lautjubelnden Stimmen”: [original article and scan](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Neue_Bahnen).
- “veiled symphonies” — Schumann describing Brahms’s sonatas in the same public article, 28 October 1853; “verschleierte Symphonien”: [original article and scan](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Neue_Bahnen).
- Heine’s eight-line “Im wunderschönen Monat Mai” — original wording, authorship, 1822–23 composition dating and 1827 collection confirmed; the existing own translation preserves the sense: [1827 text and scan, p. 112](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Im_wundersch%C3%B6nen_Monat_Mai).
- Hoffmann’s “Die Musik schließt dem Menschen …” — wording matches the linked modernised German text; own translation retains its sense, and the later first-person narrator explicitly names himself Kapellmeister Kreisler: [complete essay](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/eta-hoffmann/books/e-t-a-hoffmann-kreisleriana/chapter/6/). [Staatsbibliothek’s catalogue](https://etahoffmann.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/leben-und-werk/literat/literarische-werke/) dates the first Kreisleriana group to 1810–14; the [historical text](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Beethovens_Instrumental-Musik) identifies this essay as no. 4 of that group and documents the earlier 1813 periodical publication.
