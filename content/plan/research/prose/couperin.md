# Couperin — quotation and fact sources

English quotations in the page are our translations of the French passages identified below. Sources were searched for and read during the rewrite. Musical comparisons with the paintings are editorial comparisons, not claims that Couperin illustrated Watteau. The two ABC examples are preserved teaching schematics.

## Corrections and exclusions

- Replaced the exact 1714 date and the claim that the surviving lessons were written for the Longchamp nuns with the CMBV edition’s securely bounded 1713–17 publication and its distinction between the earlier Good Friday commission and the surviving Wednesday set.
- Removed the engraver’s disputed given name. The engraving, Bouys relationship and 1735 date remain.
- No proposed explanation of the Barricades title, invented Italian alias, direct Watteau collaboration or identification of *La Pompe funèbre* with a named deceased person is presented as fact.

### Confirmed (source, one line each)

- Sundays at court, Duval, Philidor, Alarius, Dubois, “presque tous les dimanches de l’année”, titles known at court in 1714 and 1715: *Concerts royaux* preface (1722), original in [Wursten](https://www.dick.wursten.be/couperin1.htm). Third concert has the Allemande, Sarabande grave and Chaconne légère named in the opening paragraph (same page).
- “J’ay toûjours eu un objet en composant toutes ces piéces”; “des espéces de portraits … assés ressemblans sous mes doigts”; “j’ayme beaucoup mieux ce qui me touche que ce qui me surprend”; vigorous hands not always best for tender pieces; “on ne peut enfler ny diminuer ses sons”; copyists, court duties, teaching, illness, engraving: first-book preface, [Behrend transcription](https://www.cantab.net/users/michael.behrend/repubs/couperin/pages/main.html).
- Nine lessons for one and two voices, the three of the first day already engraved (second-book preface, 1717); third-book comma sign, “sans augmentation ni diminution”; fourth-book preface (“il y a environ trois ans”, friends advising him to stop, “si les regrets nous servent a quelque chose apres la Vie”): same transcription.
- Good Friday lessons were the ones written for Longchamp; the published Wednesday lessons were not commissioned; composed and engraved between 1713 and 1717: Julien Dubruque’s historical notes, [CMBV CAH.190](https://boutique.cmbv.fr/sites/default/files/cmbv/ged/import/CAHIERS-190.pdf), p. 4.
- First two lessons for a single voice, third for two high voices, Hebrew letters set as melismas: [fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%A7ons_de_t%C3%A9n%C3%A8bres_%28Fran%C3%A7ois_Couperin%29).
- *L’Art de toucher*: something under young pupils’ feet, elbows/wrists/fingers level, mirror on the desk, teacher takes away the key, “trois quarts d’heure”, pieces by heart before tablature, aspiration (detach the note) and suspension (silence before the note), trill begins on the note above, French players dot written-equal quavers: [Sceaux transcription](https://musebaroque.fr/archives/Partitions/Couperin_clavecin2.pdf).
- *Les Nations* preface: first sonata composed in France, relative at the court of the King of Sardinia, letters of his name rearranged into an Italian name, “dévorée avec empressement”, great suites added to the sonatas: [Wursten](https://www.dick.wursten.be/couperin1.htm); English rendering in [Hewitt’s notes](https://www.portlandpiano.org/program-notes/angela-hewitt-february-5-6-2022).
- Lalande’s interim, Thomelin, Saint-Gervais from 1685, royal chapel by competition in 1693 (January quarter), organ masses of 1690 circulated in manuscript, parish offertory as slow opening, ricercar and gigue, 27 ordres, *Folies françoises* as variations, *Pièces de violes* second suite framing the *Pompe funèbre*, *La Paix du Parnasse* as closing trio, Marie-Madeleine at Maubuisson and Marguerite-Antoinette harpsichordist of the king’s chamber: [Larousse](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/musdico/Fran%C3%A7ois_Couperin/167027). Chamber harpsichordist from 1717, Louis Couperin at Saint-Gervais from 1653 and dead in 1661, forty-five/first book: [Hewitt](https://www.portlandpiano.org/program-notes/angela-hewitt-february-5-6-2022).
- Second suite of the *Pièces de violes*: Prélude, Fuguette, *Pompe funèbre*, *La Chemise blanche* (last two movements). [Savall’s note](https://alia-vox.com/it/producte/francois-couperin-pieces-de-violes-1728/) confirms 1728 and the two movements; the movement list comes from a catalogue listing seen as a search extract (earsense.org, not opened).
- Lully (top part, French violin clef) and Corelli (middle part) share the trio of the *Apothéose de Lully*; titles of the movements: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Apoth%C3%A9ose_de_Lully) (Hyperion’s note was seen only as a search extract; not opened).
- Rousset’s complete Couperin is Harmonia Mundi HMC 901442/52 (1993–95): [harpsichord discography](https://www.medieval.org/emfaq/velde/harps.htm). Nelson, Kirkby, Jane Ryan (viola da gamba), Hogwood, ℗ 1977: [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/couperin-f-trois-lecons-de-tenebres-motet-pour-le-jour-de-paques-jane-ryan/0002894787129). Savall, Maurette and Koopman: [Alia Vox](https://alia-vox.com/it/producte/francois-couperin-pieces-de-violes-1728/) (AVSA9893, a 2012 reissue; the original 1976 issue was on Astrée per search listings of Discogs/CDandLP, not opened).
