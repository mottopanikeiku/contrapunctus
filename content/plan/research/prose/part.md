## Removed or narrowed claims

- Removed the statement that Pärt **began** *Da pacem Domine* exactly two days after 11 March 2004. The checked Centre account instead says it was **completed** a few days after the attacks. The page now gives the securely documented commission, chant and memorial-concert context.
- Removed the transient ninety-first-birthday sentence and unsupported generalisation about the current catalogue, replacing them with the documented return and archive. The age itself follows from the preserved birth date.
- Kept the 1992 *Te Deum* revision because the publisher explicitly documents it; the Centre’s additional 2007 revision is noted above, not treated as a contradiction.
- Preserved all ABC, poem originals, image file names, identity fields, reading entries and lineage arrays. The score captions expressly identify both examples as schematics.

## Fact-check (independent)

Independently checked 35 claim groups: the 32 rows above, both schematic score captions and the Latin antiphon with its translation. The Centre’s archival articles, work entries and biography, the Restagno interview, the publisher’s catalogue, all four ECM album entries and the image records were fetched again. The dates and personnel distinguish premières from subsequent recordings. No protected notation, poem text, image filename or timeline date was changed.

### Correction

- Rublev collection: ‘transferred from the Tretyakov Gallery in 2024’ → ‘returned to the Lavra in 2024’. The transfer agreement was signed in summer 2023; the icon reached the Trinity Cathedral on 22 June 2024 after an intervening stay at the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour. [Russian Orthodox Church, contemporary report](https://mospat.ru/en/news/91906/). The [Tretyakov Gallery’s own caption](https://www.tretyakovgallery.ru/events/o/lektsiya-andrey-rublev-freski-i-ikony-uspenskogo-sobora-vo-vladimire-ikona-svyatoy-troitsy-01-2026/) confirms the retained c. 1425–27 date.

### Quotations confirmed

- ‘I just wanted a simple musical line, internally alive and breathing’ — Arvo Pärt to Enzo Restagno; exact wording in the [published interview excerpt](https://www.arvopart.ee/en/arvo-part/article/i-seek-a-common-denominator/). The page correctly identifies 2010 as the book’s publication year rather than a date of conversation.
- ‘Never again have I experienced the silence that took over the assembly hall after the premiere’ — Nora Pärt to Immo Mihkelson, Klassikaraadio’s *Arvo Pärt 70*, part 7 (2005); exact wording and provenance in the [Centre’s *Tabula rasa* entry](https://www.arvopart.ee/en/arvo-part/work/537/).
- ‘It was supposed to look like a voluntary decision’ — Nora Pärt to Restagno; exact wording in the [same published interview excerpt](https://www.arvopart.ee/en/arvo-part/article/i-seek-a-common-denominator/).
- ‘Da pacem, Domine … nisi tu, Deus noster’ — complete Latin wording confirmed against [Cantus Database, chant 002090, Köln 215, fol. 157v](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/334170/); the page’s English is a faithful translation. The ninth-century attribution and Pärt’s use of the melody are confirmed by the [Centre](https://www.arvopart.ee/en/arvo-part/work/182/).

### Stronger corroboration

- The *Passio* punctuation rules, character-specific durations and description of Jesus as lowest and slowest were checked directly in Paul Hillier, *Arvo Pärt* (Oxford University Press, 1997), pp. 126–32, through the [readable book scan](https://www.scribd.com/document/448690387/Hillier-P-Arvo-Part-pdf), rather than relying on the Wikipedia summary. [David Lee’s Dunedin Consort programme note](https://www.dunedin-consort.org.uk/passio-text-bit/) independently confirms the punctuation-based rhythm.
- Rublev’s Abrahamic visitors and Trinitarian subject are also identified in the [2024 institutional report](https://mospat.ru/en/news/91906/); the retained tempera-on-wood medium is recorded in the [exact image’s museum-sourced metadata](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Andrey_Rublev_-_%D0%A1%D0%B2._%D0%A2%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).
