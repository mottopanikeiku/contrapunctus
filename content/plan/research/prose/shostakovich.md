## Editorial handling

- Removed the old suggestion that practical rehearsal difficulties helped explain the Fourth’s withdrawal: no sufficiently specific evidence for that causal claim was established. Kept the documented management intervention and eventual première.
- Removed compulsory-rejoicing and Stalin-portrait explanations from the main musical narrative. The latter remains only as the subject of the clearly attributed *Testimony* controversy. No contested memoir statement supplies either of the required quotations.
- Other compressed details, including separate descriptions of the Eighth Symphony’s passacaglia and the former general sentence about the 1936 correspondence, were omitted for narrative economy, not declared false. The 1936 letters do document distress and reduced income.
- Recording recommendations are editorial judgements grounded in the reviews and archival material named above. No fresh audition was performed or claimed. Their session metadata and the protected reading list were retained.
- Protected biographical identifiers, image file strings and portrait focus, all three ABC strings, Gogol’s original Russian, the reading array and lineage arrays were left unchanged. `featured` was removed.
- Integration verification: the three-composer validator run printed `ok` and one house-style warning for “journey”. That word was removed, and `node tools/validate.mjs content/composers/shostakovich.mjs` then printed `ok`: three scores and three images checked, with no warnings or errors. The life contains 1,312 words and three margin notes; six sound sections contain 57–62 words each. No build, formatter, art-fetch or git command was run.

### Correction

- **“The First Violin Concerto and From Jewish Folk Poetry, written that year” → “The First Violin Concerto and From Jewish Folk Poetry, completed that year”.** The year is 1948, but the concerto was composed in **1947–48**, not wholly in 1948. Harlow Robinson’s [Boston Symphony note](https://www.bso.org/works/shostakovich-violin-concerto-no-1) states both the composition span and the 1955 première; [DSCH’s 1948 chronology](https://shostakovich-en.ru/59en) dates completion to 24 March. [DSCH’s Op. 79 entry](https://www.shostakovich.ru/183ru) confirms the song cycle’s 1948 composition and 15 January 1955 public première. The previous ledger’s explanation of “written that year” has been replaced to match the corrected wording.

### Confirmed quotations

- **“I’ve even started dreaming about this music”** — Shostakovich to **Boleslav Yavorsky, Leningrad, 18 October 1925**. [DSCH’s Russian chronology](https://shostakovich.ru/36ru) reproduces “Мне эта музыка даже во сне стала сниться.” It also confirms the same film running for five weeks and his repeated accompaniment. The English is the page’s own translation, not an unattributed published English translation.
- **“I was thinking that if I ever die, hardly anyone will write a work in my memory. So I decided to write one myself.”** — Shostakovich to **Isaak Glikman, Zhukovka, 19 July 1960**. [DSCH’s Russian chronology](https://shostakovich.ru/71ru) gives “Я размышлял о том, что если я когда-нибудь помру, то вряд ли кто напишет произведение, посвященное моей памяти. Поэтому я сам решил написать таковое.” The own translation is faithful. The same letter confirms the listed self-quotations and tears while playing; the [Gohrisch festival’s account](https://www.schostakowitsch-tage.de/en/shostakovich/string-quartet-no-8/) independently reproduces the passage.
- **“To my little friend Mitya Shostakovich, from the artist”** — **Boris Kustodiev’s inscription to Shostakovich, Petrograd, 1919**. The [memorial-apartment catalogue](https://shostakovich.ru/20ru) transcribes “Моему маленькому другу Мите Шостаковичу от автора”. It confirms the sanguine/charcoal medium, the separate 1923 Gaspra portrait and the present collection. The inscription remains an own translation.
- **“You are mistaken, my dear sir. I am independent. / Besides, there can be no close connection between us. / To judge by the buttons on your undress uniform, you must serve in the Senate, or at least in the justice department. / I, however, serve in education.”** — **the nose addressing Kovalyov**, in Gogol’s *The Nose*, section II. All four protected Russian sentences and the own English translation were checked against the [Academy of Sciences collected edition reproduced by the Russian Virtual Library](https://rvb.ru/gogol/01text/vol_03/01povesti/0044.htm). The passage belongs to the Kazan Cathedral encounter. David McDuff’s [Gale reference article](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/nose-nos-nikolai-gogol-1836) confirms the story’s **1836** date and the rank-based encounter; [Boosey & Hawkes](https://www.boosey.com/opera/moreDetails?musicID=896) confirms the operatic tenor/baritone casting.
- **“Muddle Instead of Music”** — the title of the **unsigned Pravda editorial, 28 January 1936**, not words attributed personally to Stalin. [DSCH’s reproduction](https://shostakovich.ru/47ru) supplies “Сумбур вместо музыки”; [Harlow Robinson’s BSO note](https://www.bso.org/works/symphony-no-5-shostakovich) uses the same conventional English title.
