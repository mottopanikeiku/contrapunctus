# Edvard Grieg — quotation and fact sources

Checked 18 October 2026. This ledger supports `content/composers/grieg.mjs`, `content/bibliography/grieg.json`, `content/geo/grieg.json`, `content/elsewhere/grieg.json` and `content/influence/grieg.json`. The hosted recordings and their rights are in `grieg-recordings.md`. Norwegian quotations are translated by the research desk; the original wording is given where the translation matters.

Principal source: Finn Benestad, “Edvard Grieg”, [Norsk biografisk leksikon](https://nbl.snl.no/Edvard_Grieg) (NBL), read in full. Benestad edited Grieg’s letters and wrote the standard biography. Secondary sources are named where used. Wikipedia was used only to find leads; nothing rests on it.

## Sound aspects

- Folk imitation rather than quotation in the Lyric Pieces, with gangar, halling and springar titles; Hardanger fiddle springar in the Op. 8 sonata; *Slåtter*: NBL.
- “Hårreisende akkordforbindelser” written at Fossli above the Vøringsfoss, born “in the head”: Grieg to Julius Röntgen, quoted in NBL. *Klokkeklang*, Op. 54 No. 6: NBL (“harmonisk sett uten sidestykke i samtiden”); fifth-based sonorities: Grimley 2016, n. 25 citing Sutcliffe.
- Songs: about 170; “alle skrevet for henne”; the poet’s “hemmeligste intensjoner”: Grieg to Finck, quoted in NBL. *Haugtussa*, eight songs published from twenty set: NBL.
- Orchestra: John Henken, Hollywood Bowl note, on the Mountain King as “a supremely well-crafted exercise in orchestral color and dynamics”; BSO note on the *Holberg* suite.

## Notation

1. **Grieg motif — schematic.** A–G♯–E and A–G–E in A minor; the two shapes named by NBL. Labelled schematic.
2. **Mountain King, bars 1–4.** Quoted from Mutopia’s typesetting of The University Society edition (1918), [Dans_l_antre_du_roi_de_la_montagne.ly](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/GriegE/O46/Dans_l_antre_du_roi_de_la_montagne/Dans_l_antre_du_roi_de_la_montagne.ly): B minor, 4/4, ♩ = 138, pp, “e sempre staccato”. Bar 1 b–c♯–d–e–f♯(accent)–d–f♯; bar 2 e♯(accent)–c♯–e♯, e(accent)–c♮–e; bar 3 as bar 1 with a final b; bar 4 a–f♯–d–f♯–a(accent, half note). Octave adjusted, melody only. Bar lengths checked with `tools/barcheck.mjs` (no complaint).
3. **Troldtog, Op. 54 No. 3, bars 2–5, right hand.** Quoted from Mutopia’s typesetting of the Peters edition of about 1891, [Troldtog.ly](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/GriegE/O54/Troldtog/Troldtog.ly): D minor, 2/4, Allegro moderato; the run d–e–f–g into a, then chords D–F–top note descending d, c♯, c, b, b♭, a in pairs. Left hand omitted; the printed metronome mark is absent, so ♩ = 100 is for playback only.

Not used: an example from the Piano Concerto. The Grainger-edited Schirmer two-piano edition (1920, archive.org `concertoforpianoo00grie`) was read for the opening page but its dense two-piano reduction was not transcribed.

## Not used, and why

- Debussy’s remark (“a pink bonbon stuffed with snow”) appears only in the lineage note, sourced to Finck 1909, p. 224, n. 1 (Finck does not give the original; Debussy’s own text was not opened). A claim, repeated by one blog, that Shaw said it of the concerto is not used.
- Grieg’s “first forte” remark on Morning Mood is quoted through Henken’s note and not found in a primary source.
- Marston’s remark that Grieg did not play the concerto after 1889 was not checked and is not used.
- The Presto, HathiTrust, OUP, UC Davis and Musopen pages returned HTTP 403 and are not cited.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026 against the sources below, each opened. NBL = Benestad, [Norsk biografisk leksikon](https://nbl.snl.no/Edvard_Grieg), re-read in full.

**Corrected**

- *Paris, April 1903.* The page said a Paris audience “hissed him off the stage”, repeating the Marston liner note (“hissed off the stage”). Jean-Michel Nectoux, [“Grieg. The Paris stay of 1903”](https://griegsociety.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Jean-Michel-Nectoux-paper-2000.pdf) (Grieg Society, 2000), describes the concert of Sunday 19 April at the Théâtre du Châtelet (Concerts Colonne): whistles and shouts of “A la porte” and “A bas Dreyfus”; Grieg left the podium until police had removed the agitators, began again amid protests, and the concert “turned to be a triumph”. The composer page and the Paris entry in `geo/grieg.json` now say this, with Nectoux added as a source. Debussy’s own review (*Gil Blas*, 20 April 1903; the abridged *Monsieur Croche* text, archive.org `monsieurcrochean00debu`) confirms the police and the disturbance. Marston remains the source for 2 May 1903, nine ten-inch sides, *Wedding Day* 2:03, and the 13 May 1903 letter to Röntgen (“I was glad to say farewell to France, and especially Paris”), which are unchanged.
- *Nordraak’s grave.* The page said Grieg “took home an oak leaf”. NBL: he took an oak leaf from the grave in Berlin and enclosed it in a long letter to Nordraak’s father. Now worded so.
- *Morning Mood note.* The remark “I imagine the sun breaking through the clouds at the first forte” rests only on John Henken’s Hollywood Bowl note, with no source for Grieg’s words; Grimley 2016, the Naxos/Engeset note quoted by Wikipedia and two further searches (English and Norwegian) gave no primary text. Removed. The Morocco fact (prelude to Act IV; Peer “washed up” on the Moroccan coast) is Grimley 2016, p. 107, and stays.
- *Output row.* “Four sonatas” corrected to five: piano sonata Op. 7, violin sonatas Opp. 8, 13 and 45, cello sonata Op. 36 (NBL works list).
- *Hjertets Melodier.* “A song cycle” changed to “a set of songs”: NBL calls it an engagement present (forlovelsespresang), not a cycle.
- *Lineage note.* “[Schumann’s concerto] was the model” softened to what is documented: Grieg “acknowledged that he had studied Schumann’s piano concerto carefully”, same key, flourish opening, no double exposition (Laurie Shulman, New Jersey Symphony note, which also dates Grieg’s revisions “through 1907”); NBL says Schumann was “trolig” (probably) the model and warns against overstating it. Debussy’s bonbon remark is now given as what he wrote in *Gil Blas*, 1903 — “on a dans la bouche le goût bizarre et charmant d’un bonbon rose qui serait fourré de neige” (Foster, *The Songs of Edvard Grieg*, revised edition, preface, [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/songs-of-edvard-grieg/preface-to-the-revised-edition/0FBFBB6591A961E40D087664E038390B)) — rather than Finck’s 1909 paraphrase (“the sensation of eating”). Debussy’s own page was not opened (the 1921 *Monsieur Croche* text is an abridged version without the sentence); the wording rests on Foster and Finck 1909, p. 224 n. 1. Rachmaninoff: Tchaikovsky Research says he began the First Concerto in March 1891 with Grieg’s concerto as model; the page now says “begun in 1891”.
- *Portrait caption.* “March 1888” removed: it is the Commons description’s date for a carte de visite first published in *The Leisure Hour* (1889); only 1888 is safe. The file is Commons public domain.
- *Recordings.* Apple Music track lists (opened) contradict two notes. The von Otter/Forsberg album (DG, 1993; 25 tracks) holds the eight *Haugtussa* songs, the six German songs of Op. 48 and eleven single songs (including *En Svane*, *Med en Vandlilje*, *Våren*); it has no song from *Hjertets Melodier*, so “including two from *Hjertets Melodier*” was wrong in the composer page and the elsewhere file. The Andsnes *Lyric Pieces* album (2002; 24 tracks) draws on Opp. 12, 38, 47, 54, 57, 62, 65, 68 and 71 — nine books, none from Op. 43 — so “the ten books” was wrong in both places. The Blomstedt *Peer Gynt* album (19 tracks: Act I prelude, Nos. 4, 5, 8–17, 19, 21–24, 26 as listed) and the Andsnes/Jansons concerto album (Grieg Op. 16 and Schumann Op. 54, six tracks, 2003) match their notes.
- *Bibliography.* The Bergen library note now says what the library page and Tchaikovsky Research support: Grieg and Nina bequeathed manuscripts, letters and printed music in 1906; the library has put Grieg’s letters to Tchaikovsky online. The Open Library work OL2994155W is a merged record (Nebraska 1988, Aschehoug 1980/1990, Peer Gynt Press 2000) that includes the Nebraska edition cited; the EBSCO Research Starters page lists the 2001 *Diaries, Articles, Speeches* (Peer Gynt Press, Columbus) in its bibliography.
- *Geo.* Paris entry rewritten (above). All other entries match NBL, Tchaikovsky Research and Finck 1909 (Cambridge, 10 May 1894).

**Verified as written** (wording, speaker, date): the *Min første Succes* passage; Ole Bull’s words; the Nordraak “mist” remark; Neupert’s letter and the 3 April 1869 première; Liszt in Rome, 1870 (Finck 1906, pp. 35–37: the monastery near the Forum, “I will show you that I also cannot”, first part too fast, cadenza best, the G♯/G exclamation across the cloister hall, “Fahren Sie fort…”); Ibsen’s letter of 23 January 1874 (Grimley n. 2) and the Beyer letter (NBL); 36 performances and the fire of 15 January 1877; “Komposten”; Holberg quotation and 3 December 1884 (BSO note by Matthew Mendez); the April 1885 letter to Paulsen; the Brodsky evening of 1 January 1888, Nina’s singing, *Hamlet* “à Edvard Grieg” and “Sympatische Geister wachsen nicht auf den Bäumen!” of 26 May 1888 (Tchaikovsky Research, opened through the Internet Archive because the live site returned HTTP 500); Cambridge 1893/1894; the *Wedding Day* preface (Henle HN 1291: silver wedding, “considerably more guests came than were invited”, autograph title, ten pages); Dreyfus letter to Colonne, four years before returning to France; *Slåtter*, “le nouveau Grieg”; Oxford May 1906; the 13 April 1907 diary entry; hospital 3 September, death early on the 4th; the Röntgen quotation of May 1904 (NBL, with its context: Grieg is asking what “so-called originality” is worth).
- Scores: *Mountain King* bars 1–4 and *Troldtog* bars 2–5 checked note by note and bar by bar against the Mutopia LilyPond sources (Univ. Society 1918; Peters c. 1891); both quotations are faithful, melody/right hand only as labelled. The Grieg-motif example is a labelled schematic and the interval names match NBL (A–G♯–E: falling minor second, major third; A–G–E: falling major second, minor third).
- Images (Commons API): Krøyer double portrait (Nationalmuseum, Stockholm; NBL 1898, Commons “ca. 1899”), Kittelsen *Skogtroll* 1906 (cover of Asbjørnsen, *Illustrerede Eventyr*, new series, 1907), Tidemand and Gude 1848: all public domain, data as stated.
- Influence edges: Liszt (Finck 1906: Liszt’s letter of 29 December 1868 from Rome invites him to Weimar; Grieg had not sent the sonata), Tchaikovsky (Tchaikovsky Research), Wagner (NBL: Bayreuth 1876, eight reports, *Tannhäuser* fourteen times), Schumann (inferred, correctly marked) and Rachmaninoff: each supported by its source.

**Unresolved**

- The “first forte” remark attributed to Grieg by Henken remains untraced; it is out of the text.
- Debussy’s original French sentence was not opened on Gallica; see above.
- “Conductor in Christiania 1866–74”: NBL has Grieg leading the Philharmonic Society for the first year and founding the Musikforeningen in 1871; Finck 1906, p. 38, has him conducting both and leaving the capital in 1874. Kept.
