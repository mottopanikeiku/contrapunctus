## Corrections and exclusions

- Replaced ‘Prix de Rome on his fourth attempt’ with the precise preliminary-failure/four-cantata sequence.
- Replaced ‘complete opera in 1890’ with the supported first staging of both halves.
- Removed the old idée fixe caption’s exact bar number because it could not be securely checked against an accessible full score. The unchanged melodic example is labelled a schematic reduction, with its omissions stated.
- Corrected the memoir’s erroneous 1822 Paris-arrival date against the modern chronology; the page uses 1821.
- Memoir scenes are not silently converted into independently witnessed events, and no unattributed aphorism was introduced.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026 against the primary text of the *Mémoires* (French Wikisource transcription of the 1870 edition), the scholarly Hector Berlioz Website (Michel Austin and Monir Tayeb; chronology, concert tables, work pages), a transcribed full score (movement PDFs on that site), the Cleveland Museum record, Gautier’s printed poem, and standard reference texts for the Fantastique and the Requiem. Labels: MEAS = read in a score or primary text; EXT = reliable external source.

### Changes

- “the unfinished opera *Les Francs-juges*” → “the opera *Les Francs-juges*, which never reached the stage”. The Berlioz Website chronology records that he completed the opera in October 1826 and that the libretto was refused by the Opéra; the complete score is lost and only fragments survive. “Unfinished” is unsupported. EXT: [biography chronology](http://www.hberlioz.com/Works/biography.htm); [Les Francs-Juges work page](http://www.hberlioz.com/Scores/sfrancsjuges.htm).
- “after failing the preliminary examination in 1826, he submitted four cantatas in successive years before winning in 1830” → “…he entered a cantata every year from 1827 and won in 1830 with the fourth”. The four cantatas are *La Mort d’Orphée* (1827, ruled unplayable), *Herminie* (1828, second prize), *Cléopâtre* (1829, no prize awarded) and *Sardanapale* (1830, first prize); the winning piece is the fourth, not a fifth after four. EXT: [Institut de France page, “The search for the Prix de Rome”](http://www.hberlioz.com/Paris/BPInstitut.html); [Conservatoire page](http://www.hberlioz.com/Paris/BPConservatoire.html) (“four attempts”).
- “writing in London on 10 April 1848” → “writing in London in the spring of 1848”. The “tyrannie des habitudes des doigts” sentence is in chapter IV before the paragraph dated 10 April (the Chartist demonstration); the chapter as a whole was written in London that spring. MEAS: [*Mémoires* IV](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/04).
- “He scarcely understood the spoken English, but knew the plays in translation” → “He knew no English and had read Shakespeare only in Letourneur’s French translation”. Berlioz: “je ne savais pas alors un seul mot d’anglais … je n’entrevoyais Shakespeare qu’à travers … la traduction de Letourneur”. MEAS: [*Mémoires* XVIII](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/18).
- “She did not come to the first performance” → “She was not in the hall for the first performance”. The 5 December 1830 premiere was timed for her return to Paris, but she was not present; she first heard the symphony on 9 December 1832. EXT: [Symphonie fantastique commentary](http://www.hberlioz.com/Scores/sfantastique.htm) (1832 concert “in the presence of Harriet Smithson”); [Wikipedia, Symphonie fantastique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonie_fantastique) (“She did not attend the premiere”); [BBC Music Magazine](https://www.classical-music.com/features/works/symphonie-fantastique).
- “Paganini had bought a Stradivarius viola” → “Paganini owned a Stradivarius viola”. The memoir has him say “J’ai un alto merveilleux, un instrument admirable de Stradivarius” and gives no purchase. MEAS: [*Mémoires* XLV](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/45).
- Margin note: “Berlioz had learnt much of its craft by rehearsing musicians himself” removed (no source for the causal claim); replaced by “In 1855 Berlioz added a chapter on conducting, and Richard Strauss later expanded the whole book.” EXT: [Berlioz in Paris, conducting section](http://www.hberlioz.com/Paris/berlioz2569e.htm) (*The Art of the Conductor* added to the revised treatise, 1855); [Treatise on Instrumentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise_on_Instrumentation) (first published 1844, conducting chapter added 1855, Strauss revision 1904–05); [Berlioz Website biography](http://www.hberlioz.com/Works/biography.htm) (publication January 1844).
- “The two halves waited until Karlsruhe in 1890 for a staging” → “Both halves were first staged together at Karlsruhe in December 1890, over two evenings”. Carthage alone had been staged in 1863; Mottl gave the complete work at Karlsruhe on two consecutive evenings because the theatre was small. EXT: [Les Troyens, 1863 page, Epilogue](http://www.hberlioz.com/Troyens/LesTroyens-e.htm).
- Works table, *Les Nuits d’été*: “a flower remembers the night it spent on a young woman’s dress” → “the ghost of a rose visits the young woman who wore it to a ball”. Gautier’s text: “Je suis le spectre d’une rose / Que tu portais hier au bal”; the rose lies on her breast in the last stanza, never on a dress. MEAS: [*La Comédie de la mort* (1838), “Le Spectre de la rose”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Com%C3%A9die_de_la_Mort_(1838)/Le_Spectre_de_la_Rose).
- Delacroix note: “the spinning wheel beside her has stopped” → “…stands beside her”, with “downcast eyes”. The museum’s description gives a seated woman “with downcast eyes and hands in her lap” and a spinning wheel; a still image cannot show that the wheel has stopped. EXT: [Cleveland Museum of Art 1933.145.11](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1933.145.11).

### Quotations confirmed

- “Shakespeare struck me like a thunderbolt”: *Mémoires* XVIII, “Shakespeare, en tombant ainsi sur moi à l’improviste, me foudroya”; the same sentence in Cairns’s translation appears on the Berlioz Website Odéon page. Speaker, recollection and September 1827 setting correct. [*Mémoires* XVIII](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/18); [Odéon page](http://www.hberlioz.com/Paris/BPOdeon.html).
- “the tyranny of finger habits”: *Mémoires* IV, “la tyrannie des habitudes des doigts”. [*Mémoires* IV](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/04).
- “I have to play all the time”: Paganini on seeing the first movement of *Harold*, “il faut que je joue toujours” (preceded by “je me tais trop longtemps là-dedans”). [*Mémoires* XLV](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/45).
- “I had money; Paganini had given it to me to make music, and I made it”: *Mémoires* XLIX, “j’avais de l’argent, Paganini me l’avait donné pour faire de la musique, et j’en fis”. [*Mémoires* XLIX](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/49).
- Father closes the book and says he is tired (“Assez, mon enfant, je suis fatigué !”): *Mémoires* II. [*Mémoires* II](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Hector_Berlioz/02).
- Juliet’s speech (III.5) matches the MIT/Globe text (“yon pomegranate-tree”); Aeneid IV.651–654 matches the Latin Library text. [MIT](https://shakespeare.mit.edu/romeo_juliet/romeo_juliet.3.5.html); [Latin Library](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/vergil/aen4.shtml).
