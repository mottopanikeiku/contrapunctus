### Corrections made

1. **Sound, “Tunes for everyone”.** *Songs of Praise* was published in 1925 (Internet Archive catalogue record, Oxford University Press, 1925; Wikipedia), not 1926. Fixed.
2. **Works, *Hugh the Drover*.** The Royal College of Music gave the first performances (five, 4–11 July 1924, billed as private dress rehearsals); the British National Opera Company’s first professional performance, under Malcolm Sargent, was at His Majesty’s on 14 July 1924 (Hyperion CDD22049 note). The Society’s shorter wording (“first publicly performed in 1924 … BNOC … Sargent”) hid the RCM staging. Rewritten.
3. **Arts, Blake plate.** “Acquired a copy of the *Illustrations of the Book of Job* in 1927” has no support in any source opened (Wikipedia gives 1927 only as the year of Keynes’s scenario). Removed; the rest of the note stands (plate 14 viewed: four winged youths above the seated Creator, the text beneath, “March 8 1825” imprint).
4. **Life, Gloucester.** The Guardian says Howells and Gurney “spent the night walking the streets”; “half the night” was an addition. Fixed.
5. **Header quotation source line.** See the table.
6. **Elsewhere.** The Guardian source title said “15 December 2020”; the first performance was 15 December 1920 (the article is dated 8 Dec 2020 and describes a 2020 re-creation). The CDA68280 note said “offstage natural trumpet”: the Hyperion booklet only says natural trumpet, and the offstage placement belongs to one performance in a review. Now “natural-trumpet fanfare”.
7. **Geo, place of death.** London Remembers (plaque entry, credited to Plaques of London) says “Died at his home, 10 Hanover Terrace, Regent’s Park”; the Society says he lived there from 1953 until his death. Entry changed from inferred to documented and the source added. The chapter’s `died.place` (Hanover Terrace) is therefore supported.

### Checked and left as written

- Birth, father’s death (9 Feb 1875), Leith Hill Place, Charterhouse, RCM, Trinity, Holst (1895), Adeline (married 9 Oct 1897, Hove; first cousin of Virginia Woolf per Society and VWF), St Barnabas 1895–99, Ingrave in December 1903, “over 800 songs and carols” (EFDSS), *English Hymnal* 1904–06 and “four original hymns” (Society), Ravel winter 1907–08: all verified.
- Tallis premiere (6 Sept 1910, about 2,000, Elgar’s *Gerontius* in the second half, aged 37), *Sea Symphony* 12 Oct 1910: verified. The tune: “Third Tune” of Parker’s Psalter (1567), Psalm 2, English Hymnal 92 “When rising from the bed of death” (Schwartz; Wikipedia). Second orchestra of nine (2+2+2+2+1) and a quartet (Schwartz). Ending on G major (Howes, via Wikipedia). The e–f–g–a–g–f–e phrase (Schwartz).
- War service: enlisted New Year’s Eve 1914; left England 22 June 1916; Salonika mid-November; commission March 1917; demobilised 15 Feb 1919 (Society). RCM teaching from 1919 (Wikipedia, citing Kennedy). Bach Choir 1921–28 (the choir’s page). Leith Hill: founded 1905 by Margaret Vaughan Williams and Lady Farrer; conductor 1905–53; first combined concert 10 May 1905 at the Public Hall (Dorking Museum). White Gates 1929, arthritis (VWF Ursula page).
- *Lark Ascending*: written 1914, war delayed it; Hall and Mendham, Shirehampton Public Hall, 15 Dec 1920; Queen’s Hall the following June; cadenza “pianissimo and sur la touche”; 6/8 melody; no folk-song borrowing (Hyperion, Guardian).
- Pastoral: 26 Jan 1922, Boult, RPS; “hardly three moments of fortissimo”; wordless soprano opens the finale. Fourth: 10 April 1935, BBC SO, Boult; dedicated to Bax; “grinding semitonal fortissimo clash”; “four-note figure”; opening returns near the end (all Matthew-Walker). Fifth: Albert Hall, 24 June 1943, LPO; smallest orchestra of the nine; low C pedal and two-horn call (Hyperion).
- Ursula Wood: met 31 March 1938; *Clerk Saunders* ballet scenario; lunch (VWF Ursula page). *Serenade to Music*: 5 Oct 1938, sixteen solo voices (BBC Music Magazine; Society). *Pilgrim’s Progress*: 26 April 1951, Hancock, Coghill; Adeline died 10 May 1951; marriage 7 Feb 1953, St Pancras Vestry Chapel; 10 Hanover Terrace; Ninth, 2 April 1958, Sargent, RPO; funeral 19 Sept 1958, Boult, *Dives and Lazarus*, *Come down, O Love Divine* (Society).
- Works table: *Five Mystical Songs* (Worcester commission, 1911), *London Symphony* (Butterworth, 27 March 1914), *Flos Campi* and *Sancta Civitas* (1925; Society), *Job* (Norwich Festival 23 Oct 1930; Cambridge Theatre 5 July 1931, per Wikipedia), Sinfonia antartica (14 Jan 1953, Barbirolli, Ritchie), *Hodie* (Howells, Worcester, 8 Sept 1954), Eighth (2 May 1956, Barbirolli), Ninth (three saxophones and flugelhorn): all verified. Folk Song Suite, Kneller Hall, 4 July 1923 (Burns; Burgess Hill SO note); Jacob’s orchestration.
- Elsewhere: LSO0900 recorded 17 and 18 April 2024 (No. 5) and 15 December 2024 (No. 9), Barbican Hall, released 26 September 2025 (LSO Live page); YouTube oEmbed author “London Symphony Orchestra”; Hyperion CDA68280 (Watford Colosseum, January 2020); SIGCD486 (Snape Maltings, September 2016, released June 2017).

LSO0900’s note that Pappano’s earlier LSO Live releases were the Fourth and Sixth is on the LSO Live page.
