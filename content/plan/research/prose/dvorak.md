# Dvořák — quotation and fact sources

The three prose quotations below were checked in English-language publications: the contemporary *Sunday Times* interview as transcribed by the Dvořák catalogue, and a rendered scan of the *New York Herald*. No modern translation has been silently presented as Dvořák’s English. The inherited ABC strings, image filenames, poem originals, reading list and lineage arrays are unchanged.

## Editorial decisions

- No core biographical claim was removed for lack of a source. The earlier unsupported psychological inference that opera taught Dvořák a particular list of compositional techniques has been replaced by documented theatre work and score borrowing.
- The interview is used for attributed reminiscences, not as an infallible chronology. Formal dates follow the documentary catalogue.
- Removed defensive discussion about whether Schwaiger’s painting was a direct source and about possible misreadings of Dvořák’s American argument. The page states the actual shared folklore, encounter with Burleigh and press remarks.
- No speculative account of the Largo’s precise *Hiawatha* scene, no supposed American folk-source quotation, and no unverified bird identification has been added.

### Quotations confirmed

- “I belonged to ours as soon as I could fiddle a little” — Dvořák to the *Sunday Times* interviewer signing as Paul Pry, 10 May 1885; same passage for the village band collecting from dancers and the earnings “handed forthwith to my father”: [full text](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/interviews/interview-for-sunday-times/).
- “I was perfectly crazy about him” (Wagner; following him through the streets of Prague) — same interview, answer on *King and Collier*. Wagner’s Prague concert, 8 February 1863: [chronology](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/life/important-dates-in-the-life-of-antonin-dvorak/).
- “These are the folk songs of America and your composers must turn to them.” — *New York Herald*, Sunday 21 May 1893, p. 28, “Real Value of Negro Melodies”, first column, lower half. Read this time from the rendered page of the [scan](https://static.qobuz.com/info/IMG/pdf/NYHerald-1893-Mai-21-Recadre.pdf): “These are the folk songs of America and your composers must turn to them. All of the great musicians have borrowed from the songs of the common people.” Secondary versions (“They are the songs of America”, Houston Symphony) are paraphrases.
- Scherzo “suggested by the scene of the feast where the Indians dance” — *New York Herald* interview with James Creelman, 15 December 1893 ([Oberlin, image record](https://scalar.oberlincollegelibrary.org/decolonizing-ethnomusicology/media/image-december-1893-article-from-the-new-york-herald-on-dvoks-new-world-symphony); wording from the [catalogue entry](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/symphony-no-9/)). Czech translation of *Hiawatha* known before America: [DAHA biography](https://www.dvoraknyc.org/bio); catalogue names Sládek’s translation.

### Facts confirmed (no change)

- Nelahozeves, 8 September 1841; father butcher and innkeeper; Liehmann at Zlonice teaching harmony and instruments; Organ School from 1857, graduation 30 July 1859; viola in the Komzák ensemble, Provisional Theatre orchestra from 18 November 1862 (nine years, left summer 1871); Bendl’s loans including Beethoven’s Septet; *King and Collier* returned as unplayable and rewritten: [biography](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/life/biography/), [chronology](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/life/important-dates-in-the-life-of-antonin-dvorak/), interview above.
- Marriage to Anna Čermáková 17 November 1873; St Adalbert’s post; 400 gulden on 8 January 1875; Brahms on the jury from Dvořák’s second application; Simrock on Brahms’s recommendation: biography (“marriage”, “financial independence”), interview.
- *Slavonic Dances* Op. 46: Simrock asked for Czech dances to match the *Hungarian Dances*; melodies Dvořák’s own; orchestration begun alongside the piano version; No. 1 a furiant in C major, No. 8 a furiant; performances in London and New York in the first year: [Op. 46](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/slavonic-dances-1-for-piano/). Op. 72 ends with a slow sousedská, No. 16: [Op. 72](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/slavonic-dances-series-2-op72-b145/). Simrock’s 1885 request for dances instead of symphonies: [Symphony No. 7](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/symphony-no-7/).
- Josefa (d. 21 August 1875), Růžena (13 August 1877, poisoning), Otakar (a month later, smallpox); *Stabat Mater* piano version February–May 1876, orchestral version completed November 1877, ten movements, bare F♯ octaves, Albert Hall 13 March 1884, “250 sopranos” in the letter to Urbánek of 14 March: [*Stabat Mater*](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/stabat-mater/).
- Symphony No. 7: Philharmonic Society commission, première 22 April 1885 at St James’s Hall, held D and diminished seventh, forty bars cut from the Adagio, Simrock’s 3,000 marks, “Anton”/“Ant.” dispute: [Symphony No. 7](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/symphony-no-7/).
- Vysoká house bought from his brother-in-law; six further children 1878–88: biography (“Vysoká”). Piano Quintet No. 2 (18 August–3 October 1887, much of it at Vysoká; cello first subject, viola second; F♯ minor dumka; finale fugato): [Op. 81](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/piano-quintet-no-2-in-a-major-op-81-b155/). Symphony No. 8 (G minor cello opening, flute theme, trumpet fanfare, flute variation, high horn trilling): [catalogue](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/symphony-no-8/), [BSO, Steinberg](https://www.bso.org/works/dvorak-symphony-no-8), [Minnesota Orchestra, Bromberger](https://res.cloudinary.com/minnesota-orchestral/image/upload/v1709183483/Uploads/PDF/Program-Notes/2023-24/005_March_2024_Dvorak_Symphony_No_8.pdf). *Dumky* (November 1890–February 1891, première 11 April 1891 with Dvořák at the piano; Brahms read the proofs): [Op. 90](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/piano-trio-no-4-dumky-op-90-b166/).
- Thurber’s admissions policy and the 23 January 1894 concert (about fifty student orchestra players under Dvořák, an all-Black choir of 130, Sissieretta Jones, Burleigh), at Madison Square Garden Concert Hall: [DAHA, Whitaker](https://www.dvoraknyc.org/thurber). Burleigh learnt spirituals from his mother and maternal grandfather and sang them to Dvořák; later his copyist: [Library of Congress guide](https://guides.loc.gov/harry-thacker-burleigh).
- Symphony No. 9: 10 January–24 May 1893, première 16 December 1893 under Seidl, Largo for cor anglais with muted strings, reminiscence of earlier themes in the finale, Fisher’s “Goin’ Home” of 1922 (Fisher a former student): [catalogue](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/symphony-no-9/). String Quartet No. 12 (8–23 June 1893, sketch in 72 hours, viola first theme over F major tremolo, Kneisel première 1 January 1894, organ at Mass): [Op. 96](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/string-quartet-no-12-in-f-major/).
- Cello Concerto (8 November 1894–9 February 1895; 86-bar orchestral introduction; *Leave me alone* in the slow movement; after Josefina’s death the longer quiet ending with the song on solo violin; Wihan’s cadenza refused in the letter to Simrock of 3 October 1895): [Op. 104](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/concerto-for-cello-and-orchestra-in-b-minor/).
- *The Water Goblin* (1896; four Erben ballads; phrases built on the rhythm of the verse; Hanslick’s objection to the subjects): [Op. 107](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/tha-water-goblin/). *Rusalka* (21 April–27 November 1900; 31 March 1901; Burian withdrew, delegation headed by Dvořák to Pták’s flat; Gamekeeper and Turnspit mocking Rusalka over a distorted leitmotif): [*Rusalka*](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/rusalka/).
