# Gershwin — quotation and fact sources

Sources consulted 18 October 2026. This evidence covers the new composer page, bibliography and geographic records. Statements about a recording’s interpretation, the visual comparison with Demuth, and the musical effect of an accent are editorial judgements; historical identities, dates and technical descriptions are supported below. Recording-file provenance has its own dossiers.

## Core documentary chronology

**Library of Congress Music Division, *George and Ira Gershwin Collection*, finding aid, revised February 2025:** https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/mu010014/mu010014.pdf . Read the biographical note on pp. 5–12, the collection history on pp. 3–5 and relevant scope statements. The PDF remains accessible when the Library’s HTML finding aid returns HTTP 403.

- P. 5: George born in Brooklyn, 26 September 1898; parents’ Russian-Jewish immigrant background. The page avoids the conflicting spellings of his registered birth name.
- P. 6: family piano acquired about 1910 for Ira; George had experimented on a friend’s player piano. Charles Hambitzer, attendance at Godowsky and Lhévinne concerts, theory with Edward Kilenyi, May 1914 departure from school to work for Jerome H. Remick at fifteen dollars weekly, sight-reading and transposition. Early piano-roll employment is distinguished from sound recording.
- Pp. 6–7: Hambitzer’s lessons extended to his death in 1918; exact start/frequency and Kilenyi’s exact teaching dates are uncertain, so the page supplies no precise lesson chronology. Gershwin left Remick on 17 March 1917, worked as a rehearsal pianist and joined T. B. Harms in 1918.
- P. 7: *Swanee*, Irving Caesar, the Capitol Revue, Jolson’s hearing the song at a party and using it in *Sinbad*; summer 1921 Columbia University courses with Rossetter G. Cole.
- P. 8: first European trip for *The Rainbow*, opening London 3 April 1923; *Rhapsody in Blue* première at Aeolian Hall on 12 February 1924; *Lady, Be Good!* opening 1 December 1924 with Fred and Adele Astaire; Concerto in F première at Carnegie Hall, 3 December 1925, New York Symphony Society, Damrosch and Gershwin. The concerto and later orchestral works, including the opera, were orchestrated by Gershwin.
- Pp. 8–9: George read *Porgy* in 1926; *Oh, Kay!* starred Gertrude Lawrence; *An American in Paris* took shape over the 1926 and 1928 European trips. The 1928 itinerary included London, Paris, Berlin and Vienna. Its Carnegie Hall première was 13 December 1928, with the Philharmonic-Symphony Society of New York and Damrosch. The official Gershwin web timeline gives a different orchestra name; the archival finding aid is preferred.
- Pp. 9–10: *Girl Crazy*, 14 October 1930; *Of Thee I Sing* and the Pulitzer’s exclusion of George; the *Second Rhapsody* growing from *Delicious*, Boston première 29 January 1932; Havana holiday in February 1932 and *Rumba* première in August; *Porgy and Bess* work from November 1933, Boston opening 30 September 1935, New York opening 10 October, title-role performers and 124-performance Broadway run; move to Los Angeles with Ira and Leonore on 10 August 1936.
- Pp. 10–12: film projects, death following brain-tumour surgery on 11 July 1937 at thirty-eight, Ira’s later work with other composers, preservation and donation of George’s manuscripts, and Ira’s death in 1983. The separate death entry https://gershwin.com/press/july-11-1937/ was also read and confirms Los Angeles, date, cause and age.

## Works, sound and reception

- **Jim Farrington, National Recording Registry essay on the 1924 *Rhapsody in Blue* recording:** https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/RhapsodyInBlue.pdf . Read all three pages. Supports the 4 January newspaper announcement; 12 February concert; two-piano working score handed to Grofé page by page; Grofé’s knowledge of Whiteman’s band; Ross Gorman’s rehearsal glissando replacing a notated run and reaching concert B-flat; 10 June 1924 acoustic Victor session; about one-third cut to fit two sides. No million-seller claim is used. Take-number disagreements between this essay and Library catalogue entries are left to the recording dossier; the composer page has no take numbers.
- **IMSLP work catalogue:** https://imslp.org/wiki/Rhapsody_in_Blue_(Gershwin,_George) . Confirms piano with original jazz-band scoring, saxophones and banjo, later Grofé versions, 1924 two-piano publication and 1942 symphonic scoring. The page distinguishes Gershwin’s authorship from Grofé’s orchestration; no late orchestral score is reproduced in ABC.
- **Orrin Howard, Concerto in F:** https://www.laphil.com/works/concerto-in-f . Read in full. Supports 1925 date, three-movement design, Gershwin’s own orchestration, Charleston rhythmic character, wind-and-brass opening of the slow movement, muted-trumpet anticipation of the piano theme, and the late recall for piano and flute. Howard identifies the French connection with Debussy and Ravel, used in the lineage note.
- **Dave Kopplin, *An American in Paris*:** https://www.laphil.com/works/an-american-in-paris . Read in full. Supports taxi horns, orchestra without solo-piano protagonist, 1928 date, Gershwin’s dated first-person description and the café/homesickness/street programme. The official catalogue entry at https://gershwin.com/catalog/ also describes the 1926 inception and 1928 horn shopping. No exact horn pitches are asserted.
- **Orrin Howard, *Cuban Overture*:** https://www.laphil.com/works/cuban-overture . Read in full. Supports original title *Rumba*, August 1932 première, Havana inspiration, four named Cuban percussion instruments positioned in front of the conductor, and Schillinger studies from 1932 to 1936 using graph paper. The page does not adopt Howard’s more sweeping value judgements about Gershwin’s technique.
- **Richard Crawford, chapter 7, ‘George Gershwin’s “I Got Rhythm” (1930)’, *The American Musical Landscape* (1993), especially pp. 213–19 and 227–30:** https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/the-american-musical-landscape-the-business-of-musicianship-from-billings-to-gershwin-updated-with-a-new-preface.html#d0e6504 . Read the chapter’s opening and its discussion of jazz recomposition. Supports George giving Ira a tune first; Ira’s more-than-two-week lyric struggle and move away from regular rhyme; the four-note figure; thirty-two-bar AABA plus two-bar tag; Merman, Rogers, Goodman and Krupa; *Fascinating Rhythm*’s uneven phrase accents; later omission of the tag; Duke Ellington’s *Cotton Tail* and bebop use of the harmonic scheme. The jazz connection is compositional and performative, not a teacher–pupil claim.
- **Three Preludes:** https://gershwin.com/publications/preludes-for-piano/ and https://imslp.org/wiki/3_Preludes_(Gershwin,_George) . Read both. The official catalogue documents the Hotel Roosevelt recital on 4 December 1926 and distinguishes the larger performed set from the three subsequently published. IMSLP gives the 1927 publication and markings, including *Andante con moto e poco rubato*. Iain Sneddon’s notes, pp. 6–7 of https://d2ajug1vehh95s.cloudfront.net/25183booklet.pdf , verify B-flat major, C-sharp minor, E-flat minor and the middle prelude’s rocking ostinato. The booklet concerns arrangements, so the page uses only explicitly identified descriptions of Gershwin’s original preludes, not the arrangers’ combinations or invented claims about how many he completed.
- **Official catalogue and song listings:** https://gershwin.com/music-search/ verifies *Rialto Ripples*, 1917, Will Donaldson; the titles and original associations of the listed songs. https://gershwin.com/publications/lady-be-good/ verifies 1924, the Astaires, *Fascinating Rhythm*. https://gershwin.com/publications/oh-kay/ verifies 1926, Gertrude Lawrence, *Someone to Watch Over Me*. https://gershwin.com/publications/second-rhapsody/ verifies *Delicious*, the concert reworking and 29 January 1932 Boston première. https://gershwin.com/publications/shall-we-dance/ verifies the 1937 film, Astaire/Rogers and both named songs. These pages were read, not inferred from URL titles.
- **Porgy and Bess:** https://gershwin.com/publications/porgy-and-bess/ verifies the creators, Theatre Guild, Rouben Mamoulian, Todd Duncan, Anne Brown, Eva Jessye Choir, songs and initial run. The song descriptions distinguish Clara’s lullaby and Serena’s lament, not an instrumental suite or a later film version.
- **South Carolina Historical Society, ‘October, 1935: The opera Porgy and Bess opens in New York’, 14 October 2021:** https://schistory.org/october-1935-the-opera-porgy-and-bess-opens-in-new-york/ . Reader extraction omitted the body, so the complete original HTML body was read. Supports the summer 1934 Folly stay, the Heywards’ cottage, DuBose’s libretto/lyric work and Dorothy’s play, objections to stereotyping alongside praise for Black performers, and the cast’s successful 1936 protest against audience segregation at Washington’s National Theatre. Later revival dates in this short article are not used. Folly is a summer visit, not a permanent residence or a claim that the whole opera was composed there.

## ABC examples

Both examples are newly written schematics and labelled as such in kicker, title and caption. They quote no Gershwin melody or copyrighted arrangement.

1. Charleston: two attacks at crotchet beat 1 and the quaver after beat 2, over a steady four-beat bass. Its musical context is Gershwin’s own description of the Concerto in F, reproduced by Howard; invented F-major pitches and accompaniment make the rhythmic relationship audible.
2. Syncopation: original melody with attacks between beats and ties over bar lines, against four regular bass beats. Tied B and E preserve their pitches across the bar. Crawford’s discussion of the song rhythms supplies the context; the notes are an explanatory exercise, not an approximate transcription.

## Recording recommendations

- **Gershwin/Whiteman, 1924 Victor *Rhapsody***: Farrington’s NRR essay above verifies performers, date, first-recording status and cuts. The recommendation explicitly says shortened; it does not call a single side the complete work.
- **Bernstein/Columbia Symphony, 1959 *Rhapsody***: Leonard Bernstein’s official discography, https://leonardbernstein.com/discography?page=21 , read in full, gives piano/conductor, orchestra, Sony/Columbia catalogue numbers, 23 June 1959 and St George Hotel, Brooklyn. The recommendation’s interpretative preference is editorial.
- **Rattle, White and Haymon, *Porgy and Bess***: Warner’s official catalogue https://www.warnerclassics.com/release/gershwin-porgy-and-bess-0 , read, confirms the principals, London Philharmonic and Glyndebourne Chorus. Its review excerpt supports the theatrical emphasis. The page omits a recording-session year to avoid confusing the original recording with the listed 2017 reissue.
- **Fitzgerald/Riddle, 1959 songbook**: https://www.ellafitzgerald.com/discography/ and https://www.nelsonriddlemusic.com/nr_albums_fitzgeraldgerswin5.htm , both read, verify 1959 and Verve. Fitzgerald’s official store explicitly credits Riddle’s arrangements: https://shop.ellafitzgerald.com/products/ella-fitzgerald-sings-the-george-ira-gershwin-song-books-5lp-box-set . The official Gershwin selected-discography page also lists the set. The recommendation is for the vocal/orchestral songbook, not the composer’s piano-roll album.
