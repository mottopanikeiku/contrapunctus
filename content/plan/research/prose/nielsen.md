# Carl Nielsen — quotation and fact sources

Sources opened 19 October 2026. Scores were read as page images (IMSLP and Royal Danish Library PDFs fetched to scratch space, not committed). The Danish letters were read from the Royal Danish Library’s online Carl Nielsen Letter Edition (`epub3.kb.dk/cnb/EPUB/`, Danish and English texts); every English rendering printed on the page is our own translation of the Danish. No art fetch, build or git command was run.

## Facts and where they come from

- Birth at Sortelung, Nørre Lyndelse, 9 June 1865; seventh of twelve children; Niels Jørgensen (“Painter-Niels”) as house painter and fiddler; children’s surname; violin at six during measles; goose-herd at Bramstrup from eight or nine; Petersborg house; Emil Petersen; apprenticeship at Ellinge; military band from 1 November 1879: Knud Ketting, Carl Nielsen biography, Carl Nielsen Society, https://www.carlnielsen.dk/gb/bio/ (English; the Danish text at https://www.carlnielsen.dk/bio was read for the quotations and the funeral). DBL (above) gives the same, and the story of the boy hammering a wooden “keyboard”; DBL says “Han fortæller selv herom” with Petersen as the narrator (he is the subject of the preceding sentence), so the chapter names Petersen as teller.
- Gade, May 1883; Conservatory from January 1884; Tofte; Rosenhoff; left end of 1886; Suite for Strings, Op. 1, 8 September 1888 (Balduin Dahl); waltz repeated; Royal Orchestra from 1889 for sixteen years; Ancker grant, 1890: Ketting and DBL.
- Dresden Ring, September 1890; Sibelius, Sinding and Joachim in Berlin; Gade’s death 21 December 1890; Paris from 26 February 1891; Anne Marie Brodersen; marriage in the English church in Florence on 10 May 1891 (a wedding party without papers in Paris on 10 April): Ketting; DBL gives Florence, 10.5.1891.
- First Symphony, 14 March 1894, Svendsen conducting, Nielsen taking the applause; autumn tour; Vienna, 5–15 November 1894; Brahms on 7 November with Dr Eduard Schiff: Ketting; CNB I/510, I/520, I/522 (diary of 17 November: “Rejste fra Wien iforgaars Kl 2½”). Brahms kept “mine Ting [CNW 56] [CNW 25]”: CNW 25 is the First Symphony; CNW 56 is the String Quartet in F minor, Op. 5 (https://www.kb.dk/dcm/cnw/document.xq?n=56).
- Hymnus Amoris, first performed 27 April 1897; the Titian painting: Ketting (“Titian’s picture of a young man who kills his beloved out of jealousy”, Padua, honeymoon) and the Carl Nielsen Edition III/1, preface, p. 1 (“painting of a jealous husband killing his wife … perhaps *The Miracle of the Jealous Husband* (fresco, Scuola del Santo, Padua)”, after Torben Meyer and Frede Schandorf Petersen, vol. 1, p. 131, from the memory of Irmelin Eggert Møller): http://img.kb.dk/export/sites/kb_dk/da/nb/dcm/cnu/pdf/CNU_III_01_cantatas_1.pdf. The painting is identified only as “perhaps” this fresco; the chapter and the image note say “the likely picture”. Anne Marie’s intended frieze and her drawing for the title page: same preface. Latin text by J. L. Heiberg, plan with Axel Olrik: Ketting.
- *Saul and David*, 28 November 1902 (Nielsen conducting the nine performances of the season); Second Symphony, 1 December 1902, dedicated to Busoni; Helios finished in Athens on 23 April 1903, first played 8 October 1903 under Svendsen: Ketting; CNW 1, 26.
- *Maskarade*: libretto by Vilhelm Andersen after Holberg; composition from 1905; accepted by the Royal Theatre December 1905 (CNW 2: https://www.kb.dk/dcm/cnw/document.xq?n=2); first night 11 November 1906, twenty performances in 1906–07 (Ketting); DBL for everyone expecting failure and for the composer losing faith after the dress rehearsal.
- Royal Theatre conductor 1908–14, resignation, last evening 30 May 1914 (*La Bohème*): Ketting; DBL.
- Fourth Symphony: begun 1914; finished 14 January 1916; first performance 1 February 1916, Music Society, Odd Fellow Palace; programme of Kunzen, Hartmann and Gade, with Nielsen’s foreword; repeat 14 April 1916; second timpani “placed directly opposite Timp. 1, at the edge of the orchestra near the audience”: CNW 28 (https://www.kb.dk/dcm/cnw/document.xq?n=28); Ketting; father’s death 22 November 1915: editorial note to CNB V/303. The foreword printed in the 1916 programme (“Music is life, and inextinguishable like it”, in Ketting’s English): the Danish text was not found and is not quoted. The letter to Bodil Neergaard of 10 April 1916 (CNB V/330) says “det er det bedste af mine Arbejder, synes jeg selv”. Which “Music Society” performance and which letter Kjerulf’s “third hearing” refers to: CNB V/334 editorial note. London, Queen’s Hall, 22 June 1923 (LSO, Nielsen): CNW 28 performance list.
- Fifth Symphony: begun autumn 1920 at Damgaard near Fredericia; first movement finished early March 1921 at Højbo, Tibberup, near Humlebæk (lent by Vera and Carl Johan Michaelsen); interrupted for *Fynsk Foraar* (finished 30 August 1921); fair copy 15 January 1922; five rehearsals; first performance 24 January 1922: CNU II/5 preface and CNW 29 (https://www.kb.dk/dcm/cnw/document.xq?n=29). Furtwängler, ISCM Frankfurt, 1 July 1927: CNW 29. Marriage separation by mutual consent, 26 September 1919, reconciliation by the end of 1922: Ketting (quoting Torben Schousboe); DBL (crisis 1915 to about 1922).
- Wind Quintet: Ketting (telephone call to Christian Christiansen, Felumb, Gothenburg from 1 February 1922, private performance 30 April on Lisa Mannheimer’s birthday, public performance 9 October 1922); CNW 70 (https://www.kb.dk/dcm/cnw/document.xq?n=70: dedicated to the five members of Københavns Blæserkvintet; performers on 9 October 1922; private performance 30 April 1922 at Herman and Lisa Mannheimer’s home); CNU II/11 preface (finished 24 April; third movement on the hymn; cor anglais story). **Discrepancy on the Mozart piece:** Ketting names Mozart’s *Sinfonia Concertante*; the Carl Nielsen Edition (note 138, after Torben Meyer) thinks it probably the Quintet for piano and winds, K. 452, and says Meyer wrongly calls it a concerto. The chapter says “a Mozart piece”. **On 30 April:** the draft carries Nielsen’s note “Uroppfördes d 30/4 1922 Söndag på min födelsedag”; his own birthday is 9 June, and Ketting says it was Lisa Mannheimer’s birthday, so the chapter gives no birthday.
- *Min Jesus, lad mit Hjerte faa*: composed 1914 or 1915, published in *Salmer og aandelige Sange* (CNW Coll. 10), 1919 (CNW 184: https://www.kb.dk/dcm/cnw/document.xq?n=184; Ketting). The Carl Nielsen Edition says “1912–1916” for the period of the hymns; DBL says the collection was composed in spring 1914. The chapter uses the CNW range for the tune.
- A Score of Danish Songs (*En Snes danske Viser*), 1915, with Thomas Laub; “23 saadanne Viser … hvoraf jeg har komponeret de 12 og Laub 11” (Nielsen to Röntgen, 5 April 1915, CNB V/209); 50 melodies or arrangements in the *Folk High School Melody Book* (1922) and 44 in the *Denmark* songbook: Ketting; *Jens Vejmand*, autograph dated 25 June 1907, dedicated to Bodil Neergaard, Op. 21 No. 3, “a national bestseller”: CNW 137 (https://www.kb.dk/dcm/cnw/document.xq?n=137) and Ketting. Poem text in the spelling of *Rugens Sange* (1906): Kalliope, https://kalliope.org/en/text/jens-vejmand (the page header gives the collection and year; the poem’s first printing in a newspaper in 1905, said in a web summary, was not checked).
- Springtime on Funen, Odense, 8 July 1922, a thousand singers, Georg Høeberg; heart attack at Damgaard: Ketting. Sixth Symphony 11 December 1925; Flute Concerto (Paris 21 October 1926, Copenhagen 25 January 1927); Clarinet Concerto (finished 15 August 1928; 14 September private; 11 October public); *Min fynske Barndom* (October 1927); *Commotio* (letter of 2 March 1931; Aarhus Cathedral, 14 August 1931, Emilius Bangert); director of the Academy from New Year 1931; admission to the National Hospital 1 October; death at ten past midnight on 3 October; funeral 9 October at Vor Frue Kirke with Wöldike at the organ; wind quintet chorale at the grave: Ketting (the Danish text confirms “slutkoralen fra Blæserkvintetten”), DBL (“begravet … Vestre”).
- Sound aspects (G minor symphony beginning and ending on C major; “progressive tonality”; the choruses of *Hymnus Amoris* and Buxtehude; counterpoint with Rosenhoff; the pure fifth): DBL. Concertos planned for all five wind players: Ketting.
- The Liszt report (a symphony in one span, “said to have come from” Liszt’s B minor Sonata, played by Henrik Knudsen): Ketting only, no source given; marked inferred in the influence batch.
- Grieg’s hut: Ketting says the Violin Concerto was begun “during a summer stay with Nina Grieg at Troldhaugen”; the year 1911 follows DBL and CNW (composition 1911).

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026 against sources fetched afresh: the *Dansk Biografisk Leksikon* entries for Nielsen and for Anne Marie Carl Nielsen; Ketting’s biography; the Carl Nielsen Letter Edition (CNB 1:54, 1:57, 1:510, 1:520, 1:526, 5:81, 5:209, 5:334, 7:180, 7:198, 7:217, 11:935A); the Catalogue of Carl Nielsen’s Works (CNW 1, 25–30, 32, 34, 70, 137, 184); the prefaces and scores of the Carl Nielsen Edition (Symphonies 4 and 5, Chamber Music 2, Cantatas 1, Songs 1); Kalliope; the Dacapo, Deutsche Grammophon and YouTube pages; Commons metadata for the portrait. Every quotation and dated claim in the composer page, bibliography, elsewhere, geo and influence files was compared with those texts.

### Changes

- **Wind Quintet begun before Gothenburg.** The page, and the Gothenburg geo entry, said Nielsen started the quintet in Gothenburg. Nielsen’s own letter of 2 March 1922 says: “I had started on a new composition — a quintet for flute, oboe, clarinet, bassoon and horn — before I came up here; I’ve got a bit further with that” (CNB 7:198, in the Danish: “inden jeg rejste herop”). CNW 70 likewise says he started shortly after finishing the Fifth Symphony and completed the work in April in Gothenburg; he travelled there on 1 February 1922 (Ketting). The page now says he had begun it before leaving and had got further by 2 March; the geo entry says he finished it in Gothenburg.
- **Violin at six.** “played back the tunes his mother sang” → “taught himself a few melodies and played them for his father”, and “was given” → “was handed” a violin that hung on the wall. Ketting says his mother handed him the violin and that he taught himself melodies and played them for his father; neither Ketting nor DBL says he played back his mother’s songs.
- **Sixth Symphony.** “It is the least often performed of his six symphonies” was not found in any source consulted (DBL, Ketting, CNW 30, the Carl Nielsen Edition preface) and has been replaced. CNW 30 lists a performance of the first movement alone under Nielsen at the Royal Swedish Academy of Music, Stockholm, on 1 November 1925, and 11 December 1925 as the first public performance; the work note and the life paragraph now say so.
- **Jens Vejmand poem date.** The poem entry said 1906 (the collection); Kalliope gives the poem as dated Jebjerg, 19 June 1905, first printed in *Politiken* on 26 June 1905, from *Rugens Sange* (1906), pp. 120–122. The entry is now dated 1905 and the note gives the first printing; the six stanzas and the first-stanza text were checked.
- **Bibliography.** The Letter Edition note said “about 8,600 transcriptions”. The site’s front page counts 7,380 letters and 492 diary entries; the figure of 8,500 in the introduction refers to the remaining letters not published in full. The note now gives the site’s own count and no longer mentions facsimiles.

### Quotations confirmed

- “The musician who does not think Wagner great is himself very small.” Diary, Dresden, 10 September 1890, after *Das Rheingold* (CNB 1:54: “Den Musiker som ikke finder Wagner stor er selv meget lille”). “sparks with steel and swords” and “most manly and energetic music ever written”: diary, 13 September 1890, after *Siegfried* Act I (CNB 1:57).
- “Well, it is still standing there, Herr Doctor.” Nielsen to Orla Rosenhoff, 13 November 1894, quoting his diary of the Wednesday visit (7 November) with Dr Schiff (CNB 1:520). The visit, Brahms’s indifference at first, the Thorvaldsen’s Museum question and the request to keep CNW 25 and CNW 56 are all in the letter. The 1868 story behind the remark is in the editorial note, CNB 1:510. Nielsen’s letter to Brahms of 25 December 1894 and the inscription “in tiefster Verehrung” (CNB 11:935A); Brahms’s “a Hansen … in G minor” through Behrend (CNB 1:526, quoting *Tilskueren*, May 1897, p. 436).
- “no programme”, “what we understand by the urge to life or manifestations of life”, “only life and movement, though different, very different, yet in a connection”: Nielsen to Anne Marie Carl-Nielsen, 3 May 1914 (CNB 5:81); the same letter asks to see her “turn the great stallion”.
- “the most elemental being of music is sound, life and movement that cut the silence to pieces”: Nielsen to Julius Röntgen, 5 April 1915 (CNB 5:209; CNW 28 gives 15 April for this letter and the Letter Edition 5 April, which the page follows by saying “April 1915”).
- “Oh, if only I could tell you how rich I feel today, because I have won your deep interest completely.” Nielsen to Charles Kjerulf, 15 April 1916 (CNB 5:334); Kjerulf’s *Politiken* review of 15 April follows the performance of 14 April (CNW 28, which marks that performance the first public one).
- “I roll a stone up a hill …”: *Politiken*, 24 January 1922, as printed in the Carl Nielsen Edition II/5 preface; the preface also gives the five rehearsals instead of three and the same-day interview.
- “this unclean trench music”, “I hate it, but it holds me captive”: Victor Bendix to Nielsen, 25 January 1922 (CNB 7:180: “denne urenlige Løbegravsmusik”, “Jeg hader den, men den fængsler mig”).
- “I have worked hard at my new quintet …” Nielsen to Anne Marie, Gothenburg, 16 April 1922 (CNB 7:217). “talk over one another” and “quite alone”: the undated programme note to Emil Holm, Carl Nielsen Edition II/11 preface (“Snart snakker de i Munden paa hverandre, snart helt alene”). “as though he meant at any price to disturb the music”: the side-drum footnote at bar 376 of the Fifth Symphony in the edition, with metronome ♩ = 116.
- “say a pure fifth in such a way that no one thinks he has heard that interval before”: DBL, “Han har selv engang ytret”, no date or occasion.

### Facts confirmed

Birth, siblings, father’s trade and name, surname, Emil Petersen and the wooden keyboard (DBL attributes the story to Petersen as narrator, as the page does); Odense band from 1 November 1879 (Ketting); Gade in 1883 and the Conservatory from January 1884 (DBL); Op. 1 first played on 8 September 1888 under Balduin Dahl (CNW 32), the Ancker grant, the Ring in September 1890, Paris on 26 February 1891 and the wedding in Florence on 10 May 1891 (Ketting; DBL on Anne Marie); first performances of Symphonies 1–5 and *Helios* (CNW 25–29, 34); the Fourth Symphony’s timpani instruction, “Timp. 2 are placed directly opposite Timp. 1, at the edge of the orchestra near the audience” (Carl Nielsen Edition II/4, score notes); LSO at Queen’s Hall on 22 June 1923 (CNW 28); the Wind Quintet’s dedicatees, performers and first public performance by Foreningen Ny Musik on 9 October 1922 (CNW 70); the hymn tune’s date range and printing (CNW 184); *Jens Vejmand* autograph of 25 June 1907 and dedication to Bodil Neergaard (CNW 137); funeral and the quintet chorale at the grave (Ketting); the Titian identification (the Carl Nielsen Edition III/1 preface says “perhaps”), the intended frieze and the title page drawn by Anne Marie; the statue’s competition and unveiling dates (DBL, Anne Marie Carl Nielsen). DBL for the C major chord at the start and end of the G minor symphony, the choruses of *Hymnus Amoris* and Buxtehude, and the Music Society from 1915; Ketting for the songs (Jacobsen through-composed, Holstein mostly strophic), the Liszt sonata hearsay, Troldhaugen, Stuttgart, Stockholm and Helsinki in 1913, Kahnt, and the remaining concerto dates.

### Other files
