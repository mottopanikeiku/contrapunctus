English quotations are our translations of the Italian passages below. The Italian opening of the *Essercizi* preface is preserved exactly as supplied. Sources were searched for and read during the rewrite. The ABC remains schematic, and manuscript dates are explicitly distinguished from composition dates.

## Corrections and exclusions

- Replaced the asserted Velasco attribution with “formerly attributed”, following the 2018 documentary biography.
- Removed the work-list’s specific “probably c. 1715” date for the *Stabat Mater*. The margin note now records the actual Rome/Lisbon uncertainty; the scoring and musical description remain.
- Retained the later Cat’s Fugue nickname, but omitted the undocumented tale of a cat supplying the notes.
- Did not repeat stories about the queen’s body, specific street music witnessed by Scarlatti, an alleged Handel contest or the idea that all the sonatas were composed after fifty. The sources do not justify those claims with the certainty they often receive.

### Confirmed (source, one line each)

- Letter of 30 May 1705 to Ferdinando de’ Medici: “Questo figlio ch’è un’aquila cui son cresciute l’ali non deve star oziosa nel nido, ed io non devo impedirle il volo”; Domenico sent to Venice via Florence: [Treccani DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giuseppe-domenico-scarlatti_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/) (cites Pagano 2015, p. 253). Domenico was nineteen (born 26 October 1685).
- Born Naples 26 October 1685, sixth of ten children; royal chapel organist and composer from 13 September 1701 at fifteen; seven Roman operas for Maria Casimira in the Palazzo Zuccari with Juvarra; assisted Baj from late 1713 and succeeded him after his death on 22 December 1714; *Stabat Mater* of uncertain Rome/Lisbon origin: same DBI entry.
- Arrival at Lisbon 29 November 1719, “impazientemente atteso dal Re”, sang accompanied by Queen Maria Anna; taught António and Maria Bárbara (born 1711); sent to Seville in 1729, court there 1729–33; Order of Santiago 21 April 1738; portrait formerly attributed to Velasco; *Essercizi* (K. 1–30) engraved in London 1738, dedicated to João V; copies of 1742 (61 sonatas) and 1749 (41), two near-twin series copied from 1752 passing to Farinelli (at court from 1737); death 23 July 1757; Maria Bárbara died 27 August 1758: same DBI entry.
- Preface to the *Essercizi* (“Non aspettarti, o dilettante o professore che tu sia … lo scherzo ingegnoso dell’arte, per addestrarti alla franchezza sul gravicembalo … mostrati dunque più umano, che critico … Vivi felice”): [Treccani, *Enciclopedia Italiana*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/domenico-scarlatti_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/). The poem `original` matches it apart from the noted normalisation of capitals.
- Venice copies: K. 87 in the 1742 volume (K. 43–93), K. 119 in the 1749 volume (K. 98–138), K. 208 in Libro III of 1753 (K. 206–235), K. 380 in the 1754 volumes, K. 466 in Libro XI of 1756 (K. 454–483): [Venice/Parma index](https://seesaawiki.jp/w/tekuteku2008c/d/HOME%20%28ENGLISH%29).
- *Stabat Mater*: ten parts (four sopranos, two altos, two tenors, two basses) with continuo; tenor opens the long vocalise at “Inflammatus et accensus”, taken up by the others; closing triple-time Allegro on “Amen”: [Mattietti’s programme note](https://www.flaminioonline.it/Guide/Scarlatti/Scarlatti-Stabatmater.html) (which dates the work 1715–19 in Rome; the page follows Fernandes in leaving place and date open).
- Kirkpatrick’s “crux”: “The meeting point in each half of the thematic material which is stated in parallel fashion at the ends of both halves with the establishment of the closing tonality” (1953, p. 255), quoted in the [Rochester dissertation](https://urresearch.rochester.edu/fileDownloadForInstitutionalItem.action?itemFileId=152094&itemId=29529), p. 85 n. 1.
- K. 141 in D minor, fast repeated notes, 3/8, binary form: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyboard_Sonata%2C_K._141_%28Scarlatti%29). K. 119 block chords spanning more than an octave: [Roevenstrunck](https://www.domenico-scarlatti.de/english/techn_ern_.html). K. 87 close hands, stepwise counterpoint, syncopated suspensions: [Hewitt](https://www.portlandpiano.org/program-notes/angela-hewitt-february-5-6-2022).
- Amigoni group c. 1750–52 with Farinelli, Teresa Castellini (about to leave Madrid for Italy), Metastasio, the artist; “C. B. F.” on the dog’s collar: [NGV](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/explore/collection/work/3701/). Ranc portrait 1729 and Velasco-attributed portrait 1738: Commons metadata.
- Horowitz, eighteen sonatas, K. 466 F minor and K. 39 A major among them: [Sony Classical track list](https://www.sonyclassical.de/alben/releases-details/horowitz-the-celebrated-scarlatti-recordings-sony-classical-originals).

### Not verified

- The descriptive claims for K. 27, K. 208, K. 380 and K. 466 (Sudbin, Larner, Zbikowski pages) were not re-opened; they are musical description, not dates or names.
- Ross (1984–85, Erato) and Hantaï (Mirare) recording details were not re-opened.
- The text dates Maria Bárbara’s marriage and Scarlatti’s move to Seville to 1729. [Wikipedia, Barbara of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_of_Portugal) gives the marriage as 1729 with the contract signed the previous year; the DBI gives the Lisbon double-wedding festivities of 11 January 1728 (*Festeggio armonico*, which Scarlatti, then in Rome, did not attend) and his orders for Seville in 1729. The text places him at neither ceremony.
