# Barbara Strozzi — quotation and fact sources

Checked 19 October 2026. This ledger supports `content/composers/strozzi.mjs`, `content/bibliography/strozzi.json`, `content/geo/strozzi.json`, `content/elsewhere/strozzi.json` and `content/influence/strozzi.json`. No recording is hosted (see the last section). Italian and Latin quotations are translated by the research desk; the original wording is given where the translation matters.

Principal sources, all opened and read in full unless noted:

- **DBI** — Beth L. Glixon, “Strozzi, Barbara”, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani* 94 (2019), https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/barbara-strozzi_(Dizionario-Biografico)/ . ## Sound aspects

- Voice and continuo: LoC blog (“Atypical for Strozzi (who usually composed music for her own soprano voice part)”); IMSLP Op. 8 and *Hor che Apollo* (two violins). DBI names the serenata *Or che Apollo è a Teti in seno* (1664); the same incipit as IMSLP’s *Hor che Apollo*. The remark on page 5 of the 1659 print is the research desk’s reading of the image: the last staff has a long run of fast notes under no new syllable.
- Forms: DBI (names she used); Ruffini p. 224 (more than half of the pieces of Op. 2 are strophic AB arias; later pieces ABCD, ABCDA); MusicWeb (alternation of recitative, arioso and aria; the description of *L’astratto*).
- Laments: 1659 print p. 76, heading “Lamento.”; Ruffini p. 225 (“Spesso l’impianto modale è in minore, specie nei ‘lamenti’”); MusicWeb (fast repeated chord under the closing line of the Cinq-Mars lament; dissonance and chromaticism on *tormento*, *morte*, *lamenti*, described across the disc). MusicWeb lists the Cinq-Mars lament as “from op. 3”; DBI and IMSLP place its first printing in Op. 2 and Ruffini says it recurs in Op. 3, so the site says Op. 2.
- Texts: DBI (poets named; “Strozzi non musicò mai versi di Battista Guarini o del Marino”). Ruffini p. 225 adds that Marino’s poetic influence reaches her through the academies; DBI’s statement is the one used. Wikipedia’s “Strozzi mostly used texts from Marino” is contradicted by DBI and is not used.
- Virtuosity and wit: DBI, last paragraph but one (“altamente virtuosistica … tagliata su misura per le sue risorse vocali … spirito arguto”, citing Rosand 1978, pp. 261–278).

## Notation

1. **Amore è bandito, bars 1–2 (voice), quoted.** Torchi (Ricordi, 1895), no. 13, p. 34, “Allo. brillante”, 12/8, two flats, *mf*. Read from the 300 dpi render of the IMSLP scan by measuring each notehead against the staff lines (bottom line E4, five lines) and checking bar 2 by eye. Page tilt shifts readings by up to 0.4 of a step at the right-hand end; every reading falls within that of the intended pitch. Bar 1: crotchet rest; F5 D5 C5 B♭4 F5 D5 F5 E♭5 C5 F5 (ten quavers, ten syllables of “A-mo-re è ban-di-to a-man-ti”). Bar 2: D5 dotted minim, F4 G4 A4 B♭4 A4 G4. Voice only; Torchi’s piano part omitted. Tempo and *mf* are Torchi’s. Whether Torchi regularised the rhythm of the 1657 print was not checked, so the caption names the edition and not the print.
2. **Descending tetrachord, schematic.** G–F–E♭–D in the bass with plain triads. Labelled invented. The only documented link is the heading “Lamento” in the 1659 print and Rosand’s 1979 title (Crossref: *Musical Quarterly* LXV/3, 346–359, doi 10.1093/mq/lxv.3.346; the article itself was not opened). The caption does not say that any Strozzi lament uses this bass.

Not used: *Lagrime mie*. The modern editions on IMSLP (Frisch and Mondrup for the Werner Icking Music Archive, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; others under various licences) were consulted as reading aids for the 1659 print, whose first system was compared by eye (bar 1: rest, e″, d♯″ with trill, d″., c″; bars 2–3 as in the edition), but no example is transcribed from a licensed edition. The public-domain 1659 print is in a clef and notation the research desk could not read to the standard needed for a quotation.

## Not used, and why

- Wikipedia’s statements that she took the name Strozzi in 1637, that a post-mortem letter alleges rape by Widmann, that Vidman was “a patron of the arts”, and that she mostly set Marino: none confirmed by DBI (and the last contradicted by it).
- barbarastrozzi.com, Britannica, Naxos, the Princeton catalogue and OUP DOIs returned HTTP 403 or a bot check; none is cited, and nothing is taken from them.
- Title of Op. 3: DBI abbreviates it (“Cantate ariete a una, due, tre voci...”) and Ruffini writes “Cantate, Ariette a1, 2 e 3 voci”; the works list normalises to “Cantate, ariette a una, due e tre voci”. The title page was not seen.
- Burney (1789) and Fétis (1870) are named in DBI’s source list only; the site does not mention them.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026 against sources opened again: DBI (treccani.it, whole entry), Ruffini PDF (pdftotext, pp. 216–227), LoC blog, MusicWeb review, LoC page images of the 1659 print (title page, dedication, p. 5, p. 76, Tavola), the Torchi scan (archive.org item imslp-a-voce-sola-op6-strozzi-barbara, rendered at 400 dpi), IMSLP wikitext for Opp. 2, 5, 8, Crossref for the three journal articles, the Cambridge Core page for *Barbara Strozzi in Context*, YouTube pages and oEmbed for both videos, and the Brilliant Classics, OAE and Tactus pages.

**Errors corrected**

- **Amore è bandito, bar 2 (ABC and caption).** The ledger and the example gave “D5 dotted minim, F4 G4 A4 B♭4 A4 G4”. Torchi’s page (p. 34, voice staff, measured against the staff lines at 400 dpi) has a crotchet D5 with a breath mark above, then eight quavers (F4 G4 A4 B♭4 A4 G4 A4 B♭4), a dotted quaver G4 and a semiquaver F4; 2 + 8 + 1.5 + 0.5 = 12 quavers. The ABC is now `d2 F G A B A G A B G>F` and the caption describes the bar as it stands. Bar 1 was checked the same way and is right: crotchet rest, F5 D5 C5 B♭4 F5 D5 F5 E♭5 C5 F5, with *mf*, “Allo. brillante”, 12/8, two flats; item no. 13 in the cover list of the fascicle (plate 97585, “Arietta: Amore è bandito (1657)”).
- **“Nearly all her pieces are for one voice”** (Sound, Voice and continuo). LoC says she “usually” wrote for her own soprano voice, and Brilliant says “most”; no source gives a proportion. Changed to “Most”.
- **“Later books set poets who wrote for the theatre”** (Sound, Texts). DBI lists the poets she set without tying them to later books, and calls Bissari, Aureli, Artale and Brunacci authors of *drammi per musica*. Reworded to match.
- **“An early all-Strozzi disc, first released in 1982”** (Recordings, Harmonia Mundi HMX 290114). Only van Veen says it, in passing, and the disc was recorded in December 1982; the page now reports his view that it was one of the first all-Strozzi discs and drops the release year.
- **Imperiale’s “all esteemed her”**. DBI’s “da tutti stimata una delle Muse di Parnaso” describes Barbara, not the house; the sentence now says so.
