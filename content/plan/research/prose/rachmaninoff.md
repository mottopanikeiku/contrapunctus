### Changes

- Pull quote source: “Letter to Leonard Liebling, 1939; published in The Musical Courier, 5 April 1943” → “Rachmaninoff to Leonard Liebling, 1939; published in The Musical Courier, 5 April 1943”. Two scholarly accounts of this one statement disagree on its form (Bullock: an interview “seemingly given in 1939 but held back for publication until a week after his death”; Frey: “he wrote to Leonard Liebling in 1939”); both cite Liebling’s column “Variations”, *Musical Courier*, 5 April 1943, p. 17. The neutral wording asserts only what both support. The quoted words, including the comma before “and I cannot acquire the new”, are confirmed. EXT: [*Rachmaninoff and His World*, Bullock’s foreword and Frey’s chapter, notes 8 and 3](https://dokumen.pub/rachmaninoff-and-his-world-9780226823744.html).

### Quotations confirmed

- “My hearty thanks to the old man for not having forgotten about me!”: Rachmaninoff to the singer Mikhail Slonov, 14/26 December 1892. It answers a St Petersburg newspaper interview of 6 December (Old Style) in which Tchaikovsky named “Glazunov … whilst over in Moscow we have Arensky, Davydov … and Rachmaninoff, who has created a wonderful opera setting of Pushkin’s poem *The Gypsies*”, so “praised his opera *Aleko*” stands. MEAS: [Tchaikovsky Research, Rachmaninoff](https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/Sergei_Rachmaninoff).
- “I write down on paper the music I hear within me, as naturally as possible”: the *Etude* article “Music Should Speak from the Heart” (continued from p. 804), December 1941; the OCR reads “I write down on paper the music I hear within me, as naturally as possible. I am a Russian composer…”. The by-line to David Ewen is as cited in Bullock/Frey’s volume (notes 22). MEAS: [Internet Archive, *Etude* December 1941](https://archive.org/download/EtudeDecember1941/EtudeDecember1941_djvu.txt).
- Poe’s opening stanza (“All the Heavens, seem to twinkle”) matches the Poe Society text; the Society’s manuscript version spells “tintinabulation [sic]” where the page prints the usual “tintinnabulation”. Pushkin’s first two stanzas match the Russian Virtual Library text. MEAS: [Poe Society, The Bells](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/bellse.htm); [RVB, Pushkin](https://rvb.ru/pushkin/01text/01versus/0423_36/1828/0467.htm).

### Other claims confirmed

- Great Gold Medal awarded 7/19 May 1892; *Aleko* premiere 27 April/9 May 1893 with Tchaikovsky applauding; Tchaikovsky’s recommendation of the opera to the Imperial Theatres (Rachmaninoff’s memoirs); Nemirovich-Danchenko’s libretto after Pushkin; Tchaikovsky “especially liked the Prelude” (Op. 3 No. 2); conservatory entry in 1883 aged ten; move to Moscow and Zverev in 1885; admission to Siloti’s piano class and Arensky’s composition class in 1888; Taneyev for counterpoint. MEAS: [Tchaikovsky Research](https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/Sergei_Rachmaninoff).
- Zverev: pupils lived in his house; “He never took a single coin from us as payment: neither for lessons, nor for board”; dressed and taken to concerts; Sunday open house; Igumnov taught Natalia Satina. EXT: [sin80, Zverev](https://sin80.com/en/artist/nikolai-zverev).
- Sofia’s death from diphtheria at thirteen; the 1889 rift with Zverev over a piano and privacy; the move to the Satin family; summers at Ivanovka from 1890; Mamontov’s private opera in 1897–98; Dahl’s treatment January–April 1900; Bolshoi premieres of *The Miserly Knight* and *Francesca da Rimini*; move to Dresden in November 1906; Mahler conducting the Third Concerto in New York; anonymous letter with Balmont’s *Bells* received in Rome; departure from St Petersburg on 22 December 1917 for a Scandinavian tour, Stockholm on 24 December, New York in November 1918; six works completed after leaving Russia; Beverly Hills in 1942; naturalisation 1 February 1943; last recital at Knoxville on 17 February; burial at Kensico. EXT: [Wikipedia, Sergei Rachmaninoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Rachmaninoff).
- *Isle of the Dead*: black-and-white reproduction seen in Paris in 1907; composed January–March 1909, premiered 18 April 1909 under the composer; 5/8 metre; he preferred the monochrome image to the painted original. EXT: [Wikipedia, Isle of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_the_Dead_(Rachmaninoff)); [Berlin Philharmonic note](https://www.berliner-philharmoniker.de/en/programme-notes/sergei-rachmaninoff-isle-of-the-dead-symphonic-poem-op-29/).
- *The Bells*: begun in Rome in early 1913, completed at Ivanovka on 27 July 1913, first performed 30 November 1913 with the composer conducting; the anonymous admirer was a Moscow cello student; chorus in all four movements, with tenor, soprano and baritone in the first, second and fourth; the finale opens with an English-horn solo. EXT: [LA Phil, The Bells](https://www.laphil.com/works/the-bells).
- Villa Senar: summer residence from 1933 to 1939; the name from Sergei, Natalia and Rachmaninoff. EXT: [Rachmaninoff Foundation](https://rachmaninoff.ch/en/history/).
- First Symphony: premiere on 15 March 1897 (Old Style), which is 27 March in the Gregorian calendar the page uses; the ledger row above says “28 March”, the English Wikipedia gives 28 March (16 March O.S.), and the Polish Biblioteka Muzyczna gives 27 March; Russian Wikipedia gives 15 March. The page’s 27 March stands. Second performance 17 October 1945 under Gauk. EXT: [Wikipedia, Symphony No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._1_(Rachmaninoff)); [Polish Music Library](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/rachmaninow-siergiej/?lang=en).
- Natalia Satina studied piano with Zverev and Igumnov (sin80 above); marriage in 1902.
