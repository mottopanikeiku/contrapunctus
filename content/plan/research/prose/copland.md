## Notation

Both examples are original teaching schematics, explicitly labelled in kicker, title and caption. Neither reproduces or approximates a copyrighted Copland melody.

- **Open spacing:** C–G–C, D–A–D, E–B–E, C–G–C, with the bass in a lower octave; four bars in 4/4. This illustrates perfect fifths/octaves and omission of the third. The stylistic link to Copland’s rural writing is supported by the Army Field Band booklet, p. 11, on *Stomp Your Foot*: octaves and open fifths.
- **3 + 3 + 2:** eight equal quavers per 4/4 bar, accents on attacks 1, 4 and 7, against four bass crotchets. The second upper accent lies between bass beats. Generic syncopation, supported as a technique by the Music for the Theatre programme note; no claim that these are the rhythms of a particular passage.

## Deliberate omissions and source disagreements

- No account of an actual Appalachian landscape inspiring the title/music; the documented collaboration is the story told.
- No assertion that Copland heard Stepp’s record, or that all folk materials are anonymous.
- No claim that Piano Variations uses a complete twelve-tone row; its four-note construction is described directly.
- No Inscape première date from the faulty official catalogue field, or claim that “Inscape” is the title of a Hopkins poem.
- No exact day of The Tender Land première because consulted institutional secondary sources disagree; year alone is sufficient here.
- No unverified private-life anecdote or medical diagnosis.
- No Copland score quotation: both ABC examples are original schematics.
- No assumption that a US-government performance makes the underlying Copland composition public domain. See the recording ledger and dossiers for the adopted territorial phonogram bases and the separate composition status.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against freshly fetched sources. Validator: `ok` for composer (2 scores, 3 images), bibliography, elsewhere and geo files after the edits below.

**Changes**

- Life, 1921–25: “a concert commissioned by the League of Composers” → “a work commissioned by the League of Composers”. The official work record gives “Commission Info: Claire Reis and the League of Composers” for the piece, premièred Boston 20 November 1925: https://www.aaroncopland.com/works/music-for-the-theatre/
- Life, 1938–44: the income-tax quotation is now “all for honoring the common man at income tax time”, attributed as Copland’s reply. The Kansas City Symphony notes print “I [am] all for …” with the verb in editorial brackets (so the original wording of the first words is not established); the words after the bracket are exact. https://www.kcsymphony.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/PROGRAM-NOTES-May-26-and-28-Final.pdf (the Fanfare official record independently gives Goossens’s suggestion of 12 March 1943 as “income tax time”: https://www.aaroncopland.com/works/fanfare-for-the-common-man/ ).
- Lineage note: Bernstein “commissioned and recorded the music” → “gave the première of *Connotations* and recorded the music”. The Connotations commission was the New York Philharmonic’s; Bernstein conducted the 23 September 1962 première (official record: https://www.aaroncopland.com/works/connotations/).
