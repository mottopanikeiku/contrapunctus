## Editorial decisions

No diagnosis is supplied for Ravel’s final illness, and the taxi accident is not made its cause. The house-style rewrite does not treat La Valse as Ravel’s declared allegory of the Habsburg collapse. The Debussy relationship remains a damaged friendship, not a fabricated lifelong feud. No new apocryphal Gershwin or Diaghilev quotation has been added.

### Confirmed (source, one line each)

- Pierre-Joseph Ravel Swiss-born engineer, Marie Delouart Basque who grew up in Madrid, factory visits, birth at Ciboure on 7 March 1875, move to Paris three months later: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Ravel); Cornejo (birth 7 March 1875, 10 PM; mother’s death 5 January 1917).
- Conservatoire admission November 1889; Bériot class from 1891; removal from Pessard’s harmony class on 1 July 1895 for lack of prizes: Cornejo. Viñes met Ravel in November 1888; premières of *Jeux d’eau* (5 April 1902), *Miroirs* (6 January 1906), *Gaspard* (9 January 1909): Cornejo.
- Fourteen performances of *Pelléas* in the first run, all attended; contributions to Lilly Debussy’s income; five Prix de Rome attempts; Rhine cruise: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Ravel). Houston lecture, 1928: “For Debussy the musician and the man I have had profound admiration, but by nature I am different from him.” ([Wedgebill Music, citing Burnett James, *Ravel*, 1983, pp. 19–20](https://wedgebillmusic.com/ravel-on-debussy/)).
- Preliminary rounds of 1905: jury decision on 13 May excluding Ravel and retaining six candidates, all Lenepveu’s pupils; yacht *Aimée* trip 2 June to late July 1905; Fauré named director on 13 June 1905: Cornejo. Dubois had requested retirement months earlier and his resignation was not connected to the affair: [Carus, Rigaudière 2024](https://blog.carus-verlag.com/en/choral-works-in-focus/ravel-and-the-rome-prize/) (the margin note “A retirement already requested” stands).
- War: lorry driver from March 1915, bowel operation after amoebic dysentery in September 1916, mother’s death in January 1917, refusal of the league (“It would be dangerous for French composers to ignore systematically the productions of their foreign colleagues …”), *Tombeau* 1914–17: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Ravel).
- Cornejo confirms enlistment in the 13th Artillery Regiment (Auto Service) on 15 March 1915, departure for the front on 10 March 1916 with a Panhard truck, missions to Verdun from 1 April 1916, hospital on 22 September 1916 and surgical intervention on 30 September 1916.
- Letter to Maurice Emmanuel, 14 October 1922: “Il ne faut y voir que ce que la musique y exprime: une progression ascendante de sonorité, à laquelle la scène viendra ajouter celle de la lumière et du mouvement.”: [Henle blog](https://blog.henle.de/en/2025/01/13/naturally-artificing-maurice-ravel-on-his-150th-birthday/) (reproduces the autograph).
- Le Belvédère from 1921, “coffee cups with holes”: [Montfort-l’Amaury museum page](https://www.montfortlamaury.fr/culture-tourisme/sites-et-monuments/la-maison-musee-de-maurice-ravel/?lang=en). The “artificial by nature” remark is attributed to Calvocoressi’s *Musicians Gallery* (1933) in the Henle essay (“naturally artificing”).
- *La valse*: first conceived as “Wien” in 1906, composed 1919–20, Diaghilev’s verdict after hearing the two-piano version with Marcelle Meyer (“a masterpiece … not a ballet”), first performance 12 December 1920, the score’s “Cour impériale, vers 1855”: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_valse). The spring 1920 date of the hearing is from the Berlin Philharmonic programme note, seen only as a search extract (not opened).
- Alajouanine’s tests of 1933 assisted by Manuel Rosenthal; Ravel recognised errors in *Tombeau* and the *Pavane de la Belle au bois dormant* and that the examiner’s piano was out of tune; no autopsy, no definitive diagnosis: [Darvesh et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11206233/). Taxi accident October 1932, absent-mindedness from 1927: Wikipedia.
- Doré engraving (seventh of eleven, H. Pisan, 1867) shows the ogre’s wife on the steps holding a lantern over the huddled children; Gause *Hofball in Wien* (1900); Bakst design (1912): Commons metadata and the image itself.
- Recording references: Monteux/LSO 1959 and Zimerman/Boulez dates as in the earlier ledger (BnF); Argerich/Abbado 1967 (DG); not re-opened.
