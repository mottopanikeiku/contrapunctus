# Satie — quotation and fact sources

Sources consulted for this rewrite. The page translates short passages from Satie’s French; the original wording and occasion are recorded below. The *Memoirs* are comic writing: their sound-measuring instruments, servant and timetable are presented as inventions, not biographical evidence about his household or diet.

| “I breathe carefully (a little at a time).” Timetable: rises 7.18, inspired 10.23–11.47 | Satie, “La Journée du musicien”, *Mémoires d’un amnésique*, **Revue musicale S.I.M., IX, no. 2, 15 February 1913, p. 69**. “Je respire avec soin (peu à la fois).” “Mon lever : à 7 h. 18 ; inspiré : de 10 h. 23 à 11 h. 47.” [Arfouilloux edition](https://editions-lescalier.com/Lescalier_Extraits/SatieEXTRAIT.pdf), printed pp. 12–13 and publication notes. The body explicitly identifies this as an imaginary working day. |
| “Time will do what I cannot do at present.” | Letter from Satie to his brother Conrad, **1893, after the separation from Suzanne Valadon**: “Le temps fera ce que je ne puis faire en ce moment.” [Centre Pompidou portrait dossier](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/offre-aux-professionnels/enseignants/dossiers-ressources-sur-lart/suzanne-valadon/le-portrait-derik-satie). The museum supplies the year but not the day; none is invented. |
| “To play this motif 840 times in succession, it would be well to prepare oneself beforehand, and in the greatest silence, by serious immobilities.” Epithet excerpt: “serious immobilities” | Heading of the *Vexations* manuscript, **usually dated 1893**: “Pour se jouer 840 fois de suite ce motif, il sera bon de se préparer au préalable, et dans le plus grand silence, par des immobilités sérieuses.” [Vexations manuscript discussion](https://www.eriksatie.fr/les-vexations). [Publication history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnossiennes) distinguishes 1893 magazine prints from the collected edition of 1913. |
| “famous mazurka by Schubert”; “Obligatory cadence (by the composer)” | *Embryons desséchés*, **1913**, respectively the Chopin funeral-march parody in the second piece and the final cadence of the third. | “to those who do not like me” | Preface to *Sports et divertissements*, **1914, published 1923**, dedicating the opening chorale: “Je dédie ce Choral à ceux qui ne m’aiment pas.” [Royal Ontario Museum article with manuscript-preface image and transcription/translation](https://www.rom.on.ca/blogs/sports-et-divertissements-unique-resource-researchers-design-history). |

## Corrections and omissions

- Made the mother’s nationality more precise: English, of Scottish descent.
- Removed speculation about how to separate Satie’s religious convictions, costumes and satire; the documented activities tell the story.
- Removed the unsupported categorical assertion that the slow piano pieces were composed specifically to hold a concert audience’s attention. The separate furniture-music occasion is described concretely.
- Removed the broad, untraced statement that later electronic ambient music borrowed Satie’s question. Cage’s documented advocacy remains.
- Removed the painting’s public-domain boilerplate and the defensive statement that this particular Puvis canvas is not named in *Socrate*.
- No unsupported quotation retained. No claim that the *Memoirs*’ diet, servant or timetable are literal autobiography.

### Confirmed (source, one line each)

- “Du reste, j’ai plus de plaisir à mesurer un son que je n’en ai à l’entendre”; B♭ “j’examinai un si bémol de moyenne grosseur. Je n’ai, je vous assure, jamais vu chose plus répugnante. J’appelai mon domestique pour le lui montrer”; F♯ “atteignit 93 kilogrammes. Il émanait d’un fort gros ténor”; “Mon lever : à 7 h. 18 ; inspiré : de 10 h. 23 à 11 h. 47”; “Je respire avec soin (peu à la fois)”: [Arfouilloux edition, extract](https://editions-lescalier.com/Lescalier_Extraits/SatieEXTRAIT.pdf). Publication: *Revue musicale S.I.M.*, VIIIe année, no. 4, 15 April 1912, p. 69; IXe année, no. 2, 15 February 1913, p. 69 (notes of the same extract).
- “Le temps fera ce que je ne puis faire en ce moment”, letter of 1893 to his brother after the break with Valadon; affair begins January 1893 at the Auberge du Clou; Valadon’s portrait, “une de ses toutes premières toiles”; Satie drawing her on music paper; pale eyes behind “binocles”; red mouth: [Pompidou dossier](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/offre-aux-professionnels/enseignants/dossiers-ressources-sur-lart/suzanne-valadon/le-portrait-derik-satie).
- *Vexations* inscription (“Pour se jouer 840 fois de suite ce motif …”), unpublished in his lifetime, first printed 1949, Cage’s relay at the Pocket Theatre on 9 September 1963, enharmonic spellings: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vexations). Duration “18h40”: [eriksatie.fr](https://www.eriksatie.fr/les-vexations).
- Birth at Honfleur 17 May 1866, shipping-broker father, English mother of Scottish descent, her death in 1872, Vinot, Conservatoire November 1879 and no diploma; 1887 move to the Chat Noir quarter (habitué, then resident pianist); Auberge du Clou and Debussy; Péladan; the one-member Eglise Métropolitaine d’Art de Jésus Conducteur (1893–95) and rue Cortot; Arcueil from 1898; Schola Cantorum from October 1905, counterpoint with Roussel; Ravel’s January 1911 SMI concert; *Trois morceaux en forme de poire* (1903, seven movements); *Parade* 1917; death from cirrhosis at Saint-Joseph on 1 July 1925: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Satie).
- Schola Cantorum diploma of 1908 signed by d’Indy (“mention très bien”, counterpoint class): [eriksatie.fr](https://www.eriksatie.fr/la-schola-cantorum-%C3%A0-39-ans). Ravel and Viñes played the third *Gymnopédie*, the second *Sarabande* and a *Fils des étoiles* prelude on 16 January 1911, Salle Gaveau: [eriksatie.fr](https://www.eriksatie.fr/le-ma%C3%AEtre-d-arcueil-un-citoyen-engag%C3%A9).
- Gnossiennes 1–3 without bar lines or time signatures, composed c. 1890, first published 1893, collected 1913, Nos. 4–6 published 1968; “Ouvrez la tête” among the French directions: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnossiennes); [PTNA](https://enc.piano.or.jp/en/musics/611).
- *Socrate*: commissioned by Princess Edmond de Polignac in October 1916, composed 1917–18, Cousin’s translation, female voices, parts first performed privately in April 1918 by Bathori with Satie at the piano: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrate).
- Puvis, *Le Pauvre Pêcheur* (1881): fisherman with a sleeping infant and an elder daughter picking flowers, allegory of Poverty: [fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Pauvre_P%C3%AAcheur). The Orsay notice returned HTTP 403 in this pass.
