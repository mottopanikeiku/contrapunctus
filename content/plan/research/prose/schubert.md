## Editorial decisions

- The illnesses remain in the historical account; uncertain retrospective diagnosis is placed in the margin.
- The ADB is used for Spaun’s transmitted German words, not its superseded dating of *Winterreise*.
- No romantic deathbed composition scene or claim that the *Unfinished* was stopped by Schubert’s death is introduced.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against sources opened directly (URLs below). Labels: MEAS = read in the source; EXT = reported by the source. No change to `schubert.mjs` was required: every quotation and checkable claim matched its source.

### Confirmed quotations

- “whose brightest hopes have come to nothing” and “In this way I want to clear myself a path towards a great symphony” — Schubert to his friend the painter Leopold Kupelwieser, then in Rome, 31 March 1824: “dessen glänzendste Hoffnungen zu Nichte geworden sind”; “ich componirte 2 Quartetten für Violinen, Viola u. Violoncelle u. ein Octett, u. will noch ein Quartetto schreiben, überhaupt will ich mir auf diese Art den Weg zur großen Sinfonie bahnen” (Deutsch, *Dokumente*, p. 235). MEAS: Peter Jost, Henle Octet preface, https://www.henle.de/media/71/f7/ca/1697725856/0562-1697725856-sync.pdf
- “I like these songs more than all the others, and you will come to like them too” — Spaun’s recollection, in his account of Schubert’s friends at Schober’s, of Schubert’s reply when Schober said he liked only *Der Lindenbaum*: “Mir gefallen diese Lieder mehr als alle anderen und sie werden Euch auch noch gefallen.” Spaun’s text says Schubert sang “die ganze Winterreise”; Wigmore establishes that the friends in fact heard only the first twelve songs (he gives the reply as “better than any others”), the second twelve being found in the autumn of 1827 and appended, so Schubert’s order differs from Müller’s. MEAS: https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Schubert,_Franz_(Komponist) and https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dc.asp?dc=D_CDA68034&vw=dc
- “I have played the sonatas in several places to much applause” — Schubert to Heinrich Albert Probst, Vienna, 2 October 1828: “Die Sonaten habe ich an mehreren Orten mit vielem Beyfall gespielt, das Quintett aber wird dieser Tagen erst probirt”; same letter offers three piano sonatas, Heine songs and the string quintet for two violins, viola and two cellos, and asks when the Trio (Op. 100) will appear. MEAS: https://schubert-online.at/activpage/briefe_einzelansicht.php?briefe_id=19&herkunft=allebriefe
- “heavenly length” — Schumann on the *Great* C major (“heavenly length of the symphony, like a thick novel in four volumes”); the review stands in the “1840” section of his *Gesammelte Schriften* (“Die C Dur-Symphonie von Franz Schubert”, pp. 195–203 of the selection), https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesammelte_Schriften_%C3%BCber_Musik_und_Musiker ; first complete public performance, Mendelssohn, Leipzig Gewandhaus, 21 March 1839; score sent to the Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde in October 1826, which gave a small payment. EXT: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Schubert)
- *Der Leiermann*: written 1822, first published 14 March 1823, set October 1827; German text of stanzas 1 and 5 as on the page (“Drüben hinterm Dorfe / Steht ein Leiermann”; “Wunderlicher Alter”); barefoot on ice, empty plate, growling dogs in the omitted stanzas. MEAS: https://www.schubertsong.uk/text/der-leiermann/

### Confirmed facts

- Born Himmelpfortgrund 31 January 1797; first violin lessons from his father; piano lessons from his brother; Michael Holzer (organist and choirmaster at Lichtental: organ, voice, harmony, figured bass); viola in the family quartet; Stadtkonvikt from November 1808 at eleven, left end of 1813; Salieri took him as a private pupil in June 1812; about 140 songs in 1815; Schober’s invitation of 1816; Vogl introduced early 1817; Mayrhofer (introduced by Spaun in 1815), lodging with him after Zseliz; *Erlkönig* sung by Vogl in March 1821 and printed on commission as Op. 1; *Rosamunde* withdrawn after two nights; *Unfinished*: two movements and sketches into a third, 1822; syphilis symptoms from 1823, typhoid officially diagnosed in 1828; torchbearer at Beethoven’s funeral; concert of 26 March 1828; burial near Beethoven at Währing; both graves moved to the Zentralfriedhof in 1888. MEAS/EXT: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Schubert
- Private music teacher of Marie and Karoline Esterházy from 1818; first song printings (*Erlafsee*, *Widerschein*, *Die Forelle*) from 1818 in supplements to Viennese newspapers and almanacs. MEAS: Austrian National Library, https://data.onb.ac.at/nlv_lex/perslex/Sch/Schubert_Franz.html ; *Am Erlafsee* printed 6 February 1818 as a supplement to Sartori’s *Mahlerisches Taschenbuch* — https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/tw.asp?w=W2021
- Counterpoint lesson with Simon Sechter in early November 1828 (4 November, with Joseph Lanz). EXT (secondary): https://strangehorizons.com/wordpress/non-fiction/articles/autumn-2008-in-the-key-of-schubert/ ; Wikipedia says he “had actually made appointments for lessons” (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Schubert).
- String Quintet: recorded by the Takács Quartet with Ralph Kirshbaum, May 2012, Wyastone; Hyperion note describes the second cello and the work as completed six weeks before his death. MEAS: https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dc.asp?dc=D_CDA67864
- Schwind, *Ein Schubert-Abend bei Josef von Spaun*, 1868, washed pencil and pen-and-ink drawing, Wien Museum (inv. 30525). MEAS: https://sammlung.wienmuseum.at/en/object/1003233-ein-schubert-abend-bei-josef-von-spaun/
- Friedrich, *Winter Landscape*, probably 1811, National Gallery: a man who has “cast aside his crutches” prays before a crucifix guarded by three firs; a Gothic church in the mist. MEAS: https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/caspar-david-friedrich-winter-landscape
- Mackerras: “natural horns, trumpets and trombones and a small string section … clarity of texture” (BBC Music Magazine, CD-80502). Lupu: “the subtle way Lupu handles the much-discussed first-time bars” (BBC Music Magazine, Decca 440 295-2).
