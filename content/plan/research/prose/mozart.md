# Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart — prose sources

The correspondence supplies the opening concert, Haydn’s praise, Mozart’s rule for opera and the visit to *The Magic Flute*. The ABC, poem originals, image filenames, reading list and lineage arrays are retained. Short German quotations are translated here; the longer preserved Italian and German excerpts keep their existing text and translations.

## Corrections and removals

- The unqualified 1782–83 date for the enlarged Lange portrait is omitted; museum documentation separates the earlier miniature from its unfinished enlargement.
- The vague claim about what Chopin’s admiration “centred on” is removed. The lineage arrays are unchanged; the prose now gives the documented Haydn performances and Beethoven cadenzas instead of attributing an unquoted aesthetic credo to Chopin.
- Carmontelle’s date is widened to the museum’s 1763–64; Schinkel’s design is tied accurately to the 1816 production.
- No conjectural Mozart death scene, Salieri-poisoning story or undocumented composer quotation has been introduced.

### Confirmed quotations and facts

- Leopold arrived on Friday 11 February 1785 and went that evening to the first subscription concert at the Mehlgrube; “a new, outstanding clavier concerto by Wolfgang, from which the copyist was still working as we arrived, and your brother did not even have time to play through the rondeau because he had to supervise the copying”; “The concert was beyond compare”. On Saturday evening Haydn and the two Barons Tindi came; the three new quartets (KV 458, 464, 465) were played; Haydn: “I say to you before God, as an honest man, your son is the greatest composer that I know in both person and name: he has taste, and beyond that the greatest knowledge of composition.” MEAS: https://dme.mozarteum.at/DME/objs/raradocs/transcr/pdf_eng/0847_LM_Nannerl_1785.pdf (letter dated 14–16 February 1785; editorial note: K. 466 played on 11 February).
- “In an opera, poetry must simply be music’s obedient daughter” — Mozart to Leopold, Vienna, 13 October 1781: “the poetry in an opera must quite simply be the obedient daughter of the music”; Stephanie is the librettist of the *Entführung*. MEAS: https://dme.mozarteum.at/DME/objs/raradocs/transcr/pdf_eng/0633_WAM_LM_1781.pdf
- Mozart to Constanze, 14 October 1791: collected Salieri and Cavalieri at 6 o’clock with the coach and drove them to the box; Salieri “from the symphony to the final chorus … a bravo or bello”; Carl collected and in the theatre (born 21 September 1784, so seven). MEAS: https://dme.mozarteum.at/DME/objs/raradocs/transcr/pdf_eng/1196_WAM_Constanze_1791.pdf
- “The bass part will come after dinner” — note to the copyist at the start of the Andante of the autograph of K. 387: “izt wird nur von diesem andante das 2te Violin und die Viola herausgeschrieben, die Baßstimme kommt erst nach tisch, das Erste Violin ist schon geschrieben”. MEAS: Wolf-Dieter Seiffert, Henle preface, https://www.henle.de/media/7f/9b/3e/1697725831/7122-1697725831-sync.pdf
- K. 488: scoring with two clarinets and no oboes, trumpets or drums; development opens with a new E major theme on strings; Adagio in F♯ minor. MEAS: Larner (above).
- K. 219: the violin enters “with an Adagio of only six bars”; minuet finale with a minor-key “Turkish” episode. MEAS: Martin Pearlman, https://baroque.boston/mozart-violin-concerto-5
- K. 271: Victoire Jenamy (1749–1812), daughter of Noverre, identified by Michael Lorenz; composed January 1777. MEAS: https://www.laphil.com/works/piano-concerto-no-9-in-e-flat-major-k-271 . K. 183: four horns, a pair each in B♭ and G. MEAS: https://www.laphil.com/works/symphony-no-25-in-g-minor-k-183
- Beethoven and K. 466: documented that he played Mozart concertos in public in 1795/96, this concerto at the Burgtheater on 31 March 1795; cadenzas for first movement and finale (WoO 58); the first-movement cadenza “preserves the syncopated rhythm and thematic material”. MEAS: https://www.lvbeethoven.org/woo058/
- Carmontelle watercolour: Musée Condé gives 1763 and 1764; Leopold, 1 April 1764: the engraver “travaille en hâte à graver le portrait que M. de Carmontelle … a très bien peint”. Image viewed: Leopold standing with a violin beside Wolfgang at the harpsichord; Nannerl holds a sheet of music. MEAS: https://www.musee-conde.fr/fr/notice/car-418-mozart-enfant-son-pere-et-sa-soeur-35ebf77d-aeeb-4330-99c0-23eedca7b244 ; https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Louis_Carrogis_dit_Carmontelle_-_Portrait_de_Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart_(Salzbourg,_1756-Vienne,_1791)_jouant_%C3%A0_Paris_avec_son_p%C3%A8re_Jean..._-_Google_Art_Project.jpg
