## Editorial decisions

- No attested biographical event was removed for lack of a source.
- Kept the motto interpretation qualified as traditional and the “Haydn” theme’s authorship open, since uncertainty is the actual provenance story.
- Did not repeat inaccurate dates or locations found in otherwise useful summaries: Hanover, not Hamburg, for the First Concerto première; 1881 for the Second Concerto; 1894 for the clarinet sonatas; the Four Serious Songs precede Clara’s death.
- Removed generic claims about what early audiences must have found difficult. The hostile Leipzig reception is documented; a single asserted psychological explanation for it is not.
- No image files, ABC strings, poem originals, reading entries or lineage-array entries were changed.

### Quotations confirmed

- “I would gladly leave out ‘German’ too and simply put ‘human’.” — Brahms to Carl Reinthaler, 9 October 1867; translated in [Richmond, Brahms–Reinthaler letters](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~jriehl/local/Choral_Music_Creed/brahms.html) (“I would gladly give up the ‘German’ … and simply put ‘human’”); German in Grimes (see above). Reinthaler’s objection (5 October 1867: the text lacks “the saving death of the Lord”) read in the same page.
- “The score must appear with a mourning border.” — Brahms to Simrock, 22 November 1877: “Die neue Symphonie ist so melancholisch, daß Sie es nicht aushalten … die Partitur muß mit Trauerrand erscheinen” ([Henle edition, introduction](https://www.henle.de/media/fc/23/61/1759224907/6004.pdf)); BSO’s “black border” is the same remark.

### Facts confirmed (no change)

- Piano Quintet: Joachim’s criticism of the string-quintet version, 15 April 1863; two-piano version February 1864; Clara’s verdict (“many of the most beautiful ideas are lost on the piano”, 22 July 1864); Levi’s letter on the final version is dated 9 November 1864 ([Hyperion note](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dw.asp?dc=W8243_67551)). The LA Phil note’s date of 5 November 1865 for Levi’s letter is wrong; “reached its final combination in 1864 after advice from Clara, Joachim and Hermann Levi” stands.
- Second Symphony: Pörtschach in summer 1877, finished at Lichtenthal (Baden-Baden) from 17 September; première 30 December 1877; trombones and tuba in the first movement, D major brass close ([Henle](https://www.henle.de/media/fc/23/61/1759224907/6004.pdf); [BSO](https://www.bso.org/works/brahms-symphony-no-2)). First Symphony: Karlsruhe, 4 November 1876; horn call conceived as a birthday greeting to Clara in 1868 (BSO, Mandel).
- *Ein deutsches Requiem*: Bremen Cathedral, Good Friday (10 April 1868), six movements, Brahms conducting; Clara present; soprano movement added May 1868; complete work at the Leipzig Gewandhaus on 18 February 1869; mother died 1865; second movement’s funeral march and the folk-like fourth movement ([BSO, Swafford](https://www.bso.org/works/a-german-requiem); [Wikipedia summary pointing to the same sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_German_Requiem_(Brahms))).
- Clarinet Quintet: Mühlfeld, Meiningen, 1891; muted strings and clarinet cadenza in the slow movement; finale a theme and five variations with a coda bringing back the opening; Brahms’s talk of retiring in 1890 ([SLLMF](https://sllmf.org/archive/notes_for_196.html)).
- *Nänie*: Feuerbach died January 1880; sketches summer 1880, completed 22 August 1881; dedicated to Henriette Feuerbach; Zürich première 6 December 1881; oboe solo, choral entries one at a time, return at “Auch ein Klaglied zu sein” (the penultimate line) to D major and the oboe melody ([BSO, Steinberg](https://www.bso.org/works/brahms-nanie)). Henriette was Anselm’s stepmother (previous entry stands).
- *Schicksalslied*: Dietrich family’s books at Oldenburg, sketch on the beach at Wilhelmshaven, first performance Karlsruhe 18 October 1871, C minor to C major, orchestral return of the opening instead of repeated words (BSO, Ledbetter).
- Klinger: piano in the studio, cycles numbered as “Opus”, *Brahmsphantasie* Opus XII with five songs and the *Schicksalslied*, Brahms received the finished work in January 1894 ([Wesleyan](https://brahmsphantasie.research.wesleyan.edu/)).
- Portrait: C. Brasch, Berlin, 1889 ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:JohannesBrahms.jpg)). Feuerbach, *On the Seashore (Modern Iphigenia)*, 1875, oil on canvas, Museum Kunstpalast, inv. M 4126 ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anselm_Feuerbach_-_On_the_Seashore_(Modern_Iphigenia)_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)).
