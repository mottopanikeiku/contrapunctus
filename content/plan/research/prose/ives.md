### Quotations confirmed

- George Ives, “Don’t pay too much attention to the sounds—for if you do, you may miss the music.” Verbatim apart from typographic punctuation; Charles's later recollection of George speaking about a stonemason's singing. No exact conversation date is documented; the page properly places it before George's 1894 death. https://charlesives.org/ives-man-his-life
- Charles Ives, “These prefatory essays were written by the composer for those who can’t stand his music—and the music for those who can’t stand his essays.” Words confirmed in the 1920 *Essays Before a Sonata*, introductory footnote; the page ends its excerpt at the source's semicolon, before the dedication. https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3673/pg3673-images.html
- Charles Ives to Harmony, c. 1927, “I can’t seem to compose any more. I try and try and nothing comes out right.” Words, addressee, tears and approximate date confirmed in Swafford's biography; the page identifies Harmony's recollection. https://charlesives.org/ives-man-his-life
- Ralph Waldo Emerson, *Self-Reliance* (1841), the four-sentence excerpt: checked against the first-edition transcription, corrected as above, with the internal omission now marked. https://anthology.lib.virginia.edu/work/Emerson/emerson-self-reliance
- Henry David Thoreau, *Walden*, “Sounds” (1854), “The whistle of the locomotive penetrates my woods…”: the entire displayed sentence matches the university transcription, with only the apostrophe regularised. https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/90/walden-or-life-in-the-woods/1542/sounds/
