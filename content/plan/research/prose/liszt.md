## Editorial decisions

Corrected Liszt’s age at his father’s death and restricted the Brendel recommendation to the books he actually plays. Removed the imprecise *Mazeppa* repeated-note claim. The Hungarian note identifies actual performers and repertories without turning the biography into a defensive aside. No quotation is attributed to Liszt merely on the authority of a quotation website.

### Quotations checked

- “The concert is me” — confirmed as our translation of “Le concert, c’est moi”: Liszt to Princess Cristina Belgiojoso, Albano, **4 June 1839**, describing his solo Rome concerts; [letter 18, La Mara/Bache edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3689/pg3689.html), with the French wording also reproduced by [János Mácsai, Liszt Academy](https://concert.lisztacademy.hu/news/the-piano-recital-115701). The page’s month-only date is accurate.
- “For a fortnight my mind and fingers have been working like two damned souls” — confirmed as our translation of “Voici quinze jours que mon esprit et mes doigts travaillent comme deux damnés”: Liszt to **Pierre Wolff, Paris, 2 May 1832**; [Francis Claudon, “L’École de la rhapsodie”, p. 219](https://ojs.ppke.hu/verbum/article/download/865/877/1132), independently corroborated for recipient, date and practice details by [letter 5](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3689/pg3689.html). “Souls” renders the substantivised *damnés*, not Bache’s softened “lost spirits”.
- *cantando espressivo* — confirmed in the [displayed Sonata score extract](https://www.classicalmusic-notes.com/wp-content/uploads/liszt-sonata-6.TC_.png), at bar 153 in the [accompanying analysis](https://www.classicalmusic-notes.com/liszt-sonata-1/).
- “New German School” — confirmed as the historical school name, not represented as Liszt’s spoken words; [Mark Berry, Royal Holloway](https://www.royalholloway.ac.uk/research-and-education/subjects/music/about-us/teacherhubmusic/franz-liszt-symphonic-poems/).
- Petrarch’s opening quatrain and the page’s own English translation were checked against the [complete Italian sonnet](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Canzoniere_(Rerum_vulgarium_fragmenta)/Pace_non_trovo,_et_non_%C3%B2_da_far_guerra) and the [Library of Congress’s 2011 recital booklet, p. 7](https://blogs.loc.gov/music/files/2011/10/2011-10-22_bruns-hammer-.pdf). The meaning and attribution are sound; the protected original has the spelling discrepancy recorded below, so exact transcription is not marked confirmed.

### Protected issues reported, not edited

1. **Petrarch original, line 3:** the supplied text begins “e volo”, whereas the linked [Wikisource transcription](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Canzoniere_(Rerum_vulgarium_fragmenta)/Pace_non_trovo,_et_non_%C3%B2_da_far_guerra) reads “et volo”. The [Library of Congress booklet](https://blogs.loc.gov/music/files/2011/10/2011-10-22_bruns-hammer-.pdf) uses modernised “E volo” but also modernises other conjunctions. This is an orthographic/source-transcription mismatch, not a mistranslation; no single fetched text substantiated the exact mixed spelling supplied. The protected poem was not altered.
2. **Walker reading citation:** “Cornell, 1983–96” mixes editions. [NII’s library record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA07946809) identifies the three-volume 1983–1996 American set as **Knopf**, corroborated for volume I by [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL21091240M/Franz_Liszt). A correction retaining those dates would read “Knopf, 1983–96”. Reading is protected, so unchanged.
3. **Chronology reading attribution:** the link goes to a Blogger-hosted site titled **Franz Liszt**, not a page identifying itself as “The Liszt Society”. The Society’s own [official site](https://www.lisztsoc.org.uk/the-society) is `lisztsoc.org.uk`. The organisational attribution could not be substantiated and should not be treated as evidence of institutional authority. Reading is protected, so unchanged. Earlier “Liszt Society” labels in this ledger refer to `liszt.org`; this independent section supersedes that attribution and supplies museum, university, primary-letter and critical-edition corroboration.
