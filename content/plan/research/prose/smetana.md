# Smetana — quotation, fact and score sources

Prepared 2026-10-19 for `content/composers/smetana.mjs`. Every source below was opened in this session unless marked “not opened”. Quotations from German or Czech are translated by the author; the original wording is given so that the translation can be checked. Page numbers for Wellek (1895) are the printed page numbers read from the Internet Archive OCR text (`https://archive.org/download/11658530bsb/11658530bsb_djvu.txt`) and may be out by one.

Abbreviations: **W** = Bronislav Wellek, *Friedrich Smetana* (Prague: Dominicus [Th. Grüss], 1895), <https://archive.org/details/11658530bsb>. **MBS** = Bedřich Smetana Museum, National Museum, Prague: online life and catalogue of works, <https://bedrichsmetana.nm.cz/en/info/life> and <https://bedrichsmetana.nm.cz/en/catalogue>. **H&R** = Ulrich Hoppe and Frank Rosanowski, “The deafness of Bedrich Smetana”, *GMS Zeitschrift für Audiologie* 6 (2024), Doc02, CC BY 4.0, <https://journals.publisso.de/en/journals/zaud/volume6/zaud000037>. **DvC** = Dvořák chronology, <https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/life/important-dates-in-the-life-of-antonin-dvorak/>. **KR** = F. V. Krejčí, *Bedřich Smetana*, 2nd edn (Prague, 1924), <https://archive.org/details/bedrich_smetana-f_v_krejci> (read in part).

## Not used or not opened

- Smetana’s diary wish “a Liszt in technique and a Mozart in composition”: sources found (classical-music.com, a Plzeň symposium abstract) disagree on the year (1843 and 1835) and were not opened; omitted.
- The note “In complete deafness” at the end of *Vltava* (W’s footnote 5, p. 58): the OCR does not make clear which poem the footnote belongs to; omitted.
- Britannica, Presto Music, Naxos (403 to curl), Supraphon album pages (404) were not opened. Clapham (1972), Large (1970) and Bartoš (1955) are lending-library scans on the Internet Archive; only their catalogue records were read.
- Sketches of Smetana’s 1848 activities (marches for the National Guard and the Student Legion, March–April 1848) are in the MBS catalogue but are not in the text.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026. Sources opened again: Wellek (1895), Internet Archive OCR (letters in the appendix, the Thorne letter, the quartet letter, the Má vlast programmes, early life, 1848–56 section, the 1882 passages); the MBS online catalogue (work pages by number, read as text); the National Museum article by Jana Plecitá on *Má vlast* (nm.cz, 30 November 2022); the Dvořák chronology; the Eulenburg miniature score of *Vltava* (pp. 1, 5, 6, rendered at 300 dpi) and the Bote & Bock vocal score of *The Bartered Bride* (p. 3).

**Errors corrected**

- **First performance of *Vyšehrad*.** The page said 14 January 1875, following Smetana’s own list of 1875 (reproduced in the MBS note) and Wellek. The MBS structured field (14 March 1875, Žofín, Slánský) and the National Museum article (“na filharmonickém koncertě 14. března 1875 za řízení Ludvíka Slánského … s Vltavou na koncertě 4. dubna”) agree on 14 March. The Life now says 14 March, at a Philharmonic concert under Slánský. The ledger’s earlier note that the text “follows” January is superseded.
- **“Who had never met Liszt”** (Life, 1848). The letter says only that he is “ungekannt” and that he ought to have asked leave first (“Zwar sollte ich zuvor um die Erlaubniß dazu ansuchen”); Wellek says he had learnt to revere Liszt through his works. Reworded to what the letter states.
- **“Stayed with him at Weimar in 1857”** (Facts). Letter III says he spent the September days of 1857 “bei Ihnen in Weimar”; Wellek says he spent a few days in Weimar on the way to Sweden at Liszt’s invitation. Changed to “visited”.
- **Programme of *Vltava*, “printed with the score”.** The Eulenburg score prints a shorter German and Czech programme (“Aus zwei Quellen entspringt sie …”) that does not have the warm and sluggish springs. The wording used on the page is Wellek’s version of the Zelený programme (“das eine warm und lebhaft, das andere kühl und schwerfällig”), so the aside now says so.
- **Sound, Programme.** “Each of the six poems takes its form from its subject” has no source for Má vlast (Wellek says it of the three Swedish poems); replaced by the fact that Wellek prints a programme for each of the six.
- **Works, Sketches Op. 4.** MBS gives “1857” in Smetana’s lists and “1858” as the year of the first edition (Veit, Prague); the note now gives both. MBS also shows Op. 4 and Op. 5 as two books of four pieces each (Wellek says eight pieces).

**Verified without change**

- Quotations, wording and addressee against Wellek: Liszt, 28 March 1848 (“in einigen Wochen könnte vielleicht — kein Smetana existieren”, signed Friedrich Smetana); Liszt’s reply of 30 March 1848 (“zu den ausgezeichnetsten, schönstempfundenen und feinstausgearbeiteten”); Gothenburg letter (“Betrachten Sie mich als einen der eifrigsten Jünger unserer Kunstrichtung”, which names Paulus, the Requiem, Paradies und Peri, choruses from Tannhäuser and Lohengrin for the winter of 1857–58; Weimar September 1857); “aus Trotz”, 5 May 1882; the Thorne letter of 11 December 1881 (“Ich höre absolut nichts, nicht einmal meine eigene Stimme”; whistling after a throat catarrh; July; right ear in October; left on 20 October 1874; roaring louder while composing; A♭ major sixth chord, four-line octave); the letter to Srb (“Ich wollte mir nämlich in Tönen meinen Lebenslauf schildern”; the movements; the *Meno vivo* in D♭ and the small-note *più facile* for the violins and viola; the “unheilvolle Pfeifen der höchsten Töne”), which Wellek dates “1878” without the day; the quotation to Čech after 5 November 1882.
