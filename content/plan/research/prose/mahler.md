# Mahler — quotation and fact sources

Light-touch review of the house exemplar. Its biographical sequence, images, most sentences and critical voice remain. Changes correct particular claims, identify quotation contexts, remove unverified assertions, move margin notes before their paragraphs and comply with the revised house style. ABC strings, image filenames, poem originals, reading list and lineage arrays were retained.

Corrected rather than discarded: the 1880 cantata chronology, scope of Wagner cuts, Strauss context of the 1902 letter, Cooke edition dates, and the closing instrumentation of *Das Lied*.

### Quotations confirmed

- “The symphony must be like the world. It must embrace everything.”: Mahler to Sibelius, Helsinki, 1907, as Sibelius recalled; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Mahler (Tom Service, *Guardian*, not re-opened).
- “My time will come when his is over.”: letter to Alma Schindler, 31 January 1902, after *Feuersnot*; German “und meine Zeit wird kommen, wenn die seine um ist”; Pickett renders “when his is up”: https://davidpickett.wordpress.com/2009/06/07/my-time-will-come/
- “That is myself”: Bauer-Lechner’s report of Mahler on *Ich bin der Welt abhanden gekommen*, 1901 (“…und: das bin ich selbst!”): https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ich_bin_der_Welt_abhanden_gekommen
- Rott “founder of the New Symphony as I understand it”, to Bauer-Lechner: Berliner Philharmoniker page above.
- Berg on the Ninth’s first movement (“allerherrlichste, was Mahler geschrieben hat”): Munich Philharmonic programme, 14 February 2025 (text extracted from the PDF; the letter is undated there; the 1912 dating, “most probably November 1912”, rests on a search extract of a ResearchGate article, not opened): https://www.mphil.de/fileadmin/Redaktion-mphil/Saisonordner/Saison_2425/Programmhefte/20250214_Hengelbrock_Programmheft_www.pdf
- Schoenberg’s letter praising the Seventh after the first Vienna performance (3 November 1909, Löwe; letter 29 December 1909 to Mahler in New York): https://mahlerfoundation.org/mahler/compositions/symphony-no-7/symphony-no-7-history/ ; https://www.brusselsphilharmonic.be/en/notes-mahler-7
- Bülow funeral, Hamburg: “the choir in the organ loft intoned the Klopstock chorale” (letter to Arthur Seidl, 17 February 1897), via https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/lies-and-epiphanies/gustav-mahlers-resurrection-and-the-apostolic-succession/3CB4D2CF70D654748E7EA167B680AAA9 (search extract; not opened).

### Facts confirmed (one line each)

- Second of fourteen children, eight dying young (Deutsche Grammophon biography via search extract; Wikipedia gives 14 children, counts differ): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Mahler . Iglau military band and garrison: https://mahlerfoundation.org/mahler/locations/czech-republic/jihlava/
- Conservatory 1875–78 (Epstein; Krenn and Fuchs for composition and harmony); *Das klagende Lied* completed 1880, entered for the Beethoven Prize, 1881 (jury Brahms, Hanslick, Goldmark, Richter; prize to Robert Fuchs): Wikipedia above; https://mahlerfoundation.org/mahler/compositions/das-klagende-lied/das-klagende-lied-versions-and-premieres/ (search extract).
- Conversion February 1897; appointed director 8 October 1897; Roller from 1903; 645th and final Vienna performance 15 October 1907 (*Fidelio*); Met debut 1 January 1908: Wikipedia above. Latecomers and claque: Marston page; lights extinguished: Habsburger.net page above.
- 1907: Maria died 11–12 July; valvular disease disclosed 14 July by Blumenthal, confirmed by Kovacs 18 July; endocarditis diagnosed February 1911 (Libman): https://mahlerfoundation.org/mahler/the-man/health/
- Freud, Leiden, 26 August 1910, four-hour walk; Jones’s biography is the source of the barrel-organ story (via Freud’s later account): https://mahlerfoundation.org/mahler/locations/netherlands/leiden/meeting-with-freud/ . Steinbach hut commissioned autumn 1893, ready June 1894: cottage page above.
- First: seven-octave A with harmonic upper octaves, descending-fourth woodwind motif, clarinet cuckoo, solo double bass “Frère Jacques”, muted (programme notes), première Budapest 20 November 1889 as a symphonic poem in two parts, “Titan” dropped: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._1_(Mahler) ; https://www.indianapolissymphony.org/backstage/program-notes/mahler-symphony-no-1-in-d-major-titan/
- Third: six movements, D minor to D major, Nietzsche in the fourth, offstage posthorn: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._3_(Mahler) . Fourth: no trombones or tuba, scordatura solo violin: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Mahler) . Sixth: A major to minor motto on trumpets and oboes over timpani, Rute on the bass drum case, “Tragische” on the 4 January 1907 Vienna programme: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._6_(Mahler) . Eighth: ninth-century hymn, Munich 12 September 1910, 170 players, 850 choristers and eight soloists (about 1,028): https://mahlerfoundation.org/mahler/locations/germany/munich/1910-concert-munich-12-09-1910/ . Ninth: ersterbend, D to D♭: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Mahler)
- Tenth: UE introduction, bars 203–208, nine-pitch chord; Cooke published 1976, revised 1989, most often performed: https://www.universaledition.com/en/Works/Adagio-from-Symphony-No.-10/P0006009 ; Wikipedia Tenth. The sustained high trumpet A comes from a search extract (Ionarts), not opened.
- *Das Lied*: six songs after Bethge, Mahler added the last lines, final sixth chord, tenor with alto or baritone: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Lied_von_der_Erde . *Ich bin der Welt*: original (high-voice) key F major, 16 August 1901; poem from Rückert’s *Liebesfrühling*, 1821: https://www.mahlercat.org.uk/Pages/SiebenLieder/SLmss.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCckert-Lieder ; de.wikipedia page above. *Kindertotenlieder* 1901–04, *Wayfarer* 1884–85 own texts: Wikipedia pages.
- Klimt, *On Lake Attersee*, 1900, first Attersee stay, square 80 × 80 cm canvas, shoreline only in a corner: https://onlinecollection.leopoldmuseum.org/en/object/4328-on-lake-attersee/ ; Secession exhibition 15 April–27 June 1902, Roller among the artists: Secession page.
- Schoenberg’s Mahler lecture, Prague, 25 March 1912, repeated in Berlin and Vienna (search extract of *Constructive Dissonance*; not opened). Mahler’s 1910 praise of Schoenberg and friendship from 1904: Schoenberg Center timeline https://schoenberg.at/en/component/content/article/bio-en
