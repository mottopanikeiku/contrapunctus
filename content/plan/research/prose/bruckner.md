## Editorial decisions

- The obsolete `featured` field is removed.
- The Linz appointment is dated January 1856 rather than conflating it with the 1855 competition and beginning of the Sechter lessons.
- The counting episode now follows Karl Waldeck’s recollection, with his companionship included; the documented 1867 crisis and treatment remain.
- Score strings are unchanged. Their captions identify pedagogical schematics rather than claiming literal quotations from the orchestral scores.
- No new portrait attribution, collection, work date or lineage entry is introduced. The rewritten epithet and comparisons are critical prose, not attributed quotations.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 49 claim groups across the identity and portrait, four facts rows, sixteen life paragraphs/asides, six sound entries, ten works, two score captions, three arts entries, three recordings and lineage prose. Musical descriptions were checked against the scholarly analyses and publisher catalogues below; the two ABC examples remain explicitly schematic. No timeline-date change was required.

### Corrections

- “After a performance of *Die Walküre* … play its funeral music” → “play the Seventh’s funeral music”. The excerpt was the funeral music from Bruckner’s Seventh Symphony’s Adagio, not music from *Die Walküre*. [Berliner Philharmoniker programme note](https://www.berliner-philharmoniker.de/en/programme-notes/anton-bruckner-symphony-no-7-in-e-major/).
- Ledger’s description of that excerpt as “Wagner’s funeral music” → “the funeral music from Bruckner’s Seventh”; same source.
- Ledger’s source for the 1877 Third première, Mahler’s presence and piano-duet arrangement: Howie chapter 5 → [chapter 4, pp. 141–48](https://www.abruckner.com/down/articles/articlesenglish/howiebrucknerbio/Chapter_4.pdf).

### Quotations confirmed

- “certainly my last comfort in Vienna”: Bruckner to Wagner, 20 May 1878, concerning Liszt’s appreciation of the Fifth; exact published translation in [Howie, chapter 5, pp. 6–7](https://abruckner.com/down/articles/articlesenglish/howiebrucknerbio/Chapter_5.pdf).
- “it is already beginning to look quite different”: Bruckner to Hermann Levi, 27 February 1888, concerning the Eighth’s revision; exact published translation in [Dermot Gault’s edition commentary](https://www.abruckner.com/Data/Documents/B8-Gault.htm).
- *Locus iste* Latin text and the translation’s “mystery beyond price” / “without fault”: [Elisabeth Maier, Austrian Academy of Sciences Bruckner-Lexikon](https://api-brucknerlex.acdh.oeaw.ac.at/article/d1e9284); also confirms four-part a cappella scoring, C major, composition on 11 August 1869 and première in the votive chapel on 29 October.
- *In te, Domine, speravi*: text and translation checked in the [LSO’s 11 February 2024 programme, pp. 6, 9](https://londonso-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2024/02/11-02-Bruckner-9-Te-Deum-web.pdf).
