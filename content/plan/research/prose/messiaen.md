## Omissions and source cautions

- Dropped the previous page’s unsourced technical explanation that harmonisation compensates specifically for aspects of birdsong timbre beyond equal-tempered single notes, and its specific account of Loriod’s differentiation of registers, attacks and simultaneous rhythms. These may be defensible, but the opened sources do not establish those formulations. Documented fieldwork, virtuosity, proof-reading and collaboration remain.
- Reduced abstract technical exposition and lists for space; this does not reject the documented *Diptyque* borrowing, the opening Quartet ostinatos, late-work titles or other omitted details.
- Retained the 1961 marriage date despite 1962 appearing in two secondary pages. Retained 1971–74 for *Des canyons* and 1988–91 for *Éclairs*, with source variants noted in the table.
- Corrected the wood-thrush note’s flowering-branch description to what the plate shows. The depicted mockingbird drama is described as Audubon’s scene, not as verified ornithological behaviour.
- NGA and New-York Historical Society object pages returned access errors, and one Commons image fetch was rate-limited. The successful Commons/NGA metadata record and Audubon’s own plate pages/image supplied the artwork evidence instead.
- The published English interviews supply the Ericsson and Loriod quotations. The short *Turangalîla* quotation was translated directly from the opened French autograph. No undocumented quotation has been added.
- Existing reading-list strings and their external links were deliberately left unchanged under the preservation contract. Newly written prose uses only permitted internal links; source URLs live here.
- Integration verification: the combined three-composer run of `node tools/validate.mjs` printed `ok` for Messiaen: three scores and four images checked, with no Messiaen warning or error. The life contains 1,275 words and three margin notes; six sound sections contain 56–60 words each. No build, formatter, art-fetch or git command was run.

### Confirmed quotations

- **“This work is a love song.”** The [Boston Symphony archive](https://www.bso.org/exhibits/koussevitzky-as-patron-commissioning-messiaens-turangalila-symphonie) identifies Messiaen’s note for the Boston première on **2 December 1949**. The [actual autograph image](https://cdn.bso.org/uploads/Archives/Images/Exhibits/Messiaen_autograph_Turangalila-note.jpg), opened and inspected, begins its second page **“Cette œuvre est un chant d’amour.”** The page’s explicitly labelled “our translation” is exact in sense; the quotation in the life uses the same words. This is a programme statement addressed to the concert audience, not an undocumented conversational remark.
- **“That’s not important, that’s just me playing the organ.”** Exact words in [Nicholas Capozzoli’s interview with Hans-Ola Ericsson, *Vox Humana*, 17 June 2018](https://www.voxhumanajournal.com/ericsson2018.html). Ericsson reports Messiaen’s answer to his challenge about discrepancies between the composer’s demands and his own playing. The page correctly dates the published recollection, not the undated original exchange; the initial capital follows its use as a complete sentence.
- **“I never called him by his first name.”** Exact clause in [Michael White’s interview with Yvonne Loriod, *The Independent*, 10 January 1999](https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/her-maitre-s-voice-1046085.html). Loriod continues that she used “Messiaen” or “maitre”. The page correctly gives the speaker, interviewer and publication date. Its short excerpt closes a clause that continues after a comma in the newspaper.
- **Revelation 10:1, 5–6.** The protected Latin matches the [Clementine Vulgate text](https://ebible.org/latVUC/REV10.htm), including the angel’s rainbow, feet on sea and land, raised hand, oath and *tempus non erit amplius*. The accompanying English is a faithful direct rendering, not a quotation attributed to a named published English Bible. Sarah Emanuel’s [Cambridge University Press introduction](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/96599/excerpt/9781108496599_excerpt.pdf) identifies the seer as John on Patmos and treats Revelation in its first-century setting.

Reported for the timeline owner: prefer **1987–91** if the row is to include the earliest work on the composition. The date was not changed under the protection contract. This is the sole unresolved source disagreement, not an unsupported claim elsewhere in the prose.
