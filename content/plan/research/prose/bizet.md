# Bizet — quotation, fact and rights sources

Research for the new composer page, bibliography, map, two listening entries and their dossiers, and official listening links. Sources checked 19 October 2026. Historical assertions are separated below from editorial readings of the music. Dates attached to a recording session, a commercial publication and a digital upload are not interchangeable.

## Sound and notation

| Claim / example | Source and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Habanera bass: dotted quaver, semiquaver, two quavers; D–A–D–A; chromatic descending voice; D-minor verse and D-major refrain | [Choudens first edition, 1875](https://archive.org/details/carmenopracomi00bize), no. 5, printed pp. 49–59; [Habanera analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habanera_(aria)). The first two bass bars were read directly from the scanned notation on printed p. 49, PDF p. 57. ABC retains the bass pitches, octaves and durations; articulation and dynamics are deliberately omitted and the caption says so. |
| Toreador Song’s F-minor verse / F-major refrain; first refrain marked piano and “avec fatuité” | Same [first vocal score](https://archive.org/details/carmenopracomi00bize), no. 14, printed pp. 128 and 131; both visually inspected. The scan lacks pp. 129–130. Our translation of “avec fatuité” is “with self-satisfaction”. [Toreador Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toreador_Song) corroborates mode and form. |
| Second ABC panel | Explicitly labelled invented schematic, not quotation or reduction: two F-minor triad bars followed by two F-major triad bars illustrate A♭ becoming A. The actual score’s modulation is not claimed to consist of these four bars. |
| José’s Flower Song asks for pianissimo on a high B♭ near its close; Nadir’s sustained romance | [Carmen musical discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen), citing Dean’s score discussion; [Macdonald, Les Pêcheurs de perles](https://hdwlabs.artsci.wustl.edu/bizet/works/Pecheurs_de_perles.html), musical contents. Character readings of softness and vulnerability are editorial. The B♭ is not identified as the aria’s literal final pitch. |
| Instrumental colour in L’Arlésienne; public and private sound in Carmen’s ending | [Bru Zane Paris exhibition](https://www.bruzanemediabase.com/exploration/expositions/georges-bizet-paris), L’Arlésienne; [Carmen musical discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen), final scene. Claims about dramaturgical effect are editorial interpretations grounded in the specified scoring and scene. |

The score copy is the 1875 Choudens first edition, plate A.C. 3082, arranged for voice and piano by Bizet, digitised from the University of North Carolina. [IMSLP’s edition listing](https://imslp.org/wiki/Carmen_(Bizet,_Georges)) identifies it as public domain and notes publication in the *Bibliographie de la France*, 17 April 1875. IMSLP’s direct PDF returned a bot check; the same original scan was inspected through [Internet Archive’s PDF](https://archive.org/download/carmenopracomi00bize/carmenopracomi00bize.pdf). This is not a modern arrangement. The original edition numbers the Habanera 5 and the Toreador couplets 14; those numbers are not silently confused with the later full score’s numbering.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against freshly fetched sources. Validator: `ok` for composer (2 scores, 3 images), bibliography, elsewhere and geo files after the edits below.

**Changes**

- Life, *Carmen* rehearsals: “the heroine refused the moral accommodation the theatre’s management wanted” → “the theatre’s directors worried about a risqué heroine who is killed on stage”. Macdonald documents the directors’ misgivings about Carmen’s “risqué character” and her violent death on stage; no source in hand says the management asked for a moral accommodation. https://hdwlabs.artsci.wustl.edu/bizet/ref/biography.html
- Life, 1875: “Bizet knew that other theatres were interested” → “Shortly before his death Bizet was planning sung recitatives for a Vienna production” (Macdonald’s *Carmen* entry, citing Léon Husson, *Le Pays*, 8 June 1875: https://hdwlabs.artsci.wustl.edu/bizet/works/Carmen.html ); the plural claim is not in the biography text.
- Life, 1875: “Vienna staged the work that October, with Guiraud’s recitatives” → “Vienna staged the work that October.” The sources conflict: Bru Zane says the recitative version by Guiraud was created in Vienna on 23 October 1875 (https://www.bruzanemediabase.com/exploration/oeuvres/carmen-meilhac-l-halevy-bizet ), while Macdonald’s *Carmen* performance list says the Vienna Hofoper production was “without Guiraud’s recitatives” (and a Vienna manuscript score elsewhere in the same entry includes them). The aside on opéra-comique still says, correctly, that Guiraud supplied recitatives after Bizet’s death.
- Life, 1875: “three more early in 1876” → “three more in 1876” (Macdonald: “three more the following year”; the last performance of the original run was 15 February 1876 per Wikipedia’s *Carmen*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen ).
- Score example 1 (Habanera bass): the ABC gave the first D as a dotted quaver (`D,,3`). Bizet’s vocal score, p. 49 (Internet Archive scan https://archive.org/details/carmenopracomi00bize , page image viewed), prints a quaver D, a semiquaver rest, a semiquaver A, a quaver D an octave higher and a quaver A (D2, A2, D3, A2; one flat; 2/4; ♩ = 72; *pp*; staccato and slurs). ABC is now `D,,2 z A,, D,2 A,,2` and the caption says dynamics, slurs and staccato marks are omitted and credits the semiquaver rest. Pitches, metre, key and tempo were already right. The Sound paragraph’s “dotted quaver, semiquaver, quaver, quaver” still correctly describes the spacing of the four attacks.
- Reading: the three entries now carry publishers (Oxford University Press 2014; Cambridge University Press 1992; Alfred A. Knopf 1958), matching the `Author, Title (Publisher, year)` format used on the Debussy page.

**Quotation confirmed**

- “You were the beginning of my life as an artist. I come from you.” — Bizet to Gounod, October 1872: « Vous avez été le commencement de ma vie d’artiste. Je résulte de vous. » Palazzetto Bru Zane exhibition text, “Bizet lui écrira en octobre 1872”: https://www.bruzanemediabase.com/exploration/expositions/georges-bizet-paris .
