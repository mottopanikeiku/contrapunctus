The rewrite retains the original ABC, image filenames, Jammes poem, reading list and lineage arrays. The two dated quotations have different kinds of authority: a surviving joint letter signed by Lili, and Nadia’s letter quoted by its recipient. Neither is presented as an invented private conversation.

## Editorial decisions

The life gives no confident retrospective diagnosis: consulted sources variously name intestinal tuberculosis, cancer or immune deficiency. Nadia’s teaching career is kept distinct from Lili’s compositional influence. No unsupported deathbed speech is supplied. The joint champagne letter is attributed as a joint letter, and the seller’s unsubstantiated claim that Delvincourt secretly helped orchestrate the prize cantata is excluded.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked all 25 claim groups in the source table against fetched sources, including the biographical dates and relationships, competition details, musical descriptions, work dates and forces, Nadia’s later career, image metadata and recording credits. No reader-facing factual correction was needed.

Quotations confirmed:

- “your young colleagues imprisoned in the château to sing the loves of Faust and Helen” — faithful translation of the joint letter to Fernand Halphen, 31 May 1913, written by Claude Delvincourt and signed by the five competitors including Lili. [Autograph facsimile and transcription](https://www.traces-ecrites.com/document/rare-lettre-de-lili-boulanger-au-moment-de-son-prix-de-rome/).
- “She died in 1918 but her words are still guiding and helping me.” — Nadia Boulanger to Christopher Palmer, November 1967, quoted by Palmer himself. [Hyperion notes](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dc.asp?dc=D_CDA66726).
- The nine lines of Jammes’s “Elle était descendue” match the text printed in the [Harmonia Mundi booklet](https://www.harmoniamundi.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/902356.58_digitalbooklet-2.pdf); the English rendering preserves the meaning. Original and translation left unchanged.

Additional primary-source checks:

- The 1913 Ricordi vocal score explicitly names Faust as tenor, Méphistophélès as baritone and Hélène as mezzo, and dedicates the work to Nadia. Its title, dedication and dramatis-personae pages were visually inspected in the [Sibley Music Library scan](https://urresearch.rochester.edu/fileDownloadForInstitutionalItem.action?itemId=24284&itemFileId=75161), linked by the [University of Rochester catalogue](https://urresearch.rochester.edu/institutionalPublicationPublicView.action?institutionalItemId=23776&versionNumber=1).
- Both photograph captions are confirmed by BnF’s own catalogue: [Lili, Meurisse 40219, 1913](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb415697971); [Lili and Nadia, Meurisse 40225, 1913](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb41569799q).
- The full orchestral recording credits and psalm texts were checked in the [Chandos CHAN 9745 booklet](https://www.chandos.net/chanimages/Booklets/CH9745.pdf). The universal wish for happiness in *Vieille prière bouddhique* is explicit in the text printed on p. 6 of the [Cambridge Philharmonic programme](https://cambridgephilharmonic.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/March-2023-programme3019.pdf).

No unresolved factual claim remains.
