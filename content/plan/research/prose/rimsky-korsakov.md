# Rimsky-Korsakov — quotation and fact sources

Sources below were located through web search and their pages or relevant PDF passages read on **19 October 2026**. Page numbers for *My Musical Life* are the printed book’s numbers, not the PDF counter. The principal text used is Judah A. Joffe’s English translation, edited by Carl Van Vechten, second revised edition, 1923. The memoir first appeared in Russian in March 1909. Its autobiographical testimony is identified as such; its judgements of other composers are not treated as neutral verdicts.

Dates in the memoir generally use the old Russian calendar. The prose normally gives a month or year; the date of the Jurgenson letter below follows its published attribution. The immutable birth/death fields have not been changed. Recording comments are editorial recommendations informed by the checked criticism below, not claims of a new listening session.

## Corrections, exclusions and source cautions

- Removed the claim that “mistaken identity” helps cause the catastrophe in *The Tsar’s Bride*. The poisoning precedes Marfa’s final failure to recognise Gryaznoy; the rewritten note gives the actual causal sequence.
- Narrowed Bilibin’s alleged involvement in the opera’s “first productions” to the documented Zimin production in 1909, distinguishing it from Korovin’s Bolshoi production.
- Removed the unsupported explanatory leap in the second schematic caption from two diminished-seventh chords to “dominant-style sonorities” floating free of a single key. The caption now describes the pitches, durations and enharmonic spelling actually present.
- Did not retain the unqualified claim that Rimsky-Korsakov’s changes established *Boris Godunov* internationally. The rewrite addresses his interventions, objections and own defence from the memoir. The suggested Library of Congress essay was found by web search but returned a 403 response; a browser attempt remained at a Cloudflare challenge. It is **not** counted as a page-read source: https://www.loc.gov/collections/moldenhauer-archives/articles-and-essays/guide-to-archives/boris-godunov/ .
- The Tretyakov article calls Zimin’s production uncensored, whereas Robinson’s preface describes a censored premiere. The page makes no claim about whether Zimin’s text was wholly uncut. Both identify the 1909 production; the explicit commander/Persian alterations are assigned only to the Bolshoi.
- The memoir contains errors and retrospective advocacy. Its mistaken description of the earliest *Night on Bald Mountain* and its judgement that Mussorgsky’s original workmanship was unacceptable are not adopted as objective facts. The latter is presented as Rimsky-Korsakov’s opinion.
- No unresolved factual claim has knowingly been left in the rewritten prose. The dispute about Zimin’s cuts remains outside the page’s assertions. No audio audition is claimed.

### Correction

- **‘Across four tales from The Thousand and One Nights’ → ‘Across four movements drawing on The Thousand and One Nights’.** The composer describes separate episodes scattered through all four movements and expressly rejects fixed narrative identities for most themes. Four movements do not establish four discrete tales. *My Musical Life*, printed pp. 247–249: https://s9.imslp.org/files/imglnks/usimg/7/72/IMSLP721391-PMLP52433-My_Musical_Life_eng_edt.pdf . This supersedes the earlier ledger’s defence of ‘four tales’ as shorthand.

### Quotations checked

1. **‘one of its best and possibly its very best pupil’** — exact words in the composer’s retrospective account, printed p. 103 of Joffe’s 1923 translation. First Russian publication in March 1909 is confirmed by the second Russian preface in the same fetched volume: https://s9.imslp.org/files/imglnks/usimg/7/72/IMSLP721391-PMLP52433-My_Musical_Life_eng_edt.pdf .
2. **‘the very essence of the composition’** — exact extract from his account of *Capriccio espagnol*, printed p. 246, same memoir URL. The surrounding text also confirms the rehearsal applause, dedication, violin-fantasy origin and third-movement brass-balance warning.
