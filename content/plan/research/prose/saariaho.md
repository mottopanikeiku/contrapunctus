## Editorial decisions and limits

- The estate timeline says the first Darmstadt visit was in 1980; the Service interview has Saariaho recall 1978. The page gives no first-visit year. Freiburg is dated 1981 from the institutional biography, estate timeline and Saariaho’s own *La Passion de Simone* note.
- The general estate timeline contains some première-year inconsistencies; individual work entries were preferred. The rewrite does not repeat the timeline’s 2008 première date for *Laterna magica* or its 2002 première date for *Orion*.
- Removed the unsupported claim that Saariaho repeatedly described the bodily effect of Finland’s seasonal light in connection with the Gallen-Kallela pairing. The painting now has a stated visual comparison grounded in museum documentation and the composer’s northern-lights note.
- Removed the earlier unsupported stylistic comparison with Ferneyhough’s rhythmic density, blanket claims about what a listener loses track of, and abstract claims about orchestral relationships that named no musical event. Replaced these with documented players, notes and actions.
- Recording notes make editorial recommendations from the documented programmes and performers, without invented judgements about balance, tempo or recorded sound. Removed imperative listening instructions.
- Removed `featured`. Identity fields, portrait file/focus, every image file, both ABC strings, the two poem originals and existing translations, reading list and lineage arrays were intentionally retained. External links formerly embedded in the arts notes now live in this ledger; the protected reading list was left intact.
- Parent validation printed `ok`, checking both scores and both images. The life counted 1,311 words, with three margin notes and five sound aspects of 58–60 words. Final recording-note wording was made more opinionated without adding historical or performance facts. No unresolved factual assertion is knowingly left in the reader-facing prose.

### Corrections

- **Diptych forces:** “Orchestra; alto flute, cello and electronics in the second work” → “Orchestra and electronics; alto flute and cello soloists in the second work”. Electronics are not confined to *…à la fumée*: the estate lists electronics and synthesizer for *Du cristal*, and synthesizer plus electronically modified solo instruments for its companion. The earlier evidence-table row was corrected too. Sources: https://saariaho.org/works/du-cristal and https://saariaho.org/works/a-la-fumee.
- **Vida translation:** “with good melodies and simple words” → “with good melodies and poor words”. The original *bons sons, ab paubres motz* contrasts good melodies with poor wording; “simple” softened that judgement. The Occitan original is unchanged. Gregory Nagy translates the phrase as “good melodies but with weak wordings”. Sources: https://www.rialto.unina.it/vidas_componimento/vida-di-jaufre-rudel-2/ and https://classical-inquiries.chs.harvard.edu/jaufre-rudel-his-distant-love-and-the-death-of-the-distant-lover-in-his-vida/.
- **Ledger only, harmonic-series description:** “Eleven harmonics above C” → “First eleven harmonics of C, including the fundamental”. The existing reader-facing caption already describes the first eleven harmonics correctly; the fundamental is the first, not an additional overtone. No ABC or caption change. Source: https://phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/strings.html.

### Confirmed quotations and translated texts

- “Music, from the start, was completely my own thing.” — Saariaho to Tom Service, interview published 2011, printed p. 3; exact wording: https://saariaho.org/media/pages/texts/e1e5ddfc6a-1767627091/meet-the-composer-kaija-saariaho-and-tom-service.pdf.
- “I don’t want the machine to compose for me – I like composing!” — Saariaho to Service, same publication, printed p. 11; exact excerpt after the introductory “And”: https://saariaho.org/media/pages/texts/e1e5ddfc6a-1767627091/meet-the-composer-kaija-saariaho-and-tom-service.pdf.
- “Lanquan li jorn son lonc e may … No-m valon plus que l’yverns gelatz.” — all seven displayed lines compared with the estate’s Rudel text; differences are apostrophe/interpunct typography only. Twelfth-century lyric persona recalling an unnamed distant love, not an authenticated address to the historical countess; the page’s English translation preserves the stanza’s sense: https://saariaho.org/works/lonh and https://classical-inquiries.chs.harvard.edu/jaufre-rudel-his-distant-love-and-the-death-of-the-distant-lover-in-his-vida/.
- “Jaufres Rudels de Blaia si fo mout gentils hom, princes de Blaia. Et enamoret se de la comtessa de Tripol, ses vezer, per lo ben qu’el n’auzi dire als pelerins que venguen d’Antiocha. E fez de leis mains vers ab bons sons, ab paubres motz.” — exact three-sentence anonymous *vida* opening; no named personal addressee. English checked, with “poor words” restored as above: https://www.rialto.unina.it/vidas_componimento/vida-di-jaufre-rudel-2/ and https://classical-inquiries.chs.harvard.edu/jaufre-rudel-his-distant-love-and-the-death-of-the-distant-lover-in-his-vida/.
