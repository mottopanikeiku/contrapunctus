Work titles and the preserved sonnet are not presented as biographical testimony. Critical descriptions of the music and recordings are editorial judgements; the sources below establish the musical details and recording identities on which they rest.

## Editorial limits

No newly sourced lineage entries were needed, so both arrays are preserved. Neither a definite asthma diagnosis nor certain authorship of the Seasons sonnets is asserted. All ABC strings, image files, poem text, reading entries and identity fields remain the supplied material; captions now distinguish the two schematics from Vivaldi’s score.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 31 factual claim-groups: the 25 rows above, plus the six supplementary checks below. Repeated facts across the life, works, captions and lineage prose were checked together. The preserved Spring sonnet and its translation were also compared with the text printed in the Boston Baroque note; the two instructional score examples remain explicitly identified as schematics, not quotations.

### Changes

- Painting date ‘1738’ → ‘c. 1738’. The owning museum dates Canaletto’s *Bacino di San Marco, Venice* ‘about 1738’, not securely to that year. [Museum of Fine Arts, Boston](https://customprints.mfa.org/detail/478331/canaletto-bacino-di-san-marco-venice-about-1738).
- Source-ledger locator ‘*Griselda*, Act I, scene II’ → ‘Act II, scene II’. Costanza’s *Agitata da due venti* follows the second act’s second-scene heading. The page itself did not give an act number. [Complete libretto](https://www.librettidopera.it/griseldazg/rid.html).

### Supplementary checks

1. More than five hundred concertos; the *Gloria*’s approximate date, twelve movements and forces: [Thomas May, Nashville Symphony](https://www.nashvillesymphony.org/classical-program-notes/antonio-vivaldi-gloria-r-589/).
2. *Ritornello* literally means ‘little return’; the opening material’s recurrence is described as a form, not an invariable rule: [W. S. Rockstro in Grove’s *Dictionary*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_Music_and_Musicians/Ritornello), with the musical examples in [Martin Pearlman’s Seasons note](https://baroque.boston/vivaldi-four-seasons).
3. Bach transcribed six concertos from Op. 3: [George Parsons, Signum/Hyperion booklet note](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dc.asp?dc=D_SIGCD807). The four-harpsichord arrangement changes Vivaldi’s B minor to A minor: [Netherlands Bach Society, BWV 1065](https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-1065). Publication by Roger in Amsterdam in 1711 independently confirmed by [La Biennale](https://www.labiennale.org/en/music/2024/music-performances/antonio-vivaldi).
4. Canaletto’s painting, approximate date and Boston collection: [owning museum](https://customprints.mfa.org/detail/478331/canaletto-bacino-di-san-marco-venice-about-1738).
5. *Ottone in villa*, 1713, Vicenza, three acts and five characters: [University of Bologna, Corago](https://corago.unibo.it/opera/Z000030184).
6. *Orlando*, 1727, three acts, Ariosto source, Ruggiero and obbligato flute; Ghezzi caricature in the Vatican: [Teatro La Fenice programme, pp. 13–20](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Orlando_furioso_per_web.pdf).

### Quotations confirmed

- ‘It is impossible to put on the opera without Girò, because no woman like her can be found.’ Faithful translation of ‘far l’opera senza la Girò non è possibile, perché non si può ritrovare simile donna’: Vivaldi to Guido Bentivoglio, 16 November 1737, quoted in [Michael Talbot, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/antonio-vivaldi_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/).
- ‘Tightness in the chest.’ Faithful translation of ‘strettezza di petto’ in the same 16 November 1737 letter; the distinction between Vivaldi’s symptom and a later asthma diagnosis is retained. [Talbot](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/antonio-vivaldi_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/).
- ‘Vivaldi has become one of my intimate friends, to sell me concertos very dear.’ Faithful translation of ‘Vivaldi s’est fait de mes amis intimes, pour me vendre des concertos bien chers’: Charles de Brosses to de Blancey, Venice, 29 August 1739. The immediately adjacent copyist boast, purchase, Pietà visits and gondola description are in the same letter. [Primary text, letter XVIII](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Lettres_famili%C3%A8res_%C3%A9crites_d%E2%80%99Italie_T.1/%C3%80_M._DE_BLANCEY._%E2%80%94_Suite_du_s%C3%A9jour_%C3%A0_Venise).
- Anonymous Spring sonnet and supplied translation: [Boston Baroque’s transcription and translation](https://baroque.boston/vivaldi-four-seasons); the page does not assert Vivaldi’s authorship.

No unresolved reader-facing claim remains. The previously recorded 27/28 July death-date discrepancy is a night-boundary disagreement between sources, not grounds to replace the protected 28 July metadata: the independent [Nashville Symphony biography](https://www.nashvillesymphony.org/classical-program-notes/antonio-vivaldi-gloria-r-589/) also gives 28 July.
