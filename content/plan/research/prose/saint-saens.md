## Discrepancies

- Date of the jubilee concert: the Société gives 2 May 1896, Henle’s preface (citing the letters) says it “ultimately took place on 2 June 1896”. We follow Henle.
- Gounod’s letter: *Musical Memories* (Rich) dates the Sunday as 11 December 1853, the Société’s French quotation says 18 December. The text says only “December 1853”.
- The Société’s 1882–1888 page says Liszt died on 31 May 1886; the site’s Liszt chapter and all other sources give 31 July. We avoid the date.
- The Société says he left the Madeleine on 15 April 1877 but also “until December 1877”; the geo record gives the year only.
- Chrudim: the Société names it; English sources say “a small Austrian village”. The text follows the Société.
- Hail California: the Société reports its first audition at the Paris Opéra on 28 April 1917, so the text says only that he took it to California.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026. Every source below was opened: the eleven Société Camille Saint-Saëns chronologies (camille-saint-saens.org, 1835–1852 to 1919–1921), *Musical Memories* (Gutenberg #16459), Macdonald’s BSO note, the Bru Zane *Danse macabre* page, Henle HN 1144 preface (Peter Jost; read in the English column), Borg-Wheeler’s Hyperion note, Hyperion’s and Pristine’s product pages, the Marston page, LiederNet, GregoBase 3441 (gabc), the Commons API for the five images, and French Wikipedia on Gaspard Deguerry.

**Corrected**

- *Father’s death.* The page said he died “a year to the day after his wedding”. The Société gives the marriage at Saint-Sulpice on 24 November 1834 and the death on 30 December 1835, “three months after the birth”. Now: “when the boy was not yet three months old”.
- *The Commune and the curé.* The page said that when the Commune was proclaimed in March “the curé of the Madeleine was shot”, and the geo entry said the same. The Société’s entry for 18 March is itself garbled (it says Deguerry “vient d’être fusillé”). French Wikipedia, citing the Lyon diocesan archives, has the abbé Deguerry imprisoned on 5 April and shot at La Roquette on 24 May 1871. The Société has Saint-Saëns, “ancien garde national et organiste”, leaving for London and staying to the end of May. Both texts now say that, and give 24 May for the shooting.
- *Liszt and the Organ Symphony.* The page and the influence edge said he had “played it through to Liszt, who had months to live” (Macdonald, BSO note). The Société says Liszt died “sans avoir pu l’entendre” (and gives a wrong death date, 31 May; he died on 31 July 1886); the Cleveland Orchestra note has him playing only parts of the work in progress. The sources do not agree, so the claim is out. What they share is kept: Saint-Saëns meant to dedicate the work to Liszt, who died that July, and the dedication reads “to the memory of Franz Liszt”. The edge `saint-saens-organ-symphony--liszt-memorial-dedication-1886` is rewritten the same way, with Wikipedia (citing Fallon 1973, p. 375: Liszt’s letter of 19 June 1886 acknowledging the intended dedication) added.
- *Organ Symphony, Part II.* “A scherzo is followed by a Presto in C major” corrected to Macdonald’s account: a scherzo in C minor whose middle section is a Presto in C major, with scales for the piano; the organ’s first entry is the soft D♭ chords in Part I; a C major organ chord opens the finale.
- *Last works.* “His last works were sonatas for oboe, clarinet and bassoon” is out: the Société has him writing songs (*Vieilles chansons*, July 1921) after the sonatas and orchestrating a romance and the *Valse nonchalante* in Algiers. The work table said the sonatas were “written in Algeria”; the Société says he composed them there and finished them after returning to Paris (14 April 1921). Now: “Begun in Algeria in the spring of 1921 and finished in Paris.”
- *Panama–Pacific Exposition.* “The French musicians’ representative” → “a representative of French artists” (Société: “représentant des artistes français”). The geo entry said he conducted “three concerts, including *Hail California*”; the Société says only “trois grands concerts de ses œuvres” and gives the first audition of *Hail California* as 28 April 1917 at the Opéra. “Including *Hail California*” removed.
- *Hyperion set.* Recorded September 2000, released September 2001 (Hyperion page); the composer page and the elsewhere file said “2000–01”. Corrected to 2000 / “September 2000”.
- *Héglon.* Marston spells the mezzo “Meyriane Héglon”; the elsewhere file had “Meyrianne”. Aligned to Marston.
- *Las Palmas.* The Société says only “aux Canaries” for April 1890; the entry now says so, and that the coordinates mark Las Palmas.

- Recordings: Marston track list (Saint-Saëns, 26 June 1904, 24 November 1919; Héglon sides from *Ascanio* and *Samson*); Pristine PASC448 (Munch, Nies-Berger, Philharmonic-Symphony Orchestra of New York, 1947/48; MusicWeb’s second review gives 10 November 1947).

**Unresolved**

- Where *Le Carnaval des animaux* was written: the Société names Chrudim; English sources say “a small Austrian village” (Wikipedia, citing Stegemann). The page follows the Société. The same Wikipedia page gives 3 March for the Lebouc performance; Mardi Gras 1886 fell on 9 March, the Société’s date.
- The 1916 “cannot sit still” quotation: the Société prints it without a recipient.
- “The first film score” is the Société’s own claim.
