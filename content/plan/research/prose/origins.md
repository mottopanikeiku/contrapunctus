# Origins prose: sources and editorial decisions

This records the historical and musical sources used for the rewrite of `content/pages/origins.mjs`. The page schema has no `sources` field. ABC examples, lesson ids and numerals, image files, listening ids and times, and the original poem and translation were retained.

## Pythagoras, strings and the page’s opening

- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, ‘Pythagoras’](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2026/entries/pythagoras/): Croton around 530 BC; no surviving writings by Pythagoras; the much later smithy story and the failure of its supposed hammer-weight experiment. The page calls the story a later legend, not a witnessed event.
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, ‘The History of Western Philosophy of Music: Antiquity to 1800’](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hist-westphilmusic-to-1800/), sections on Pythagorean ratios and Boethius: octave 2:1, fifth 3:2, fourth 4:3; transmission through Boethius and the quadrivium. The inverse string-length ratios in the lesson assume equal string material, thickness and tension.
- [Mark Lindley, ‘Acoustical rudiments’, Staatliches Institut für Musikforschung](https://www.simpk.de/forschung/publikationen/online-publikationen/1-lindley.html): simultaneous modes of string vibration, the harmonic series, pure intervals and the Pythagorean and syntonic commas. The seventh partial’s approximately 31-cent difference from equal-tempered B♭ and the eleventh’s approximately 49-cent difference from F♯ are rounded applications of the standard cents definition. These are explanatory tuning measurements, not measurements of a recording.
- [Vatican Museums, Raphael’s ‘School of Athens’](https://www.museivaticani.va/content/museivaticani/en/collezioni/musei/stanze-di-raffaello/stanza-della-segnatura/scuola-di-atene.html): Pythagoras and the diatessaron tablet; Euclid teaching geometry. The image note describes the figures in this fresco, without inventing a historical encounter.

## Chant and Hildegard

- [Rebecca Maloy’s review of Thomas Forrest Kelly, *Chant and Its Origins*, The Medieval Review](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/16936): Pepin and Charlemagne’s reforms, the Franco-Roman formation of Gregorian chant and early notation. Gregorian attribution is distinguished from personal authorship by Gregory I.
- [Kevin N. Moll, ‘Plainchant: Notation and Theory’, *Early Music in the West*, East Carolina University](https://ecu.pressbooks.pub/earlymusicinthewest/chapter/plainchant-notation-and-theory/): neumes, staff notation, pitch letters and clefs, the eight-mode system, psalm tones, hexachords and the later Guidonian hand. The Greek modal names belong to different historical systems; the lesson does not identify a medieval Dorian chant with an ancient Greek Dorian scale.
- [GregoBase, *Dies irae*, chant 1198](https://gregobase.selapa.net/chant.php?id=1198): opening melody and its source in the 1961 *Liber Usualis*, p. 1810. The original ABC and source-image link remain in place. Uniform modern note values in the example are a display convention, not a reconstruction of medieval rhythm.
- [International Society of Hildegard von Bingen Studies, ‘Music’](https://www.hildegard-society.org/p/music.html?m=0): the women’s community, chant repertory, melodic range and *Ordo virtutum*.
- [Hildegard Society FAQ](https://www.hildegard-society.org/p/faq.html?m=0): *Scivias* dated 1141–1151; Rupertsberg and the musical drama of the Virtues. No specific first performance of *Ordo virtutum* is asserted.
- [Hildegard’s letter to the prelates of Mainz, Latin with English translation](https://www.ldysinger.com/@texts2/1170_hildegard/02_hymns_music.htm): letter 23, dated 1178–79; the interdict following burial of an excommunicated man and its prohibition on sung worship. The edition identified there is Lieven Van Acker, *Epistolarium*, pars I, CCCM 91 (1991), pp. 61–66.
  - The page’s words “lose their share in the angels’ praises in heaven” are a fresh translation of `consortio angelicarum laudum in celo carebunt`.
  - Speaker, addressees and date appear in the narrative. The warning concerns people who silence praise without sufficient justification; it is not offered as an unqualified threat against anyone who restricts singing.
- [Commons catalogue for the Hildegard image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hildegard_von_Bingen.jpg): the Rupertsberg *Scivias* miniature, divine inspiration and the scribe. The date 1151 in the prose belongs to the completed book, not to the miniature.

## Guido of Arezzo

- [Massimo Scapin, ‘Papa Giovanni XIX e Guido d’Arezzo, mille anni dopo’, 19 April 2024](https://lanuovabq.it/it/papa-giovanni-xix-e-guido-darezzo-mille-anni-dopo): reproduces the Latin from Guido’s *Epistola ad Michaelem* describing John XIX examining the antiphoner and learning an unfamiliar verse before leaving his seat. This is the source for the opening scene and lede. The incident is paraphrased, with no invented dialogue. ‘Around 1028’ allows for the differing chronologies of the visit.
- Moll’s [plainchant chapter](https://ecu.pressbooks.pub/earlymusicinthewest/chapter/plainchant-notation-and-theory/) supports the teaching explanation: the staff fixes the placement of tones and semitones; the first six phrases of *Ut queant laxis* provide the syllables; overlapping hexachords and mutation developed the system. The hand is treated as a later teaching tradition, not as Guido’s documented invention. Later `si` and `do` are kept separate from his six-syllable system.
- [Web Gallery of Art, the Guidonian hand in Elias Salomonis](https://www.wga.hu/html_m/zgothic/miniatur/1251-300/3italian/061itali.html): *Scientia artis musicae*, c. 1274, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, MS D.75.inf., f. 6r. The image note refers to this surviving teaching image.

## Pérotin and Notre-Dame

- [Rob C. Wegman, ‘Anonymous IV and the Antiqui’ (2020)](https://www.robcwegman.org/opera/Rob%20C%20Wegman%20-%20Anonymous%20IV%20and%20the%20Antiqui%20%282020%29.pdf), especially pp. 148–49: Latin and English of Anonymous IV’s account of Léonin, Pérotin, the great book, revised clausulae, and four-part *Viderunt omnes* and *Sederunt principes*. Wegman dates the English writer’s account after 1280.
  - “Pérotin the Great” translates the attested `Perotini Magni`; the narrative attributes the name to the later writer.
  - A specific Christmas 1198 première is not asserted. The surviving witness is later than the music.
- [Moll, ‘Theory and Practice of Polyphony in the First Millennium’](https://ecu.pressbooks.pub/earlymusicinthewest/chapter/theory-and-practice-of-polyphony-in-the-first-millennium/): ninth-century *Musica enchiriadis*, parallel and modified organum.
- [Moll, ‘Sources, Genres, and Technical Issues of Gothic Polyphony’](https://ecu.pressbooks.pub/earlymusicinthewest/chapter/b-sources-genres-and-technical-issues-of-gothic-polyphony/): rhythmic modes, ligatures, organum, discant, clausulae, motets and the Florence manuscript. Its conventional earlier date for Anonymous IV is superseded in the narrative by Wegman’s more recent treatment.
- [Commons catalogue, opening of *Viderunt omnes*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:P%C3%A9rotin_-_Viderunt_omnes.jpg): Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, MS Pluteo 29.1, thirteenth century.

## Machaut and the ars nova

- [Alice V. Clark’s review of Anne Walters Robertson, *Guillaume de Machaut and Reims*, The Medieval Review](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/15661): service to John of Bohemia; Reims canonry; supervision of collected manuscripts; Marian worship and the memorial arrangements of Guillaume and Jean de Machaut. The old coronation story is confined to a corrective note.
- [Moll, ‘Ars Nova: New Approaches to Musical Art after 1300’](https://ecu.pressbooks.pub/earlymusicinthewest/chapter/b-ars-nova-new-approaches-to-musical-art-after-1300/): tempus and prolation, duple and triple division, signs, colour and talea, and hocket.
- [Moll, ‘Repertoires and Practical Manifestations of the French Ars Nova’](https://ecu.pressbooks.pub/earlymusicinthewest/chapter/c-repertoires-and-practical-manifestations-of-the-french-ars-nova/): Machaut’s sacred and secular repertories and fixed song forms.
- [Commons catalogue for the Machaut miniature](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Machaut_1.jpg): Nature presents Sense, Rhetoric and Music; fourteenth-century manuscript image.
- The five-note/three-duration explanation is a direct reading of the retained schematic ABC. The second D falls on the last beat of bar 2; both cycles restart together after fifteen attacks. No historical composition is attributed to this invented example.

## Dufay, Josquin, Palestrina and Tallis

- [*Nuper rosarum flores*: occasion, score structure and Latin text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuper_rosarum_flores): Florence Cathedral’s consecration on 25 March 1436 by Eugene IV; the chant-based lower voices; the golden rose and the people’s prayer. No claim equating the motet’s proportions with the cathedral’s measurements is made.
- [DIAMM, Dufay’s *Missa L’homme armé*](https://www.diamm.ac.uk/compositions/69097/): attribution, Mass cycle and surviving manuscript witnesses.
- [Kerry McCarthy, ‘Ave Maria Program Notes’, Cappella Romana (2019)](https://cappellaromana.org/ave-maria-program-notes/): the 1502 advice to Ercole I d’Este comparing Josquin and Isaac, their different salary demands, the phrase “he composes when he wants to”, and Ercole’s choice. The quotation is seven words from the translation printed in the programme. The adviser is identified only as the singer described by this source.
- [Josquin’s *Ave Maria … virgo serena*: text and musical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ave_Maria_..._virgo_serena): four opening entries, paired voices, and the final homophonic petition. ‘O mother of God, remember me’ translates `O Mater Dei, memento mei`; these are the motet’s words, not a biographical utterance by Josquin.
- [Francesco Saggio, critical edition of *Missa Papae Marcelli*, Carus (2024), University of Pavia abstract](https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/1510580): six-voice Mass, Rome publication in 1567, Agazzari’s 1607 rescue story, manuscript/print differences and disputed attribution of the seven-voice second Agnus Dei. The former categorical statement that the Mass expands to seven at its ending has been removed: the textual situation needs a specialist note rather than that generalisation.
- [Italian reference account, *Missa papae Marcelli*](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missa_papae_Marcelli): syllabic, homorhythmic declamation and changing vocal groups. The edition above takes precedence over the reference article on disputed history and the second Agnus Dei.
- [Sara Kemsley, Cantate Choir programme on *Spem in alium*](https://www.cantate-choir.info/spem-in-alium-programme-notes/): forty parts in eight five-part choirs, separate and combined groups. The narrative uses the customary approximate date around 1570. No patron, first venue, circular disposition or precise première is asserted.
- [Visit Gent, ‘Heavenly notes and angelic song’](https://visit.gent.be/en/heavenly-notes-and-angelic-song): the altarpiece’s singers, organ, harp and bowed instrument; the second angel operating the bellows. No claim about recovering the singers’ exact pitches from their mouths is repeated.

## Instruments

- [St Mary’s, Lübeck: Dieterich Buxtehude](https://www.st-marien-luebeck.de/en/music/dieterich-buxtehude): appointment in 1668 and Bach’s journey in 1705; his extended stay. The page paraphrases the journey and overstayed leave without quoting the site’s loosely presented biographical extracts.
- [Organ Historical Society, ‘Pipes and Pitch’](https://organhistoricalsociety.org/OrganHistory/works/works04.htm): 8-, 4- and 16-foot pitch, stopped pipes and compound stops.
- [Lute: construction and historical development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lute): Arabic transmission, courses, frets, finger-plucked polyphony, tablature and extended bass strings of the theorbo. ‘ʿŪd’ refers to the medieval Arabic ancestor, not a claim that the present-day European lute developed from the present-day oud.
- [Ephraim Segerman, ‘On Praetorius and English Viol Pitches’, *Chelys* 17 (1988), pp. 24–27](https://vdgs.org.uk/chelys/17chelys.pdf): reproduces Praetorius’s account of six strings in fourths with a third in the middle, and the treble/tenor/bass family.
- [Musical Instrument Museum, ‘See a Rare Andrea Amati Violin in MIM’s Europe Gallery’](https://mim.org/news/see-a-rare-andrea-amati-violin-in-mims-europe-gallery/): Amati’s four-generation family, the surviving violin from the 1560s, Cremona and its materials. No claim about Stradivari’s disputed apprenticeship remains.
- [Harpsichord: mechanism](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpsichord): the key, jack, plectrum and damper; little dynamic change from touch alone. The mobile URL successfully returned the complete mechanism section where the ordinary URL returned only an introductory summary.
- [Caravaggio’s *The Lute Player*: Hermitage version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lute_Player_(Caravaggio)): lute, violin, music books and Arcadelt madrigals. The image note interprets those visible objects; it does not identify an uncertain model or claim the painting records a particular performance.

## Monteverdi and opera

- [Tim Carter, ‘Claudio Monteverdi: A Chronology’, *Journal of Seventeenth-Century Music* 18/1](https://sscm-jscm.org/jscm-issues/volume-18-no-1/claudio-monteverdi-a-chronology/): Artusi’s 1600/1603 publications; the Fifth Book (1605); Mantua, 24 February 1607; Giulio Cesare’s explanation in the 1607 *Scherzi musicali*; the 1609 score; San Marco in 1613 and the later Venetian theatre works.
- [Tim Carter, ‘Monteverdi and Some Problems of Biography’](https://sscm-jscm.org/jscm-issues/volume-18-no-1/monteverdi-and-some-problems-of-biography/): the Artusi controversy and the relation of words and harmony. ‘The words govern the harmony’ is a paraphrase, not a fabricated quotation.
- [Mauro Masiero, ‘L’Orfeo di Monteverdi – Le basi’, 1 July 2024](https://www.quinteparallele.net/compositori/lorfeo-di-monteverdi-le-basi/): Carlo Magni’s letter to his brother in Rome, 23 February 1607, with the Italian `tutti li interlocutori parleranno musicalmente`; plot, Charon’s bass and regal, instrumental replies in *Possente spirto*, alternative vocal lines in the 1609 score, and the ferryman’s refusal followed by sleep.
  - “All the characters will speak musically” is a fresh translation of the quoted Italian. The narrative supplies writer, recipient, places and date.
  - The article’s speculative reconstruction of the première’s ending is not used. Nor is its incorrect identification of the regal as a free-reed instrument: the page simply calls it a small reed organ.
- [Monteverdi Digital Text Edition, *Cruda Amarilli*](https://www.monteverdi-dte.com/madrigal-book/cruda-amarilli/): Guarini’s Italian, the 1605 first edition and opening position in Book V. The lover’s comparison of Amarilli with an unheeding snake is a paraphrase of that text.
- [Early opera history](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origini_dell%27opera): Bardi’s Florentine discussions, Galilei, Caccini, *Dafne* (1598), Peri’s *Euridice* (1600), and *Le nuove musiche* (1602). No recovered sound of Greek tragedy is claimed.
- [Commons record for the Strozzi portrait](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bernardo_Strozzi_-_Claudio_Monteverdi_(c.1630).jpg): c. 1630, Ferdinandeum, and identification through the engraving in *Fiori poetici* (1644). The existing qualified artwork title is retained.

## Continuo and temperament

- [Agostino Agazzari, *Del sonare sopra’l basso con tutti li stromenti e dell’uso loro nel conserto* (Siena, 1607), critical transcription edited by Frans Wiering](https://tmiweb.science.uu.nl/text/critical-edition/agadel.html): foundation instruments (p. 3), figures and 4–3 (p. 5), chordal weight according to the number of voices (p. 6), over-busy lute players (p. 8), and the organist’s library compared with a lawyer’s (p. 12).
  - “Soup and confusion” is a fresh translation of `zuppa, e confusione`. The complaint and the library joke are attributed to this book, not invented speech.
- The C-major inversions and 4–3 suspension are direct explanations of the retained schematic ABC. Its chord spacing is one possible realisation, not an original continuo source.
- The [Stanford historical survey](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hist-westphilmusic-to-1800/) supports Rameau’s 1722 treatise and his account of harmony.
- [Lindley, *Valuable Nuances of Tuning for Part I of J. S. Bach’s Das wohl temperirte Clavier*](https://www.simpk.de/forschung/publikationen/online-publikationen/mark-lindley-valuable-nuances-of-tuning-for-bachs-wtc-i.html), with its [acoustical introduction](https://www.simpk.de/forschung/publikationen/online-publikationen/1-lindley.html) and [tuning schemes](https://www.simpk.de/forschung/publikationen/online-publikationen/mark-lindley-valuable-nuances-of-tuning-for-bachs-wtc-i/standard-titel.html): fifth/octave discrepancy, pure thirds, quarter-comma meantone, extra keyboard notes, equal temperament, unequal circulating temperaments and the 1722 collection’s twenty-four keys. The page does not endorse one speculative Bach temperament.
- [Web Gallery of Art, Ter Borch’s *The Concert*](https://www.wga.hu/html/t/terborch/3/concer.html): Berlin painting, c. 1675, bowed bass and harpsichord. The old unqualified identification as a cello was replaced with ‘bowed bass’: the museum search record calls it a viola da gamba, while WGA calls it a cello. The picture establishes the instruments’ presence, not the music being played or an actual continuo realisation.

## Limits of the rewrite

No imagined dialogue or invented first-person reminiscence has been added. Classroom examples remain explicitly schematic. Modern recordings keep their existing pointers; their short descriptions no longer instruct the listener or pretend to reconstruct medieval performance. Claims about disputed premières, instrument identities, authorship and portrait identification are either omitted or separated from the narrative in notes. Unnecessary catalogue excursions (Petrucci’s edition, Viadana’s collection and later Cremonese alterations) were cut for focus, not presented as disproved facts.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against sources opened in this pass.

**Changes**

- “Carlo Magni” → “Carlo Magno” (lesson viii, opening paragraph). The courtier’s name is Magno in the scholarly accounts, which describe him as a Gonzaga court official writing on 23 February 1607 to his brother Giovanni Magno in Rome; “Magni” appears only in the Masiero article. MEAS: <https://rivista.dilef.it/articoli/prima-del-libretto-per-musica/1451> (prints the Italian letter and names “Carlo Magno”); EXT: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Orfeo> (pointer). The quoted words are unchanged and correct: “… dimani sera il Ser.mo S.r Prencipe ne fa recitare una … che sarà singolare posciache tutti li interlocutori parleranno musicalmente” — “all the characters will speak musically”. The letter’s writer is in Mantua (“Hieri fu recitata la Comedia nel solito scenico Teatro”). First performance on 24 February 1607 before the Accademia degli Invaghiti: <https://sscm-jscm.org/jscm-issues/volume-18-no-1/claudio-monteverdi-a-chronology/>.
- Ghent Altarpiece note (lesson vi, `arts`): “other angels play an organ, a harp and a bowed string instrument … a second angel’s hair is just visible behind the organist” → “other angels play an organ and a harp, and one holds a bowed string instrument … a strand of a second angel’s hair shows at the far left of the organ panel”. Visit Gent says the vielle angel “does not play the vielle, but simply holds the instrument”, and that the bellows angel’s hair shows “on the far left of the panel”. MEAS: <https://visit.gent.be/en/heavenly-notes-and-angelic-song>.

**Quotations**

- Hildegard to the prelates of Mainz, letter 23 (1178–79): Latin “consortio angelicarum laudum in celo carebunt”, said of those who impose silence on a church’s praise “sine pondere certe rationis” (Baird translation: lose “their place among the chorus of angels”, “without just cause”). The page’s “lose their share in the angels’ praises in heaven” is a faithful rendering; the nuns could “only recite” the office. MEAS: <https://www.ldysinger.com/@texts2/1170_hildegard/02_hymns_music.htm> (Van Acker, CCCM 91, pp. 61–66).
- Guido to Michael of Pomposa, *Epistola ad Michaelem*: Pope John “non prius destitit, aut de loco in quo sedebat, abscessit, donec unum versiculum inauditum … edisceret, ut quod vix credebat in aliis, tam subito in se recognosceret” — matches the opening paragraph (paraphrase, no quoted words). MEAS: <https://lanuovabq.it/it/papa-giovanni-xix-e-guido-darezzo-mille-anni-dopo> (reproduces the Latin). Dating: Treccani puts the encounter no later than 1032 (John XIX’s death) and the *Micrologus* from 1026; the page’s “around 1028” and “around 1025–28” stand. <https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/guido-d-arezzo_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/>.
- Josquin and Isaac, Ferrara, 1502: “it is true that Josquin composes better, but he composes when he wants to, and not when one wants him to, and he is asking 200 ducats in salary while Isaac will come for 120 — but Your Lordship will decide”; written by one of the duke’s singers; the duke chose Josquin. MEAS: <https://cappellaromana.org/ave-maria-program-notes/> (McCarthy). The page quotes six words and states the price difference.
- Agazzari, *Del sonare sopra’l basso* (Siena, 1607): lutenists who “non fanno altro che tirare, e diminuire dal principio al fine, e massime in compagnia d’altri stromenti, che fanno il simile, doue non si sente altro che zuppa, e confusione”; and the organist who would need “maggior libraria, che qual si voglia Dottor di legge”. MEAS: <https://tmiweb.science.uu.nl/text/critical-edition/agadel.html> (Wiering’s transcription). “Soup and confusion” is a faithful translation.
- Anonymous IV: “Perotini Magni”, “quadrupla optima sicut Viderunt, Sederunt”, Leonin’s “magnum librum organi de gradali et antifonario pro servitio divino multiplicando”; the writer’s treatise dates from “some time after 1280”. MEAS: Wegman, “Anonymous IV and the Antiqui” (2020), <https://www.robcwegman.org/opera/Rob%20C%20Wegman%20-%20Anonymous%20IV%20and%20the%20Antiqui%20%282020%29.pdf>, pp. 148–49.
- Ut queant laxis (`original`): the six half-lines open Ut, Resonare, Mira, Famuli, Solve, Labii, then Sancte Iohannes; Latin text and the ut–re–mi–fa–sol–la derivation, plus later *si* from “Sancte Ioannes” and *do* for *ut* by Doni. MEAS: <https://lanuovabq.it/it/papa-giovanni-xix-e-guido-darezzo-mille-anni-dopo>.

**Facts confirmed**

- *Dies irae*: GregoBase transcription (c4 clef) reads f e f d e c d d / f fg fe dc e f e d / a cd d dc e f e d, from the *Liber Usualis*, Solesmes 1961, p. 1810; the lesson’s ABC (F E F D E C D D … A, C D D D C E F E D) reproduces it note for note. MEAS: <https://gregobase.selapa.net/chant.php?id=1198>.
- Dufay, *Nuper rosarum flores*: composed for the consecration of Santa Maria del Fiore by Eugene IV on 25 March 1436; both tenors use the introit *Terribilis est locus iste*; the upper voices’ text speaks of the pope’s gift of roses (the golden rose, plural in the text) and the prayer of Florence’s people. MEAS: <https://www.diamm.ac.uk/documents/175/09_Du_Fay_Nuper_rosarum_flores.pdf>.
- Machaut: nearly two decades in the service of John of Bohemia (d. 1346), then canon of Reims; the *Messe de Nostre Dame*, a Marian votive Mass for an altar in the nave of Reims Cathedral, also intended as a memorial for himself and his brother; the 1364 coronation link was removed by Robertson and Leech-Wilkinson. MEAS: <https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/15661> (Clark’s review of Robertson). Date c. 1360–65, first four-voice Mass by a known composer: <https://cappellaromana.org/machaut-messe-de-nostre-dame/>.
- Palestrina, *Missa Papae Marcelli*, six voices; Agazzari first told the rescue story in 1607. EXT: <https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/1510580>.
- Tallis, *Spem in alium*: forty parts in eight five-part choirs; first performance placed in 1570 or 1571 by Denis Stevens. EXT: <https://www.cantate-choir.info/spem-in-alium-programme-notes/>.
- *L’Orfeo*, *Possente spirto*: after the five tercets, replies by two violins, two cornetti, double harp and bowed strings; 1609 print gives a plain vocal line and an ornamented line; Charon, with regal, is unmoved; Orpheus’s lament puts him to sleep. MEAS: <https://www.quinteparallele.net/compositori/lorfeo-di-monteverdi-le-basi/>.
- Amati violin: made in the 1560s from maple, spruce, ebony and rosewood; Andrea Amati patriarch of four generations of Cremona makers; now in the Musical Instrument Museum, Phoenix. MEAS: <https://mim.org/news/see-a-rare-andrea-amati-violin-in-mims-europe-gallery/>.
- Hildegard image: Rupertsberg *Scivias* miniature, dated “etwa 1175” and described as Hildegard receiving inspiration and passing it to her scribe (`date` “Late twelfth century” stands). MEAS: <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hildegard_von_Bingen.jpg> (upload log).
- Ter Borch, *The Concert*: Berlin Gemäldegalerie, c. 1675 (also dated 1673–75), a woman in white satin seen from behind in the foreground playing a viola da gamba (WGA calls it a cello) and a second woman at the harpsichord. MEAS: <https://www.wga.hu/html/t/terborch/3/concer.html>; the Berlin museum description is quoted in search results only (<https://smb.museum-digital.de/index.php?oges=227016&t=objekt>), not opened.
- Ghent Altarpiece: singing angels around a lectern with an open score; the organist angel with a second angel working the bellows; harp and vielle angels. MEAS: <https://visit.gent.be/en/heavenly-notes-and-angelic-song>.
- Pythagoras: emigrated to Croton about 530 BCE; wrote nothing; the smithy story is “clearly false” because hammer pitch is not proportional to weight. MEAS: <https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2026/entries/pythagoras/>. Cents: seventh partial 968.8 cents above the fundamental, 31 cents below the B♭ at 1000; eleventh partial 551.3 cents, 49 cents below F♯ at 600; 81:64 versus 5:4 for the Pythagorean and pure thirds; the diagram plots partials 1–16. MEAS by arithmetic and `src/build.mjs` (`harmonicSeriesSvg`).
- Isorhythm example: the talea (long–short–short) has four beats, the colour has five pitches; the second D falls on the last beat of bar 2; the pattern repeats after fifteen attacks. MEAS from the ABC (fifteen notes in five bars).
- Continuo example: C held over F then G, bass F→G, 4–3 resolving to B and C. MEAS from the ABC.
