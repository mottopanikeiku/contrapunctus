## Claims omitted or narrowed

- The exact assertion that Bridge’s lessons began in 1927 is replaced by the securely dated 1927 introduction. The archive article places the lessons in early 1928; other biographies use 1927.
- The earlier generic assertion that Adès returned to Britten’s individual-versus-society operatic problem is replaced by the documented festival connection; no unsupported direct borrowing is asserted.
- The Constable note no longer makes an unsourced negative claim about the absence of influence. It gives the actual regional and festival connections.
- ABC notation, poem originals, image filenames, identity metadata, reading list and lineage arrays were left unchanged.

## Fact-check (independent)

Rechecked all 40 grouped claims in the table above, including the four quotations from testimony, both poetic extracts, dates and personnel, musical descriptions, photographs and art, recording credits and the prose lineage connections. Sources were fetched independently; the Wikipedia entries were not used as final authority. Interpretative descriptions of the music and recordings remain editorial rather than claims of a new listening session.

### Change

- “He was still a student when he began writing songs for the children at his brother’s school” → “In 1933 he began writing songs for the children at his brother’s school.” The RCM confirms the overall 1930–33 period, but the retrieved institutional pages do not establish the month of departure; searches surfaced conflicting July and December dates. The [British Library autograph catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001991534) dates the earliest identified song to 2 November 1933. The plain year is independently secure, without inferring that formal enrolment and attendance ended together. The school and 1933–35 span are also confirmed by the [Britten Pears Arts song catalogue](http://www.brittensongs.org/collection/friday-afternoons/) and [publisher](https://www.boosey.com/cr/music/Benjamin-Britten-Friday-Afternoons/4561).

### Quotations confirmed

- “I used to get sent to the other side of the room” / “Bridge would play what I’d written and demand if it was what I’d really meant”: exact words in Britten’s 1963 *Sunday Telegraph* recollection, separated only by the page’s attribution; [Britten Pears Arts reproduction](https://www.brittenpearsarts.org/news/work-of-the-week-17-variations-on-a-theme-of-frank-bridge).
- “I write music, now, in Aldeburgh, for people living there”: exact opening clause of Britten’s 1964 Aspen Award speech; [archive account](https://www.brittenpearsarts.org/news/britten-abroad-part-one).
- “I shall never forget the impact made on me by the Japanese theatre”: exact words, Britten’s 1958 broadcast to Japan; [archive account](https://www.brittenpearsarts.org/news/benjamin-britten-curlew-river-and-sumidagawa).
- “I am here as your mouthpiece and I live in your music”: exact words, Pears to Britten, 21 November 1974; [letter transcription and photograph](https://www.brittenpearsarts.org/news/my-beloved-man-the-letters-of-benjamin-britten-and-peter-pears), with the date independently corroborated by [Thornton Miller, p. 8, note 10](https://www.britishmusicsociety.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/6-TM-24-01-22.pdf).
- Crabbe’s six lines beginning “He nursed the feelings”: confirmed in [*The Borough*, Letter XXII](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5210/pg5210-images.html), with the modernised contractions explicitly acknowledged by the existing caption. The excerpt follows the apprentices’ deaths. Birthplace/year and 1810 publication checked in [Leslie Stephen’s *Dictionary of National Biography* entry](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Crabbe,_George).
- Owen’s “Anthem for Doomed Youth”: all fourteen lines checked against the [University of Virginia’s transcription of the 1920 edition](https://anthology.lib.virginia.edu/work/Owen/owen-anthem); the 1917 composition year is confirmed by the [Academy of American Poets’ dated manuscript account](https://poets.org/text/draft-wilfred-owens-anthem-doomed-youth).
