## Hosted recordings: pitch, speed and identification

- Both transfers sound about 27 cents above A = 440 (estimate), within a quarter-tone of the written pitch; no speed correction.
- The carrier names no suite. A pitch-class comparison with the printed allemandes and gigues of the four 1687 suites gave the best match at written pitch for the F major suite on both sides (correlations 0.59 and 0.72; the next best at written pitch 0.44 and 0.51). Alignment tests on harpsichord chroma did not separate the candidates, so the identification is inferred, is marked as inferred in the dossiers, and the listening notes name no suite and no bar numbers.
- Strain boundaries (Allemande about 0:40, 1:23, 2:08; Gigue about 0:42, 1:23) come from chroma recurrence and level dips and are marked passage-level.
- Allemande 3:04 and Gigue 2:34 against 2:28–3:16 and 1:33–2:42 for the same dance types on Verlet’s 1998 recording (Apple Music listing).

- Titon du Tillet’s age at death, the Mercure galant of December 1691 (“première musicienne du monde”), the number of performances of *Céphale et Procris* (five or six in secondary sources) and the exact premiere day were not used.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 19 October 2026 against sources opened again: Cessac’s France Archives biography; the Philidor catalogue (introduction, list of works, collection pages for 1687, 1707, the sonatas, the trio sonatas and cantata book I); Titon du Tillet, *Le Parnasse françois* (Internet Archive OCR and the page image of p. 635); the 1694 libretto and the 1694 score (Internet Archive OCR); IMSLP wikitext for *Céphale et Procris*; Radio France’s biography; Opéra Royal page; MusicWeb reviews (Jan 2019, van Veen); iTunes lookup records for the four albums; YouTube and Spotify oEmbed; National Gallery and Louvre records; Steve Wiberg’s edition (PDF glyph positions read with PyMuPDF against the staff lines, and the pages viewed as images); the Commons file of the Crépy medal (viewed).

**Corrections made**

- **Geo, birth point.** The entry placed her birth on the Île Saint-Louis and marked it “documented”. No source consulted names her parish; Cessac says only “Paris, baptisée le 17 mars 1665”, and the island is her father’s church. The point now marks Paris, and the childhood entry on the island is marked “inferred” (from her father’s post; the dedication of 1687 mentions only “chez mon Père”).
- **Geo, court entry.** Radio France dates her entry to the court to 1682, at seventeen; this is now in the entry beside Titon’s “barely fifteen” and Cessac’s three years. The 1680 start stays marked inferred.
- **Influence, Corelli edge.** van Veen wrote that she “does not follow his example slavishly, neither in the number of movements nor in the order of tempi”; the claim had turned this into “did not follow Corelli”. Reworded. The edge also said Brossard “copied” the sonatas in 1695; Brossard’s catalogue says she lent him the originals that year “pour les faire copier”, so both the edge and the Life now say that.
- **Works, Pastorale (EJG.20).** “Voices and instruments” was a guess; the dedication says only that she “mis une Pastorale en Musique”. Now “Scoring unknown”.
- **Sound, Violin sonatas.** “Manuscripts of 1695” became “dated about 1695” (Philidor: “1695 ca”); the Italian movement indications in source B are Brossard’s, which the sentence does not claim otherwise.

**Verified without change**

- Dedication of 1687 (exemplar B, complete text in the Philidor page): “quelques dispositions pour le Jeu de Clavessin … Vous ordonnâtes que l’on prist soin de m’y eslever”, the pastorale, five consecutive performances “formées par des Princesses même de la Cour”, the Dauphine, the divertissement for Mademoiselle de Nantes (“prest assez à tems”), the three operas; signed “Elizabeth Jacquet”; title page “De Mad.elle de La Guerre … ruë Guill.me Isle Nre Dame”; privilege January 1687; “Gravé par H de Baussen”; Mercure galant, March 1687, pp. 238–239, contents and the remarks on violin or viol and the lute (the review says she is “vingt ans”, which the page does not use).
- Titon (p. 635 by the page image; the date of death reads “27. Juin 1729”, “inhumée à Saint Eustache”): barely fifteen; Montespan “trois ou quatre ans”; Marin de La Guerre organist of Saint-Séverin; improvising for half an hour; the list of works; “jamais personne de son sexe …”; only son, at eight, died in his tenth year; last works unprinted with her heirs. Cessac: debut at five, three years with Montespan, marriage 23 September 1684, lessons and concerts on the island, first opera composed in France by a woman, cantatas 1708 and 1711, the third collection to the Elector of Bavaria, the Lesage duet, the Te Deum of August 1721.
- Mercure galant, August 1707, pp. 193–197 (full text in the Philidor page): Marly, duc de Tresmes, “deux jours après … à son petit couvert”, “Les Sieurs Marchand”, “elles ne ressembloient à rien”; Journal des sçavans, 1 August 1707, p. 496, and 7 January 1709, p. 13 (“d’un genre nouveau”, “le Badinage & le Merveilleux de la Fable”). Prices and places of sale on the 1707 title page; privilege 3 June 1707.
- Catalogue numbers against the Philidor list of works: EJG.01–04, 05–06, 08–13 (manuscript sonatas), 14–19 (1707 sonatas, in D minor, D major, F major, G major, A minor, A major), 20, 23, 24, 25–30, 31–36, 37–40, 50. The recueil page for cantata book I numbers *Esther* EJG.24; the list of works, which the page follows, numbers it EJG.25.
- *Céphale et Procris*: title page “Tragédie en musique, représentée par l’Académie Royalle de Musique … Au Palais Royal … Ballard … M.DCXCIV”; prologue for Flore, Pan and Nérée with nymphs, fauns, tritons; Act V (Procris struck by a shot from Céphale, libretto ends with Céphale’s speech, “Fin du cinquième & dernier Acte”); score title page “par Mademoiselle de la Guerre” and the dedication (“m’ont donné le courage d’entreprendre celle-cy”, signed “Jacquet”); IMSLP: “only Ballard’s partition réduite survives … the upper two instrumental parts and the basse continue”.
- Wiberg edition, both quotations, pitch by pitch: Chaconne l’Inconstante, bars 1–4 (RH D4–F♯4–A4 dotted minim; B3–E4–G4 minim and rest; the two-voice bars 3 and 4; LH D3 C♯3 B2 / rest E2 F♯2 tied / F♯2 G2 A2 B2 G2 / A2 minim with rest, A1 D2; two sharps; couplets labelled to “5e Couplet”) and Menuet, bars 1–4 (RH A5 B♭5 A5 G5 A5 / F5 G5 F5 E5 F5 / D5 E5 C♯5 / D5 D5 E5 F5 G5; LH D4 C♯4 / D4 A3 / B♭3 G3 A3 / F3 E3; no key signature). Both ABC examples match the edition. The inventory of dances in the suites matches the edition’s contents list.

**Left as it stood, with a caveat**

- “Marin de La Guerre died in 1704”: only Encyclopedia.com gives it; the English Wikipedia says only that her husband was among the relatives who died after her son. Kept; it should go if a second source is not found.
- *Jeux à l’honneur de la victoire*, “c. 1691”: van Veen’s review of 2019 says “in about 1691”; Philidor’s notice was not opened. Kept.
- The Opéra Royal page says she was presented to the king “at the age of eight”; the dedication and Cessac say five, and the page follows them. It also reports six performances of *Céphale et Procris*; the page gives no number.
