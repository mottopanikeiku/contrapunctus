## Editorial decisions

- Removed the former assertion that Wagner’s mother married Ludwig Geyer: the domestic relationship is established, the marriage itself is not securely documented.
- Kept the antisemitic publications and Nazi institutional afterlife in the main narrative, stated directly; did not use Jewish colleagues as a defence.
- The mid-April 1859 quotation is not given the adjacent excerpt’s 10 April date.
- No portrait/image files, ABC strings, poem originals, reading entries or lineage-array entries were changed.

## Fact-check (independent)

Second-pass check of `content/composers/wagner.mjs`: both letters re-read in the original German (one from the rendered page), catalogue numbers, dates and the Bayreuth and Rheingold claims re-opened. No text changes were needed in this file.

### Quotations confirmed

- “I must write neat scores for as long as I live on earth!” — Wagner to Liszt, 14 June 1854: “… ich muss saubere Partituren schreiben, solange ich lebe auf Erden!”, following “Suche mir keinen Schreiber: Mad. Wesendonck hat mir eine goldene Feder … geschenkt” ([Richard-Wagner-Refugium](https://www.richardwagner-refugium.de/der-ring-des-nibelungen/), which dates the letter and places it after the request for a copyist to write out the *Rheingold* score).
- “Only mediocre performances can save me! Completely good ones must drive people mad.” — Wagner to Mathilde Wesendonck, mid-April 1859: “nur mittelmäßige Aufführungen können mich retten! Vollständig gute müssen die Leute verrückt machen”. Read from the rendered p. 34 of the [Minden programme](https://wagner-verband-minden.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Programmheft-Tristan.pdf): the passage is signed “Mitte April 1859”; the neighbouring letters are dated 10 March, 10 April, 29 May and 29 October 1859. The prose’s “April 1859” stands.

### Facts confirmed (no change)

- Weinlig lessons, Geyer household in Dresden (with Wagner’s mother, marriage not documented), Würzburg–Magdeburg–Riga posts, *Die Feen* unperformed in his lifetime, *Das Liebesverbot* closed before its second performance (1836), Meyerbeer’s support for *Rienzi*, Bakunin and the May 1849 uprising, *Meistersinger* completed at Tribschen in 1867 and given in Munich on 21 June 1868, *Tristan* première on 10 June 1865 under Bülow, wedding on 25 August 1870 and the *Siegfried Idyll* on Christmas morning for Cosima’s birthday, 1876 festival with three cycles under Hans Richter, death on 13 February 1883 at Ca’ Vendramin Calergi: [Wikipedia, Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner) (used as a pointer; each point also appears in the sources below).
- *Siegfried Idyll*: first heard on the stairs at Tribschen, Christmas morning 1870; sold to Schott in 1878, the published version with multiple strings ([Wagner Werk-Verzeichnis note](https://www.richard-wagner.org/rwvi/en/news/news-committee/?collection_id=3075), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegfried_Idyll)).
- Catalogue numbers WWV 63, 70, 75, 86 (A–D), 90, 91, 96, 103 and 111, and composition spans (Tannhäuser 1842–45, Tristan 1854–59, Meistersinger 1861–67, Wesendonck songs 1857–58): [Cercle Wagner catalogue](https://www.cerclewagner.be/catalogue-complet/).
- *Rheingold*: 136 bars of E♭ major; six harps and eighteen anvils; Wagner tubas conceived in summer 1853; Ludwig II’s order of August 1869 overriding Wagner for the Munich première; Lilli Lehmann on the Bayreuth swimming machines (an iron rod “certainly 20 feet high”): [Berliner Philharmoniker, *Das Rheingold in seven stories*](https://www.berliner-philharmoniker.de/en/blog/salzburg-easter-festival-2026/das-rheingold-in-seven-stories/).
- Bayreuth: sunken, covered orchestra pit; side boxes dropped; building funds stalled until Ludwig’s credit (loan of 1874); first festival of 13 August 1876 with three Ring cycles ending in a large deficit; *Parsifal* on 26 July 1882; burial in the Wahnfried garden; Cosima enforcing the repertoire from 1886: [Bayreuth Festival, history](https://www.bayreuther-festspiele.de/en/festival/history/).
- Hitler: first entered Wahnfried on 1 October 1923; attended the festival each year from 1933 to 1940 ([Jeremy Adler, *London Review of Books*, 28:13](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v28/n13/jeremy-adler/my-dear-dear-friend-and-fuehrer!)).
- Portrait: Lenbachhaus dates Lenbach’s *Richard Wagner* 1881/82 ([Lenbachhaus](https://www.lenbachhaus.de/en/museum/collection/franz-von-lenbach)); Commons’ “circa 1882/83” is looser, so the caption follows the museum. Rackham’s plate: *The Rhinegold & The Valkyrie* (London: Heinemann, 1910), p. 4 ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rhinegold_and_the_Valkyries_p_004.jpg)). Leighton: *Royal Academy Pictures and Sculpture*, 1907, p. 70 ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edmund_Blair_Leighton_-_Tristram_and_Isolde_(Royal_Academy_of_Arts,_1907).jpg)).
