# Baroque era prose sources

For `content/eras/1-baroque.mjs`. Sources below were opened and read. The score captions describe the existing, unchanged ABC examples.

- [Music of the Baroque: What is Baroque Music?](https://www.baroque.org/baroque/whatis) — Venice’s first public opera house in 1637; continuo; concerto and ritornello; da capo aria; Handel’s London opera and oratorio; dance suites.
- [Bach-Archiv Leipzig: chronology](https://www.bachmuseumleipzig.de/en/neutral/johann-sebastian-bach-%E2%94%80-chronology) — Leipzig appointment in 1723, cantata duties and copying by pupils; the 1724 chorale cycle; the Collegium Musicum and Café Zimmermann from 1729.
- [Bach’s memorandum of 23 August 1730, Dutch translation](https://bach.wursten.be/documenten/bach-memorandumNL.htm) — requests to the Leipzig council, vacant instrumental posts and students supplying missing players. The essay paraphrases this document; it does not quote the translation.
- [Vivaldi Venice: violin teacher](https://www.vivaldivenice.it/en/biography-of-antonio-vivaldi/violin-teacher-p3) — employment at the Pietà in 1703; girls’ musical training and public performances.
- [Couperin: original prefaces](https://www.cantab.net/users/michael.behrend/repubs/couperin/pages/main.html) — the quotation comes from the **1713 first book**, not the 1722 third book: “j’ayme beaucoup mieux ce qui me touche que ce qui me surprend”. The essay’s “I much prefer what moves me to what surprises me” is our translation. The prefaces also discuss the harpsichord’s inability to swell individual notes, expressive timing and prescribed ornaments.
- [Netherlands Bach Society: BWV 870](https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-870) — the two collections of twenty-four prelude-and-fugue pairs, the first in 1722 and the second assembled around 1740–42. The essay avoids treating 1742 as a single unambiguous composition date.
- [BnF: Rameau’s Traité de l’harmonie](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb31174641b) — Paris, Ballard, 1722; the treatise’s subjects include composition and accompaniment.
- [Yale School of Music: natural-trumpet masterclass](https://music.yale.edu/2011/04/01/student-viewpoint-master-class-in-natural-trumpet) — valve-free natural trumpet, harmonic series and high-register playing.
- [Channan Willner, “More on Handel and the Hemiola”, Music Theory Online 2.3 (1996)](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.96.2.3/mto.96.2.3.willner.html) — sarabande rhythm, including second-beat weight and its interaction with hemiola.
- [Wallace Collection: A Dance to the Music of Time](https://wallacelive.wallacecollection.org/eMP/eMuseumPlus?module=collection&objectId=65042&service=ExternalInterface) — commission for Giulio Rospigliosi, future Clement IX; dancers, Father Time’s lyre, hourglass and bubbles. The earlier assertion that the patron supplied opera libretti is omitted: this catalogue entry did not establish it.
- [IMSLP: Handel, Ode for St Cecilia’s Day, HWV 76](https://imslp.org/wiki/Ode_for_St._Cecilia%27s_Day,_HWV_76_(Handel,_George_Frideric)) — composition and premiere in 1739; Dryden’s 1687 text; instrumentation and movements naming trumpet, flute and other instruments.

The existing poem, artwork metadata, era dates and notation remain unchanged. Generalised claims about fugue as a historical summit and a single affect governing all movements were replaced with descriptions of musical procedures.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 18 October 2026 against sources opened in this pass. No change to `1-baroque.mjs` was needed. Labels: MEAS = read in the cited text, score or arithmetic; EXT = stated by the cited institution or scholar.

**Quotations**

- Couperin, “j’ayme beaucoup mieux ce qui me touche que ce qui me surprend” — preface to the *Premier Livre* (1713), addressed to the public, not the 1722 third book. MEAS: <https://www.cantab.net/users/michael.behrend/repubs/couperin/pages/main.html>. The English is a faithful translation. The same preface says the harpsichord’s sounds cannot be swelled or diminished and describes the signs for ornaments (“agrémens”), as the essay says.
- Dryden, *A Song for St Cecilia’s Day* (1687), stanza 1, lines 1–10: wording matches the *Examen Poeticum* text (spelling modernised, “heavenly” for “Heav’nly”). MEAS: <https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/song-st-cecilias-day-1687>. The same text has “The trumpet’s loud clangor / Excites us to arms”.

**Facts confirmed**

- Venice’s first public opera houses opened in 1637. EXT: <https://www.baroque.org/baroque/whatis>.
- Bach appointed cantor of St Thomas’s, Leipzig, in 1723; senior choirboys helped copy parts; the second cycle (1724) is the chorale cycle; Bach took over the Collegium Musicum, based at Café Zimmermann, in 1729. EXT: <https://www.bachmuseumleipzig.de/en/neutral/johann-sebastian-bach-%E2%94%80-chronology>.
- Memorandum of 23 August 1730: *vacat* against the third trumpet, timpani, viola, cello, violone and third oboe; the gap “von denen Studiosis, meistens aber von denen Alumnis” filled; boys who play instruments taken from the second choir on feast days. MEAS: <https://bach.wursten.be/documenten/bach-memorandumD.htm>.
- Vivaldi hired as violin teacher at the Pietà in 1703; the girls’ orchestra concerts were a Venetian attraction. EXT: <https://www.vivaldivenice.it/en/biography-of-antonio-vivaldi/violin-teacher-p3>.
- *Well-Tempered Clavier* Part I dates from 1722; Part II was compiled around 1740–42. EXT: <https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-870>.
- Rameau, *Traité de l’harmonie* (Paris, Ballard, 1722), with books on composition and accompaniment. EXT: <https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb31174641b>.
- Handel, Ode for St Cecilia’s Day, HWV 76: composed 15–24 September 1739, first performed 22 November 1739 at Lincoln’s Inn Fields; flute, two trumpets and timpani in the scoring; soprano aria “The Soft Complaining Flute”; tenor aria and chorus “The Trumpet’s Loud Clangour”. EXT: <https://imslp.org/wiki/Ode_for_St._Cecilia%27s_Day,_HWV_76_(Handel,_George_Frideric)>.
- Sarabande second-beat emphasis. EXT: <https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.96.2.3/mto.96.2.3.willner.html> (Willner and Grave on “second-beat emphasis”).
- Poussin, *A Dance to the Music of Time*, about 1634–6; painted for Giulio Rospigliosi, later Clement IX; Time plays a lyre; one putto blows bubbles, the other holds an hourglass. EXT: <https://wallacelive.wallacecollection.org/eMP/eMuseumPlus?module=collection&objectId=65042&service=ExternalInterface>.
- Trumpet harmonics: partial spacing follows the harmonic series (octave, fifth, fourth, major third, … closing to seconds above the eighth partial). MEAS by arithmetic from the series; no source cited.
- Both ABC examples checked bar by bar: the suspension (F held over bass F then C, falling to E) and the descending-fifths chain (roots C–F–B–E–A–D–G–C; F–B is a diminished fifth). The captions match.

**Protected data**: no discrepancy found (Dryden `original`, hero date c. 1662–65 for the Vermeer, Poussin date).
