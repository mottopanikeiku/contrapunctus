# Ligeti — prose sources and quotation ledger

Research for `content/composers/ligeti.mjs`, 18 October 2026. Sources below were located through web search and their page text was read. Search summaries alone were not used as evidence. The numbered entries cover the biography, concise facts, musical descriptions, work notes, art pairings, portrait and recording recommendations.

## Preserved notation

Both ABC strings are unchanged. Neither is represented as a transcription.

- First example: an invented four-part chromatic canon; entries begin one crotchet apart, and the delayed versions accumulate adjacent pitches. The caption explicitly calls it a schematic and distinguishes its exposed lines from Ligeti’s much denser writing. Its notes can be described directly from the preserved ABC; the relation to Ligeti’s practice is supported by the *Requiem* commentary and Griffiths’s *Atmosphères* note.
- Second example: an invented A-only miniature followed by D, illustrating the first *Musica ricercata* piece’s pitch-class restriction. The rhythms and dynamics are expressly identified as illustrative. Nothing in the caption claims they reproduce Ligeti’s score.

## Source problems and deliberate omissions

- Direct reads of Schott’s main site returned HTTP 403. Its official Ligeti microsite and, for the quartet, Schott Music London supplied accessible primary catalogue records instead.
- The Prado collection and easy-reading pages returned HTTP 403; the attempted scholarly Bruegel catalogue returned a certificate error. The painting’s metadata and visible figures were checked in the accessible reference page; the opera connection has an independent Vienna State Opera source.
- Cambridge’s page for Ligeti’s foreword to Arom’s *African Polyphony and Polyrhythm* supplied bibliographical metadata but not the foreword text; its PDF link returned the same landing page. It was not treated as read evidence for the 1982 encounter. Serrou’s full firsthand interview supplies that account instead.
- The Pierre Gervasoni interview was readable in Josh Ronsen’s English translation, but the *Le Monde* original presented a client challenge. No quotation from that translation is used.
- The Southbank Aimard page and Presto’s DG review page returned HTTP 403. Accessible BBC Music Magazine, MusicWeb and label records supply the recording notes instead.
- The revision date is corrected from 1996–97 to 1996: Ligeti’s own [German account](https://de.gyorgy-ligeti.com/werk/le-grand-macabre) says “die Neufassung entstand 1996 in etwa zehn Monaten”, and [Music Austria](https://db.musicaustria.at/en/node/100814) independently lists 1996. The revised première was in 1997. The previous general statement about replacing spoken dialogue is left out for concision: Ligeti describes selective replacements and cuts, while retaining Nekrotzar’s proclamation and some shouted exchanges.
- Generic claims that the late études directly offered Adès a new rhythmic route, or that Ligeti’s orchestral fields directly shaped Saariaho, are omitted from the lineage prose because no sufficiently specific mechanism was established here. The protected lineage arrays remain intact.
- The broad claim that late works left young composers models preserving a sense of pulse is removed as an unspecific closing judgement. The life ends with Ligeti’s last concerto discussion and death.
- The protected reading list, identity fields, portrait file/focus, both ABC strings, artwork file, poem original and lineage arrays are unchanged. Obsolete `featured` is removed.
