## Editorial decisions

- No attested biographical event was removed for lack of a source.
- Moved the 1842 *Va, pensiero* encore caution out of the life. The previous page already described the nationalist-encore tale as unsupported; it has not been promoted into fact. The documented 1901 Toscanini commemoration takes its place in the margin.
- Kept Cairo’s 1869/1871 distinction and San Marco’s identity despite contradictory shorthand in some otherwise useful sources.
- Kept notation, image files, the Shakespeare original, reading entries and lineage-array entries unchanged.

### Quotations confirmed

- “Sedici anni di galera!” — Verdi to Countess Clara Maffei, 12 May 1858: “Dal Nabucco in poi non ho avuto, si può dire, un’ora di quiete. Sedici anni di galera!” ([Conoscere Verdi, Rizzo](https://www.conoscereverdi.it/rubrica/1)).
- “… nei versi ricordati bene che non vi deve essere parola inutile: tutto deve dire qualche cosa” — Verdi to Francesco Maria Piave, 4 September 1846, on *Macbeth* ([Treccani, Epifani](https://www.treccani.it/magazine/lingua_italiana/speciali/melodramma/8_Epifani.html)). The translation in the life is faithful.
- Verdi’s Macbeth reply after Lady Macbeth’s death, “È il racconto di un povero idiota; vento e suono che nulla dinota” (Act IV): same Treccani page.

### Facts confirmed (no change)

- Conservatoire refusal 1832, Lavigna until 1835, Barezzi’s backing, marriage 1836, Virginia (b. 1837), Icilio (b. 1838), *Oberto* at La Scala in November 1839, election to parliament in 1861 on Cavour’s invitation ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giuseppe-verdi/)); Margherita’s death in June 1840 and *Un giorno di regno* in September 1840 (previous ledger).
- *Va, pensiero* at the transfer of the remains: Toscanini, chorus of 820 (“900” in other accounts), 27 February 1901 ([Giuseppe Verdi, last years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi)); the Casa di Riposo built 1896–99 and opened 1902.
- Brera: Hayez’s *Portrait of Alessandro Manzoni*, 1841, commissioned by Teresa, fifteen sessions, the round tobacco tin copied from life ([Pinacoteca di Brera](https://pinacotecabrera.org/en/collezioni/collezione-on-line/portrait-of-alessandro-manzoni/)). Chassériau’s picture: oil on wood, 1850, RF 3897, “Othello racontant à Desdémone sa vie (Shakespeare, Othello, acte I, scène 3)” ([Louvre](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010060228)). Boldini’s Verdi: pastel, 1886, Galleria Nazionale d’Arte Moderna, Rome ([Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/wd/FgEQF8uoob7fhg)).

### Score examples

- *La donna è mobile*: the ABC pitches and rhythm agree with the notated theme in Commons’ [“La donna e mobile theme”](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_donna_e_mobile_theme.png), given there a minor third lower in G major: three repeated notes; dotted semiquaver–demisemiquaver–crotchet turn; repetition a step lower (D♯ D♯ D♯ | F♯ E C♯ | C♯ C♯ C♯ | E D♯ B in B major). The tenor’s written treble-clef line sounds an octave lower, as the caption says. The IMSLP Ricordi PDFs sit behind a bot check and were not used.
- *Libiamo*: the Escudier piano-vocal score ([Internet Archive, 31761034914499](https://archive.org/details/31761034914499), “Brindisi nell’introduzione”, Allegretto, M.M. ♩.=69, B♭, 3/8, vocal entry on the page after the orchestral introduction) shows two quaver rests and the pickup F, then D as a dotted crotchet on “bia-”, then D–F–D on “mo li-bia” and D–F–D on “mo ne’ lie”. The caption (“pickup F jumps a sixth to D … returning lightly between the same two notes”, B♭ major, 3/8) agrees.

### Protected data

- ABC X:2 writes the first D as a tie into the next bar (`!>!d3-` then `d`). In the printed score the second D carries its own syllable (“mo”), so the two Ds are separate notes; the ABC tie merges them into one long D. Not edited; the caption wording is unaffected.
- Macbeth speech original agrees with the [Folger text, 5.5](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/macbeth/read/5/5/) word for word; only the punctuation differs.
