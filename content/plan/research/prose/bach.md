## Editorial limits

No identity, reading-list, poem, image or lineage-array changes were needed. ABC strings are preserved. Teaching is described as practical work; no anecdotal personal utterance has been manufactured to enliven it. The uncertainty over temperament, choir size and the Goldberg commission belongs in the margin, not in every sentence of the life.

## Fact-check (independent)

Checked 40 factual claim-groups: the 31 rows in the original table and the nine supplementary groups below. Repeated statements across the biography, work list, sound discussion and captions were checked together. Both prose quotations and both preserved hymn extracts were checked against fetched texts; all work entries were included.

### Change

- BWV 846 caption ‘rests added after the final E complete the example’ → ‘the final E is lengthened and rests complete the example’. The displayed extract makes the E at bar 2, beat 3 a quaver, whereas Bach continues with semiquavers. The original-pitch claim is correct. The protected ABC is unchanged; the caption now acknowledges the small rhythmic adaptation as well as the inserted rests. [Opening two bars, reproduced score](https://tonic-chord.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Bach-Prelude-and-Fugue-No.1-in-C-major-BWV-846-Analysis-2.png); [subject and stretto locations](https://tonic-chord.com/bach-prelude-and-fugue-no-1-in-c-major-bwv-846-analysis/).

### Quotations confirmed

- ‘They are all born musicians.’ Faithful translation of ‘Insgesamt aber sind sie gebohrne Musici’: Bach to Georg Erdmann, Leipzig, 28 October 1730. The surrounding descriptions of his wife’s soprano, eldest daughter and family ensemble agree with the same paragraph. [Letter transcription](https://bach.de/leben/brief.html).
- ‘a strange authority with little devotion to music’. Faithful translation of ‘eine wunderliche un der Music wenig ergebene Obrigkeit’ in the same letter, 28 October 1730. His wish for another appointment and the shortfall of more than one hundred thalers also appear there. [Letter transcription](https://bach.de/leben/brief.html).
- Luther’s first stanza of *Aus tiefer Not* and the supplied translation: the page’s ‘Dein gnädig’ Ohren kehr zu mir’ wording is present in the [Lutheran hymnal](https://lg.concordiabuch.de/lieder/315/aus-tiefer-not-schrei-ich-zu-dir). The [Bach Foundation](https://bachipedia.org/werke/bwv-38-aus-tiefer-not-schrei-ich-zu-dir/) supplies the 1524 attribution and the variant actually sung in BWV 38.
- Nicolai’s *Wachet auf* extract and supplied translation: words, sequence, author and date confirmed against the [Netherlands Bach Society’s text and commentary](https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-140/).
